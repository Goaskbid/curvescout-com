What it actually does

and why it beats a map app
🌀

It optimises for corners, not minutes

A navigation app hands you the fastest line and calls it done. CurveScout ranks roads on bend density, flow, surface, scenery and how cleanly you escape traffic - then still shows you the numbers behind the score so you can disagree with it.

🎩

A butler, not a dashboard

Before you turn a wheel you get one verdict - go, caution, or not today - written from live weather, the freezing level against the summit, daylight left versus real riding time, open fuel and camera density. It tells you the reason, not just a number.

🏔️

Pass outlook before you climb

Nothing ruins a day like riding two hours to a closed barrier. We read the forecast at the summit itself - freezing level, snowfall, coldest hour - and put the official road-status links right next to the verdict.

⛽

Everything along the road

Fuel with opening hours and 24/7 flags, bike shops for when something breaks, fixed speed cameras, police and hospitals, food that is open right now, swim spots, and viewpoints that tell you which summits you are looking at.

📖

A roadbook you can actually use

Numbered checkpoints with leg and cumulative distance, gradient-shaded elevation, expected speed painted onto the map, and GPX with proper fuel and summit symbols for your Garmin - or one tap into Google Maps.

✈️

Works where the signal doesn't

Save your shortlist and the roadbooks come with you. Alpine valleys eat reception; your checkpoints, warnings and map pins keep working anyway. Add it to your home screen and it behaves like an app.

Three steps

  1. Say where you are. One tap on your location, or type any town, pass or coordinate.
  2. Pick or swipe. Take the ranked board, explore the alternatives side by side, or swipe keep-or-pass until your shortlist looks right.
  3. Read the briefing, then ride. Verdict, roadbook, services, GPX. Helmet on.
