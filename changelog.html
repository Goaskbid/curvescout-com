What changed on CurveScout

CurveScout changes often. This page says what changed, when, and why it matters on the road. Fixes to facts come first, because a wrong distance or a wrong name costs a rider a day.

Rides offered to search engines

Not every ride is offered to search engines at once. We start with the rides measured on the stored road, with complete facts and a real description, and add more every Monday, best first, never more than a tenth at a time. Every ride stays in the planner whether or not a search engine has it.

  1. 2026-11-19 - 121 more rides offered to search engines, best first (now 1,322).

Releases

  • 24 Nov 2026 Long multi-day trips no longer run the phone out of memory (the road network now needs about half as much). The height profile fits the screen instead of scrolling sideways, and the day ribbon of a trip wraps. The border marker on the map shows plain words. Mountain pass pages: the live status follows the same rules as the app (a report that has ended shows as ended, a future one as scheduled), "Watch this pass" and a daily "Today on the passes" board; a cycle-route closure no longer marks a pass closed.
  • 23 Nov 2026 (night) New: Mountain passes - 388 road passes with height, road and season, the rides that really cross each one (checked on their stored road), a small map of those roads around the summit, the passes nearby, and live status where a public source covers the area. Ride pages and country pages link the passes they cross. Also in this release: fuel tips tied to the route they were checked for, stored ride roads drawn only for the stops they were routed for, five round rides no longer called one-way.
  • 23 Nov 2026 Ride navigation: "whole rest of the ride" now hands every stop still ahead to Google Maps, in parts where the phone needs them - none left out. Bend calls are spoken only where the bend check clears them or where you switched on bend notes from map data; a turn now cuts in over a bend note. Fuel tips say what the map shows - no price claims, and no "last fuel" unless the map check was complete. 267 named roads now describe themselves with their own facts; the N-260 in the Pyrenees is called by its own name.
  • 21 Nov 2026 2044 rides that went out and back the same way are now real loops: the same stops, but the way back takes another road, motorways and ferries left out; a ride out to one place now often comes home by another road too. Distances, riding times, legs and pages are measured on the new road. Nearby sights no longer list other rides' turning points.
  • 22 Nov 2026 Ride navigation: every button on the riding screen now says what it does - Find, Voice, Centre, Hand off, More - under its symbol, in English, German and French.
  • 22 Nov 2026 Smoother start on phones: the Scout button no longer covers the first field, it follows you once your start point is set. Bigger header links and map buttons for thumbs on the ride and Thursday pages.
  • 22 Nov 2026 Ride navigation: the start screen now offers the way to ride with the phone locked or on CarPlay / Android Auto - the ride handed to Google Maps with every stop still ahead, or as a track for OsmAnd. The privacy note says it plainly: the route is worked out on your phone and your stops are sent nowhere.
  • 22 Nov 2026 Ride navigation: the arrival time now allows for typical traffic on the road ahead (weekday rush hours, the Friday getaway, the Sunday return) and says so when it matters; live traffic is one tap away in the menu. Pocket mode saves more battery: with the screen black, the map is no longer redrawn while the voice keeps guiding.
  • 22 Nov 2026 Cleaner ride page on phones: the road summary reads on one line, the map switches (avoid motorways, tolls, ferries, start, preview, offline) sit in two tidy rows. In the ride navigation, the hand-off to Google Maps for the rest of the ride is labelled for what it gives you: live traffic.
  • 22 Nov 2026 Tap a ride card (or its Map button) and the ride's own little map opens under its picture, the road drawn through it. In the ride navigation, routes are now worked out on your phone from CurveScout's own road network. The app's code is delivered more compact. All 20,206 rides that go out and back the same way are being routed as real loops, motorways and ferries left out; they change over once the routing is done.
  • 22 Nov 2026 Ride navigation, clearer and calmer: points of interest as a slim strip at the bottom edge instead of a card on the map, every sentence spoken in full (a story interrupted by a turn carries on afterwards), fuel only as an insider tip or as the last station before a long stretch without, a warmer way of telling, each story linked to its Wikipedia article and licence. The map uses only OpenFreeMap tiles.
  • 21 Nov 2026 Rally calls on scenic stretches: on a ride's scenic stretches and on long curvy runs the navigation calls the bends ahead like a rally co-pilot (left 2, right 3), from map data and only with a good GPS fix - you can switch it off in the menu.
  • 21 Nov 2026 Ride navigation tells better stories: the big sights first (the lake before the village school), about 30 seconds of real history for each, never the same one twice in a year. It also names pass tops with their height, biker places, a restaurant at the top and, now and then, an independent fuel station that is often cheaper. Nothing opens over the map any more.
  • 21 Nov 2026 Hosting move prepared: the site can now also be served by Cloudflare, with the same addresses, the same saved routes and the same live pass reports.
  • 19 November 2026 - Copyright and terms of use rewritten: what is ours, what you may do, and what needs permission. AI training on CurveScout content is not allowed. The best-rides table is free to read and cite.
  • 19 November 2026 - Every ride name is now checked against the map in every build. Sharing images for each country, drawn from that country's own rides. Weekly release of more rides to search engines. More links between guides, pass calendars and Thursday rides.
  • 19 November 2026 - Road closures stop showing as closed when the report ends. 65 rides that end where they start no longer say "One way". The navigator rejects a route line that misses a stop. Data files are always checked against the server, so nobody rides on yesterday's text.
  • 18 November 2026 - 601 rides from the old city collections were named after places their roads never reach. They now carry the names of the towns they really visit, with matching photos and stop names. The install pop-up is gone.
  • 17 November 2026 - One high point per ride, the same everywhere. Shorter, specific titles and descriptions. A real legal page, a press page, a site index and an open table of the best rides.

Found something wrong? The press and corrections page says how to tell us, and the sources page says where every fact comes from.
