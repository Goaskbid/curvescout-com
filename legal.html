What CurveScout will not do

  • No account, ever, to plan a ride. Everything before you set off works with nothing typed and nothing stored. It costs us the ability to email you.
  • Nothing paid into a ranking. A road is ranked on how it rides, never on who pays. Ads, where you allow them, sit beside the rides and never change their order.
  • No invented roads. Where the geometry has not been traced we say so instead of drawing a line between two points. It costs us a fuller-looking map.
  • No borrowed routes. Every ride here comes from open data or our own scouting. We will not take another app’s catalogue, however easy it would be.
  • No faces, no number plates. Photographs show places. A person who happened to be photographed on a pass is not our content.
  • No pretending to know. When a figure is estimated it says estimated, and when a source is stale it says when it was last checked.

Ride at your own risk

Every route, distance, altitude, timing, road status and pass opening on this site is information, not advice, and may be wrong or out of date. Conditions change hourly: weather, ice, gravel, roadworks, closures, landslides and local rules. Posted signs, barriers and the instructions of police or road authorities always win - over this site, over your navigation device, and over anything you read here.

Riding a motorcycle carries risk that no website can remove. You alone are responsible for your speed, your machine, your licence and insurance, your fitness to ride and your judgement on the day. What CurveScout is and is not liable for is set out once, under Terms below. If a route looks wrong on the ground, believe the ground.

Where speed cameras are shown, they are shown for awareness, not to help you avoid enforcement. In countries where showing or carrying camera warnings is restricted - Switzerland, Germany, France, Austria and Italy among them - CurveScout does not show them at all. The legal speed limit applies whether a camera is there or not, and in some countries carrying or using a camera-warning device is itself an offence. Camera positions come from OpenStreetMap, are frequently incomplete, and their absence means nothing.

What is stored, and where

Everything the app remembers is kept in your own browser (localStorage) on your own device. There is no server-side account and no database of users. That is:

  • Your saved rides - the ones you starred.
  • Your last search - the start town, range and riding style, so the board is there when you return.
  • Your bike figures - litres per 100 km and the pump price you typed.

None of it is sent to CurveScout: this is a static site with no CurveScout server, account or user database. Clearing your browser storage, or the button below, erases everything this site keeps on this device.

What the button cannot erase. When you plan or ride, your browser asks outside services for a map, a route, a search result, the weather or a photograph. Each of them receives that request - your IP address and the places or points you asked about - and may keep it in its own server logs for the period it states (listed under "Who your browser talks to" and "Where the data comes from" below). The button does not reach those logs. You can ask each service directly; write to us at the address below and we will tell you which services a feature uses and help you reach them.

Cookies

This site sets no cookies of its own. Advertising cookies are only set if you allow advertising in the consent dialog, which you have not. There are no tracking pixels and no social plug-ins. Nothing is counted. Advertising is the one thing that needs your consent, which is why the consent dialog asks before any ad loads. Your saved rides and settings are kept in your browser’s storage because you asked the site to remember them - a save, a language, a switch. Storage that is strictly necessary for a feature you explicitly request is exempt from consent under the EU ePrivacy rules (Art. 5(3)); Swiss law (Art. 45c Telecommunications Act) requires that you are told about it and can refuse it, which this page and the erase button above do. When you open a saved ride, its coordinates are sent to the routing service to draw the road, like any other route.

Spoken directions and read-aloud

Spoken text uses either the CurveScout studio voice, which runs in your browser once its model has downloaded, or a voice your browser or operating system provides. Some of those system voices are network voices (for example voices named “Google …” or “… Online”): with one of them, your browser sends the sentence being spoken to that voice’s provider. CurveScout prefers voices that run on your device when they are about as good, and never sends the text anywhere itself. Your device’s speech settings decide which voices exist.

CurveScout Ride: a riding aid, not a certified navigation system

CurveScout Ride shows the planned route on the map and reads it aloud. It is a planning and riding aid. It is not a certified navigation system and not a safety device. Turns, bend notes and rally calls, pass tops, sights, fuel tips and warnings come from open map data (OpenStreetMap), Wikipedia and public feeds; they can be incomplete, out of date or wrong. Road signs, signals, barriers, the instructions of police and road authorities, and what you see in front of you always come first. If a spoken instruction does not match the road, ignore it.

Set the route and the settings before you ride and operate the phone only when you are stopped. Mount it securely and only where the law of the country you ride in allows. Keep the volume low enough to hear traffic.

Bend notes and rally calls are read from the shape of the road on the map. They are an indication, not a measurement, and never a safe speed. Speed cameras are announced only in countries where that is lawful, never in Switzerland, Germany, France, Austria or Italy, and only if you switch them on.

Fuel tips name stations, never prices; whether a station is open, or cheaper, is not guaranteed. The stories about places are shortened adaptations of Wikipedia articles under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 licence; each story names and links its article and the licence, and the shortened text is shared under the same licence.
