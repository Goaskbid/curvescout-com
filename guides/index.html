Start with a region, then check the road

These country collections include source-backed planning references. The wider reading library remains global; source coverage is not equally complete everywhere.

Switzerland motorcycle routes

Start with the particular passes and the start town, not a country-wide best month. Use the ride cards below to compare recorded distances, then open the roadbook to check which climbs and return legs are actually included.

Austria motorcycle routes

Separate a scenic toll-road visit from an unrestricted all-day journey. On an Austrian mountain-road plan, the admission time and vehicle conditions can matter as much as the length of the route.

Norway motorcycle routes

Build a Norway ride around the named road sections, ferry connections and actual overnight stop. A scenic name or a route line does not answer whether the whole journey is available on your travel day.

United States motorcycle routes

Use a road-specific plan, not a single US riding season. The featured collection is a starting point; each park road, state highway and connecting leg needs the authority relevant to that section.

Canada motorcycle routes

Start with the province and the exact road corridor. The Canadian collection includes different regions; a British Columbia road message cannot establish access elsewhere in Canada.

Australia motorcycle routes

Choose the region before the month. A touring plan in the tropical north and one on a southern coastal road need different seasonal context and separate road-status checks.

New Zealand motorcycle routes

Read the complete route and the relevant highway conditions together. A map can suggest a connection while an incident, work zone or local-road closure still changes what is possible.

Japan motorcycle routes

Use regional seasonal context and road-specific access information. A single best month for all Japan hides differences between the intended regions and high roads.

Argentina motorcycle routes

Choose the specific road corridor and identify each authority involved. A national-road reference alone cannot confirm a provincial connection or an international crossing.

Chile motorcycle routes

Treat a long scenic corridor as a sequence of road sections and stops. The country collection helps discover routes; it is not a single access statement for every road in Chile.

South Africa motorcycle routes

Begin with the road and province. For Western Cape rides, check the provincial closure information; do not carry its status into a different part of South Africa.

United Kingdom motorcycle routes

Use the authority for the road and nation in your plan. A national traffic directory is a starting point, while small-road conditions may need a more local source.

France motorcycle routes

Use the French roadbook to identify the exact department and road sections before checking a pass. A national traffic overview and a local high-pass notice answer different questions.

Italy motorcycle routes

An Italian mountain itinerary can cross several road-owner areas. Use the route to identify each pass and approach, then consult the relevant authority rather than one national season label.

Germany motorcycle routes

Treat the motorway transfer and the scenic road as separate planning questions. Choose the actual roadbook first, then check the authority responsible for each part of the journey.

Spain motorcycle routes

Use the actual road and travel date to check Spanish traffic information. A broad regional weather expectation cannot establish whether a particular section is available to your motorcycle.
