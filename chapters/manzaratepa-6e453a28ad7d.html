Manzaratepa

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Tepa means mound, and in southern Tajikistan the plains are dotted with them: hills built up over centuries from the remains of mud-brick towns, each layer burying the one before. Manzaratepa, near the town of Vose' in the Khatlon region, is one of these ancient settlement mounds, part of a network of sites in the fertile valleys between the Panj and Vakhsh rivers that were inhabited from the Bronze Age through the Kushan and early Islamic periods. Archaeological work has revealed traces of fortifications, houses and ceramics, and the sites are part of Tajikistan's long, rich and little-known history along the Silk Roads.

The landscape is a broad valley of cotton fields, orchards and villages, with the foothills of the Pamir rising to the east and the mountains of Afghanistan visible to the south on clear days. Summers are long and very hot, winters mild, and the irrigation canals that feed the fields have been dug and redug since ancient times. Kulob, the main city of the area, has a long history and the mausoleum of Mir Sayyid Ali Hamadani, a fourteenth-century Sufi scholar from Persia.

For riders, Kulob is the gateway to the southern route of the Pamir Highway, which heads east from here toward Kalai-Khumb and follows the Panj river along the Afghan border, one of the great adventure roads of the world. The roads in Khatlon itself are mostly paved, with fast, straight stretches and some rough sections, potholes and unpredictable traffic, including livestock and slow-moving trucks. Police checkpoints are common, so keep your documents handy.

Vose', about five kilometres away, has fuel and basic shops, and Kulob, around twenty kilometres away, has a wider range of services. Fuel quality can be variable, so fill up at larger stations when you can. Food is simple and filling: plov, the rice dish with meat, carrots and onions, shashlik, grilled skewers, fresh bread and fruit from the orchards, especially melons and grapes in late summer.

April, May and October are the months to aim for. Summer heat can be extreme, often above forty degrees. Climb the mound for the view; pottery shards in the dust underfoot are a quiet reminder of just how long people have farmed this valley, and they belong exactly where they lie.
