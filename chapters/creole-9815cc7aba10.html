Creole

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Creole is a village you ride through almost without noticing, and yet it belongs to one of the most unusual landscapes in the United States. East of the Texas border, along the Louisiana coast, the marsh is broken by low ridges of oak and live oak that rise a metre or two above the flat. These are cheniers, the old beach ridges left behind as the Mississippi's sediment pushed the coast outward. Highway 27 follows some of them, and the live oaks bent by Gulf winds are one of the real sights of the Creole Nature Trail.

The area has deep Cajun and Creole roots. Families settled these ridges in the nineteenth century for the cattle and the fishing, and the marsh and cheniers still shape their lives. The Rockefeller Wildlife Refuge, a large state-managed area nearby, protects the wetlands and carries on research into marsh management and waterfowl. Visitors are welcome in some areas, though a motorcyclist should check what is open before riding off the main road. The place names alone, Creole, Grand Chenier, Little Chenier, give you a sense of the French heritage that still runs through the language and food.

The riding is flat, straight, and quiet. The road runs between marsh and ridge, with the horizon always visible, and the sky takes up most of what you see. It is not a technical ride, and many people will find it slightly hypnotic. The risks are crosswinds, heavy rain from afternoon storms, and the occasional animal on the road, so keep your speed sensible and your eyes open. In spring and autumn the oaks fill with migrating birds, and the sight of a tree dripping with warblers on a cool morning is something you will remember.

Services are scarce along this part of the route. Cameron, around thirty-six kilometres to the west, is itself small, and the bigger towns of Sulphur and Lake Charles are a long way back. Fill your tank well before you set out, bring plenty of water, and be ready for mosquitoes whenever you stop near the marsh. The best seasons are the cooler ones, and a clear winter day can be lovely. High summer is hot and humid, with the added risk of tropical storms, and the road can flood quickly in a surge.
