Kovirke Area 2

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The village of Dannewerk gave its name to the great monument that surrounds it, and it is a small place with a big story. About a thousand people live here, in a scatter of farms and houses along the road, and a museum on the edge of the village does what a museum should: it explains why the grass banks in the fields matter. Inside you find a model of the whole system, tools and weapons from the excavations, and a clear account of the many phases of building, from the timber palisade of the eighth century to the brick wall of the twelfth.

This section, Kovirke Area 2, lies a couple of kilometres south of the village, in farmland, and the best way to appreciate it is to see it after the museum. The Kovirke is generally understood as a later addition, built to strengthen the defences in the Viking Age, and it forms an extra line south of the older walls. In 2018 UNESCO recognised the whole complex, along with Hedeby, as a World Heritage Site, and visitor numbers have grown, although the place is still very quiet.

The ride into Dannewerk is easy, with small roads through farmland and low hills, many of them lined with hedges and the occasional avenue of oaks. Traffic is mostly local, and the surfaces are good. The land is open and the wind constant, so lean into it. Spring brings fields of yellow rape, summer brings tall corn, and autumn brings misty mornings and a smell of plough.

Groß Rheide and Kropp lie nearby, and Kropp, with about six thousand people, is the nearest place with fuel stations and supermarkets. Dannewerk itself has a pub or a bakery at best. Take a coat, because the museum is small but the walk afterwards is worth doing, and the weather can turn. Stand on the rampart for a minute and look south across the fields. That is the view the sentries had, and the horizon has barely changed.
