Sossusvlei Desert Route point 2

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Most people know this stretch by one number. Dune 45 is named for its distance from the park gate at Sesriem, forty-five kilometres, and it is about as far as many visitors go on a first morning, which means it is climbed, photographed and trampled long before the heat arrives.

The Namib sand sea is among the oldest living deserts on Earth, and the dunes are red because of iron oxide that coats each grain and darkens with age and exposure. The oldest, deepest-coloured dunes are the ones furthest from the coast. They are shaped by winds that blow in different directions through the seasons, which is why the crests are so sharp and the star dunes have several arms. A rider who has only seen sand in pictures is usually struck by how silent it is, and by the way a single footprint can survive for hours.

The riding is almost an afterthought, but it is a very good afterthought. The tarmac is smooth, wide and nearly empty before nine o'clock, with long curves that follow the flats between ridges. The corners are gentle, but sand can drift across them, and the sun low in your eyes at dawn makes it hard to see the thin tongues of it. Keep your speed honest, stay off the soft verge, and expect the occasional oryx at the edge of the road.

If you plan to climb the dune, leave the bike on firm ground and go barefoot or in light shoes, because riding boots fill with sand immediately and the last part of the ridge is a slow, comic staircase. The walk takes the better part of half an hour and the view from the top is of a corrugated red world running in lines to a horizon of heat haze.

Water, hat and sun protection matter more than anything else here. There is no shade at all, and the sun at midday can turn a casual stop into something you regret. Shortly after sunrise is the sweet hour.
