Traismauer - Kastell Hufeisenturm

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

At Traismauer, one Roman tower still stands high enough to be seen from the street. The Hufeisenturm, the horseshoe tower, is a rounded corner tower of the old cavalry fort Augustianis, named for its shape, and it was part of a defensive wall that ran for hundreds of metres around the Roman garrison. The brick and stone, laid in neat courses, survived because later builders used it as part of the town's medieval walls, and it has been preserved and restored in recent decades.

The fort stood on the Danube Limes, the northern frontier of the Roman Empire in this region, where the river formed a natural border with the Germanic peoples to the north. Soldiers here patrolled the river, watched for trouble and collected tolls, and they were supported by a civil settlement nearby, where traders, craftspeople and families lived. UNESCO placed the western Danube Limes on the World Heritage list in 2021, and this tower is one of the fragments that shows what the whole system looked like.

The walk around the tower is short, but it gives a good sense of scale. The walls rise several metres, and the pattern of the stones shows where repairs were made. Nearby is the Roman Gate, a surviving medieval structure which incorporates earlier material, and a small museum in the town explains the finds. The setting is quiet, with narrow streets and well-kept houses, and few visitors.

Traismauer lies in the Traisental, a region of orchards, vineyards and farmland, on the doorstep of Lower Austria's wine country. The roads here are quiet and smooth, with easy curves and long views over fields, and cyclists share them in summer. Fuel, groceries and a few inns are available in the town, which has about 2,750 inhabitants, and larger towns like Tulln and St. Polten are within easy reach. The Danube itself is a short ride north, and a ferry or bridge takes you across to the other bank.

Ride in spring, when the fruit trees are in flower, or in autumn, when the grape harvest brings tractors and the smell of must. Midsummer afternoons can be hot, and thunderstorms are occasional.
