Oquossoc

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Oquossoc is hardly a village at all, more a crossing of roads and water where Rangeley Lake narrows toward Mooselookmeguntic. Two long lakes meet here, a lake plane or two sits at the dock, and a few buildings gather around the junction of Routes 4 and 16, with Route 17 branching off south. If you blink you will miss it, but the junction is the key to the whole region, and you will pass through it more than once on any loop.

The name is thought to come from an Abenaki word for something like the place where the water narrows, and the surroundings suit it. Mooselookmeguntic, the big lake to the west, has a name usually translated as moose-feeding place, and it lives up to it: at dawn and dusk the shallows of coves and inlets often hold a cow moose and her calf, standing knee-deep and pulling up water plants. Loons nest on the quieter shores, and the lakes freeze for months in winter, long enough that people have fished through the ice here since sporting camps first opened.

Riding is the reason to stop. Route 4 and Route 16 run through the village and along the lakeshore with long, easy curves and glimpses of blue between the spruce, with little traffic outside summer weekends. Route 17 south climbs to the Height of Land, a short ride up, where a pull-off gives one of the best views in Maine: Mooselookmeguntic and Richardson lakes laid out below, with mountains rolling off to the horizon. The descent toward Rumford is a long, winding run through forest, with sections of rough pavement and frost heaves.

Services here are slim. The village has a small store and a couple of places to eat in season, but the dependable fuel and supermarket are in Rangeley, a few miles east, so fill up there. The nearest big town is a long way off, and cell coverage is patchy, so plan accordingly. The best months are June, September and early October, and in May the black flies can be fierce. In winter, snowmobiles are the main traffic and the roads are rarely clear, so consider this a summer and early autumn destination only.
