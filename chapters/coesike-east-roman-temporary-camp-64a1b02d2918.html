Coesike East Roman temporary camp

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The Romans were a marching people, and every night on campaign they dug in. Coesike East is one of those night camps, a temporary enclosure of banks and ditches thrown up by a column on the move, probably around the time the frontier was being organised, long before anyone thought of building a wall. Nothing much stands above ground now. What survives is a faint rectangle in rough pasture, visible mostly to people who already know what they are looking for, or to aerial photographs in low winter sun.

The corner is lovely. You are on the high ground south of the South Tyne, with Haydon Bridge about six kilometres away in the valley and Hexham, the real town with the supermarkets and the fuel, some thirteen kilometres off. Acomb lies to the east. Haydon Bridge is a village of a thousand-odd people with a couple of shops and cafés, and it sits on the river with a handsome old bridge, but if you need serious petrol, take it in Hexham rather than assuming.

The roads in this country are what make the stop. They climb out of the valley onto moorland with the Pennines blue to the south, and the lanes are narrow, hedged or walled, and sometimes unfenced across open fell, so sheep appear around corners with total confidence. Gradients are modest but the exposure is real, and wind comes off the moor with a sharp edge. Surfaces vary from smooth to broken at the edges, and frost lingers late in the shaded dips. Early morning in May, with lapwings and curlews calling over the fields, is about as good as northern England gets.

Not far to the north runs the Stanegate, the road the Romans built before the wall, linking Carlisle with Corbridge, and many of the forts and camps around here belong to that earlier frontier line. Understanding this makes the landscape click: the wall was a later, grander idea, but the real military life of the area was the road and its stopping places.

Bring a flask, wear something windproof, and if you do get off the bike for a look at the field, remember it is working farmland, so keep to the gates and tracks.
