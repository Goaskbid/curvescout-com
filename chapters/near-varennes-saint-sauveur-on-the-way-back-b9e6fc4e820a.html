Near Varennes-Saint-Sauveur, on the way back

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The Bresse is flat, green and fiercely proud of its chickens. This stop lies deep in it, between Varennes-Saint-Sauveur and Saint-Trivier-de-Courtes, close to the line where Saône-et-Loire meets the Ain. The landscape is a quilt of small fields, hedges, damp meadows and woods, crossed by slow rivers that drain north towards the Seille and the Saône.

Look at the farmhouses. Many are timber-framed with brick infill, low and long, and some still carry the strange cheminée sarrasine, a tall, ornamented chimney with a little roof, a regional speciality that has nothing to do with Saracens despite the name. Saint-Trivier-de-Courtes is known for them, and several are preserved in the area. The other icon is the Bresse chicken, with its white feathers, blue feet and red comb, the first poultry in France to win its own appellation.

Louhans, the nearest larger town, holds a big weekly market under long medieval arcades, with live poultry sold alongside everything else. It is one of those French market scenes that has barely changed in a century.

As for swimming, the Bresse is not a lake country. The rivers are slow and muddy, and most ponds are private fishing waters. Look for designated bathing areas in local leisure parks, which open in summer, or simply accept that this is a rest stop rather than a plunge.

The riding is relaxed. The roads are straight or gently bending, often lined with trees, with good visibility and little traffic. The danger comes from farm machinery, mud at field entrances after rain and the occasional loose dog. Wind can be strong across open ground.

Fuel and shops are in the larger villages and in Louhans. Order chicken if a restaurant offers it, cooked in cream with morels if you are lucky.
