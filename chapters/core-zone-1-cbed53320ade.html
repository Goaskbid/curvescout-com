Core zone 1

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Core zone is bureaucratic language, and the thing it describes is simply the heart of the hill: the central area of the Qal'at al-Bahrain archaeological site, where the oldest and most important remains lie. It sits on the north coast of Bahrain, between the shore and the palm gardens, and a short distance from the suburbs of Jidd Hafs and Madinat Isa. The idea of a core zone, with a buffer around it, is how UNESCO protects its sites from being swallowed by the surrounding city, and this one was marked out when the site was inscribed in 2005.

What is under your feet matters more than what is on the surface. Excavations here have cut down through seven main layers of occupation, going back to around 2300 BC, and they show a town that grew into a city, with walls, a street plan, houses, a harbour, and a grand building that may have been an administrative centre. Dilmun was a trading society that sat between the Mesopotamian cities and the civilisation of the Indus, and the pottery, seals and weights found here prove it. The Bronze Age is not abstract here: you can see the stones that built it.

Walk slowly. The ground is dusty, uneven and exposed, and the sun reflects hard from the pale stone. Look for the exposed walls, the foundations of houses, and the places where archaeologists have left their trenches open. A hat, sunscreen and water matter far more than a good camera. In the evening, the sky turns pink and the shadows lengthen, and the details of the ruins stand out.

The approach is easy. From Manama a coastal road runs west along the shore, flat, straight, with roundabouts and moderate traffic, and the site is signposted. Winter is the right season. In summer the heat is merciless, and the walk to the tell becomes an endurance test. If you have a choice, come late in the afternoon, and stay for the lights at sunset, when the old fort on the summit glows and the Gulf behind it fades from blue to grey.
