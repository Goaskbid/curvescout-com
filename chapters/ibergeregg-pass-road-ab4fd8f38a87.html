Ibergeregg pass road

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

At 1,406 metres, the Ibergeregg is not one of the great Swiss passes, but it is one of the most enjoyable short ones, and local riders know it well. It links the Schwyz basin, with its two jagged Mythen peaks, to the Alpthal and Oberiberg side, and it climbs through a landscape of cow pastures, scattered chalets and mixed forest. The road is narrow in places, with sharp bends, moderate gradients and sudden long views over the Schwyz valley as it opens up behind you.

Schwyz gives its name to the whole country. The town was among the early members of the Swiss Confederation, and the Swiss flag and the name Switzerland both grew from it. The charter of 1291, often cited as the founding document of the Confederation, is kept in a museum in the town. You do not need to care about history to enjoy the ride, but it adds a bit of weight to the view as you climb.

The road itself rewards a steady rhythm. On the Schwyz side the first hairpins come quickly, and the surface can be patchy after winter. Near the top it eases into a rolling stretch across the pasture, with the ski lifts of the nearby Hoch-Ybrig area visible in summer as lines on the hillside. The descent toward Oberiberg is gentler, with more open views and a calmer feel. Traffic is usually light on weekdays, though sunny weekends bring cyclists and other bikers, and cattle sometimes cross the road or graze beside it.

Weather deserves respect. Cloud collects on the ridge, and fog can appear quickly, reducing visibility to a few metres. The pass can close in heavy snow, usually in winter, so check conditions in shoulder seasons. In summer, afternoon thunderstorms are common, so start early.

Schwyz, about six kilometres from the foot of the climb, is the nearest town of any size, and Einsiedeln is just beyond the other side. There are mountain restaurants near the top that serve simple food in season, and the benches on the pasture are good for a quiet break.

The cow bells are part of the experience. In summer they ring across the slope, and with the wind and the smell of cut grass, you feel properly in the Alps.
