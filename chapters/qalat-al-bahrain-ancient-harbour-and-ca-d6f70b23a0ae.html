Qal’at al-Bahrain - Ancient Harbour and Ca

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Qal'at al-Bahrain is a hill, and the hill is made of cities. For about four thousand years, people have built on this spot on the north coast of Bahrain, and every generation has laid its houses and walls on top of the one before, until the mound, called a tell, rose over the surrounding palm gardens. Archaeologists have peeled back the layers to find the capital of the Dilmun civilisation, a trading power of the Bronze Age that linked Mesopotamia with the Indus valley, with Bahrain as the entrepot where copper, pearls, timber and grain changed hands. UNESCO listed the site in 2005.

On top of this ancient mound stands a stone fort that most visitors photograph first. The Portuguese took control of Bahrain in 1521 and built or rebuilt a fortress here, and later rulers, Persian and Arab, held it in turn. The walls are of rough local stone, with a moat that once filled with seawater, and the towers look out over the Gulf. The little museum at the foot of the site shows the objects found in the excavations, including pottery, seals and tools, and gives a clear sense of how far back the story goes.

The setting is flat and hot, so go late in the day. The fort stands above a stretch of shore where fishing boats still launch, and from the walls you can see the skyline of Manama to the east, with the towers of the financial district rising out of the haze. At sunset the light turns the old stone the colour of honey. Palm gardens surround the base of the tell, still watered by springs, and the smell of damp earth and sea is strong when the wind comes from the north.

Riding here is easy: the coast road from Manama runs along the shore, with straight, flat stretches, roundabouts and a steady flow of cars, and the site is clearly signposted. Parking is simple. Fuel is available in Manama and the nearby suburbs of Jidd Hafs and Madinat Isa, all within a few minutes. Opening hours change, so check before you go. Winter is the season for walking the ruins, and a cold drink from the cafe nearby tastes better than anything else on a hot day.
