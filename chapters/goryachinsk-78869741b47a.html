Goryachinsk

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Goryachinsk takes its name from goryachiy, hot, and the hot springs are the whole story. The village sits on the eastern shore of Lake Baikal in Buryatia, a short distance from Turka, and has been a spa for generations, with mineral water emerging from the ground at high temperature and being piped into bathing houses and sanatoria. The first thing you notice is the contrast: a huge, cold, deep lake on one side and steaming water on the other.

The ride here from Ulan-Ude follows the road across the Selenga plain and then along the shore, with the horizon slowly filling with the Barguzin mountains. It is a gentle road, mostly straight with easy bends, mixed forest and farmland, and the driving is relaxed. The surface is generally fine but can have rough patches, and in summer you share it with holiday traffic and the occasional cow. The shoreline here is sandy and shallow in places, and the sunsets across the water are long and orange. Early autumn and late spring are the quietest times.

Goryachinsk is a spa town in the Russian tradition. People come to take the waters for their health, staying in old sanatoria with long corridors and treatment rooms, and the local mineral springs are said to help with joint and nerve problems. A local belief holds that the water has curative properties, and visitors often bring bottles home. A railway never arrived here, and the road remained the only link for much of the twentieth century, which is partly why the place retains a sleepy character. Cafes serve Baikal omul and Buryat dumplings, and you will hear both Russian and Buryat spoken in the shops.

The nature around the village is gentle and wide. Pine forest runs along the shore, the beaches are pale and sandy, and the smell of warm resin mixes with the sulphur tang of the springs. The lake is cold and clear, and in summer swimmers brave the water despite the temperature. The park around Goryachinsk shelters birds and small mammals, and in autumn migrating geese pass overhead.

Fuel is limited in the village, so top up in Ulan-Ude or at the larger stations along the road. Bring a towel if you want to try the springs, and allow time for a relaxed evening. It is one of the calmest stops on the Baikal shore.
