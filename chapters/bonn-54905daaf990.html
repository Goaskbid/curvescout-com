Bonn

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Bonn is a university city that spent forty years pretending to be a capital, and it carried it off with a modest air. After the war it became the seat of the West German government, and the former federal quarter along the Rhine still has the clean, low-key look of ministries designed not to impress. Before that, Bonn had been a seat of the Cologne prince-electors, whose baroque palace now houses the university, and it was also the birthplace of Beethoven, whose small house in the old town is one of the most visited composer museums anywhere.

A good time to be here is early April, when the cherry trees on the Heerstrasse and the lanes of the Altstadt burst into pink. The blossom is a local ritual, and photographers appear in force. For the rest of the year the town has a calm university rhythm, with students on bicycles, cafes on the market square and an easy riverside promenade along the Rhine. Take the little ferry across to Beuel if you want a view back to the town and the Siebengebirge, the line of old volcanic hills on the far bank, topped by the ruin on the Drachenfels.

For riders, Bonn is a staging post rather than a destination in itself. The city has over three hundred thousand people, so fuel, parking and food are never a problem, although the traffic is slow at rush hour and the one-way systems can confuse. The good riding begins out of town, either south along the Rhine toward Bad Honnef and the Siebengebirge, or west into the Eifel foothills where the roads tighten and climb. The Ahr valley, once a famous wine stretch and badly hit by floods in 2021, lies roughly an hour away and is recovering.

If you want one quiet hour, walk through the botanic garden behind the palace at Poppelsdorf. It is shaded, green and rarely crowded, and it gives you a little of the old electoral Bonn without a ticket queue. Then head out and ride.
