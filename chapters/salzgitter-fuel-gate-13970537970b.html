Salzgitter fuel gate

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Salzgitter is not a town for postcards, and that is exactly why it makes a good fuel gate. It was built around steel. In 1937 the Nazi government founded a huge ironworks here to exploit local iron ore, and the city that grew around it was a collection of villages merged into one, with a sprawling industrial landscape. Today the steelworks still operates, and the skyline is a mix of chimneys, rail yards and housing estates, softened by green belts and lakes.

With over a hundred thousand people, the city is large enough that fuel is never an issue. Stations line the main roads, supermarkets are open late, and there are cafes and restaurants of all kinds. It is a place to fill the tank, check the bike, and eat something sensible before heading into the hills of the Harz or the quiet countryside to the south. Riders arriving from the north across the flat plain of Brunswick find it a useful marker.

There is more to the area than industry. Salzgitter-Bad, the old spa and salt-springs district of the city, has a pretty historic centre with half-timbered houses, and a church, and the name of the city comes from the brine springs that were worked here for centuries. The surrounding countryside has low hills, the Salzgitter Heights, covered in forest, with a few quiet roads that give a good ride for those with time.

The roads in the area are a mix of fast main roads, good for covering distance, and minor lanes through villages and fields. The surface is generally good, the traffic is heavy around the works at shift times, and the weather can be changeable, with the wind sweeping over the flat land from the north. In winter fog is common.

The best choice is to treat Salzgitter as a base and a service stop rather than a destination, and to enjoy the contrast with the forest roads that wait to the south. A hearty meal here costs less than in the tourist towns of the Harz, which is a welcome bonus.
