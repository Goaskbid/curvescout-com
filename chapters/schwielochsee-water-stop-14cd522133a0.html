Schwielochsee water stop

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Schwielochsee is a broad, shallow lake south-east of Berlin, where the Spree widens out before continuing north, and it is among the larger natural lakes in Brandenburg. The shoreline is a mix of reeds, pine woods and small villages with sandy beaches, and the water is warm enough in summer for easy swimming. It is a lake for holidays rather than drama, and none the worse for that.

The surrounding country is quiet, flat and half-forgotten. To the south lie the heaths and the former lignite landscapes of Lusatia, and to the north the forest of the Dahme-Heideseen. The nearest towns are small: Friedland, with about thirty-six hundred people, lies a little to the south, and Beeskow, sixteen kilometres away, has a medieval castle and a town wall. Both have fuel and basic services, and Beeskow is the better place for a proper meal.

Riding here is easy and unhurried. Roads run along the lake edge and through the pine forest, mostly smooth and empty, with the occasional sandy patch near the shore. The curves are mild and the traffic light. In summer, the weekends bring campers and caravans to the lakeside sites, and you will want to avoid the main road toward the camping areas on a Friday evening.

A swim stop is simple. Several villages have public beaches with a small kiosk or a restaurant, and in the afternoon the water is warm and clear. Bring a towel and a little cash, as card payment is not universal here. After a swim, a plate of fried fish and potatoes tastes better than it has any right to.

The light over the lake in the evening is a soft, flat gold, and the reeds go dark against the sky. It is an excellent place to slow down at the end of a long day, with the smell of woodsmoke and warm sand drifting across the shore, and no one in a hurry.
