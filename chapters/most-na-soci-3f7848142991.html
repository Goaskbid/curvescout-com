Most na Soci

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Most na Soči is a small village at a place where water gathers. Here, the emerald Soča river meets the Idrijca, and the combined flow is held back by a dam built in the late 1930s, which created a long and beautifully coloured reservoir. The lake stretches back into the hills between Tolmin and Kanal, and the water often takes on a milky turquoise that makes a long photo stop worthwhile. The houses cluster around the old bridge, which gives the place its name, since most simply means bridge.

The Soča valley is well known to riders for the river itself, a clear, alpine stream that runs through narrow gorges and wide gravel flats in tones of blue and green found almost nowhere else. The ride from Tolmin leans along the water on gentle curves, with views of forested slopes, small side valleys, and a few old stone bridges. The road surface is usually good and the curves are easy, making it a relaxed afternoon ride rather than a technical one, and the valley is rarely too crowded outside July and August. In summer, expect kayakers and rafters, and in spring, meltwater turns the river even more luminous.

The valley has a darker history, too. During the First World War it was the Isonzo front, where Italian and Austro-Hungarian armies fought twelve battles in two and a half years, and the hills above are still marked with trenches, tunnels and cemeteries. The nearby town of Kobarid has a museum devoted to the war, and Ernest Hemingway wrote about the retreat from here in A Farewell to Arms. When you look at the peaceful water you should keep that in mind.

For fuel, go to Tolmin, about three and a half kilometres away, a modest town with stations and shops, since Most na Soči itself is very small. Cafes and guesthouses by the water serve local food: trout from the river, cheese from the highlands, and a dish of frika, a crisp potato and cheese cake that is a mountain staple. The nearest big city is Nova Gorica, well down the valley toward Italy. Spring and early autumn are the loveliest times, with softer light and fewer visitors.
