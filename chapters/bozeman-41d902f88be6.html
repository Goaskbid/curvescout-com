Bozeman

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Bozeman lies in the Gallatin Valley, a broad, flat bowl of farmland at about 4,800 feet, ringed by mountains on every side. The Bridger Range stands to the northeast, the Gallatin Range to the south and the Tobacco Roots to the west, and snow stays on the high summits well into summer. The town started in the 1860s as a supply point for miners and settlers, named after John Bozeman, who blazed a trail from Wyoming to the Montana gold fields through the hunting grounds of the Sioux, a route that led to war.

Today it is a lively college town with some forty-odd thousand residents, centered on Montana State University. Main Street is lined with restaurants, breweries, outdoor shops and cafes, and the energy is that of a place that is growing fast. The Museum of the Rockies is well known for its dinosaur collection, including a remarkable set of Tyrannosaurus rex skeletons found in eastern Montana, and the paleontologist Jack Horner, who advised on the Jurassic Park films, worked there for decades.

For riders, Bozeman is a gateway. To the south, Highway 191 follows the Gallatin River through a canyon of cliffs and pine toward Big Sky and Yellowstone, with long bends and the river running beside you. The scenery is fine and the traffic can be heavy in summer, with trucks and rafting buses. Bridger Canyon Road to the north gives a quieter option through ranchland and aspens, and Hyalite Canyon, south of town, has a reservoir and waterfalls at the end of a narrow road. Bozeman Pass to the east, on the old route of the Northern Pacific Railway, is the quick way to Livingston.

Weather deserves respect. Summer days are warm and dry, with cool nights, and afternoon thunderstorms can build over the ranges. Snow can fall in any month at higher altitude, and the high passes see closures in winter. Wind funnels through the valley, especially in spring and fall.

Services are excellent. Fuel is available at stations all over town and in nearby Belgrade, and the city has motorcycle shops, grocery stores and plenty of breakfast spots. If you are heading toward the park, fill up before the canyon, since stations are sparse along the Gallatin.
