Salva - Modruț

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Salva lies where the Someșul Mare valley opens into a broad bowl in Bistrița-Năsăud county, with the Rodna mountains standing to the northeast and a ring of low wooded hills closer in. The town is a railway junction, a lorry stop on the road through the valley, and the kind of place that looks ordinary until you ask about its past.

The Năsăud district around here has an unusual history. In the eighteenth century the Habsburg crown raised a border-guard regiment among the local Romanian peasants, who received land and some privileges in return for military service on the frontier, and this produced villages unusually proud of their schools and their literacy. That tradition shaped the region's character, and you still see it in the large village schools and the way people talk about education. The railway line from Salva runs north to the timber country of the Vișeu valley, where a narrow-gauge forest railway has become a tourist attraction.

As a ride, Salva is the doorway rather than the destination. The main road along the Someș is wide, well surfaced and busy with trucks, and it is quicker than it looks, so watch for overtaking on blind bends. Turn off onto the smaller roads and the pace drops, with tight villages, orchards and farm traffic, and surfaces that go from good to ragged in a few hundred metres. Summers are warm without being oppressive, and the best times are May and early autumn, when the slopes are green or gold and the Rodna ridges show snow or the first dusting of winter.

Fuel and food are easy here. Salva and the neighbouring Nimigea de Sus are big enough for stations, shops and cafés, and a plate of sarmale, cabbage rolls, or a grilled mici with mustard comes cheap and quickly. The spot rewards a short stop and a look at the hills, not a long stay, but it is a handy base for the roads into the Rodna foothills.
