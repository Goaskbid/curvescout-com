Olympic Peninsula Loop point 4

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Past Lake Crescent the road turns west and wanders on through country that feels properly remote. This section runs through the valley of the Sol Duc and the Soleduck River area, with thick forest, ferns, moss on the trunks and the occasional logged hillside showing where timber has been cut.

The road is where the pleasure lies. Highway 101 here is a classic two-lane mountain-edge highway with long straights broken by flowing curves and a few tighter turns where it follows a creek. Traffic thins out the further west you go, and on a weekday morning you can have sections almost to yourself. The surface is good for the most part, though the logging trucks wear grooves in places, and the shade means damp patches can hang around well into the day.

The Olympic Peninsula has a long Native American history, and the Quileute, Makah, Hoh and Klallam peoples have lived on this coast for thousands of years. Logging and fishing shaped the later settlement, and the timber industry still shapes the economy: small mills, log trucks and clear-cuts in various stages of regrowth mark the landscape. The tension between old-growth protection and logging was the defining political fight of the Pacific Northwest in the 1980s and 1990s, and you can read it in the scenery here, with protected forest on one side of a ridge and working timberland on the other.

Wildlife is the extra. Black-tailed deer step out at dawn and dusk, elk occasionally appear in meadows, and bald eagles hunt over the rivers. Slow down at those times of day, and watch the verge. The climate is mild but wet; even in summer a cold front can roll in from the Pacific, and temperatures drop quickly. A packable layer, a visor that shields rain, and a bit of patience for slow vehicles all make the ride better.
