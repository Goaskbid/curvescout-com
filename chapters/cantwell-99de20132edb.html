Cantwell

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Cantwell is a small community of a couple of hundred people at the junction of the Denali Highway and the Parks Highway, in the shadow of the Alaska Range, and it is a place where railroad, highway and wilderness converge. The Alaska Railroad passes through, and some trains stop here, and the town sits in a broad valley of spruce and tundra with big mountains on its southern horizon. The country has long been home to Athabascan peoples, who travelled and hunted through this country for thousands of years.

The mountain that dominates the region is Denali, the highest peak in North America at over twenty thousand feet, whose Athabascan name means the high one. It lies to the north-west, and the national park that protects it begins about twenty-seven miles up the Parks Highway. On a clear day, the view from the highway near here can include the peaks of the range, but Denali itself hides behind clouds more often than not, and visitors who see it are considered lucky.

The ride in from the east along the Denali Highway ends here, after a long run across open tundra and past glacial lakes, and Cantwell feels like a very welcome sight, with its lodges and cafes. The road surface improves as you approach, and the pavement comes as a relief after the gravel. Beyond the junction, the Parks Highway runs north into the mountains and through the pass toward the park entrance, with views of forest and peaks, and the traffic is much heavier, with tour buses and campervans.

You can find basic services in Cantwell, including a few places to stay and eat, and fuel, but it is a small place, so supplies and opening hours can be limited. Larger centres are Healy to the north and Talkeetna to the south, each a good distance away. Weather can change rapidly in the Alaska Range, with rain, snow and wind arriving even in summer, so be ready. The days are long in summer, and the evening light on the mountains can be glorious, while autumn colour comes early, in late August. Winter here is long, dark and very cold.
