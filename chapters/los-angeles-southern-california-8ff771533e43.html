CURVESCOUT / PLACE WRITE-UP

Los Angeles / Southern California

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Joshua trees and creosote bush spread out toward a horizon cut by the San Bernardino Mountains to the south, and on a clear morning the snow on the higher ridges looks borrowed from another state. Adelanto, a town of some thirty-odd thousand, is the sensible place to sort fuel and a breakfast burrito; Victorville sits right next to it with more of everything.

This is where the Big Bear loops begin their long haul upward. Riders coming from the Los Angeles side usually cross the mountains through Cajon Pass, drop into the desert, and then turn back up toward the rim, so the flat ground here is a kind of warm-up lap, a lull before the road starts to climb through pines. The Mojave River, which spends most of its life underground, makes a long arc nearby, and old Route 66 once strung together the little desert towns along it. If you have an appetite for a detour, the National Trails Highway still carries traces of that road.

Be honest about the weather. Summer afternoons out here are hot enough that the bike wants to sit in the shade, and spring brings wind that leans on you from the west with real conviction. Early morning is the right hour to leave: the air is cold and clean, the light is low and long, and you reach the mountains before the heat does. In winter, check the pass conditions before you climb, because the rim roads above can ice over while the desert floor sits in sunshine.

The finish of a day here tends to be quiet. You come back down to a plain full of warehouses and solar fields, with the last light turning the mountains pink, and the feeling is of having been somewhere genuinely high. Water is easy to buy in town, and it is worth carrying more than you think you need, because the gap between gas stations can feel longer on a hot road than it looks on a map.
