Malchin lake view

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Malchin is a lake town in the heart of what is called Mecklenburg Switzerland, a name that makes visitors from the Alps smile, because the highest hills here are not much more than a hundred metres. But the name has a point. After the flat plains of northern Germany, this area of rolling moraine, deep lakes and beech woods does feel like a small world of its own, shaped by the last ice sheet that left behind a landscape of ridges, hollows and scattered boulders.

The town sits between two lakes, the Malchiner See and the larger Kummerower See, with the Peene river flowing out through meadows toward the Baltic. Two big brick churches, St. Maria and St. Johannis, stand over the old town, and their towers can be seen from far across the water. The streets are quiet and the buildings a mix of Gothic, baroque and plain functional, reflecting centuries of trade in grain and cattle. The region also produced the Low German writer Fritz Reuter, born in nearby Stavenhagen, whose humorous stories of country life are still loved here, and a museum in his birthplace tells his story.

The lake view is the point of the stop. From the shore, the water spreads out in a broad sheet, with reeds along the edge and fields rising gently on the far side. In summer, sailing boats and canoes dot the surface, and in autumn mist lies over the lake in the morning. Birds are plentiful, with swans, grebes, herons and, if you are lucky, a white-tailed eagle circling high above the water.

For riders, the roads here are among the most pleasant in the north. Narrow tree-lined lanes wind between lakes and manor houses, with gentle gradients and good views, and the surface is generally decent. Many of the villages have a manor, a church and a few cottages, and the estates, once the seat of local nobility, give the landscape a faded grandeur. Traffic is light, and a relaxed pace is the right one. Watch for farm vehicles, and for deer on the roads at dusk.

Malchin has about eight thousand people, with fuel, shops and cafés, so it is easy to stop here. Neukalen, a smaller town nearby, has a quiet harbour and a few places for food. Smoked fish and local cake are the usual snacks, and you can eat them on a bench by the water.

A mild afternoon in late spring or early autumn is the time to come, when the light is soft and the lake is calm.
