Seowon

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Dosan Seowon is a school, and it is the school of a man who is still on the thousand-won note. Yi Hwang, better known as Toegye, was the greatest Neo-Confucian philosopher of Korea's Joseon dynasty, and after he died in 1570 his students built this academy beside the Nakdong river where he used to teach. It opened a few years afterwards and became the model for hundreds of others. The site was listed as one of nine Neo-Confucian academies of Korea on the World Heritage list in 2019.

The buildings are plain and beautiful: low tiled roofs, wooden floors, open halls where students sat in rows to read the classics, a shrine behind, and a library of old woodblock prints. A dormitory lies to one side and a small pavilion looks out over water. Everything is deliberately modest, because the Confucian ideal was learning without luxury. The setting does the rest, with the river below, the hills behind and a stand of old trees in front, so that the scholars could study in the company of landscape.

The approach is the best part for a rider. From the city of Andong, famous for its mask dances and its food, the country roads run north through orchards, rice terraces and small farming villages, then wind up along the shore of a reservoir formed by a dam on the Nakdong. Traffic is light, the bends are gentle and the scenery changes with the seasons. Andong has plenty of fuel, and the smaller villages have little else, so fill up in town.

Andong is the place to eat. The local dish is jjimdak, braised chicken with glass noodles and vegetables, and Andong soju is a stronger, older kind of liquor than the clear green-bottle stuff. Salted mackerel, ganjaengi, is another local pride.

Come in autumn when the leaves turn on the slopes, or in spring when the plum and cherry blossoms scatter. The site is quiet, so you may have the whole courtyard to yourself. Take off your shoes where signs ask, and keep your voice low, as the place expects.
