Chantilly to

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Mickey Mouse is the unavoidable neighbour here. On a summer afternoon the roads around this part of the Seine-et-Marne are full of coaches and rental cars, and the air in the car parks smells of fried food and hot tarmac.

Riding here is mostly a matter of survival and patience. Roundabouts follow one another, the surface is smooth because everything is new, and the road layout is designed around cars and buses, not around anyone who wants to enjoy a corner. If you reach this point on a weekday morning or after dark, it is far easier. A Sunday evening, when the day trippers all head home at once, is the worst time to be on the roads around Esbly or Val d'Europe.

Even so, the place has a history you can feel if you look. Before it was a new town, the Brie plateau here was farmland and small villages, and a few old centres survive among the housing estates, with a stone church and a lane of old houses. Esbly, to the north, sits on the Marne and has a railway line into Paris and a pleasant riverside, so if you want to stretch before the last section it is a good place to do it. The Marne itself winds through meadows and poplars, and is the nicest corner of the area.

Fuel and food are easy, almost too easy. Val d'Europe has a shopping centre and plenty of chain restaurants, and the larger stations stay open late. Esbly, about five kilometres away, has a more human scale, with a baker, a small supermarket and a station. Parking is rarely a problem unless you are heading into the theme park itself, in which case you will need to follow the signs and pay.

As a finish to a day on the Chantilly forest roads, this is the moment when the quiet ends and the capital's gravity pulls you in. The best advice is to take a last look at the green behind you, and then commit to the final stretch.
