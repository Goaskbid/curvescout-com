FUGLENAES Fuglenes

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Fuglenes is the headland at Hammerfest where one of Europe's long scientific ideas comes to an end. In the early nineteenth century the astronomer Friedrich Struve ran a chain of survey points stretching from the Black Sea to the Arctic Ocean to measure the shape of the Earth, and the northern end of that chain is marked here by a stone monument. It is a modest column on a rocky point, but it carries a World Heritage listing, and the idea of someone carrying a triangulation across ten countries makes you look at the little stone differently.

Hammerfest is often called the northernmost town in the world, a claim that depends on how you count, and it is one of the places that proudly says so anyway. It was among the first towns in Europe to have electric street lighting. In 1944 the retreating German army burned and destroyed nearly everything, so almost all the buildings are post-war, plain and practical, built in a hurry for people returning to nothing. The town's setting, with steep hills dropping into a harbour, is strong enough to carry it. Across the water the gas plant on Melkøya has become a feature of the skyline, and the polar bear society in the town hall is a curious little museum of Arctic hunting.

Getting here is a ride in itself. The road from Skaidi crosses the island of Kvaløya, climbs through bare tundra with scattered birches, and drops towards the sea with big views of the fjords. Surfaces are generally good but the weather can change quickly, with fog or wind coming off the Barents Sea, and in winter the road is often closed or convoy-driven. Reindeer are everywhere, standing on the tarmac in the long summer evenings, and you need to treat them as a regular hazard.

The town has fuel, shops and cafes, and the harbour has fish on the menu, often cod and Arctic char. Summer light lasts around the clock for weeks, which makes a late ride feel quite unreal, and in the darker months the northern lights are common over the harbour. Wrap up warmly even in July, because a wet wind from the north will cut through thin gear.
