Valongo Wharf Archaeological Site

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

There is hardly any monument here, and that is part of what makes it powerful. The Valongo Wharf in Rio's port district is a sunken stone landing, unearthed in 2011 under the street when the area was being renovated, and it is believed to be the principal place where enslaved Africans arrived in the Americas. Historians estimate that hundreds of thousands of people came ashore at Valongo in the first half of the nineteenth century, after being shipped across the Atlantic in conditions that many did not survive. It was built in 1811 to move the trade away from the city centre, and was later covered by a new quay built for the arrival of a bride for the emperor. The stones lay hidden for the better part of two centuries.

UNESCO recognised the site in 2017 as a place of memory. The surrounding area, the Pequena África or Little Africa, was where freed people and their descendants built communities around the port, with samba, candomblé and street culture taking shape in this neighbourhood. Nearby sites like the Cemitério dos Pretos Novos, a burial ground for those who died soon after landing, add to the sense of how close the trade was to everyday life in the city.

For riders, this is an urban stop. The port district has been redeveloped as Porto Maravilha, with new tram lines, wide avenues and big museums, and the streets can be confusing with one-way systems and construction. Park only in a place you can see or that is attended, as street crime happens in parts of central Rio, and leave valuables behind. Weekday daytime is much busier than Sunday, when the district is quiet and easier to look around. Fuel is available in the city, though stations are fewer in the dense centre, so fill up on the outer roads.

Give it time. A guided walk through the district brings more than a quick photo, and the nearby Museum of Tomorrow and the Rio Art Museum are close enough to visit by the same afternoon. A cold drink and a pastel, the fried turnover sold everywhere, will do for a break. The Niterói side of Guanabara Bay is visible across the water, and the long bridge links the two cities. Be respectful: this is a memorial as much as an archaeological site.
