Osinovets

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Osinovets is a small settlement on the western shore of Lake Ladoga, the largest lake in Europe, and the place where the Road of Life reached the water. From here, in the winter of 1941 and 1942, trucks drove onto the ice and across the lake to the east shore, carrying food into the besieged city. The distance was about thirty kilometers, and the route was bombed and shelled. When the ice thinned, vehicles broke through and disappeared. In the summer, supplies went by barge and ship, and the harbor at Osinovets was one of the main loading points.

The area keeps the memory. The Osinovets lighthouse, a tall tower that stands on the shore, has guided ships since the early twentieth century, and nearby monuments and a museum recall the siege. A memorial called the Broken Ring, near Kokorevo, consists of two curved arcs of concrete with a gap between them, and it stands where the road met the lake. In winter, a short stretch of the ice route is sometimes recreated, though the lake's ice is unreliable and no one should assume it is safe.

The lake itself is a presence. Ladoga is wide and often rough, with a horizon like the sea and sudden storms, and the shore is a mix of sand, stones and reeds with pine forest behind. In summer, the water is cold and clear, and in the long evenings of June and July, the sun sets late and the sky stays pale. The air smells of pine resin and water, and the wind from the lake can be cold even on warm days.

For riders, the roads to Osinovets are simple and flat, running through forest and past small villages, and they can be busy on weekends. Surfaces are generally acceptable, though frost damage leaves holes and cracks. Plan to ride in summer or early autumn, since the shoulder seasons bring mud and fog.

There are few services at the shore, and fuel is best bought in Vsevolozhsk or in Imeni Morozova, the larger town sixteen kilometers away. A small cafe or shop near the lighthouse may be open in summer. Mosquitoes can be fierce, so cover up in the evening.
