Rougon

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Rougon is a handful of stone houses piled on a rock above the Verdon, with the shell of a medieval castle on top and a view that justifies the whole detour. You come to it on the D952, the north-rim road that climbs from Castellane, and the village sits just off the highway at about nine hundred metres, a short climb away from the traffic. Most visitors drive past on their way to the viewpoints. Those who stop find a quiet street, a small church, and cats asleep on warm walls.

The ruined keep above the village is worth the short, steep walk. From the top you look straight down on the point where the river leaves the open valley and enters the first real narrows of the gorge, with the water far below, the colour of a swimming pool in an old postcard. Wild thyme and juniper grow among the stones, and in warm weather the whole hillside smells of herbs. Griffon vultures, reintroduced to these cliffs in the 1990s, circle on the thermals, and if you are lucky you will watch one close enough to count its feathers.

The road between Castellane and Rougon is a good one, a series of long, flowing bends with the gorge appearing and vanishing on one side and pine woods on the other. Surfaces are reasonable but patched, and rock fall is a possibility after storms. Summer weekends bring crowds to the viewpoints, so the best riding is in the morning or in the shoulder seasons, when the light is clear and the air cool. Winter can bring snow and ice at this altitude, and the high roads may be closed for a time, so check before you set out in the cold months.

The village has a small café or two in season, but not much else, and the better places to eat are back in La Palud or Castellane. Rougon works best as a pause for a few minutes of silence before the road carries on towards the famous viewpoints a little further along.
