McKenzie Bridge

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

McKenzie Bridge is a handful of buildings along the McKenzie River on the western flank of the Oregon Cascades, and it is one of those places that remains a village because the forest around it is too large to leave room for anything else. The river here runs cold and clear, a startling blue-green over pale stones, between banks of Douglas fir, western hemlock and cedar. The air smells of wet bark and rain, and the moss on every trunk and rock gives the whole landscape a soft, cushioned look.

The McKenzie is one of Oregon's best-loved rivers, and the drift boat, the high-sided, flat-bottomed boat built for fly fishing in whitewater, is said to have been developed on it. Anglers still come in numbers for the trout, and the river also draws rafters, hikers and photographers. Upstream, two waterfalls, Sahalie and Koosah, plunge over black lava ledges, and further along a pool called the Blue Pool, fed by underground water that emerges through the lava, glows an unreal turquoise. The river vanishes beneath a lava flow in places and reappears, a trick of the volcanic geology that makes the landscape stranger than it first looks.

The ride on Oregon 126 and the road up to McKenzie Pass is good. The highway follows the river through forest in gentle bends, with the sound of water beside the road and little traffic outside holiday weekends, and it forms part of a loop with the high-country route over the pass. Rain is frequent, particularly in autumn and winter, and the road can be damp and shaded, with mossy patches and fallen needles in the corners, so keep the pace sensible. Summer is the driest time, though morning mist hangs along the river, and September brings cool, clear days.

Services are thin. There are a few cabins, a small store and a ranger station, but it is best not to count on anything being open. Fuel is not reliably available, and the nearest places with several services are Sisters over the pass, or the towns in the Willamette Valley to the west, so arrive with a full tank and some food and water.

Walk down to the river for a few minutes. The water is so clear that the stones look close enough to touch, and it is cold enough to numb your hand in a second.
