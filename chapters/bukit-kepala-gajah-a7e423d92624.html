Bukit Kepala Gajah

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Bukit Kepala Gajah, Elephant Head Hill, is one of the limestone outcrops in the Lenggong Valley of Perak, a landscape where the Malay Peninsula's earliest people left their tools in the soil. The valley lies along the Perak river in the north of the state, among steep green karst hills, rubber plantations and small villages, and UNESCO inscribed it in 2012 as a site of archaeological heritage, giving it a reputation as one of the oldest in Southeast Asia.

The most famous finds are stone tools found at Kota Tampan, which are dated to about seventy thousand years ago and lie beneath a layer of volcanic ash from the eruption of Toba in Sumatra, one of the largest eruptions in history. That finding helped scholars argue that early people survived the catastrophe in this region. Other caves nearby yielded Perak Man, a skeleton about eleven thousand years old, the oldest complete human skeleton found in Malaysia, unearthed in a cave in 1991.

The hills themselves are strange and beautiful: sheer white cliffs hung with vines, caves with bats and swiftlets, and green forest at the foot. Hornbills fly over, and monkeys watch from the trees. The roads are narrow and winding, partly shaded by rubber trees, with farm vehicles and slow lorries, and the surface is decent but can be slick after rain. Heat and humidity are constant, with afternoon storms in the monsoon seasons.

Lenggong, the small town, has fuel, a few food stalls and a museum that displays the finds, and the old royal town of Kuala Kangsar lies to the south with its golden-domed Ubudiah Mosque. Eat Perak specialities at roadside stalls, such as river fish and sweet tea. Check what is open before visiting, since archaeological sites are often fenced and need permission, and keep to marked paths. The best light is early morning, when mist hangs between the hills.
