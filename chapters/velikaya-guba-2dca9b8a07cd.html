Velikaya Guba

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Velikaya Guba is a village on the Zaonezhye peninsula, a long finger of land that stretches into Lake Onega and holds some of the most distinctive landscapes in Karelia. The name means Big Bay, and the place sits on a sheltered stretch of the shore, with the lake spreading wide and grey to the horizon and low islands scattered across it. The peninsula is an old and rocky place, with granite ridges, thin soils and forests of pine and spruce, and a scattering of small villages whose wooden houses lean in the wind.

The geology here is unusual. The rocks of Zaonezhye are among the oldest sedimentary formations on the planet, about two billion years old, and they contain shungite, a black carbon-rich mineral that is found almost nowhere else. It has been used for centuries as a pigment and a filter, and Peter the Great took an interest in the springs that came from it. Today shungite is sold in souvenir shops as a healing stone, and the claims for it are bigger than the evidence, but the black rock itself is fascinating.

The village has a rich folk culture. The old Karelian and Russian traditions of the North, with their songs, laments and epics, were recorded here in the nineteenth century, and the area has been a source for collectors of folklore and for painters. Wooden houses with carved window frames and big stoves, small chapels, and fish drying on racks are still part of the scene.

For riders, the roads on the peninsula are narrow and winding, with sharp bends and frost heaves, and some sections are unpaved. They are slow and beautiful, with sudden views of the lake between the trees. Distances are modest but the going is slow, so allow plenty of time. The best season is summer, from June to August, when the days are long and the water is calm, and the worst is the spring thaw, when mud and flooding affect the roads.

There is little in the way of services, and fuel must be arranged beforehand in Petrozavodsk or Medvezhyegorsk. A few small shops sell basic supplies, and homestays offer meals of fish and potatoes. It is a place to slow down and listen.
