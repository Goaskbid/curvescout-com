Khorog

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Khorog sits on the edge of Afghanistan, with only a river between them. The town lies at about 2,200 metres in the valley of the Gunt and Panj rivers, in the Gorno-Badakhshan region of Tajikistan, and from its streets you can see across the Panj to the villages and mountain tracks of the Afghan Pamir, close enough to hear the dogs. About thirty thousand people live here, the regional capital, and its character is calm and rather quiet, with tree-lined streets, Soviet-era buildings and a river promenade. A park beside the river is a pleasant place for a walk.

The people are mostly Pamiri, Ismaili Muslims who speak their own eastern Iranian languages, and their traditional houses, built around a central room with five pillars symbolising important figures in the faith, are a feature of the region. The Aga Khan Development Network has done a great deal here since the collapse of the Soviet Union, supporting a university campus, a hospital and the local economy. The Pamir Botanical Garden, at about 2,300 metres, is among the highest in the world and grows plants from across Central Asia. Apricots, mulberries and walnuts are sold in the market.

For riders, Khorog is a main stop on the Pamir Highway, one of the great high-altitude roads on earth. From here the road leads east and north across the high plateau, passing Ak-Baital Pass, at over 4,600 metres, and on to Murghab and Osh in Kyrgyzstan. A second road follows the Panj river southwest through the Wakhan corridor, with spectacular views of Afghan villages across the river. Surfaces vary from good tarmac to broken asphalt and gravel, and washouts and rockfalls are common. Altitude, cold and long distances between services make this a serious expedition, and you should prepare spares, tools and acclimatisation time.

Fuel is available in Khorog, but quality can be inconsistent, and farther along the highway stations are scattered, so carry spare fuel. Travellers need a permit for the Gorno-Badakhshan region, usually obtained with an e-visa, and security conditions should be checked before travel. The best season is from about May to October. Simple homestays offer bread, soup and tea, and the hospitality is very warm.
