Mazinan

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The label belongs to a ride that includes the caravanserai of Miandasht, and that tells you what kind of land this is: a section of the old Silk Road, where traders crossed the high plain with camels and mules between the Caspian region and the cities of the east. The caravanserais, walled inns built a day's travel apart, with a courtyard, stables and rooms around the edge, are among the most striking buildings in Iran, and the best survive as ruins of brick and mud.

The landscape is arid, a wide, gravelly plain with low ridges, dry riverbeds and patches of irrigated green where a qanat brings water to a village. Fields of wheat, melon and saffron are grown in places, and the air is dry and clear. At dawn and dusk the light on the hills turns pink and gold, and nights are cold. The silence is broad, and the sky is big. Spring brings a brief green to the steppe.

The road across the plain is straight and fast, with long distances between settlements, a mix of trucks, buses and local traffic, and the occasional herd of sheep or goats on the verge. The surface can be rough on minor roads, and the wind blows dust. Summer heat is severe, and winter nights can be freezing.

Sabzevar, the nearest large town, has fuel, restaurants and hotels, and you should fill your tank there and carry water. Check the current rules for foreign visitors and for motorcycles before you plan this ride, because conditions can change. Spring and autumn are the best seasons.
