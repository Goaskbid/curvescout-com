Col des Écharmeaux

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Écharmeaux is a pass in the French sense of the word: a gap in a rounded range of green hills, not a gasp of rock and snow. The col sits in the hills that separate the Beaujolais vineyards from the upper Loire country, a watershed where streams to the east head for the Saône and the Rhône and those to the west, near Chauffailles, join the Loire. You climb through pastures and cattle, past dark plantations of Douglas fir that the Beaujolais hills are known for, and arrive at a modest saddle with no drama at all.

The drama comes from the approach. On the Beaujolais side the road climbs through a series of tidy bends, with the wooded slope close on one side and long views opening out over the Saône plain on the other. It is a road for rhythm rather than speed: medium-radius corners, a clean surface in most places, and the odd farm tractor to remind you whose lane it is. The western side, towards Chauffailles, drops into softer, more open land, with the Charolais cattle you may know from the beef trade grazing the fields.

Weather matters more than the number on the sign would suggest. The hills catch cloud, and on a cold morning you can ride from sunshine at the bottom into a damp white mist at the top and out again. Summer is the best season, though late spring is lovely, with the meadows full of flowers. Snow is not unknown in winter, and the col is usually passable but can be slick, so treat it with caution outside the warm months.

Chauffailles, about eight and a half kilometres away, is a small town of four thousand with a station and shops, while Belleville, on the Saône side, is the bigger option at some distance. If you want coffee, plan it for one of the villages rather than the pass itself. What you get at the top is a quiet moment in the trees, a view back toward the vines, and the satisfaction of having crossed a divide you never knew was there.
