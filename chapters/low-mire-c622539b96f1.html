Low Mire

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Low Mire lies in the quiet farmland behind the Cumbrian coast, near Dearham and Maryport, where the land slopes gently down to the Solway Firth and the hills of southern Scotland rise across the water. It is an area of small fields, hedges and scattered farms, with the Lake District fells visible to the south-east on clear days.

The Roman presence here was significant. Hadrian's Wall ended at Bowness-on-Solway, but the Romans extended their defences down the Cumbrian coast with a series of milefortlets and towers, to watch for raiders crossing the firth. Maryport was the site of a large Roman fort, Alauna, and the Senhouse Roman Museum, on the cliffs above the town, holds a remarkable collection of altars found on the site. The fields around Low Mire may hide traces of that frontier zone, though there is little to see above ground.

The riding in this part of Cumbria is relaxed and scenic. The coast road from Maryport to Silloth runs along the shore with views across the firth, and inland lanes wind between farms and villages. The roads are narrow and can be muddy, with tractors and livestock, but traffic is light. The Lake District is close, and within half an hour you can be climbing into the fells on roads like the route through Cockermouth to Buttermere. Weather is changeable, with rain and wind from the west, and the light over the Solway can be spectacular in the evening.

Maryport, six kilometres away, is a small harbour town with fuel, shops and cafés, and its Georgian streets and harbour are pleasant for a walk. Aspatria and Dearham are smaller villages with basic services. The local food is hearty: Cumberland sausage, a long coiled pork sausage, and sticky toffee pudding, which claims Cumbrian origins. On a clear evening, standing near the coast and looking across to Criffel and the Scottish hills, it is easy to imagine Roman sentries watching the same view, waiting for boats that never came.
