Hachizuka Kofun

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Hachizuka is one of the stranger tombs in the Furuichi cluster, for anyone who has got used to the keyhole shape. The ride labels it by a town called Kashihara, but you are really on the Kawachi plain, below the Kongo and Katsuragi mountains, among the fruit orchards and commuter lines that thread through Habikino and Fujiidera.

What I like about the whole group is how mixed the tombs are. The giants get the headlines, but the surrounding mounds are a social map: round ones, square ones, keyholes, big, small, spread across the plain in a pattern that shows who ranked where in the Yamato court of the fifth century. Hachizuka sits alongside this, reminding you that the tomb builders did not use one template but a whole vocabulary. Walk around it and you will find a small information board, a path along the fence and a surprising number of cats.

Riding between these sites is easy but dull. Narrow lanes, constant traffic lights, kids in uniform on bicycles after school. Park the bike near one of the small lots, and walk. Fuel and food are right there in Habikino and Fujiidera, with their station forecourts and ever-present convenience stores, and Osaka itself has a food culture worth taking seriously: okonomiyaki on the griddle, kushikatsu skewers, takoyaki octopus balls from a street stall.

Habikino is also known for its grapes, and the hillsides above the plain grow them for local wine, which makes an unexpected addition to a heritage day. If it is September, look for the roadside stands.

Come in the cooler months. The plain gets steamy by July, and the afternoon light is flat and hazy. In early April the cherry trees along the nearby lanes bloom and the whole neighbourhood seems to exhale, and a mound that looks like ordinary woodland suddenly feels like part of a very old conversation between the living and the dead.
