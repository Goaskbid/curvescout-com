Aalborg (area 57.1,9.9)

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Aalborg sits on the Limfjord, the broad, shallow waterway that cuts across northern Jutland and turns the northern tip of Denmark into an island, and the city has always been shaped by the water. It was a medieval trading town on the narrowest crossing, and today a bridge and a tunnel carry traffic over the fjord to Nørresundby on the north bank. The waterfront has been redeveloped with a promenade, a concert hall and the Utzon Center, designed by the architect of the Sydney Opera House, who had family roots in the area, and the old brick warehouses are full of cafes and bars.

The town is famous for aquavit, the caraway-flavoured spirit, and the Aalborg brand is known across Scandinavia. The local pub culture is relaxed, and on a summer evening the streets around Jomfru Ane Gade are loud and cheerful. The Viking connection is strong too, because across the fjord at Lindholm Høje lies a big Viking Age burial ground with stones set in the shape of ships, on a grassy hillside with a view. Further south near Hobro is Fyrkat, a ring fortress built around 980 under Harald Bluetooth with a circular rampart and four gates, one of a group of such fortresses in Denmark that were laid out with remarkable geometric precision.

The riding in this part of Jutland is quiet and spacious. The land is mostly flat to gently rolling, with big fields, small woods and hedgerows, and the roads are well kept and speed limits strictly observed, with cameras in many places. The wind is a constant, and it can be strong off the fjord and the North Sea. Corners are mild, so the pleasure is in rhythm and sky rather than technical demands. Traffic is light outside the city, and the early morning light on the water is lovely.

With over a hundred thousand people in the city, there are stations everywhere, and all the services you could need. Danish prices are high, so a packed lunch can save money, though a hot dog from a roadside stand is a proper local treat. The smell of Aalborg is of salt water and the faint sweetness of the aquavit distillery. Plan your day around a long loop south toward Fyrkat and back along the fjord, and leave time for the burial ground at Lindholm Høje, where the sheep graze among the stones.
