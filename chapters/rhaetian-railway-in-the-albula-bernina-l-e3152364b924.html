Rhaetian Railway in the Albula / Bernina L

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The Rhaetian Railway's Albula and Bernina lines were built in the early twentieth century to connect the Engadine with the rest of Switzerland and with Italy, and they are masterpieces of mountain railway engineering, with spiral tunnels, viaducts and gradients that seem impossible for a train. They were listed by UNESCO in 2008 together with the landscapes they cross, and St. If you ride in this area, you will see the red trains everywhere, and you will often be riding beside them.

The Albula line climbs from Thusis to St. Moritz through a series of spiral tunnels and viaducts, including the famous Landwasser viaduct near Filisur, where the train emerges from a tunnel in a cliff and crosses a deep gorge on tall stone arches. The Albula pass road, at 2,312 metres, runs parallel to the railway for part of the way, a narrow, winding road with spectacular views, closed in winter. The Bernina line runs from St. Moritz over the Bernina pass, at 2,253 metres the highest point of the railway, and down to Tirano in Italy, without rack assistance, with the circular viaduct at Brusio, where the train loops around a stone spiral, a highlight.

The Bernina pass road, at 2,328 metres, is one of the great alpine rides, with views of the Morteratsch glacier and the Piz Bernina, the highest peak in the eastern Alps, from the Montebello bend. The road descends into the Valposchiavo, through tight bends and past lakes, to Tirano. It is generally open all year, though conditions can be difficult in winter.

St. Moritz is the glamorous heart of the Engadine, a resort famous for its winter sports, luxury hotels and shops, set above a lake surrounded by mountains. The Upper Engadine is a high, broad valley with a chain of lakes, larch forests and villages with sgraffito-decorated houses, and the light here is famously clear and bright. Romansh, Switzerland's fourth national language, is still spoken in the valley.

St. Moritz has fuel, shops and restaurants, though prices are high. Fuel stations are also found in other villages of the Engadine, and Davos, about thirty-four kilometres away, is another larger town. Try an Engadiner Nusstorte with a coffee, and ride the Bernina early in the morning, when the light on the glacier is at its best and the road is quiet.
