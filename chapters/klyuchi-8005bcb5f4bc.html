Klyuchi

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Klyuchi is where Kamchatka's volcano country becomes unmissable. The village of about ten thousand people sits on the Kamchatka River at the foot of the Klyuchevskaya group, a cluster of volcanoes that includes Klyuchevskaya Sopka, which at roughly 4,750 metres is the highest active volcano in Eurasia, along with the broken cone of Bezymianny, the snow-dusted Kamen and, to the north, the long, spiky mass of Sheveluch. You do not need to go anywhere to see them. On a clear morning they stand at the end of the street.

Volcanic ash is a fact of life. It dusts the roofs, the gardens and sometimes the road, and locals have long since stopped being surprised by a grey sky that is full of ash rather than cloud. Eruptions in the group have shaped the history of the village, and they keep a slight edge on the day. Houses here are low and sturdy, the gardens are surprisingly productive in the rich volcanic soil, and fishermen on the Kamchatka River land salmon in season.

The road from Kozyrevsk is a continuation of the main northern highway, and it carries a fair amount of local traffic. The asphalt is mixed, with long gravel and repair sections that can be dusty or muddy, and the bends are wide. It is easy riding technically, but the combination of distance, weather and loose surface demands attention. Rain and fog can drop in suddenly, and cold nights follow warm days. Late July to early September is the best period, though ash and haze can obscure the view.

With ten thousand people, Klyuchi has what you need: fuel, shops, a few cafes and basic accommodation. It is the last good chance for supplies before the road turns toward Ust-Kamchatsk or toward the climbing routes on the volcanoes, and the sensible approach is to fill up completely and buy food here. Taste red caviar and smoked salmon from the market if you can, and ask about the volcano walks, which are serious undertakings that need local guides and good weather. For a rider, the pleasure is simply to sit by the river in the evening and watch the cones turn pink as the sun goes down.
