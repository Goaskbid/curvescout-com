CURVESCOUT / PLACE TO SEE

Yokohama

Opening times, access and services can change - check before you go.

Today it is Japan's second-largest city, with more than three and a half million people, and the old brick warehouses by the harbour, now shops and restaurants, stand next to the high towers of Minato Mirai.

At sea level, this is the flattest of starts, and the stop is mostly about logistics. The atmosphere is relaxed and friendly, and you will see all kinds of machines.

Chinatown, the biggest in Japan, has hundreds of restaurants, many offering steamed buns, noodles and dim sum, and it is lively until late in the evening. Seafood from the bay and the nearby Miura peninsula is fresh and varied, and a morning at one of the fish markets can be fun.

The ride from here goes south toward Kamakura and the Miura peninsula, a lovely stretch of coast with views of Fuji on clear days. Be ready for traffic on weekends, when day-trippers fill the roads, and take care at the many tolls and junctions. The air has a salt tang, the breeze is steady, and the quiet hours before dawn are the best for riding. In summer the humidity can be heavy and typhoons can bring strong winds and rain in early autumn. Parking for motorbikes in the central district is limited, so look for designated areas, and remember that illegal parking is quickly ticketed.
