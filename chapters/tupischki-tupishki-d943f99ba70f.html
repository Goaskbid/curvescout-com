Tupischki Tupishki

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Tupishki is a small place in the Ashmyany district of Belarus, a green and gently rolling country of low hills, lakes, farms and birch woods close to the Lithuanian border. The ride it belongs to is built around Mir Castle, the great brick and stone fortress that the Ilyinich and later the Radziwiłł families built from the sixteenth century on, and the whole of this region has a history of magnate estates, small wooden churches and the long, tangled story of the old Grand Duchy of Lithuania.

The nearest town of any size on the data is Halshany, about four kilometres off, and it has a ruined castle and a Franciscan church, built in the early seventeenth century under the Sapieha family, which was known for its large, handsome brick front. The wider landscape is of potato fields, hay meadows and storks nesting on telegraph poles, with villages of painted wooden houses with carved window frames. The light in late summer, low and golden over the stubble, is one of the pleasures of the country.

For riding, the secondary roads are narrow, flat and sometimes rough, with long straights between small villages, and tractors, carts and cyclists in the way. There is little crowding. Spring thaw leaves frost heaves and potholes, and winters are cold with snow lying for months, so the practical season runs from May to September. Distances between fuel stations can be larger than you expect, though main roads have decent coverage.

Belarus is a country that asks foreign motorcyclists to think ahead. Entry rules, visas, vehicle insurance and border crossings have changed repeatedly, and the situation with neighbouring countries has made land crossings difficult, so check the current rules with official sources before planning anything. Carry papers, keep to the speed limits, which are policed with cameras, and expect checkpoints. Halshany has basic services and fuel, but for a larger choice of food and lodging the district centre, Ashmyany, and the capital Minsk are better. Pastries and dumplings called draniki, potato pancakes, are the staple of most roadside cafés, usually served with sour cream.
