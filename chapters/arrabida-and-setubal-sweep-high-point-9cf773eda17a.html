CURVESCOUT / PLACE TO SEE

Setubal

Opening times, access and services can change - check before you go.

The Moors held the castle until it fell to the first king of Portugal in the twelfth century, and it was later the headquarters of the Order of Santiago, a military order whose knights helped defend the frontier. The fortress, now partly a hotel, has a bastion walk that is worth the climb.

Castelão is the signature red grape, and the sandy soils produce wines with a distinct, earthy flavour. Each September the Festa das Vindimas fills the town with music, parades, and the treading of grapes, and the smell of crushed fruit carries on the air. A tasting at one of the nearby cellars is a pleasure, though a rider must be wary.

The riding around the hill is varied and pleasant. There are blind bends, patchy surfaces and sudden dips, and spring brings wildflowers while summer brings hot dry air and the scent of pines. The road up to the castle itself is steep, with a tight hairpin and little room for parking, so a motorcycle is a great advantage.

Fuel and services are no problem.

If you plan an evening ride, the light on the castle at sunset, a warm gold on pale stone, is one of the loveliest sights in the region. Be careful afterward on the descent, when the road is in shadow and the tyres cool.
