Nærøyfjord Area

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The Nærøyfjord is narrow in a way that photographs struggle to show. Branching from the Aurlandsfjord, itself an arm of the great Sognefjord, it squeezes to about 250 metres across at its tightest, with mountain walls that climb to nearly 1,800 metres on either side and cast the water in shadow for much of the day. UNESCO listed it together with Geirangerfjord in 2005, and the setting at Gudvangen at its head is dramatic even by Norwegian standards, with waterfalls dropping from hanging valleys and snow patches lingering into summer.

What makes it special is how little has changed. Small farms on ledges high above the fjord, some abandoned and some still in use, speak of generations who lived with avalanches and falling rock. Nearby Undredal, a small village on the Aurlandsfjord, is known for its goat cheese, and you can often taste it at a farm or a shop. The old valley road once reached the plateau at Stalheim by a ferocious cobbled track, and the Stalheimskleiva, one of the steepest roads in northern Europe with thirteen hairpins, is still open in summer for the brave.

Riding here is a mix of tunnels and mountain views. The long Gudvanga tunnel, over eleven kilometres, takes the main road through the rock between Gudvangen and Flåm, and the older roads over the hills and along the fjord offer the real scenery. Surfaces are generally good, but tunnels are damp and cold, so a clean visor and a layer for warmth are wise. Expect tour buses in July and August, and be patient behind them on narrow bends. Weather can change quickly, with rain closing in even on a bright morning.

Fuel is the practical limit. This is remote country, and the nearest larger centres are Voss in one direction and Sogndal in the other, about thirty-five kilometres from this point. Stations in the smaller villages exist but close early, so do not count on them. Bring a snack, and fill up before you commit.

The fjord is best seen on the water. Small ferries and quiet electric boats run from Gudvangen and Flåm, and the silence gliding between the cliffs is remarkable. If you ride, aim for early morning or late evening, when the road is quiet, the water is still and the light pours down the cliffs.
