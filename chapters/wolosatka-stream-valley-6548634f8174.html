Wolosatka stream valley

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The Wołosatka is a mountain stream in the far south-eastern corner of Poland, draining the slopes below Tarnica, at 1,346 metres the highest summit of the Polish Bieszczady. Its valley is part of Bieszczady National Park and of the World Heritage listing of primeval Carpathian beech forests: dark, mossy woods of beech and fir where dead trunks are left to fall and rot, and where bears, wolves, lynx and wildcats still move freely. Above the treeline the ridges open into the połoniny, high grass meadows that turn tawny in autumn and give the range its rolling, almost Scottish silhouette.

This was once a lively corner of the old Polish-Ukrainian borderland, home to Boyko villages, Orthodox churches and Jewish townsfolk. War and the forced resettlements of the 1940s emptied it almost completely, and nature crept back over orchards, cemeteries and foundations. At Wołosate, at the head of the valley, a stud farm breeds the sturdy Hucul mountain horses, and the trailheads up to Tarnica fill with hikers on summer weekends. Turka and the Ukrainian villages over the ridge look close on the map, but the frontier here is closed to casual crossing; this is a dead end in the best sense.

For riders the draw is the Wielka Pętla Bieszczadzka, the big Bieszczady loop through Lesko, Ustrzyki Dolne, Ustrzyki Górne, Wetlina and Cisna, which has become a classic Polish motorcycle tour. The road down to Wołosate branches off it near Ustrzyki Górne, a narrow ribbon along the valley floor with forest pressing in on both sides. Surfaces have improved a lot over the years, but expect frost damage, gravel in corners, logging trucks and walkers on the verge. Late September and October, when the beeches blaze copper against blue sky, are glorious; winter is long and snowy.

Fuel is the thing to plan here. Lutowiska is the nearest village of any size, but for a reliable fill and a supermarket think of Ustrzyki Dolne or Lesko, and do not run the tank low on the loop. Ustrzyki Górne has a few eateries serving pierogi, trout and żurek sour soup to hungry hikers, and simple guesthouses dot the valleys. The phone signal comes and goes, which some people regard as a feature.
