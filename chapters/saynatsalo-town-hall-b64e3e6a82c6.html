Säynätsalo Town Hall

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Alvar Aalto won the competition for this building in 1949, and it was finished in 1952, a village council hall on a small island in Lake Päijänne that has become a pilgrimage for architects. Säynätsalo sits just across a short bridge from the town of Muurame, with Jyväskylä, the city where Aalto spent so much of his working life, a few kilometres north; the island is now part of Jyväskylä itself. The town hall is a cluster of red brick and timber around a raised courtyard of grass, and the council chamber stands over it with its high roof, fan-shaped wooden trusses and a hush of slanted light.

I find it the most human of Aalto's buildings, because he seems to have asked how a small community would want to meet. Brick was cheap and local, and he used it with a generous hand, letting ivy and moss climb over the courtyard walls. The ground-floor library and the mingled public rooms are still in use, and you can usually enter the courtyard and walk up the steps. Check summer opening times and guided tours before you go, as the interior access depends on the season and on whether the council chamber is in use.

The riding approach is one of Finland's best kinds of unremarkable. Roads curve through spruce and birch forests, past red barns and a succession of small lakes, with gentle grades and a few blind crests. Expect smooth tarmac but watch out for elk, especially at dusk, as they are both common and deadly. In summer the evening light never quite disappears, and Päijänne glitters in the long gold hours. Winter riding is not advisable.

Fuel and food are no problem. Muurame has nearly nine thousand people and the usual supermarkets and petrol stations, and Jyväskylä beyond it has everything. Finnish cafes serve a good cup of coffee with a cinnamon bun, and you will often find a lakeside spot to sit with a thermos. Take care with speed limits: Finnish fines are scaled to income, and cameras are common. Mosquitoes in June are fierce, so keep a jacket on near the water.
