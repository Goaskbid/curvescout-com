Lake Charles

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Lake Charles is the gateway to the Creole Nature Trail, an All-American Road that loops south from the city across the marshes of southwest Louisiana to the Gulf. It is a flat, low, watery place where the horizon is mostly sky and the road often runs on a raised bank between bayou and rice field. This is Cajun country, and you notice it first in the language on the radio and the boudin sold at gas stations, a rice-and-pork sausage that locals eat on the way to work and consider a proper breakfast.

The city itself has about seventy thousand people and a history tied to timber, rice and later to petrochemicals along the Calcasieu Ship Channel. Its lakefront is pleasant at sunset, and the casinos on the edge of town draw a large share of visitors from Texas, a short drive west. Hurricanes have shaped the recent story. Laura in 2020 and Delta soon after damaged a huge number of buildings, and recovery has been slow in places. The old downtown has some fine architecture, and the Mardi Gras here is a big local tradition, with a rural version called the Courir de Mardi Gras in nearby villages.

For riders, the appeal is wide open scenery rather than twisty roads. South of town, Highway 27 crosses the Calcasieu and runs through marsh to Cameron, with views of egrets, white pelicans and herons, and the occasional alligator sunning on a bank. This is among the best birding in North America, particularly in winter and during spring migration. The Sabine National Wildlife Refuge has a boardwalk across the marsh that is worth the stop. Afternoon thunderstorms are common in summer, and mosquitoes can be fierce, so bring repellent and rain gear.

Wind is a real factor, especially near the coast where there is nothing to break it, and the roads can be slick after rain. Stations are plentiful in Lake Charles and Westlake, but fuel thins out quickly along the trail itself, so start with a full tank. For lunch, look for a roadside place with crawfish, gumbo or a plate of shrimp and rice, and plan for the heat.

Fall and spring are the best seasons, when the air is dry enough to enjoy and the birds are moving.
