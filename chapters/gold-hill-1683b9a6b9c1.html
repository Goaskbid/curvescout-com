Gold Hill

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Gold Hill lives up to its name in a modest way. A gold strike in the early 1850s brought miners into this stretch of the Rogue River valley in southern Oregon, and the town grew where the river cuts through a narrow stretch of rock, with a small downtown of brick storefronts and a bridge across the water. Today it is a quiet place of about twelve hundred people, with a diner or two, a hardware store and the feel of a place that lets the interstate pass on the other side of the hill. Locals fish for salmon and steelhead in the Rogue, and rafting companies run the stretches of river below.

For riders finishing the Rogue Umpqua Scenic Byway, it is a gentle end after a ride that begins in the forests near Diamond Lake and follows the Umpqua and Rogue rivers through volcanic country, with a long run of gradual curves, firs and cedars and views of the water below the road. The byway uses a combination of state highways, and the final approach through the valley is flatter, with orchards, vineyards and hay fields on the valley floor.

The area has history that deserves attention. Just across the river, the two flat-topped Table Rocks rise above the valley, a pair of basalt mesas sacred to the Takelma people who lived here before settlers came. In 1853 a treaty was signed at the foot of one of them, and violence in the following years, the Rogue River Wars, ended with the forced removal of Native people to reservations on the coast. Hiking trails lead to the top of the Table Rocks, and the wildflowers in spring are remarkable.

Gold Hill is small, so for a bigger choice of fuel, food and bike repair head to Central Point or Medford, about thirteen kilometres and a little further away. Wine is a feature of this valley, with a number of vineyards and tasting rooms, though riders will want to be careful with that. Spring and early autumn are the best seasons, with clear air and mild days, while summer afternoons can reach well into the thirties, and winter brings fog and frost.
