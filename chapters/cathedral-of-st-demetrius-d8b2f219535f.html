Cathedral Of St Demetrius

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Vladimir has a talent for making you look at walls. The Cathedral of St Demetrius, built in the 1190s for Prince Vsevolod the Big Nest, is smaller than the Assumption Cathedral down the street, but its limestone surface is covered in carved relief, hundreds of figures: saints, griffins, lions, flowers, and in the middle of it all King David playing his harp with a crowd of animals around him. Almost nothing is repeated, and some of the stone has worn so smooth that the figures seem to be melting. Photographers love it in the late afternoon when the sun slants across the carving.

The cathedral stood in the princely court, and Vsevolod, the last great ruler of the old Vladimir principality, got his nickname from his huge family of children. The church was his private chapel. Later restorations in the nineteenth century changed some of the details, and not everyone loves the repairs, but the sculptural effect remains one of the most remarkable in medieval Russia. It is part of the same UNESCO listing as the Assumption Cathedral and the monuments of Suzdal.

The surroundings are an easy city walk: a park on the bluff, old merchants' houses, a modest museum, and cafes on the main street where you can sit and warm your hands with tea. Vladimir is a city of some three hundred thousand people, so fuel stations and food are never an issue, and the ring road gets you away from the one-way streets quickly. Traffic is heavy on weekdays, and drivers here are impatient, so a Sunday morning is a calmer time to arrive.

Beyond the city, the countryside is gentle and wooded, and the roads toward Suzdal and Bogolyubovo take you through fields, birch and meadows with onion domes in the distance. It is not twisty riding, and it is not meant to be. The point is the unhurried, slightly melancholic landscape that fed centuries of Russian painting and poetry. Summer evenings are long and warm, autumn brings colour, and winter is hard, so plan on May to September. Check the latest travel advice before planning a trip to Russia.
