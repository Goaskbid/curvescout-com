Grimeton Radio Station

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Six steel towers, each one about a hundred and twenty-seven metres tall, stand in a flat Halland field south of Varberg, and from a distance they look like a fence for giants. This is Grimeton, a long-wave transmitter built in the early 1920s so that Sweden could talk to America without relying on cables that others controlled. Inside the long brick hall sits an Alexanderson alternator, a huge spinning machine that makes the signal, with no valves and no transistors, and it still works. On a couple of days a year, including Christmas Eve, the station fires up and sends a message in Morse code, a ritual that draws enthusiasts from across Europe. UNESCO listed it in 2004, and it is unusual among heritage sites in being an old machine you can hear.

The countryside is Halland at its most ordinary and most pleasant: low fields, hedged lanes, farmhouses painted red or white, and pockets of beech forest. Riding here is relaxed. Roads are good, gradients barely exist, and bends are gentle, with the sea a few kilometres to the west. Coastal Route 158 and the small lanes toward Tvååker give you a quiet way in, and traffic is mostly local. Elk do roam this part of Sweden, so be careful at dawn and dusk, particularly in autumn.

Visits to the hall are usually done on a guided tour, and the museum explains how the station worked, with the generators, the operators' rooms and the old telegraph equipment. Check opening times before you ride out, as the season is shorter than you might expect, and the long winters mean limited access. A small cafe usually serves coffee and cinnamon buns, which is really all you need.

For fuel and a proper meal, head the eight kilometres into Varberg, a seaside town of about thirty-four thousand people with a medieval fortress on the harbour, a sea-bathing tradition that goes back to the nineteenth century, and a cold-water bath house on the rocks. Tvååker is smaller but has basic services. The best months are June to August, with long warm evenings, though spring and early autumn are quieter and cheaper.
