Mammoth Cave National Park

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Under the rolling hills of south-central Kentucky lies the longest known cave system in the world, with more than four hundred miles of mapped passages and surveyors still finding more. Mammoth Cave was carved by the Green River and its tributaries over millions of years, working through a thick layer of limestone capped by a protective layer of sandstone. At the surface it is a karst landscape of sinkholes, springs and disappearing streams, with oak and hickory forest covering the ridges and a quiet green river winding below.

People have used the cave for thousands of years. Native Americans explored deep into its passages, leaving cane torches and mussel shells, and in the early nineteenth century saltpeter was mined here for gunpowder during the War of 1812. Later it became a tourist attraction, and a young enslaved man named Stephen Bishop became one of its first great guides and explorers, mapping chambers in the 1830s and 1840s. The cave became a national park in 1941 and a World Heritage Site in 1981.

For riders the area is a pleasant and mostly gentle ride from Nashville, which lies a bit over an hour to the south. Interstate 65 gets you there fastest, but the old roads through Cave City, Brownsville and the Green River valley are more interesting, with rolling farmland, tobacco barns and country churches. Within the park, the roads are narrow and curving, with a small free ferry that carries vehicles across the Green River on a back road. It is a nice detour on a warm day and a sign of how little the area has been rushed.

Cave tours are the main reason to come, and they require tickets, often booked in advance, particularly in summer and on weekends. Some tours cover long distances underground and involve many steps, so check what the tour demands and dress for the cool temperature underground, roughly constant all year. Bats have declined sharply because of white-nose syndrome, and visitors may be asked to follow decontamination rules.

Fuel and food are best handled in Cave City, a small tourist town just outside the park with gas stations and roadside diners, while Glasgow, a bit farther, is bigger. The park has a lodge, a campground and a visitor center with a cafeteria, though hours vary by season.
