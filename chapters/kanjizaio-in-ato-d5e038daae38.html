Kanjizaiô-in Ato

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Of all the buildings that once stood in Hiraizumi, Kanjizaio-in is one of the quietest. Only the ruins survive, a pond and a pattern of foundations beside the larger garden of Motsu-ji, but together they give a sense of the scale of the twelfth-century city. The temple was built by the wife of Fujiwara no Motohira, the second lord of the Northern Fujiwara, and the pond was designed to resemble a Pure Land paradise.

The garden has been excavated and partly restored, with a lake whose shoreline was shaped to look natural, a small island and a causeway. In spring the water reflects the new green of the trees, and in autumn the maples turn red around the edge. The old paths through the grass are marked and you can walk around, with signs explaining where the halls, the bridge and the bell tower stood. The site is shaded and quiet, even when the main attractions are busy, and you will often have a bench to yourself.

The Fujiwara rise and fall are the backdrop. They governed from Hiraizumi for four generations, until Minamoto no Yoritomo, the first shogun, attacked in 1189 and the capital burned. Fire and later neglect did the rest, and what survived is the memory in the gardens, along with the gold hall at Chuson-ji. The story has a classic shape: wealth, art, then conquest and silence.

The roads around Hiraizumi are quiet and rural, passing between rice paddies and small farms, with views of low forested hills. Ichinoseki is the nearest city for fuel, food and lodging. Weather is cool and clear in autumn and the snow starts in December. Mornings are best for the light and fewer visitors. A walk here is not strenuous, but a rider in jacket and boots will want to take it slowly and enjoy being off the road.
