Tossa de Mar

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Tossa is the town that made me understand why people fall for the Costa Brava. A ring of medieval walls and round towers, the Vila Vella, sits on a rocky headland above a curve of beach, and the old town behind it still has the tangled lanes of a fishing village. The walls date to the Middle Ages and were built to keep out pirates, which tells you something about the sea they were guarding against. A lighthouse crowns the point, and the whole thing glows orange in the evening light.

Hollywood found it too. The 1951 film Pandora and the Flying Dutchman was shot here, with Ava Gardner, and there is a statue of the actress on the hillside above the old town that people still photograph. Artists, including Marc Chagall, spent time in Tossa in the 1930s, and the town has been polite about its fame ever since, mostly by being busy in July and August and calm the rest of the year.

For riders the draw is the road. The GI-682 between Tossa and Sant Feliu de Guíxols is one of the best-known coast roads in Catalonia, a narrow ribbon of tight bends cut into cliffs, with pine and holm oak crowding the edge and glimpses of small coves between the trees. It rewards a smooth rhythm rather than speed, since the corners tighten without warning and the surface can be gritty after rain. In peak summer it clogs with coaches and rental cars, so ride it early in the morning or in the shoulder months from April to June and again in late September. Winter days can be wonderfully empty, though you will meet the odd damp patch under the trees.

The other direction, toward Lloret de Mar, is a very different mood: a bigger resort town with long beaches and plenty of traffic. Tossa itself has about five thousand residents, so it has the usual run of fuel, cafes and parking, though parking near the old town is hard on a summer weekend and a bike is much easier to squeeze in than a car. Sit with a coffee or a cold drink down by the beach, look up at the walls, and take a minute before you commit to that coast road, because once you are on it the sea is below you and the cliff is right beside your shoulder.
