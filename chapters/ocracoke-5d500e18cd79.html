Ocracoke

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Ocracoke is only reachable by water, and that single fact shapes everything about the place. The island is a long sliver of sand at the bottom of the Outer Banks, with one village, one main road, and an atmosphere of being slightly cut off from the rest of the country. Most visitors arrive by the free ferry from Hatteras, a crossing of roughly an hour, and the arrival is a small event: the boat swings into the harbour, the brown pelicans watch, and the village appears with its white-painted houses and a little lighthouse.

The history is colourful. Ocracoke was a haunt of pirates in the early eighteenth century, and Edward Teach, better known as Blackbeard, was killed in a fight with a Royal Navy force off the island in 1718. The lighthouse, built in 1823, is the oldest still operating in North Carolina, a short, stubby white tower that looks nothing like its tall cousin at Hatteras. A small British cemetery holds the graves of sailors from HMS Bedfordshire, a trawler sunk by a German U-boat in 1942. Islanders have a distinctive way of speaking, sometimes called the Hoi Toider brogue, and a strong sense of their own identity.

For a rider, the island is a quiet loop. NC 12 runs straight along the dunes from the northern ferry landing to the village at the southern end, about thirteen miles, with wild ponies in a pen and sea oats on the dunes. There are no technical corners, but the speeds are low and the atmosphere is so relaxed that you hardly notice. Wind and sand can cover the road, and storm surges have flooded the village in recent years, so conditions vary. The village is easily explored on foot, and many visitors park the bike and wander the harbour.

You will find petrol on the island, as well as shops, cafes and seafood restaurants, though prices are higher than on the mainland and hours can be short out of season. Summer and holiday weekends are crowded, and ferry reservations may be needed for some routes, particularly the longer ones from the mainland, so check ahead. Bring insect repellent, because mosquitoes and biting flies can be fierce in warm months.
