Butha-Buthe

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Butha-Buthe means place of lying down, and the name comes from the story of the Basotho people retreating to the flat-topped mountain above the town in the 1820s, when war and famine were tearing through the region, before their leader Moshoeshoe moved on to found his stronghold at Thaba Bosiu. Today it is a lively northern Lesotho market town of a few thousand people in its centre, with a few more in the surrounding villages, set in a wide valley under steep brown mountains. It is the first real stop after the border crossing from South Africa, a short distance away.

For riders it is the natural first fuel and food stop in Lesotho, and a good place to get the feel of the country. The town has fuel, shops selling loaves and tinned goods, simple restaurants; carry a little local currency, loti or South African rand, for small purchases. The roads out of town climb into the mountains toward Moteng and Mahlasela passes, with good tarmac, long open bends and views across layers of ridges, though the surface can have patches of damage and the odd pothole. Livestock, including donkeys, goats and cattle, are a constant hazard, and so are minibus taxis stopping without warning. Weather in the mountains changes fast, and winter can bring snow, ice and fierce cold.

Lesotho is a country of mountain farming, and life here follows a seasonal rhythm. Men on horseback in woollen blankets pinned with large safety pins, and conical straw hats, move herds between pastures, and women carry water and loads on their heads. The blankets are not just clothing: they are a statement of identity and status, and patterns often carry meanings. The Basotho language has a rich tradition of poetry and song, and you will hear singing from schools and churches.

The landscape is high and open. Almost the whole country lies above a thousand metres, with a vast plateau cut by deep river valleys, and the highest peaks reach well over three thousand. Grass covers the slopes in summer, turning gold in winter, and the air is thin, bright and cold. Bearded vultures and black eagles soar above the ridges, and the rivers run clear over rock.

Carry your passport, vehicle papers and insurance for border formalities. Fill the tank in town, since the next stations may be a long way off, and dress warmly.
