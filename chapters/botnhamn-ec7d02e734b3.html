Botnhamn

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Botnhamn is a tiny fishing settlement on the north side of Senja, facing the open sea, and it is an end of the road in the best sense. The little harbour is sheltered by rocky headlands, with a handful of houses and boat sheds, and behind it rise steep, jagged mountains that drop straight into the ocean. Finnsnes, over thirty kilometres away, is the closest town of any size, so supplies need to be sorted earlier. The area has been settled for thousands of years, with traces of Sami and Norse inhabitants, and fishing has been the main livelihood for most of that time.

The road from the south follows the fjords and headlands, with a mix of tight bends, tunnels and wide views, and the surface is generally good. It is a quiet road, and in the early morning, you may have it to yourself. Wind can be strong near the coast, and rain showers move through quickly, so waterproofs are wise. In summer, the midnight sun lights the peaks in gold, and you can ride late into the evening. The best months are June to August, though even then it is cool.

Nearby, at the village of Bergsbotn, a viewing platform juts out over the fjord, with a dramatic view of the mountains and the water below, and it is one of the most photographed spots on the island. Senja has a reputation as a smaller, wilder version of Lofoten, with fewer crowds and a similar landscape of sharp peaks and white beaches. Whales sometimes appear off the coast in winter, and sea eagles are common throughout the year. The coast has beaches of pale sand that look tropical until you put a toe in the water.

There are limited services here, so plan accordingly. Fuel should be taken in Finnsnes or another larger village, and there is no guarantee of a cafe, though the occasional roadside kiosk may sell coffee and waffles. In many places, the best meal is a simple one: fresh fish, bread and cheese eaten beside the sea. A flask of hot coffee and a warm jacket are all you need, and the stillness of the evening is the reward.
