Namazgoh Mosque

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Many Central Asian cities used to keep a namazgoh, an open prayer ground just outside the walls, where the whole male population gathered for the great feast days, in rows on the bare earth, led by an imam at a niche in a wall. The one at Samarkand lies on the southern fringe of the old town, a little way from the dense maze of lanes around the Registan, and it tells the story of how the city lived rather than how its rulers showed off. The structure, with its niche, a screen of arches and later additions, has been repaired and rebuilt over the centuries, so it is best seen as a layered place rather than a single period piece.

The ordinary character of the spot is part of its charm. Neighbourhood life goes on around it, with children in school uniforms, old men on benches, and families walking to a nearby market. Women's visits are usually restricted around prayer times, and everyone should dress modestly, with covered arms and legs, and leave shoes at the door when entering any prayer space. There is little signage, so a little local knowledge helps.

The riding is urban. Samarkand's streets here are a mix of broad avenues and narrow residential lanes, with taxis, buses, bicycles and the odd donkey cart. The surfaces are mostly decent, though ruts and manholes can surprise you, and drivers are accustomed to improvising. Early morning is the best time, when the light is gentle and the streets are quiet. Spring and autumn are the best seasons, while summer afternoons are blazing and winters are chilly.

You are close to everything in the city, with fuel stations on the main roads, supermarkets, bakeries and tea houses within a few minutes, and the Registan about two to three kilometres away. The bazaar nearby is the place to try warm non bread, dried fruit, and sweet halva, and tea houses serve plov at lunch. Many Uzbek cars run on compressed gas, so petrol pumps are less common than you would expect, and it pays to top up when you see one. Keep small notes for tipping and market buying, and be prepared to be greeted warmly, for the curiosity about foreign riders is considerable.
