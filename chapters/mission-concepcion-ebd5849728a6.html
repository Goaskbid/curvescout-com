Mission Concepcion

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Mission Concepción is the quiet one, and many people think it is the best preserved. Its full name is Nuestra Señora de la Purísima Concepción de Acuña, and its stone church was completed in the 1750s, which makes it the oldest unrestored stone church in the United States still in use. It stands on a low rise a few miles south of downtown San Antonio, with two bell towers, a dome and a facade that is plain but beautifully proportioned. The Franciscans moved the mission here in 1731 from East Texas, where drought and conflict had driven them out, and here they found the river and a better chance.

Inside you can still see the traces of the original frescoes, with geometric patterns in faded reds and ochres on the walls and ceiling of the sacristy. The colors were applied with natural pigments and have lasted far longer than anyone expected. It is a chance to see how the mission interiors were meant to look, bright and decorated rather than bare stone, and it is more touching than grand. The mission also saw fighting in 1835, when a small Texan force defeated a larger Mexican detachment in the Battle of Concepción, a prelude to the siege at the Alamo.

The ride is easy and urban. The Mission Trail follows South Mission Road and then the river, with the old acequia irrigation ditches visible in places. Streets are shaded by live oaks and the traffic is moderate outside rush hour. Be aware that summers in San Antonio are very hot, with temperatures often in the high thirties Celsius, and the best hours are early morning or late afternoon. In winter, a cold front can drop the temperature suddenly and bring a light ice.

Mission Concepción is still a working parish church, so visitors are asked to be quiet and respectful, particularly during services. The grounds are open, and there is a small visitor center run by the National Park Service. Entry is free. A bench in the shade of the courtyard is a good place to sit and listen to the sounds of the city fade.

Fuel and food are close by. San Antonio has stations everywhere, and Southtown to the north has cafes and bakeries. Try a pan dulce and coffee before you move on.
