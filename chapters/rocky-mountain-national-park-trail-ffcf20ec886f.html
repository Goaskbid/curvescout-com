Rocky Mountain National Park Trail (area 39.4,-104.7)

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

This is the broad ridge that separates the South Platte drainage from the Arkansas, a plateau of ponderosa pine, scrub oak and hay meadows at roughly two thousand metres, with the Front Range a blue wall away to the west. On a clear day, Pikes Peak stands out to the south as a great pale dome.

The nearest towns are the suburban sprawl of Parker and the smaller settlements of Ponderosa Park, Franktown and Elizabeth. It is ranch country that is rapidly turning into commuter country, with new houses spreading across what were once grazing fields. Highway 83, the Parker Road to the locals, runs south along the divide toward Colorado Springs, a good road with gentle rises and sweeping views, although it fills with commuter traffic at rush hours. Franktown has an old-time feel, with a historic schoolhouse and the Castlewood Canyon State Park nearby, where a collapsed dam and red sandstone cliffs make a good short stop.

For riding, this is the quiet plains approach to the Rockies. Back roads of gravel and tarmac lace the area, with long straights and rolling hills, and the sky is huge. Weather swings quickly: thunderstorms build on summer afternoons and can drop hail without warning, and in winter the divide catches snow when Denver sees rain. Wind blows steadily from the west, sometimes hard.

Practicalities are easy, because Parker and Castle Rock have fuel, supermarkets and cafés in plenty. Treat this as the start of the day, a place to top up and warm up before the mountains. A morning leaving here with the sun low behind you and the peaks lit pink ahead is as good a start as the Front Range offers. Pronghorn and deer graze the fields at dawn, and hawks hunt from fence posts.
