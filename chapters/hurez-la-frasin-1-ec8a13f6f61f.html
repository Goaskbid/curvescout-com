Hurez - La Frasin 1

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

This corner of Sălaj sits along the Roman frontier of Dacia, which makes it a more serious place than its gentle hills suggest. West of Zalău the Meseș mountains rise in a long wooded ridge, and through them runs a natural gate, the Meseș Pass, which the Romans fortified as part of their northern defences. The great site of Porolissum, with its fort, civil settlement and amphitheatre, stands on a ridge nearby, and the watchtowers and small works scattered across the hills belong to the same system.

The Roman frontier here was not a wall but a chain of lookouts and forts on high ground, linked by tracks, so the best way to read it is to climb a hill and notice how far you can see.

For riding, the roads in the area are a pleasure. They climb in long, open bends through beech and oak woods, drop into small valleys with farms and orchards, and give the occasional view across to the Meseș ridge. The surface changes between good, rough and patched, and gravel collects on corners after rain, so be careful on descents. Traffic is thin on the side roads, though the main route through the pass carries lorries and a steady flow of cars. Spring and autumn are lovely, with blossom or colour, and summer mornings are cool under the trees; winter can bring ice to the shaded corners, so be wary from November.

The nearest towns are small, with Cizer, Stârciu and Buciumi offering little beyond a shop and a bar, and Crasna the biggest at about fifteen kilometres. For reliable fuel, a meal and a bed, Zalău is the answer, a county seat with several stations and restaurants. Fill up there before heading into the hills, bring water, and give yourself time to stop on a ridge and look west toward the line that Rome once drew across these woods.
