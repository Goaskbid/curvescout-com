Feldioara - Cetățeaua

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The Făgăraș mountains are the highest wall in Romania, and from the Olt valley at Ucea they rise like a grey and white curtain across the southern sky, with Moldoveanu and Negoiu, the country's two highest peaks, hidden somewhere in the ridge. Down in the valley, near the villages of Ucea de Jos and Feldioara, the Romans had a fort at a site called Cetățeaua, guarding the Olt route that formed part of the frontier system of Dacia. The remains, earthworks and fragments in the fields, are included in the Frontiers of the Roman Empire listing for Dacia, added by UNESCO in 2024.

The Olt valley here is a broad, green corridor of fields, meadows and villages, with the Saxon and Romanian communities of the Țara Făgărașului, the land of Făgăraș, living side by side for centuries. Many villages have fortified churches or old wooden churches, and the region has a strong tradition of folk music and costume.

For riders, this is the gateway to the Transfăgărășan, one of the most famous roads in the world, which starts at Cârțișoara a short distance to the west and climbs in a series of dramatic hairpins to the Bâlea Lake at over two thousand metres, before tunnelling through the ridge and descending south toward Curtea de Argeș. The high section is open only in summer, usually from early July to the end of October, depending on snow, and it is busy with tourists, so ride early in the morning or on weekdays. The road along the Olt valley itself is flat and fast, with heavy truck traffic.

Ucea de Jos is a small village, and the nearest town with full services is Victoria, about six kilometres away, a town built in communist times around a chemical works, with fuel and supermarkets. Făgăraș, to the east, is a larger town with a well-preserved medieval fortress. Food in the region is hearty: mountain cheeses, smoked meats, cabbage rolls and the soups that Romanians eat with almost every meal.

Come in summer, when the Transfăgărășan is open and the meadows full of flowers, or in early autumn, when the forests turn and the high road is quieter. Look up at the mountains from the valley in the morning light, and plan your ascent.
