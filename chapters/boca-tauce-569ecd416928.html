Boca Tauce

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Boca Tauce is where the south road into Teide National Park meets the one that cuts across the caldera, a junction sitting a little over two thousand metres up on Tenerife's volcanic spine. There is no village, just tarmac, pale pumice, and the rust-coloured Roques de García standing off to the east like a row of melted candles. The mountain itself, Teide, rises behind them to 3,718 metres, the highest point in Spain, and on a clear morning it looks close enough to hit with a thrown helmet.

The ride up is a slow education in how a landscape changes. From Vilaflor the TF-21 climbs through Canary pine forest, long-needled trees that survive fire by sprouting from the bark, and then the trees give out and you are in a different planet. The surface is generally good, the corners open and rhythmic rather than tight, and the air thins enough that the bike feels a touch lazier on the straights. Weekends bring coaches and rental cars crawling to the viewpoints, so go early or late. In winter this is a genuine mountain: snow and ice can close or slow the road, and it is properly cold when the coast below is in T-shirt weather. Dress for the altitude, not the beach.

The ground here is the floor of Las Cañadas, a vast collapsed caldera ringed by cliffs, with Teide built up inside it by later eruptions. Black lava fields, stands of golden retama broom that flower in late spring, and a silence that carries. The Guanches, Tenerife's original inhabitants, considered the mountain a place where the devil lived, and standing among the lava you can see why. The park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the lack of light pollution makes the night sky famously clear.

Plan fuel before you climb. There is nothing in the park, and Vilaflor is small, so the safer move is to fill up in Guía de Isora or Adeje on the way. Water, coffee and a basic lunch can be had at the Parador and the visitor facilities a few kilometres along, at tourist prices. Come down the same way you came or follow the TF-38 towards Santiago del Teide, a quieter descent with views toward La Gomera on a good day.
