Qalaat Deir Kifa

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Southern Lebanon has a castle on almost every second hilltop, and Qalaat Deir Kifa is one of the less-known ones, a ruined fortification above the village of Deir Kifa in the rolling hills of Jabal Amel, between the Litani river and the coast at Tyre. Like many sites in this landscape, it has been built on, fought over and reused across several eras, and today what remains is stone walls, scattered blocks and a view across a countryside of olive groves, tobacco terraces and stony ridges.

Its better-known neighbour gives a sense of the region's history. A short distance to the east, the Crusader castle of Beaufort, Qalaat al-Shaqif, stands above the Litani gorge near Arnoun, one of the great medieval fortresses of the Levant. In the twelfth and thirteenth centuries this was a contested frontier between Crusader lordships and Muslim states, and smaller forts and watchtowers like the one at Deir Kifa guarded roads and valleys between larger strongholds. The area's history runs much deeper, too: Tyre on the coast was one of the great Phoenician cities, and its Roman ruins and hippodrome are among the finest in the eastern Mediterranean.

On a map the riding looks appealing. Roads loop through the hills from Nabatieh toward Tyre and the coast, with constant changes of gradient, tight corners through villages and long views over valleys to Mount Hermon, white with snow in winter and spring. In practice, southern Lebanon has been a war zone in recent years, with heavy fighting along the border and in the villages throughout this area, and damage and unexploded ordnance are real risks. Anyone thinking of riding here must check current official travel advice first, stay on main roads, and be guided by local people about what is safe.

Practicalities depend on the situation at the time. Nabatieh, around fifteen kilometres away, is the nearest large town, with fuel, food and shops in normal conditions, and Tyre has everything a traveller would need. Lebanese food is a pleasure anywhere: manakish from the bakery in the morning, a plate of hummus and grilled meat at midday, and strong coffee scented with cardamom.

If the time comes when the hills are calm again, this will be a beautiful corner to ride, with spring wildflowers on the terraces, the smell of thyme on the wind, and a castle wall to sit on while the sun goes down over the sea.
