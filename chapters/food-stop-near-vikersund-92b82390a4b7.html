Food stop near Vikersund

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Ski flyers have soared further at Vikersund than almost anywhere else. The giant hill above the village, Vikersundbakken, is one of the largest ski flying hills in the world and has seen several world records, with jumpers flying well over two hundred metres. Even in summer, the scale of the in-run and landing slope is astonishing, and the view from the top over the lake is spectacular.

Vikersund lies at the northern tip of Tyrifjorden, a large lake west of Oslo, surrounded by forested hills and farmland. The area is part of Modum, a municipality with a rich industrial past. A short ride north, at Åmot, the Blaafarveværket was once a major producer of cobalt blue pigment, exported around the world in the nineteenth century; the old works are now a museum and art gallery, with a beautiful setting by a waterfall.

As for the riding, it is pleasant and varied. Roads follow the shore of the lake, with gentle curves and views over the water, and climb into the hills, with forests and farms. Traffic is light, but the main roads can be busy on weekends. Speed limits are low and enforced, and wildlife can be a hazard. The weather can change quickly, and the lake can create mist in the mornings.

Vikersund has fuel, shops and cafés, making it a good place for a break. Try a Norwegian pastry or a light lunch before continuing towards the Atlantic coast, a long ride to the north-west through mountains and valleys.
