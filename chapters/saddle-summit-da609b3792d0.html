Saddle summit

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

At 2,020 metres, the high point of the Saddle Road is a spot where the Big Island makes you feel its scale. You are on a broad, tilted plateau between Mauna Kea and Mauna Loa, and everything around you is volcanic: black lava from flows within recent human memory, scrubby grass, a few pale ohia trees, and a sky that can be absolutely blue or absolutely gone, depending on which minute you arrive.

The road here is the island's great cross-country route, and for years it was notorious. Rental companies forbade it, the surface was narrow and broken, and the military used it more than tourists did. The modern highway is a different animal. It was rebuilt into a wide, smooth road with good sight lines, and bikers now use it as a fast, easy way to go from Hilo to the Kona side. The curves are gentle and the camber is kind. The conditions are the challenge: strong crosswinds off the volcano slopes, sudden banks of cloud, and in the early morning a cold that bites through a summer jacket. Wet patches, sometimes slick, appear in the shade.

What is interesting is the geology. Mauna Loa is the largest active volcano on Earth by volume, and its flows have come close to this road more than once, most recently in the 2022 eruption, which sent lava toward the highway before it stopped. You are riding across ground that is, by geological standards, not finished yet. The older Mauna Kea, to the north, is dormant, and its flanks hold the astronomical observatories. Pohakuloa, the large military training area nearby, explains the occasional convoy and the sign for unexploded ordnance off the road. Stay on the pavement.

Waimea is the nearest decent-sized town, about thirty kilometres away, with petrol, food and a very different, cattle-ranch feel at the edge of the hills. Hilo to the east and the Kona coast to the west also have everything. Bring warm kit even if the beach was hot, and expect to feel slightly breathless if you hike anywhere.
