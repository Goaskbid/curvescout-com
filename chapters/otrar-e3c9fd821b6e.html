Otrar

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Otrar is where a single diplomatic disaster helped change the history of Asia. In 1218, the governor of this city, an official of the Khwarezmian empire, seized a trade caravan sent by Genghis Khan and executed its merchants, and when the Mongol ruler's envoys protested, one of them was killed too. The Mongols responded with a campaign that destroyed Khwarezm and, in time, much of Central Asia. Otrar was besieged and sacked, and its governor reportedly met a grim end.

The city stood where the Arys river met the Syr Darya, in a fertile belt of irrigated land, and it was a major stop on the Silk Road long before the Mongols. It was also the birthplace of Al-Farabi, the tenth-century philosopher and scholar often called the Second Teacher after Aristotle, and it was where Timur died in 1405, while leading an army toward China. Today the site, called Otrar-Tobe, is a large mound with fragments of walls, streets and a citadel, standing in flat, open farmland.

The ride from Turkistan is straightforward: paved roads across the plain, passing the small village of Qogham, with the occasional pothole and plenty of livestock. The landscape is flat, hot and dry in summer, with fields and canals around the river, and vivid green in spring. It is easy riding, but also demanding in its own way, with long straight sections that can lull you, side winds, and heat. Early morning or evening makes the most sense, and a good hat is better than any fashion.

Fuel and food are not available at the site, and Qogham is a small village, so arrive with a full tank from Turkistan, which has all the services you need. A small museum nearby displays finds from the excavations, and the visit takes an hour or two. There is little shade and no great spectacle, only broken walls and a sense of scale, so it appeals most to people who like history. Bring water, take your time, and perhaps stand on the mound at sunset, when the dust turns the light gold and the ruin looks as it did to the last caravan.
