Wallsee - Kastell

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Wallsee is one of those Danube villages that people cycle through on the long riverside path without guessing how much history sits under their tyres. A castle rises on a rock above the river, and beneath and around the village lie the remains of a Roman fort that guarded this stretch of the Danube frontier. It is part of the Danube Limes World Heritage Site, inscribed in 2021, which follows the river from Bavaria through Austria to Slovakia, a line of forts, watchtowers and towns that held the northern edge of the empire for centuries.

There is not much Roman masonry to see, but the setting makes the point. The river is broad here, and from the high ground above it you can see a long way upstream and down, perfect for watching boats and anyone trying to cross. The later castle uses the same logic, and has dominated the village since the Middle Ages.

Wallsee lies in the Mostviertel, the cider country of Lower Austria, named after Most, the fermented juice of pears and apples. In April the old pear trees that line the roads and fields burst into white blossom, and the whole landscape looks like it has been dusted with snow. Farm inns serve Most with cold meats and bread, and in autumn you can buy fresh juice and fruit at the roadside.

The roads around here are gentle rather than dramatic, rolling through farmland and orchards, with views of the river and, on clear days, the Alps to the south. The main road along the Danube is pleasant, and the bridge at Wallsee or nearby crossings lead to the north bank, where the hills of the Mühlviertel and the Strudengau gorge downstream offer more curves. Watch out for cyclists on the riverside roads, especially in summer.

Wallsee itself is a small market village; for a wider choice of fuel and services, Perg, eleven kilometres away across the river, is a straightforward option, and Amstetten to the south has more. The power station at Ybbs-Persenbeug downstream and the town of Grein, with its old theatre, make good stops if you are heading east along the river.
