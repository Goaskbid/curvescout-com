Teahupoo

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The road to Teahupoo ends rather than finishes. You run down the south side of Tahiti Iti, the small round peninsula hanging off the main island like a second thought, through villages with names that sound like a drumbeat, and at the end the tarmac simply gives up at a river mouth and a patch of grass. Beyond is the bush, a footpath and then the cliffs; nobody drives around the far side.

Out on the reef a few hundred metres offshore is the reason the whole world knows this tiny place. Teahupoo is one of the heaviest waves on earth, a thick lip of water that breaks over a shallow reef, and the name is often translated by locals with a grim joke about a wall of skulls. When a big south swell arrives, boats and jet skis crowd the channel and the road is lined with photographers. The Olympic surfing contest of 2024 was held here, and the tall judging tower on the reef became part of the skyline. On a flat day it is just a calm blue lagoon and a pass, and you can sit on the sea wall and wonder what the fuss is about.

The ride in has its own charm. From Taravao the road runs along the coast with the lagoon on one side and thick green slopes on the other, the bends easy, the surface decent but patched, and the traffic mostly local pickups and the occasional tour van. Watch for children and dogs in the villages, and for loose coral grit at junctions. The light is lovely late in the afternoon, when the ridge above the village turns gold and the air smells of frangipani, woodsmoke and salt.

Plan fuel before you come. Taravao, twelve kilometres or so back, is the sensible place to fill up, and there is little in the way of petrol out here. Small snack stands and a few guesthouses serve fresh fish with rice and coconut, and a lunch of poisson cru with a cold drink while you watch the lagoon is the correct way to end the road.
