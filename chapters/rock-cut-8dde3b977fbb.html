Rock Cut

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Rock Cut is a place where the road runs into a cliff and the cliff loses. The engineers blasted a deep trench through a ridge of ancient Precambrian rock here, high on Trail Ridge Road at more than twelve thousand feet, and the trench left steep walls on either side, with the highway squeezed between them. You stop at the car park nearby, climb a short paved path and step out onto the alpine tundra, with the world dropping away on all sides and the wind pulling at your jacket.

The tundra is the real subject. Above the treeline, the land is covered with a thin carpet of tiny plants that grow close to the ground to survive the cold, the wind and the short growing season, and in July they burst into flower: moss campion, alpine forget-me-not, sky pilot and paintbrush. The plants are fragile, and a footprint can damage them for years, so stay on the paths. Yellow-bellied marmots whistle from the rocks, pikas squeak in the boulder fields, and herds of elk may graze the slopes in summer. Overhead, the sky is a deep, hard blue, and clouds can drop in within minutes.

The road is the highest continuous paved road in the United States, and Rock Cut is one of the places where you feel it. The ride up from Estes Park is a smooth, steady climb, with long bends, views over valleys and peaks, and increasingly bare, rocky terrain as you leave the forest. At the top, the wind can be cold and strong, and summer thunderstorms can bring lightning, hail and sudden temperature drops, so start early and keep an eye on the sky. The air is thin, and the altitude can make you light-headed or breathless, so go slowly and drink water.

Trail Ridge Road is closed by snow for much of the year, usually from mid-October to late May, and snow can fall in any month. There are toilets at Rock Cut but no other services, and you should fill up in Estes Park, about fourteen kilometres below, where there are petrol stations and shops. Bring warm gloves and a windproof jacket, even in July.

Walk the short path onto the tundra, stand still and listen to the wind. It is a quiet and very big place.
