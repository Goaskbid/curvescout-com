Zone C

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Zone C of the Coffee Cultural Landscape lies north of Armenia, in the steeper, higher country of Caldas, near Filadelfia and Aranzazu. This is the same UNESCO landscape, but it feels different from the Quindío end: the slopes are bigger, the farms more scattered, and the volcano Nevado del Ruiz, which looms to the east, often stands out against the sky on a clear morning. The eruption of that volcano in 1985 caused one of Colombia's worst disasters, and its presence remains part of local life.

The towns here are small and traditional, with a main square, a church and a few cafés. Filadelfia, with just over five thousand people, is a quiet coffee town, and Aranzazu is slightly larger. Neira, farther on, is a bit bigger still. Fuel is available in these towns, though Manizales, the regional capital, has far more choice. Farms along the roads put out coffee beans to dry on patios, and the smell of roasting and drying beans drifts through the villages.

The riding is intense. Roads follow ridges and twist down into river valleys, with hairpins, blind corners and sheer drops, and the surface can be rough after rain. Heavy trucks carry coffee, bananas and building materials, and the driving style is assertive. Take it slowly, sound your horn on blind bends and stay alert for landslides, which are common during the wet seasons, from April to May and October to November.

Altitude moderates the heat, so mornings are cool and the air is fresh. Bring a light jacket and rain gear. Culturally, this is paisa country, with a love of music, hospitality and strong coffee, and visitors are usually welcomed with a smile. Try a freshly brewed cup at a roadside farm, with a piece of panela, the hard brown sugar of the region, and watch the clouds roll over the hills as the afternoon turns grey.
