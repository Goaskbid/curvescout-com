Shiryaevo

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Shiryaevo lies in the Samara Bend, the great loop of the Volga where the river swings around the Zhiguli Mountains. The hills here are not high, topping out near three hundred and seventy meters, but they rise suddenly from the water in forested cliffs and ravines, and in a country of flat plains they make a spectacular sight. Limestone and dolomite form the rock, and the slopes carry oak, linden and maple, with steppe plants on the south-facing ridges. The Zhiguli Nature Reserve protects part of the range and some rare plants that survived the Ice Age here.

The village is best known for a painter. In the summer of 1870, the young Ilya Repin came to Shiryaevo with fellow students to sketch the Volga, and the sketches he made of barge haulers became the basis for his famous painting Barge Haulers on the Volga. A small museum in the village commemorates his stay and displays his drawings, and the quiet streets above the river give a sense of what the countryside looked like then. The hills are also rich in legend. The folk hero and rebel Stenka Razin is said to have hidden here with his band of river pirates in the seventeenth century, and many caves and cliffs carry his name.

The riding is a high point of the Volga region. The road along the river follows the shore in gentle bends, with views of the water, the hills and passing ships, and climbs into the wooded slopes in places. The surface is variable, with good stretches and patches of broken asphalt, and traffic is lively on summer weekends when people from Samara and Tolyatti come to swim and picnic. Early morning is the best time, with mist on the water and few other travelers. The weather can be hot in July and August, and thunderstorms build quickly in the afternoon.

There are few services in the village itself, so plan for fuel and food in Zhigulevsk or Samara. A cafe or two operates in summer, serving fish and tea.

Take time to walk up to a viewpoint above the village. From there, the Volga seems enormous, and the hills fall away in folds of green and blue.
