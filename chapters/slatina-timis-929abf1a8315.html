Slatina-Timiş

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Slatina-Timiș lies in the long corridor of the Timiș river, the natural route that runs south from Caransebeș toward the Danube at Orșova, with the Semenic Mountains to the west and the Țarcu and Godeanu ranges rising to the east. It is a working village of around three thousand people, spread along the main road and the river, with fields on the valley floor and forest on the slopes. Most people pass straight through on the E70, but for anyone riding the Transsemenic, this is where the mountain road comes down from the Semenic plateau to meet the valley.

This corridor has always mattered. The Timiș and Cerna valleys together form a passage through the mountains between the Banat plain and the Danube, and the Romans used it for their road to Dacia after the Danube was bridged, with forts along the way. Later it became part of the Habsburg military frontier, where border regiments of peasant soldiers guarded the edge of the empire against the Ottomans, and the villages still show the regular layout of that era. The railway from Timișoara to Orșova and the Iron Gates follows the same line, threading between the hills beside the road.

The ride down from the Semenic toward Slatina-Timiș is a pleasure: a long descent through thick beech forest, with bends that come one after another and occasional glimpses of the valley below. The surface can be broken in places and the road narrow, so keep some margin, especially in the wet. Once in the valley, the main road is wide and fast, with good sightlines but plenty of lorries, as this is a key route to the Danube and the border. The scenery is pleasant rather than dramatic, with the river, fields and villages, and the higher peaks to the east sometimes holding snow well into spring.

For practical needs, Slatina-Timiș is a small place, and services are limited. The reliable choice for fuel, food and other supplies is Caransebeș, about twenty kilometres to the north, a market town with stations, shops and restaurants. Heading south, the next good stops are Băile Herculane, the old spa town in the Cerna valley, and Orșova on the Danube. In summer the valley can be hot and still, while the mountains above stay cooler, so time your descent for the afternoon if you want to arrive in the warmth.
