Near Ogrodzieniec

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Ogrodzieniec Castle is the biggest ruin on the Eagles' Nests Trail, and the first sight of it is a proper surprise: a long, ragged line of walls and towers sitting along a limestone ridge above the village of Podzamcze, with strange pale rocks poking out of the woods all around it. It looks like a film set, and in fact it has been used as one. The Jura upland here is full of such outcrops, which makes the whole hilltop seem to grow out of the stone.

The castle began as a medieval fortress and then grew under the Boner family, rich bankers and royal officials in the sixteenth century, who turned it into a magnificent Renaissance residence. Its decline came with war: in the seventeenth century Swedish troops did heavy damage during the invasion Poles call the Deluge, and the place was never fully rebuilt. Fire and neglect did the rest, and the ruins stood roofless for generations. Today you can climb through the gate, up the towers and around the courtyard, with views over the village and the forest. Legends cling to it, including a black dog said to roam the grounds and a ghost of Boner's wife, and the guides are happy to tell them.

The ride here from the south is a good one, a quiet run across the upland with long open straights, gentle hills and small villages with whitewashed churches, the road hugging the ridge before it drops toward Podzamcze. Surfaces vary on the secondary roads, and heavy trucks use the main routes near Zawiercie, so keep an eye on your mirrors. The best light is late afternoon, when the limestone turns warm and the shadows of the walls stretch across the grass.

Podzamcze has a busy tourist strip at the foot of the castle, with car parks, stalls, grill bars and ice cream, and it can feel crowded in July and August. Arriving early or on a weekday makes the experience better. The village of Ogrodzieniec has a few thousand inhabitants and shops, and Zawiercie, about ten kilometres away, is the larger town if you need a bigger selection of fuel or repairs.

Rock climbers also love this area, and you may see ropes dangling on the cliffs. Bring sturdy boots if you plan to climb the towers.
