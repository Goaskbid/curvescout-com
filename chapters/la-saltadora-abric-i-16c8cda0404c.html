La Saltadora- Abric I

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Albocàsser sits on a limestone shelf in the northern Maestrat, a farming village of about fourteen hundred people with a stone church, a market-day bustle and almond trees all around. A few kilometres from it, the Gasulla ravine and its neighbours hold Levantine rock paintings, and La Saltadora, the Jumper, is one of the shelters, named perhaps for a leaping figure or a nimble ledge. The Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula was added to the UNESCO list in 1998, and Abric I is a small but significant piece of that story.

The country around it is quietly beautiful. Olive terraces climb the slopes, shepherds' huts of dry stone, the barraques, dot the fields, and the smell of wild rosemary is constant. In spring the hills are green and spangled with wildflowers; in late summer they are tawny and loud with cicadas. Birds of prey ride the thermals above the ridges, and wild boar leave their tracks on every path.

The ride out is simple. Albocàsser is on a fine network of country roads that connect the villages of the Maestrat, and the surfaces are generally good, with gentle gradients and curves that fit a relaxed pace. Fuel and shops are limited, though the village has basic services, and the larger coast town of Alcalà de Xivert is the better bet for filling up before climbing into the hills. Nights can be cold even in spring, with a sharp drop after sunset.

For lunch, look for a village restaurant serving rabbit with snails, lamb with garlic, or a thick vegetable stew, with a glass of the local red. Tourism here is calm and respectful, and the rock shelters deserve the same attitude: look, photograph without flash, and leave everything as you found it. If you plan to see the paintings, find out first which sites are open and when, since access is controlled and some require a guide. Early morning is the best time to enjoy the silence of the ravines.
