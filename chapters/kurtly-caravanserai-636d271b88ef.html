Kurtly ‘caravanserai’

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Kurtly sits on the flat plain near Merv, in the south of the Karakum, and the caravanserai that gives the place its label is a reminder that the old Silk Road was a network of small stops as much as great cities. A caravanserai was a fortified inn where merchants and animals could rest, drink and sleep within walls, usually a day's travel from the next one. Some were grand, with arcades and domed rooms. Others were simple compounds of mud brick. Few survive intact, and the ones in Turkmenistan are often reduced to low walls and mounds.

Merv itself is about thirty kilometres away, and the surrounding region is the Murgab oasis, one of the oldest irrigated lands in Central Asia. The Murgab river dries into the desert here, and canals draw its water off to feed cotton and wheat. Local rides connect Kurtly with other sites, including the mosque and mausoleum of Talkhatan Baba, a mud-brick building from the eleventh century that is still a place of local pilgrimage, and the Kelte Minara, a short, stubby, tiled minaret.

The landscape is flat and almost treeless, with irrigation ditches, camels and sheep, and fields of cotton in summer. Roads are straight, the surface changes without warning, and the sun is hard from May to September. Wind blows dust across the road in dry weather. Bayramaly, about thirty kilometres away, is the nearest big town and should have fuel and basic food, while Mary is the regional centre. In Turkmenistan, fuel is cheap by European standards but not always easy to find on rural roads, so fill up whenever you can.

Travel here needs planning, since foreign visitors normally need permits and a guide, and motorcycle rules may be strict. Check before you go. When you reach Kurtly, give yourself a few minutes to look at the walls and think about the people who rested here, tired, thirsty and probably worried about the next day's road. The place rewards silence and attention more than photographs. Late afternoon, with long shadows and cooler air, is the time to be here.
