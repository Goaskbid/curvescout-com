Qachas Nek

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Qacha's Nek is a border town in the far southeast of Lesotho, perched on a ridge at the top of a pass that the town takes its name from, with a sheer drop on one side and a view across a sea of ridges on the other. It is a remote place by any standard, and the border post, leads over the pass to the South African town of Matatiele, a short ride away. About twenty-five thousand people live in the district, and the town itself is a modest cluster of shops, government offices and guesthouses.

The ride in is a long climb through mountain country, on roads that are mostly paved and sometimes broken. The route from the north runs through high valleys with wide views, and the final approach to the town winds up the escarpment in a series of bends with fine views. The road toward Sehlabathebe is a different matter: it is gravel and rough, and leads to a remote national park, so it is a ride for experienced riders on suitable bikes. Weather can be cold, wet and misty, and in winter snow can close roads. Livestock, minibus taxis and the odd rock on the road all demand attention.

The town has a history as a frontier. The pass was long used by herders and traders moving between Lesotho and the Eastern Cape, and the border was drawn along the Drakensberg ridge in the nineteenth century when the territory became a British protectorate. Remote mountain communities here have preserved much of the traditional Basotho way of life, including the herding of cattle and sheep and the building of round stone huts with thatched roofs. The Sehlabathebe National Park, southeast of town, is a UNESCO World Heritage extension for its natural and cultural value, with rock art and a lake in a high, wild plateau.

Nature is rugged and cold. The highlands are covered in grass and shrub, with rocky outcrops and the occasional stand of trees, and the rivers are clear and cold. Rare wildlife includes the bearded vulture and the Maloti minnow, a fish found only in these mountain streams, and the sky is huge. In winter, the peaks wear a mantle of snow.

You can buy fuel and food in town, which is useful, as supplies thin out quickly in the mountains. Bring passport, vehicle papers and insurance, carry warm clothes, and allow extra time for border formalities and for the road.
