Nyamata

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The church at Nyamata is a plain brick building with a corrugated iron roof, and what it holds is the memory of one of the worst weeks of the twentieth century. In April 1994, during the genocide against the Tutsi in Rwanda, thousands of people from the surrounding villages took refuge in the church, as they had in earlier waves of violence, assuming that a house of God would protect them. It did not. Killers entered with grenades, machetes and clubs, and in the days that followed some ten thousand people died in and around the church. Today it is a memorial. The clothes of the victims lie in piles on the pews, the altar cloth is still stained, and in an underground vault the remains of tens of thousands from the district are kept in coffins and on shelves.

I am telling you this plainly because the place deserves plain words. Nyamata is one of four genocide memorials, with Murambi, Bisesero and the one in Kigali, that UNESCO added to the World Heritage list in 2023. It is a place to go with respect, to be quiet, and to listen to the guides, many of whom are survivors.

The setting is the Bugesera, a low, dry region of hills and wetlands south of Kigali, with lakes and papyrus swamps, fields of cassava, banana groves and small farms. Kigali, with three quarters of a million people, is about twenty kilometres away, and has all the fuel, repair shops and hotels you could need. Nyamata itself is a busy town with petrol stations, shops and cafes.

Riding in Rwanda is easier than many expect. Roads are generally well surfaced, traffic is orderly, and helmets are required for riders and passengers. Motorbike taxis, motos, are everywhere, and drivers are used to bikes. Watch for pedestrians, cyclists with huge loads and goats. The dry seasons, June to September and December to February, are best, and rain can come hard in March to May.

Dress modestly and ask before photographing inside the church. Photography is often restricted, and it is better to leave the camera in the bag. The memorial is free, though donations are welcome. Give yourself an hour, and some quiet time afterwards.
