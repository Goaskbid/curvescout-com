Castolon

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Castolon is an old army outpost that turned into a cotton-farming village and then into a quiet park service stop, with a few adobe buildings and a trading post clustered near the river. It sits in the southwestern part of Big Bend National Park, where the Rio Grande draws a long green line through the pale desert, and across the water lies Mexico, with only a rowing boat's width of difference. The place is small enough to see in ten minutes and interesting enough to spend an hour.

The history is blunt. In the years around the Mexican Revolution, the border was a dangerous place, and the US cavalry kept a camp here to protect settlers from raiders. Afterwards farmers planted cotton on the river flats, and the store, known as La Harmonia, served workers on both sides of the river. The company went away and the farming faded, but the store survives, selling cold drinks, snacks and supplies in a long, shaded building.

The ride to get here is the whole point. Coming from the east on the river track, you pass through creosote flats, along dry washes, with the Chisos Mountains bulging to the north and the Mexican range sitting low and blue to the south. The surface is dirt and gravel, sometimes rocky and sometimes silty, and rain can make a mess of it. The paved route on the west side is the Ross Maxwell Scenic Drive, which comes down from the mountains and passes some of the best views in the park, including a long view toward Santa Elena Canyon, where the river has cut a slot more than three hundred metres deep through the limestone.

A short drive from Castolon is the trailhead for that canyon, and it is worth a walk in the late afternoon when the cliffs glow. Watch the heat, and carry plenty of water, because there is little shade.

Services are thin. The night sky is among the darkest in the lower forty-eight, and a clear night out here is a quiet reminder of how big the country is.
