Ancient Dam Of Ma Rib Southern Bank

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The southern end of the Ma'rib dam is the quieter twin of the northern one, and it is the place that makes clearest how the whole scheme worked. Across the Wadi Dhana, the Sabaeans raised a long earthen embankment, and on the south bank they built a stone sluice complex and a system of channels that sent the flood water into the fields on that side. The stones are huge and neatly dressed, and the walls curve around the spillway in a way that suggests careful hydraulic planning.

The dam is usually dated to around the eighth or seventh century BC, and it was repaired and raised over the following centuries. Inscriptions on the stones, in the elegant angular script of ancient South Arabia, record these works and name the rulers who ordered them. At its height the system irrigated a large oasis, and writers in antiquity described Arabia Felix, happy Arabia, as a land of gardens and rich harvests. Some of that reputation must have come from this valley. Around the sixth century AD the dam broke for the last time, and the community of the oasis, already weakened by political troubles, never fully recovered.

The ruins are impressive in the sheer scale of the effort. Standing on the southern sluice and looking across the dry bed of the wadi, the northern gate is visible in the distance, a reminder of how large the structure was. A modern dam was built upstream in the 1980s with help from the United Arab Emirates, named after Sheikh Zayed, and it now holds back water that the ancient builders would have recognized as a gift.

Visiting is a different matter. Ma'rib has been a battleground in Yemen's civil war, and the region has seen fighting, displacement and risk. Foreign governments advise against travel, and no one should plan to ride here without up-to-date information, which currently says stay away. It is better to remember the place through its history.

If the situation improves, the site is a short drive from Ma'rib town, where fuel and basic food were once available. Go early to avoid the heat, and bring plenty of water.
