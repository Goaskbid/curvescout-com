Atlantis

Opening times, access and services can change - check before you go.

The name Swartland means black land, from the dark renosterbos scrub that once covered the hills, and the landscape is rolling farmland, with wheat fields that turn from green to gold and then to stubble as the year goes on.

The coast lies about twenty kilometres west, at Yzerfontein, where the cold Atlantic washes onto long beaches and small rocky coves, so a swim is possible but bracing. Water along this coast comes from the Benguela current and is rarely above fifteen degrees Celsius, so do not expect warmth even in January. Inland, the ground rises to a ridge of granite hills, and the farm roads are mostly gravel or tarmac patched by the heat, with light traffic and long clear views.

Darling has a small claim to cultural fame. The pace is slow and the people are friendly, which makes it a good place to stop for breakfast.

On a motorbike the roads are open and mostly straight, with gentle undulations and wind that can build in the afternoon. Surfaces are fair, though some farm roads are gravel and corrugated. The nearest city of any size is Atlantis, about thirty kilometres away. Carry water, since shade is rare, and expect cool mornings and hot afternoons even in the middle of winter.
