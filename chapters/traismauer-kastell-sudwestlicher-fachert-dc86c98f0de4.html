Traismauer - Kastell südwestlicher Fächert

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Traismauer is a small wine town on the south side of the Danube, just where the Traisen river comes down from the Alpine foothills to meet the big river, and it has been a fortified place for something like eighteen centuries. The Romans called their fort here Augustianis, one link in the chain of camps and watchtowers that guarded the frontier of the province of Noricum. In 2021 the Danube Limes was added to the UNESCO list, and Traismauer is one of the places where you can still see the old walls doing a job.

The south-western corner is the bit to look for. Late Roman builders strengthened their forts with projecting towers, and a fan-shaped corner tower like the one here was designed to let defenders cover the walls on both sides. Much of the town grew up inside and on top of the Roman footprint, so the medieval gate tower and later houses lean on masonry that was laid by soldiers. It is a quiet, almost domestic kind of history; you walk between gardens and garages and suddenly notice that the wall in front of you is Roman.

Riding in, you come across the flat Tullnerfeld, a broad plain of fields and orchards where the roads are straight and the main hazards are tractors, crosswinds off the river and the occasional village speed check. Head west and things get far more interesting: Krems is about twelve kilometres away, and beyond it the Wachau opens out, with terraced vineyards, apricot orchards and the river road through Dürnstein and Spitz. That stretch is lovely on a weekday morning and can be slow on summer weekends when every cyclist and coach in Lower Austria has the same idea.

Traismauer itself is big enough for basics. You will find a café or a Heuriger, the local wine taverns that open when they have new wine to sell, and the Traisental around the town is known for its Grüner Veltliner. For a proper choice of fuel stations and shops, Krems is the reliable bet. The nearby Danube cycle path means bakeries and benches are never far off.

The light here is soft and wide, with the river hidden behind its floodplain forest and the hills of the Wachau rising blue to the west. On a hot afternoon the shade of the old walls is a fine place to stop, eat something and imagine the frontier as the Romans saw it: the river, the forest and the unknown on the far bank.
