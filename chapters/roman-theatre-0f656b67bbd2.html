Roman Theatre

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The Roman theatre in Mérida is one of the best-preserved in the world, and it still does the job it was built for. It was commissioned around 16 to 15 BC by Marcus Agrippa, Augustus's right-hand man, and the semicircle of stone seats rising up the hillside could hold roughly six thousand spectators. Behind the stage, a tall double colonnade of Corinthian columns was rebuilt in the twentieth century from fallen fragments, and it is the part that most visitors photograph. The curved tiers face south-west, so the evening light turns the stone a deep gold.

Every summer the Festival Internacional de Teatro Clásico fills the seats with a modern audience watching Greek tragedies and Roman comedies, usually under a warm night sky, a tradition that began in 1933. The amphitheatre is next door and held around fifteen thousand people for gladiator fights; together the two form the heart of the archaeological site. If the stone has an odd feel under your boots, it should: the seats were designed to separate social classes, with the best places nearest the stage reserved for the elite and the highest seats for the poor and for women.

For riders, the logistics are simple. Mérida is a good-sized town with all services, including petrol stations on the ring road and bike-friendly parking in the streets around the site. The ride here begins with flat, open roads across the Guadiana plain, where long straights run between olive trees and sheep country. Heat is the main enemy, so carry water, wear ventilated gear and plan to be on the road by early morning in summer. In the cooler months, the plain is green and quiet, and storks nest on every available pylon and church tower.

Tickets for the site usually cover several monuments together, so you can wander between the theatre, the amphitheatre and the Roman house of the Mithraeum if time permits. The place is busiest in the middle of the day, so arriving early or late is a quiet pleasure. After that, take the road east toward the Guadalupe hills and let the plain unroll behind you. It is a gentler start to the day than most mountain rides, and a good reminder that some of Spain's best roads begin with a very old stage.
