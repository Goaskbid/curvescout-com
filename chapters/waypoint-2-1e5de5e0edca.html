Waypoint 2 (area 36.3,-121.8)

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Big Sur is where California's coast shows its teeth. Between Carmel and San Simeon, the Santa Lucia range rises almost directly from the Pacific, with slopes that climb more than a thousand metres in a few kilometres, and Highway 1 is bolted to the cliff like a ledge. The area gets few services, no big towns and a lot of fog, and because the mountains cut the coast off from the interior, it stayed wild long after the rest of California was settled. In 1937 the highway opened, after many years of work, and made the coast accessible by car for the first time.

The drive is famously slow, and for good reasons. The road twists over every headland and into each creek canyon, with sharp bends, narrow shoulders and sudden drops, and speed limits drop to thirty-five or lower in places. Rockfalls and landslides are common, particularly after winter storms, and the road has been closed for months at a time, as it was after slides in 2017 that took out whole sections, so it is wise to check the state of the highway before you set out. Even in good conditions, a long line of cars can follow a camper at twenty-five, and a pull-out is the only escape.

The scenery repays the patience. Waves crash on rocks far below, sea otters lie in the kelp offshore, and in winter grey whales pass close to the cliffs on their way south. Coast redwoods fill the damp canyons, and the smell of salt, sage and eucalyptus fills the air. Pfeiffer Beach, with its purple sand, and McWay Falls, which drops onto the beach, are the best-known sights, though small pullouts hide others. The weather can be clear at one headland and fog-bound at the next.

Services are thin here. A few lodges, restaurants and a small store operate along the road, and there is a station in the Big Sur valley, with prices well above the state average, but supply can run out in busy periods. Carmel, to the north, and Cambria, to the south, are the sensible places to fill the tank. Mobile signal comes and goes.

Autumn tends to bring the clearest days and the lightest traffic. Take a layer and a camera, and do not try to cover this road quickly.
