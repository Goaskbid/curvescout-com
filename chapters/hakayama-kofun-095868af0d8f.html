Hakayama Kofun

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Hakayama Kofun is one of the quieter satellites that crowd around the giant tomb of Emperor Ojin in southeastern Osaka. The name is plain enough: Hakayama means tomb mountain, and that is how the neighbourhood treated it for a very long time. The ride calls the nearest town Kashihara, but the ground is the Kawachi plain at the foot of the Kongo range, in the Furuichi district, where the Yamato elite built a necropolis of well over a hundred mounds.

It matters because of its position. When you stand near it you can see how the great tomb and its smaller companions form a deliberate group, with the satellites placed around the main mound like a court around a king. Excavations around Ojin's tomb and its neighbours have turned up horse trappings, clay figures, iron tools and even the remains of ritual objects, and archaeologists read the layout the way a historian reads a seating plan. Hakayama is part of that layout, a small piece of a very large sentence.

The setting is more domestic than monumental. Streets run right up to the moat, a footpath circles the mound, and there is usually a gardener or a pensioner out for a morning stroll. Wildlife is better than you might expect: egrets stalk the moat water, dragonflies hover in summer, and in the woods on the mound there are bush warblers calling in spring.

The riding itself is city traffic, so lower your expectations. This is a place to put on a hat and walk. Fuel is no problem, as the surrounding towns are crowded with petrol stations, and every corner has a vending machine selling hot canned tea in winter and cold in summer.

If you have time, head up to the hills above Habikino and look back across the plain; from a little height you can see the tomb group as a series of dark green patches in a sea of rooftops. Spring cherry blossom and late autumn colour are the best times, with winter mornings clear and cold.
