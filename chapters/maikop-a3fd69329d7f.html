Maikop

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Maikop is the capital of Adygea, a small republic that sits like an island inside Krasnodar Krai, and it is the last proper city before the northern edge of the Caucasus starts to climb. With around 144,000 people it feels more relaxed than most regional capitals of its size, laid out on a wide grid of tree-lined avenues on the Belaya River, which comes down brown and fast from the mountains in spring. Khanskaya and Kuzhorskaya are the nearest villages, and the foothills begin visibly just to the south.

You come here to fill up, eat and sleep, and to prepare for the mountains. Fuel stations are plentiful on every approach, and that matters, because the roads that climb toward Dakhovskaya and the Lago-Naki plateau are lined with small settlements and very little else. The flat approach from the north crosses open farmland, and as you ride south the horizon turns blue and then green, with the wall of the Caucasus building ahead. It is one of the more satisfying slow reveals in southern Russia. Traffic in the city is ordinary, and the best advice is to be out by mid-morning so the hills are ahead of you for the day.

The city has a deeper past than its tidy streets suggest. A Russian fort was founded here in the 1850s, towards the end of the Caucasian war, and around the area there are Bronze Age burial mounds, among them the famous Maikop kurgan, whose gold and silver finds are held in St Petersburg. Archaeologists use the name Maikop culture for an early civilisation of the northern Caucasus, so you are riding through a landscape with real prehistoric importance, even if you will not see it from the saddle.

Adygea itself is home to the Adyghe people, a Circassian group, and their language, cheese and hospitality are the first things you notice. The local soft cheese is worth buying for a roadside lunch, and you will find it in the markets and in small cafes. Food here is hearty and unhurried.

The route out heads up the Belaya valley, with beech and oak forest closing in, the river getting clearer and the road narrowing into a proper mountain ride. A rider who arrives early and leaves with a full tank, warm layers for the plateau and some local cheese in the pannier has done it right.
