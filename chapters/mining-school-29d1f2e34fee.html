Mining School

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Colonial mining needed more than brute strength. It needed men who could read a gauge, repair a pump and keep records, and so the mining school at Sawahlunto is a small clue to how the Dutch tried to build a local skilled workforce. Education in the Indies was tightly rationed, and a technical school was a rare opening for the sons of Indonesian families, even if the best jobs stayed with European engineers.

The building belongs to a style found across the old town: whitewashed walls, tall shuttered windows, wide verandas to catch breezes and keep out the downpours. Inside such schools the training would have covered drawing, mechanics, basic mathematics and the practicalities of working underground. Walk the surrounding streets and you will see how the school fitted into a planned settlement, with officers' housing, a hospital, a market, and the mine entrances never far away.

There is not much here for those who want drama. The pleasure is in the details: the pattern of the roof tiles, the old iron drains, the stone steps worn smooth. The valley setting, with steep ridges all around and mist rolling down in the evening, is part of the character, and the town is quiet and cool in the early morning. Weekends bring local families, and school groups often come for field trips.

Coming from Solok, the road climbs and twists for about nineteen kilometres through plantations and small villages. Traffic is mostly trucks and scooters, and the surface can be broken at the edges. Wet afternoons are the norm in the wet season, so try to see the site before lunch. Fuel and a proper meal are best sorted in Solok, with a smaller choice in Sawahlunto. The Minangkabau dish of rendang, dark with spices and cooked down for hours, is a good reward after a day among the colonial buildings.
