Villa Valmarana Zen

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Not every Palladian villa sits on a hill with a view. Villa Valmarana at Lisiera lies on the flat Vicentine plain east of the city, among fields of maize and soy, irrigation ditches and poplar rows, and it was built for a family whose wealth came from the land itself. Palladio designed it in the middle of the sixteenth century, and it belongs to the family of villas around Vicenza that UNESCO lists together with his town palaces. The house follows his idea of a working farm dressed as a Roman temple: a central block with a portico, flanked by long service wings, the barchesse, that once held grain, tools and animals.

What to take from it is that Palladio was a practical builder as much as a poet. The long wings were barns and granaries, the cellars stored wine, and the owner could walk from his front door to the field without leaving the composition. The villa stands in a village so small it barely registers on the map, Lisiera, with a few houses and a church, and the nearest sizeable place is Monticello Conte Otto, a few kilometres away, with Vicenza itself beyond. The house is private and visits are limited, so check ahead and expect to see it from the road.

Riding on this plain is gentle, direct and sometimes dull. Roads run dead straight along canals and field edges, with sharp right-angle turns at farm tracks, and in winter fog hangs thick for days. In summer the maize stands head high and blocks the views at every junction, so approach crossings carefully. The pleasure is in what lies beside: Vicenza's arcaded streets and the Basilica Palladiana, a short ride off, and the low Berici Hills to the south, with winding lanes and hilltop sanctuaries such as Monte Berico. Those roads are better for riding than the plain.

Monticello Conte Otto has petrol stations and a few places to eat, and Vicenza has everything. Try baccalà alla vicentina, the local salt cod stewed in milk and onions, and a glass of Durello or Garganega. Spring brings blossom, and September brings the harvest and the light.
