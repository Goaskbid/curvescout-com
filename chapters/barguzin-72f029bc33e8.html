Barguzin

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The Barguzin Valley is one of those places that gets its identity from being closed in on both sides. A long, flat basin runs for around two hundred kilometres between the Barguzin range, which separates it from the eastern shore of Baikal, and the Ikat range on the other side, and the river that gives the valley its name winds along the middle before reaching the lake. The town of Barguzin, with roughly six thousand people, is one of the oldest Russian settlements in the region, begun as a fortified outpost in the middle of the seventeenth century, and the older streets still carry a faint trace of that frontier past.

Riding into the valley feels like entering a separate world. The road is mostly straight and open, with views of the snow-streaked ridges on one side and fields of grass and wildflowers on the other. In summer the meadows are bright with colour, and cattle graze right up to the road, so keep an eye out for animals. The surface is a mix of reasonable asphalt and rougher stretches, and distances between villages are long. Fuel is available in the town, but not everywhere along the valley, so treat it as the place to fill up before pushing on.

The Barguzin range holds the Barguzin Nature Reserve, which is often described as the oldest in Russia, founded in 1916 to protect the sable. The mountains behind it are a wilderness of cedar forest and bare ridges, home to brown bears, elk and the sable that gave the reserve its purpose. You will not ride into it, but you feel it in the air and in the shape of the horizon. A cold wind named after the valley, the barguzin, blows down toward the lake and can turn a calm morning into a rough crossing for boats.

Local food is simple and filling: omul from Baikal, smoked or salted, and the warm dumplings that appear on every table. If you can, take an hour to wander the town and watch the daily routine, because Barguzin is not a museum but a living place where people still move to the rhythm of the seasons. Late June to early September is the best window, with long days and mild nights.
