The Flow Country

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Most people who first see the Flow Country think it is empty. It is not; it is simply flat, wet and enormous, and it takes a while to understand what you are looking at. Spread across Caithness and Sutherland in the far north of Scotland, this is the largest expanse of blanket bog in Europe, a landscape of peat built up over thousands of years, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2024. The surface is a patchwork of sphagnum mosses, cotton grass and heather, studded with countless small pools called dubh lochans that shine like broken mirrors when the sun comes out. Under your feet, the peat holds an extraordinary amount of carbon.

The birds are the stars. Greenshank, golden plover, dunlin and red- and black-throated divers breed here, along with hen harriers and merlins hunting low over the bog. At Forsinard, the RSPB has a visitor centre in the old railway station and a short boardwalk trail leading to an observation tower, which helps make sense of the scale. In the 1980s, tax incentives encouraged large conifer plantations on the bog, causing serious damage; decades of restoration work since then, including felling trees and blocking drains, are one of the great conservation stories of Britain.

Riding through the Flow Country is a unique experience. The roads are mostly single track with passing places, like the A897 through Strath Halladale, and they demand courtesy and patience: pull in to let others pass and wave thanks. The surface is often broken at the edges, and sheep and deer wander onto the road without warning. The wind can be relentless, and the weather can change from bright sunshine to horizontal rain in minutes. In summer, the light lasts almost all night; in winter, the days are short and dark.

Plan your fuel carefully. Thurso, nearly forty kilometres away, is the nearest town with full services, and petrol stations in the countryside are few and far between. Halkirk has a few amenities, but do not rely on finding fuel or food in the smaller hamlets. Bring warm clothing, rain gear and midge repellent, as the midges can be fierce on still summer evenings.
