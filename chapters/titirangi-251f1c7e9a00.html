Titirangi

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Titirangi feels like the last stop before the wild west of Auckland, and in a sense it is. The village sits on a ridge at the edge of the city, with cafes, a few shops and houses hidden among native bush, and beyond it the Waitākere Ranges roll west toward the Tasman Sea. The name is usually translated from Māori as fringe of heaven, and on a clear evening, looking out over the Manukau Harbour with the light fading behind the hills, it is hard to argue.

The Waitākere Ranges are old volcanic hills covered in dense rainforest: kauri, rimu, nikau palms and giant tree ferns that close over the road like a green tunnel. Kauri dieback, a disease that kills these ancient trees, has led to the closure of many walking tracks, so check which are open and clean your boots at the stations if you walk. The Arataki Visitor Centre on Scenic Drive, a few minutes from the village, has a superb lookout over the forest and the harbour and explains the area's nature and Māori history.

Scenic Drive is the road for riders, winding along the spine of the ranges from Titirangi toward Swanson, with tight, blind bends, steep drops and a surface that can be greasy under the trees after rain. It is narrow and popular with cyclists and locals who know every corner, so keep it tidy. From it, roads drop down to the black sand beaches of the west coast, Piha and Karekare, where the surf is powerful and the rips are dangerous; swim only between the flags where lifeguards are on duty.

The rides that start here head the other way, south out of the city. The Hobbiton ride goes down through the Waikato to Matamata and the green, rounded hills where the film set of the Shire was built on a sheep farm. The East Cape adventure is a much bigger undertaking, a long journey around the remote eastern tip of the North Island, with Māori communities, empty beaches and long stretches between towns. Both begin with Auckland's motorways, which are congested at rush hour, so time your departure carefully.

Titirangi village has cafes and a small supermarket, and fuel is easy to find in nearby New Lynn, Glen Eden or the suburbs toward the motorway. Auckland's weather is changeable, humid and often wet, and the ranges catch more rain than the city, so pack waterproofs even when the morning looks bright.
