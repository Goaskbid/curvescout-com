Borownia Mining Field

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Borownia is the quiet cousin among the flint mining fields, and that is part of its charm. Where Krzemionki has a visitor centre and a guided underground route, Borownia is a forested landscape of low hills, in which the traces of Neolithic mining appear as a scatter of shallow hollows on the forest floor. It is one of the sites in the UNESCO listing of 2019, and its value lies in showing how widely the mining spread across the district, rather than in a single dramatic display.

The area is part of the foothills of the Świętokrzyskie Mountains, a region of old rounded ridges, rich in ancient rocks, some of the oldest in Poland. The forests are mostly pine and oak, with birch on the sandy ground, and in late summer the air is full of the smell of resin and warm needles. Mushroom pickers walk the woods in autumn, and you may see them at the roadside with baskets of boletes. Deer, foxes and wild boar live in the woods, and the birdsong in spring is loud.

Nearby, the small town of Ćmielów, about five kilometres away, has been making porcelain since the late eighteenth century, and its factory and museum are well worth a visit. Ożarów, a little farther, is known for cement. Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, the larger town, was a centre of steel making for generations, and some of the old industrial buildings are still standing.

Riding in the area is gentle and unhurried, along small roads through forest and fields, with easy bends and few vehicles. The surface varies, from fine tarmac to patched and potholed stretches, and gravel can lie at the corners, especially after farm traffic. In summer, thunderstorms build in the afternoon and can be violent. It is not a place for speed, but for the pleasure of low hills and long shadows.

For fuel, the best choice is Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, about thirteen kilometres away, which has stations, supermarkets and a good selection of cafes. Smaller places nearby may have a shop but not a pump. Bring water, since walking in the forest in summer is thirsty work.

The best time to see the landscape is in the early morning, when the mist hangs among the trees. Borownia rewards those who like to look at the ground and imagine what lies beneath.
