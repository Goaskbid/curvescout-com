Domoni

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Domoni is the old capital of Anjouan, a fishing town on the east coast with a medina that feels like another century. It was an important centre of the island's Swahili-influenced culture, a place of scholars, merchants and boat builders, and it kept a sense of its own dignity even after the capital moved to Mutsamudu. The streets are narrow lanes of coral stone and whitewash, with old carved doors, balconies, and mosques of simple elegance, and the town curls round a small harbour where boats are pulled up on the beach.

The ride to Domoni along the coast of Anjouan is a pleasure and a test. The island is a steep volcano, with mountain flanks running down to the sea, and the road snakes along it, winding in and out of villages, past clove, vanilla and banana plantations, and under mango trees that shade the verge. Bends are tight, pedestrians often take up half the road, and shared taxis stop where they please, so a relaxed pace is not an option but a necessity. Some surfaces are smooth concrete, some are patched and rough, and rain can bring sudden slides of earth. From June to October the weather is drier and the sea clearer.

What makes Domoni special is its atmosphere. In the early morning, you will see fishermen mending nets and carrying the catch up the beach, women in brightly patterned wraps called shiromani, and children heading to Quranic school. The town is also known for its role in the Comorian tradition of the grand marriage, a lavish ceremony that can involve a whole community. The air carries the smell of cloves drying, woodsmoke, and frying fish, while the call to prayer rises over the roofs. Local dishes include fish in coconut milk, rice, and cassava leaf, and tea sweetened with ginger may be offered with a smile.

The town has a population around fifteen thousand, so basic services exist, including small shops and simple food stalls. Fuel can be found on the island, but supply is not always steady, so ask in advance and fill up wherever you can. Modest dress is wise, with covered shoulders and knees, and a polite greeting in French or a few words of Shimaore will warm every conversation. If the ride continues toward Moroni, remember that it is on a different island, and a flight or boat is needed.
