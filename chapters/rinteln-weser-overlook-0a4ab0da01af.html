Rinteln Weser overlook

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Rinteln is a Weser town that sits in a narrow bend of the river, with the ridge of the Weser Hills on one side and low farmland on the other. The overlook is up on that ridge, where the road leaves the town and climbs through trees to a spot with a good view of the valley. From it you can see the river wind through the meadows, the town's church spires and red roofs in the bend, and the next line of hills in blue haze behind.

The town below has an old and slightly surprising history. For nearly two centuries, from the early seventeenth century until the early nineteenth, Rinteln had its own university, a rare thing for a place so small. It was founded by a local count and closed under Napoleon's reorganisation of the region. The market square still has fine half-timbered houses from the Weser Renaissance period, and a big Gothic church, and the streets around have an unspoilt, compact feel that comes from having been mostly bypassed by industry.

The riding up here is easy and engaging, rather than testing. The road climbs through beech wood in a handful of bends, and the surface is mostly smooth. In the valley, the road runs along the river through villages with their farms and orchards. Cyclists use the Weser cycle path heavily, so expect them on summer weekends, and give them room. Morning light on the water is lovely, and evening gives the valley a golden glow that photographs well from the overlook. In late autumn mist collects in the river bend and the ridge stands above it like an island.

For services, Rinteln is the obvious answer, with about twenty-eight thousand people, fuel stations, supermarkets and a good range of cafés and restaurants in the centre. The overlook itself has little more than a parking area and perhaps a bench, so bring your own water if the day is hot. A short walk in the beech wood will do you good before you head back down to the river road and onward. The Weser Hills are a landscape that does not shout, and rewards riders who slow down.
