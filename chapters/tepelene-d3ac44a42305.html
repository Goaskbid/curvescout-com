Tepelenë

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Tepelenë is a river town with a big name in Balkan history. It stands at the point where the Vjosa meets the Drino, in a gap between hills in southern Albania, and for anyone moving between the coast and the south it has always been a natural gate. This is where Ali Pasha, the ruler known as the Lion of Ioannina, was born in the middle of the eighteenth century, and his fortress here was one of the bases from which he built a semi-independent state that ran deep into Greece. In 1809 Lord Byron came through and described the pasha's court in Childe Harold, which is why English visitors sometimes arrive with a copy.

The fortress walls still stand on a rise above the river, though much of what you see is a later rebuild. In more recent times Tepelenë was a different kind of place: under communism it was a site of internment, and the town carries that memory quietly. Today it is a working town of around twelve thousand people, with a central square, a few small hotels and a strip of cafés where people take their evening walk. The surrounding land is dry, rocky and open, with olives, pomegranates and scrub oak.

For riders, Tepelenë is a junction. The main highway from Fier and Vlora meets the road toward Gjirokastër and the Greek border, and the smaller road to Memaliaj and onward to Berat opens a quieter route through farmland and river flats. Traffic can be heavy on the main road, with trucks, minibuses and the occasional donkey cart, and driving habits are lively, so ride defensively. Surfaces on the trunk roads are generally fine, with local repairs, while the smaller roads need more care. Summer is very hot, so aim for early starts, and keep water with you.

Tepelenë is large enough to have several fuel stations, shops, banks and restaurants, so it is a sensible place to top up before heading into the mountains toward Gjirokastër or the Osum area. Grilled meat and fresh bread are easy to find, and the river air is a welcome relief from the heat. Spend a few minutes on the walls of the fortress and look at the two rivers together; it explains the town better than a guidebook can.
