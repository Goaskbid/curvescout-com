Cholpon-Ata

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Cholpon-Ata is the unofficial capital of the Issyk-Kul north shore, a long string of beaches, guesthouses and Soviet-era holiday camps on the edge of a lake so big and deep that it feels like a sea. Issyk-Kul, which means warm lake, almost never freezes despite lying at about 1,600 metres, thanks to its depth and slight salinity. On the far side, the snowy ridges of the Terskey Ala-Too rise above the water, and on a clear day the contrast between blue lake, golden shore and white mountains is startling.

The town has about eighteen thousand people and a rhythm that changes dramatically with the seasons. In July and August, holidaymakers from Kazakhstan, Russia and Bishkek fill the beaches, and the main street is lined with cafes, souvenir stalls and fairground rides. In spring and autumn it is quiet and mild, and the lake is yours. Just outside town lies an open-air field of petroglyphs, boulders scattered across a grassy slope, carved with ibex, deer and hunters by people living here thousands of years ago, in what feels like a gallery laid out on a hillside.

The riding along the north shore is easy and pleasant. The road is mostly good tarmac with moderate traffic, passing through villages, orchards and small beaches, with the water on one side and the mountains on the other. It is not technical, but it is scenic, and you can set a relaxed pace and stop whenever you like. Watch out for cows, slow limits in the villages, and holiday traffic on summer weekends. The wind off the lake picks up in the afternoon, so mornings are calmer.

As a place to rest, Cholpon-Ata has everything: plenty of fuel stations, supermarkets, ATMs, hotels and guesthouses, and a whole street of restaurants serving fried lake fish, shashlik and fresh bread. Swimming is possible in summer, though the water is cool and slightly salty. It is a good place to take a day off, wash the dust from your gear and plan the next stretch, whether you are heading east toward Karakol or west back to the capital.
