Tahoe City

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Tahoe City is the unofficial capital of the lake's north shore, a small town that has the shops, bars and bike racks of a resort without ever feeling too big. It sits at the point where the Truckee River leaves Lake Tahoe, the lake's only outlet, flowing north and east to the Nevada desert. A small dam controls the outflow, and the nearby Fanny Bridge, named for the view people have when they lean over the rail to watch the trout, is a good place to see how a river is born.

The town's history is tied to logging, railroads and tourism. In the nineteenth century, the surrounding forests were cut to supply timber and fuel for the mines of Nevada's Comstock Lode, and the area later became a summer retreat for people escaping the heat of the cities. Just up the valley is Palisades Tahoe, formerly Squaw Valley, which hosted the 1960 Winter Olympics and put the region on the world map. A small local museum covers the story, and the wide bike trail along the river is a pleasant stroll if you want to get off the bike.

The riding along the north shore is excellent. Highway 28 and the road to the north follow the shore in an easy rhythm of long bends, with glimpses of blue water through the pines and the odd bigger view of the Sierra. Traffic can be heavy on summer weekends, and the speed limit is low in town, so it is more of a cruise than a charge. Early morning, before the beaches fill, is lovely, with soft light and cool air that smells of pine and warm dust.

Tahoe City has everything a rider needs: fuel, groceries, outdoor shops, cafes and restaurants, mostly along the lakefront and the main street. A coffee on a deck in the morning sun, or a burger and a beer at the end of the day, is easy to find. It is also a good place to refill before the long southern leg of the lake loop. Be ready for variable weather in the mountains, and remember that snow can linger into late spring and return early in autumn.
