Segment Portal del Inca - Río de La Sal (area -26.4,-69.6)

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The name Portal del Inca suggests a gate, and in a way it is one: a threshold on the road that the Incas drove south across the desert from the Andean heartland. Qhapaq Ñan is the name of the whole system, a network of tracks that stretched for thousands of kilometres and carried armies, tax goods and runners with messages. In the Atacama it was built simply, since the ground was hard and dry. Stones were cleared and lined up to mark the path, and the tambos, or roadside stations, stood at intervals with stores of food and water for officials.

Because this is a segment rather than a single monument, you will not find a ticket office or a signboard that explains everything. What you see is a dry high plain of pale volcanic soil and rock, with distant cones of dormant volcanoes and the odd salt flat shining like spilled paint. Silence is part of the place, broken only by wind, and in the early morning the air is crisp enough to see your breath even in the warm months. The riding is open and exposed. Tracks branch off the main routes, some firm and some sandy, and mining trucks run on the better ones.

The wind is the main hazard. It can come up fast in the afternoon and push a bike sideways across a gravel road, so travel in the early hours when the air is still. Altitude saps the engine and the rider, so take it easy and drink water often. There is no mobile signal in many places, and a breakdown can mean a long wait, which makes it wise to ride with a companion or to tell someone your plan. Night temperatures drop sharply, even in summer, and frost is common in winter.

Fill the tank completely in Diego de Almagro or in Copiapó before you leave the paved road, and carry enough water and food for a full day beyond what you think you need. Late spring and autumn give the best conditions. In these months the light is soft, the days are warm, and the old road feels less like an ordeal and more like a long, strange privilege.
