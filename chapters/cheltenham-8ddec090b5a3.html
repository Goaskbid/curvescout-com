Cheltenham

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Cheltenham is a spa town of the Regency age, all pale stucco, iron balconies and tree-lined avenues, and it wears its elegance lightly. A mineral spring was found here in the early eighteenth century, and when King George III visited in 1788 the town became fashionable overnight, with grand terraces and a promenade lined with trees built to match. The Pittville Pump Room, a Greek-style building of 1830, still offers a glass of the water for the brave.

The town's best-known event is the Cheltenham Festival, four days of horse racing in March at Prestbury Park, with the Gold Cup the highlight, drawing crowds from across the country and, especially, Ireland. The composer Gustav Holst, who wrote The Planets, was born here in 1874, and his birthplace is a small museum. Edward Wilson, who died with Captain Scott on the return from the South Pole, also came from Cheltenham. The government's communications headquarters, GCHQ, sits in a ring-shaped building on the edge of town, a secretive presence.

For riders, Cheltenham is the western gateway to the Cotswolds, the limestone hills that rise abruptly to the east in an escarpment. The roads climb from the town to Cleeve Hill, the highest point of the Cotswolds at about 330 meters, with views across the Severn Vale toward Wales. Beyond, narrow lanes wander through honey-colored villages such as Winchcombe, Stanway and Broadway, past stone walls, sheep pastures and manor houses. The surface is generally good, but the lanes are narrow and busy with tourists, cyclists and tractors, so patience is essential, and summer weekends are slow.

Cheltenham has every service, with fuel, supermarkets, restaurants and hotels, though town traffic can be heavy at rush hour. Charlton Kings and Shurdington, nearby, are smaller. The best months are May and September, with warm days and fewer crowds, and October brings golden foliage on the beech woods. Winter is damp and cold, with the odd frost on the hill roads. Watch for horse boxes and farm vehicles in the lanes.
