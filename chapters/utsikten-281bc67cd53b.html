Utsikten

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Utsikten means the view, and at Gaularfjellet that is exactly what you get: a cantilevered platform of steel and glass pushed out over the edge of a mountain, with the valley dropping away below. The viewing point stands on the road over the Gaularfjellet, the mountain that separates the Sognefjord from the Førde district, and it looks down over a vast, folded landscape of rock, forest and river, with the fjord somewhere in the haze beyond. Nearby, the water of the Gaula comes down in several falls, and the whole slope is cut by streams. Spray from the falls drifts across the road in spring.

The road over the mountain is a prize for a rider. On the Sogn side it climbs in a series of tight hairpins and long, graceful bends, on a good surface with views at every turn. Short tunnels, stone walls and white railings line the way, and the gradient is steady and well planned. The cars here are often caravans and tourist coaches, and the road is narrow in places, so take your time and use the pull-outs. Early mornings and weekdays are quiet. In wet weather the road is greasy, and on the exposed sections the wind is strong.

The Gaularfjellet road is one of the Norwegian scenic routes, and the viewing platform is one of the structures built to mark it. Architects were asked to design places where people could stop without spoiling the landscape, and the results are often daring. This one gives a view of the mountain as a living thing, shaped by ice and water over thousands of years. The cliffs here show the layered, twisted rock of an old mountain chain, worn smooth in places and sharp in others.

Balestrand and Høyanger, on the road to either side, are small, and Sogndal is the nearest town with a good choice of services, so start the day with a full tank. A kiosk or café may be open in summer, but check before you rely on it. The best light is in the late afternoon, when the sun slants down the valley and turns the rock gold. Take a warm layer, as the platform is exposed, and the wind can be cold even on a bright day.
