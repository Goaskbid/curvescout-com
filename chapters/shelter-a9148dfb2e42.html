Shelter

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Pull up near the shelter and you are in the southeastern part of Utrecht, a stone's throw from the university campus of De Uithof and a short ride from the city's canals. The label does not say which shelter, and in this district there are several, so treat it as an introduction to the countryside along the Kromme Rijn, the old Rhine course that once carried the river's main flow past Utrecht before it silted up and moved south.

That old river is the key to the place. Romans had a frontier along it, medieval bishops cut it into the landscape of estates and bridges, and a series of country houses was strung along its banks. When the Dutch decided in the nineteenth century to defend their heartland with water rather than walls, they pegged the Waterline to the same low ground, and the forts and bunkers that dot the eastern side of Utrecht grew out of that decision. A shelter in this area is likely to be part of that story, whether it was a bus stop, a picnic hut or a bunker.

Riding here is slow and civilised. The roads are narrow and often shared with riders on cycle tracks, and the speed limits are low, especially close to the campus. Treat it as a rolling museum rather than a motorway. If you want a few relaxed kilometres between city and country, the lanes toward Bunnik and Rijnsweerd are pleasant, bordered by hedges and old trees, and Amelisweerd, the wooded estate beside the river, is lovely when the beech leaves turn.

Fuel is easy in Utrecht, a city of nearly three hundred thousand, and you will find it on the ring roads. Food is better in town: cafés fill the canal wharves along the Oudegracht, and a coffee near the Dom, the 112-metre cathedral tower that still dominates the old city, is a good way to finish. Weekday mornings avoid the worst of the student traffic, and spring blossom in the fruit orchards south of the city is gentle and lovely.

The wind off the polders can be sharp, so a windproof layer helps even in summer.
