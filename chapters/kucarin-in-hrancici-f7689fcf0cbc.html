Kučarin in Hrančići

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Grey limestone slabs lie in the grass at Hrančići like a congregation that sat down six hundred years ago and never got up. These are stećci, the carved medieval tombstones that are found across Bosnia and Herzegovina and its neighbours, and the group at Kučarin is one of the sites included in the UNESCO listing of stećci graveyards in 2016. Some are plain chests, some are gabled like little houses, and a few carry reliefs of spirals, vines, shields or figures with raised hands that still nobody fully agrees how to read.

The place lies in the hills above the upper Drina, in the stretch of valley between Foča, Ustikolina and Goražde. The Drina here is that unreal green you see in photographs, cutting through steep wooded slopes, and the main road along it is a fine ride in its own right: a two-lane route that follows the river closely, with long fast bends, a few tighter sections where the valley pinches, and some uneven patches and gravelly verges. Watch for trucks, slow farm vehicles and drivers who overtake on faith. Side roads up to villages like Hrančići turn narrow and steep, with surfaces that can change from good tarmac to broken asphalt without warning.

History in this valley is heavy and close. Ustikolina has one of the oldest mosques in Bosnia, rebuilt after the war of the 1990s, and Goražde was a besieged enclave during that conflict; a quiet word with locals will tell you more than any guidebook. Foča, upstream, is the base for rafting on the Tara and the Drina, and in summer you will see roof racks full of boats.

For practical stops, Goražde is the biggest town nearby, with petrol stations, shops and plenty of cafés along the river where a strong Bosnian coffee arrives with a glass of water and often a cube of Turkish delight. Foča has the same. Smaller places have bakeries selling burek in the morning, which is all the fuel a rider really needs until lunch.

Spring and early autumn are the sweet spots, when the slopes are green or golden and the heat is manageable. Midsummer in the valley can be scorching, so ride early. Spend a few quiet minutes among the stones, trace a carved spiral with your eyes, and then roll back down to the river with the feeling of having met someone very old.
