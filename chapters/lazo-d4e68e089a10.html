Lazo

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Lazo is a small village of about three and a half thousand people, set between the Sikhote-Alin hills and the Sea of Japan in the south of Primorsky Krai. It is named for Sergei Lazo, a Civil War figure remembered across the Russian Far East, and the same name is carried by the district and by the nature reserve that covers a large part of the mountain land behind. If you want one word for the setting, it is green: dense mixed forest, rivers running clear and fast to the coast, and a sea that on some days looks almost tropical under a hazy sky.

The coastal road that brings you here is quiet and has a character of its own. The surface varies between good asphalt and patched sections, the bends are moderate, and the gradients are gentle, so it is a relaxed ride rather than a technical one. Fog and rain are the usual hazards, especially in summer when the monsoon pushes damp air in off the sea, and a cold, wet morning can turn into a hot, bright afternoon. Autumn is the best season, with dry weather, clear light and the forest turning every shade of red and gold. Spring comes late and can be cold.

Fuel is available in the village, though you should not expect much choice or late hours, and the nearest big town is far away, so fill up before heading in. Shops stock the basics, and a couple of cafes serve plain Russian dishes: soup, dumplings, fried fish, and the strong tea that appears automatically. In season, ask about local seafood, as this coast has plenty of it, and sellers by the roadside offer fresh and dried fish in summer.

The reserve itself is worth thinking about even if you only pass its edge. It protects one of the richest tracts of temperate forest in the world, a place where northern spruce and southern vines grow side by side, and where the Amur tiger, the Himalayan bear and the sika deer all live. The odds of seeing any of them from the road are slim, but footprints in the mud or a rustle in the undergrowth remind you that the forest is full. Keep your speed down at dusk, when deer step out without warning, and keep your eyes up.
