Uholka - Shyrikyi Luh

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The Uholka and Shyrokyi Luh massif, in the Carpathian Biosphere Reserve of western Ukraine, is the largest tract of virgin beech forest in Europe. It lies in the upper valley of the Tisza, in Zakarpattia, among steep slopes between roughly four hundred and twelve hundred metres, and it forms a key part of the UNESCO listing for the primeval beech forests of the Carpathians and other regions of Europe, inscribed in 2007. Here the trees have never been logged, so the forest has the structure that old Europe once had: giant trunks, fallen and rotting wood, moss, fungi, and gaps where a tree has died and light floods in.

Walking in it is like entering a cathedral. The beech trunks are grey, smooth and tall, and in spring the forest floor is covered with wildflowers before the leaves close the canopy. In autumn the whole slope turns copper and gold. Brown bears, lynx, wolves and wildcats live here, though you are unlikely to see them, and the woods are rich in birds, including woodpeckers and owls. The forest, with its fallen giants, is a living laboratory for scientists who want to understand what unmanaged woodland looks like.

The nearest settlements are small mountain towns such as Drahovo, Kolochava and Berezovo, which have a long tradition of farming and forestry. Kolochava has an open-air museum of old village life, with wooden houses and tools, and it is a good place to understand how people lived in these valleys. The roads here are narrow, patched and often unpaved in the villages, with livestock and horse carts as everyday traffic, so a robust bike and a relaxed pace are advisable.

Anyone planning a ride here should check the current situation carefully, since the war in Ukraine has changed travel conditions throughout the country, and rules can shift quickly. Beyond security, the practical points are simple: petrol is sold in Khust and the other bigger towns, but not in the hills, and weather in the Carpathians changes rapidly, with cold nights even in summer. Late spring and autumn are the best seasons for the forest itself. Take simple provisions, because shops are small, and the food in village houses is hearty, with bryndza cheese, mushrooms and cornmeal mamalyga.
