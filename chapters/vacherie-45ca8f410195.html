Vacherie

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Vacherie is a small community on the west bank of the Mississippi, and it is a village of cows and plantations, as the name suggests: vacherie is the old French word for a cattle farm. This reach of the river is where Louisiana's sugar-planting history survives in its most concentrated form, and within a few kilometres you can visit three large plantations that each tell a different part of the story.

The most photographed is Oak Alley, famous for its avenue of twenty-eight live oaks that run from the house to the river, planted long before the mansion was built. The oaks are enormous, their branches meeting overhead in a tunnel of shade, and the white-columned house at the end of the avenue is one of the best-known images in the American South. Nearby, Laura Plantation tells the story of a Creole family, with a focus on the women who ran it and the enslaved people who worked it, and its colourful buildings reflect French and Caribbean influences. Whitney Plantation is different: it is a museum devoted to the experience of slavery, with memorials, restored buildings and the names of those who were enslaved on the estate. It opened in the 2010s and has become a major site for historical understanding.

The region is also the heart of Louisiana's Creole and Cajun culture, and the local food is excellent. Gumbo, jambalaya, boudin and andouille sausage appear on local menus, and the sugar cane harvest in autumn fills the air with a sweet, smoky smell from the mills. The land is flat and green, the sky wide, the river hidden behind its levee, and the pace slow.

The ride on the River Road is easy, with long straight stretches, low speed limits through small communities and light traffic, though sugar trucks are heavy in harvest season. The surface is generally decent, but gravel and mud may collect at the verge. Summer is hot and humid, with thunderstorms, and the pleasant months run from late autumn to spring. Watch for the occasional ferry crossing and railway line.

Fuel is available in Lutcher, a few kilometres away, and in larger places such as Gramercy and Chackbay, so fill the tank and bring water. Small cafés near the plantations serve lunch, and a stop under the oaks with a cold drink is a fine way to pass the afternoon.
