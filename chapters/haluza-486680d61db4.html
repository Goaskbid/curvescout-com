Haluza

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Halutza is a ghost of a city in the sand. Founded by the Nabataeans as a waystation on the incense route from Arabia to the Mediterranean, it grew in the Roman and Byzantine periods into the most important city of the Negev, known in Greek as Elusa, with a theatre, churches and a famous school of rhetoric. Then it faded, the sand moved in, and today only fragments of walls, a small theatre and the remains of churches poke out of the dunes south-west of Beersheba. It is part of the UNESCO-listed Incense Route and desert cities of the Negev, along with Avdat, Mamshit and Shivta.

The Nabataeans were masters of desert survival, collecting every drop of rain in cisterns and channels, and their trading network carried frankincense and myrrh from southern Arabia to the ports of Gaza and beyond, making them rich. At Halutza, the remains are less spectacular than at Avdat or Shivta, but the setting, among the shifting sand dunes of the western Negev, is evocative and lonely.

The riding in the Negev is about light, space and heat. Roads are long and straight across the desert, with wide views and few curves, and the surface is generally good on main roads. Off-road tracks lead to the archaeological sites, but they can be sandy and soft, so check conditions before venturing off the tarmac. Wind can be strong, and dust storms are possible, especially in spring. Summer temperatures are high, so ride early or late in the day and carry plenty of water.

Beersheba, about twenty-two kilometres away, is the main city of the Negev, with fuel, food, lodging and every service you might need. Ofakim and Yeruham are smaller towns nearby. Beersheba has its own ancient site, Tel Be'er Sheva, also listed by UNESCO, and a lively Bedouin market tradition.

The desert here has a stark beauty, with dunes, low hills and the occasional acacia tree, and in winter, after rain, it can turn surprisingly green, with wild flowers carpeting the ground. The light at dawn and dusk is extraordinary, turning the sand gold and pink. Security conditions in the region can change, so check current advice before travelling, and stay on marked routes. Halutza will not overwhelm you the way Avdat does, but out here among the dunes, with the wind moving sand over Byzantine stones, it has a lonely dignity of its own.
