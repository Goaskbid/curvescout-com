Stuzhytsia - Uzhok

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Where the Bieszczady mountains of Poland meet Slovakia and Ukraine, there is a stretch of forest that has hardly been touched. The Stuzhytsia-Uzhok area, on the Ukrainian side of the watershed near the Uzhok pass, is one component of the Primeval Beech Forests of the Carpathians, a World Heritage group that spans several countries and continues into the Polish Bieszczady and the Slovak Poloniny. The trees here are mostly beech, with fir and sycamore, and in the oldest stands, some of the trunks are huge, with fallen logs lying where they dropped and rotting into the soil, a kind of forest that has almost vanished from Europe. The wildlife is what you might expect from such a place: brown bears, wolves, lynx, and the European bison, which was reintroduced to the Bieszczady in the 1960s.

The landscape is very different from the Polish plain. Long grassy ridges called poloniny rise above the tree line, with views over wave after wave of forested hills, and the villages in the valleys are few and small. Before the Second World War many of them were home to Lemko and Boyko people, who were resettled after the war, leaving old orthodox churches, overgrown orchards and cemeteries in the woods. Today the Bieszczady is known as a wild, quiet corner for hikers and riders.

The roads here are some of the best in Poland for riding. The Bieszczady loop, from Lesko to Ustrzyki Dolne and Wetlina and back, has twisting roads through beech woods, with climbs to viewpoints and views across the poloniny. The road toward Ustrzyki Górne climbs to open ridges with big views. In summer, tourist traffic and tractors can slow things down, and in autumn the leaves are slippery. Winters are long and snowy.

The border region carries constraints. The site itself lies in Ukraine, and since the war began in 2022 travel across the frontier here is not advisable, so treat this stop as a view of the forest from the Polish side. Check which crossings are open, and respect the national park rules. Fuel is available in Lesko and Ustrzyki Dolne, but Lutowiska, the nearest listed village, has little. Carry food and water, bring bear awareness, and aim for September and October when the colours are at their best.
