Tasikuluulik

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

In the green fjords of South Greenland, roughly where the ice sheet meets sheep country, lie the ruins of a church that has outlasted the Norse people who built it. Hvalsey, known in Greenlandic as Qaqortukulooq, is the best preserved of all the Norse ruins in Greenland, its gable and walls of local stone still standing against the sky beside a quiet fjord. In 1408 a wedding was held here, and a letter written about it is the last written record of the Norse in Greenland. After that, silence, and no one is sure exactly what happened to the settlers.

I should be honest about the ride. There are no roads linking Greenland's towns, and no road to this site. Qaqortoq, the largest town in the south with about three thousand people, lies some thirty-eight kilometres away, and Narsaq is similar, but the way between them and Hvalsey is by boat across the fjord, or on foot or by quad along farm tracks. Motorcycles do not feature in this landscape in any normal sense. What you get instead is a boat trip and a short walk, with the meltwater streams, the smell of crowberry and grass, and the sound of gulls and sheep.

The setting is the real attraction. The land looks gentler than the popular picture of Greenland, with green pastures, birch and willow scrub, and the occasional drifting iceberg in the fjord. Summer, from June to August, brings long light, milder temperatures, and mosquitoes that can be a menace on still days. Weather changes quickly, with fog and wind arriving without notice, so boats may be delayed or cancelled. The farming tradition here is an old one: the Norse kept cattle, goats and sheep, and today's Greenlandic sheep farmers carry on the practice, which is part of the Kujataa cultural landscape that UNESCO recognised in 2017.

Practical matters rest in Qaqortoq, which has fuel, shops, a hotel and a harbour from which local boat operators run trips in the summer. Book in advance, since schedules are short, and carry warm clothing and waterproofs, plus insect repellent. Treat the ruins with care. They are protected, and the stones are delicate, so please do not climb on the walls. A quiet hour sitting beside the old church is the best way to feel the weight of a lost story.
