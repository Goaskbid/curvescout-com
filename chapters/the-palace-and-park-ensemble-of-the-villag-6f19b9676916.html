The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag (area 59.7,29.9)

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Ropsha is one of those places where the scenery is gentle and the history is anything but. The village sits on the low Izhora Upland southwest of St Petersburg, between Krasnoe Selo and the coast road at Lomonosov, and the palace that gives the estate its name looks down over a chain of spring-fed ponds and a slow, wooded park. It was here, in July 1762, that the freshly deposed Emperor Peter III was held after his wife Catherine seized the throne, and here that he died a few days later under circumstances nobody at court cared to explain. Catherine became "the Great"; Ropsha became a footnote with a chill on it.

The house itself has had a hard couple of centuries. It passed through several noble owners, was used and abused in the Soviet years, and took heavy damage during the Second World War, when the front line ran close by. What you find today is a handsome but battered classical palace, partly fenced and long awaiting restoration, set in a park whose terraces, cascades and old trout ponds still hint at how grand it once was. The springs here are clear and cold, and the ponds were famous in imperial times for supplying fish to the court table.

For riding, the roads around Ropsha are the pleasant surprise of the Petersburg hinterland. The upland lifts the land just enough to break the flatness of the Gulf shore, and the lanes toward Kipen, Russko-Vysotskoye and Gostilitsy roll through birch and spruce woods, small fields and scattered dachas. Surfaces vary a lot: some stretches are newly laid, others are patched asphalt with frost heave and potholes that appear without warning after a wet spring. Keep your speed honest through the villages, where dogs, chickens and kids on bicycles all share the road with you. Summer evenings are the reward this far north; in June the light lingers almost to midnight and the woods smell of resin and wet moss.

Practicalities are simple. Ropsha has small shops, but for proper fuel and a choice of places to eat, the larger settlements along the main roads, such as Gorbunki toward Peterhof or the town of Krasnoe Selo, are the better bet. Bring water and a snack, park somewhere you can watch the bike, and give yourself half an hour to walk down past the ponds. It is a quiet, slightly melancholy spot, and that is exactly why it sticks in the memory.
