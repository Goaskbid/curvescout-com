Ancient Capua

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Santa Maria Capua Vetere does not look like much as you ride in, a busy Campanian town of tangled traffic and apartment blocks, but under and around it lies ancient Capua, once one of the richest cities in Italy. The Via Appia passed straight through, and its great amphitheatre, second in size only to the Colosseum, still dominates the edge of town. This was where the gladiator schools trained their fighters, and it was from a school in Capua that Spartacus broke out in 73 BC with a band of fellow slaves, beginning the revolt that shook Rome for two years. The amphitheatre and a small gladiator museum nearby are the obvious stops, and the Mithraeum under the town, with its painted scene of the god slaying a bull, is one of the best preserved in Italy.

The town is part of the stretch of the Via Appia that runs from the plain of Capua down toward the sea at Formia, a section included when the Appian Way was inscribed on the UNESCO list in 2024. The ancient road, built from 312 BC onward, linked Rome with the south and eventually the port of Brindisi, and Capua was one of its most important stops. The royal palace of Caserta, a few kilometres away, is the other great monument of the area, built by the Bourbon kings in the eighteenth century to rival Versailles.

As for riding, this is the Campanian plain, flat, fertile and intensely busy. Traffic is chaotic by northern European standards, with scooters, trucks and cars weaving in all directions, and the roads can be rough and badly patched. Keep your wits about you, and do not expect to make good time. The reward comes when you head for the hills: north toward the Monte Maggiore range or the volcanic slopes of Roccamonfina, where quieter roads wind through chestnut woods and olive groves with views back over the plain.

Santa Maria Capua Vetere has all the services you need: fuel, cafés, bakeries and pizzerias. This is mozzarella country, the buffalo mozzarella of Campania, so stop at a local dairy shop and try it fresh, preferably still warm. Park close to the amphitheatre where you can keep an eye on the bike, give yourself an hour for the ruins and the museum, and then decide whether Caserta deserves the afternoon.
