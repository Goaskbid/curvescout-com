Kakinoshima Site

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The Jomon people lived in Japan for more than ten thousand years, and the Kakinoshima site belongs to the story of how they did it. It lies in southern Hokkaido, on the Oshima peninsula, the knuckle of land that reaches toward Honshu across the Tsugaru Strait, with the nearest town of any size, Honcho, about twenty kilometres off. The country here is gentle, a mix of low hills, farmland and a coast that opens to the strait.

Jomon culture is named for the cord-marked pottery its makers decorated, and it lasted from about fifteen thousand years ago to roughly three hundred BC. These were hunter-gatherer-fishers who nevertheless built permanent villages, stored food, traded obsidian and jade and buried their dead in ritual ways. Several Hokkaido and northern Tohoku sites were inscribed as World Heritage in 2021 to show that settled life developed without farming. Kakinoshima is among the places associated with that story, and it is a quiet, open site with markers and displays rather than grand buildings.

Riding here is easy and rural. Roads on the peninsula are well surfaced, with long straight sections and mild bends, and traffic is light outside the ferry traffic from Honshu. In summer the air is cool and clear compared with Tokyo, and the sea on the horizon gives a sense of space. Fog can drift in from the strait, particularly in early summer, and winter brings deep snow that closes many side roads.

Fuel and food are limited near the site itself, so plan to refuel in larger towns such as Honcho or at the stations on the main road. Small restaurants in the area serve seafood, particularly scallops and squid, and local dairy products are good. The historical interest is quieter than a castle or temple, a reminder of how long people have lived by this sea. Take time to read the markers, and notice how the landscape feels almost unchanged.
