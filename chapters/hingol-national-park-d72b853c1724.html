Hingol National Park

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The Hingol River reaches the Arabian Sea through one of the strangest landscapes in Pakistan, and the national park around it is the largest in the country. It covers a huge expanse of dry mountains, dunes and badlands where wind and water have carved the sandstone into towers, buttresses and needles. Two of them have nicknames that every visitor picks up: one the Princess of Hope, a profile against the sky, and the other the Sphinx. The colours range through cream, rust and pale yellow, and the light in the early morning is superb.

The Makran Coastal Highway, opened in the early 2000s, is what makes it reachable. It runs west from Karachi along the Balochistan coast to Ormara, Pasni and Gwadar, a single ribbon of tarmac with sea on one side and desert on the other. Riding it is a matter of distance and heat: long, empty straights, a handful of gentle climbs, and only the odd small village for company. The Buzi Pass section, with its climbs and bends, is where the road does anything interesting, and the views are huge.

Hingol also has something rarer: a pilgrimage. The Hinglaj Mata temple, a cave shrine near the river, is one of the holiest Hindu sites in Pakistan, and every spring pilgrims travel from across the country and beyond to visit it. The place is sacred to Muslims and Hindus alike in local tradition, and the pilgrimage has been going on for centuries. Mud volcanoes in the area, small cones that bubble cold clay, are another local curiosity and also considered sacred.

For the traveller the logistics matter more than the poetry. Mobile coverage is patchy. This is remote country, and the security situation in Balochistan can change; check current advice, ride in daylight, and tell someone your plan. Temperatures in summer are extreme, so the winter months from November to February are the sensible season.

Wildlife is part of the scene, too, with ibex and urial in the hills, and the Hingol river itself holds crocodiles. A quiet visitor at dawn has the best chance of seeing something move.
