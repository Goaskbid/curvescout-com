Hana

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

There is a particular kind of satisfaction in arriving at Hana, which has little to do with the town itself. Hana is small, a post office and a scattering of houses, a bay, a few shops, and the sense that the world has been politely asked to stay outside. You come here because the ride was long and the road gave you no choice. A little over fifty miles, ridden at the honest pace the highway demands, takes a good part of a morning, and the pleasure is in having done it.

The surroundings are a quiet kind of dramatic: pastureland sloping up to the flanks of Haleakala, the sea on the other side, and old lava rock walls in between. Hana has long been a ranching and farming community, and before the road was built it was reached mostly by boat. Its isolation has shaped it. People here talk about the place with a kind of protective fondness, and a rider who behaves like a guest will be treated like one. Hana Bay has a black sand beach and a small park, good for a quiet half hour with a sandwich bought somewhere on the way.

Fuel is the question to settle before the ride, not after. The town does have a small station, but it keeps short hours and is not something to rely on. The reliable plan is to fill the tank in Paia or Kahului and keep a margin for the way back, which is the same distance on the same road. Food is simple: a general store, a few lunch stands, and a couple of cafes that can run out of things by mid-afternoon. Bring cash and patience.

From here, the road continues south around the volcano, past Kipahulu, where Charles Lindbergh is buried in a small churchyard by the sea. The surface changes to patched and sometimes rough, with long, exposed stretches that open into a very different, dry landscape on the far side. Plenty of riders turn back at Hana, and that is perfectly respectable. If you do, leave by early afternoon so that the descent through the rainforest happens in daylight and the tour vans are in front of you rather than behind.
