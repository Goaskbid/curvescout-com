Căpâlna - Hotroapă

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Căpâlna sits in the Someș country northwest of Dej, in a landscape of long, rounded hills that the Transylvanian plateau does so well. Orchards, hayfields and small woods climb the slopes, villages sit where streams meet, and the horizon always seems to hold another soft ridge.

What you can count on is the sense of rural Transylvania at ordinary speed. The nearest villages, Poiana Blenchii, Gâlgău and Vad, are small, quiet places where life is built around farms, a church and a shop, and you may see a man mowing a steep bank with a scythe even today. The Someș river drains the area and has carried salt, timber and grain for centuries, and nearby Dej, about sixteen kilometres away, grew up as a salt-trading town where the Someșul Mare and Someșul Mic rivers meet. Salt was the region's old wealth, and the whole district carries traces of its mining and carting.

On the bike, the roads here are the enjoyable kind that do not demand attention. They wind through the hills with a succession of gentle bends, short climbs and easy descents, and surfaces vary from smooth to patched. Gravel from field tracks and mud near gates can turn a quiet corner treacherous, so keep a little margin. Traffic is light and mostly local, and dogs and geese can appear from nowhere. The best season is May to June, when the grass is high and the air smells of elderflower, or September, when the light is long and warm and the plum harvest is on.

Fuel and a meal are in Dej, a town of about thirty-eight thousand with several stations, supermarkets and restaurants serving grilled meat, soup and local pastries. The villages have little beyond a shop and perhaps a bar, so do not count on fuel at Căpâlna itself. Take a few minutes to enjoy the hush, because a place like this is gone as soon as you start the engine.
