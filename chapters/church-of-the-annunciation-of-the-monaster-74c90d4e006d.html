Church of the Annunciation of the Monaster

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Moldovița is the monastery that makes people go quiet in the courtyard. It sits in a green pocket of the Bukovina hills, ringed by a stout defensive wall with corner towers, and inside it the Church of the Annunciation is painted from the ground to the eaves with saints, prophets and battle scenes in blues, reds and ochres that have survived some five centuries of Carpathian weather. It was founded in the time of Petru Rareș, in the 1530s, and it belongs to the famous group of painted churches of northern Moldavia that UNESCO lists together.

The paintings were never decoration alone. They were a Bible for a congregation that mostly could not read, and the outside walls tell the stories in sequence. Look on the southern wall for the Siege of Constantinople, a crowded scene with Moldavian and Ottoman anxieties folded into a Byzantine subject, and for the long Tree of Jesse, which tangles the ancestors of Christ in a vine. The monastery is lived in, with nuns working in the grounds and a small shop that sells their weaving and woodcarving, so cover your knees and shoulders, speak quietly, and avoid using a flash on anything inside.

Getting here is half the pleasure. The valley road climbs from the Moldova river past Vama and Câmpulung Moldovenesc, through villages of steep shingled roofs and haystacks propped on poles, and the corners are gentle enough that you can look at the hillsides while you ride. Traffic is mostly local cars and the occasional timber lorry, and the asphalt is generally fair, though patched in places after winter. A small mountain road crosses the saddle toward Sucevița, another of the painted churches, and it makes a good loop if the day allows. Early morning is best, before the tour coaches arrive, and the beech and spruce woods are at their finest in October when the slopes turn copper.

Moldovița village and Vatra Moldoviței both have guesthouses, cafés and small shops, and you can buy a bowl of ciorbă, sour soup, or a plate of mămăligă with sour cream and cheese without trouble. Leave yourself an hour. The place rewards anyone who stays longer than the usual ten minutes.
