Siyadi Majlis

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

A majlis is a sitting room, and in the Gulf it is much more than that. It is where the men of a household received guests, drank coffee, settled disputes and did business, and in a pearl merchant's house it was the room where deals were struck. The Siyadi Majlis was part of the family's holdings in the old town of Muharraq, and it is the sort of space where you realise that pearls were sold by trust and conversation as much as by weighing and counting.

Picture the scene. Guests sat on cushions along the walls, with a brass pot of coffee, a dallah, being poured into tiny handleless cups, always a small amount, and the host would keep refilling until the guest shook the cup to signal enough. Dates and sweets were passed round. Buyers from Bombay or the other Gulf ports might arrive with a purse and a trained eye, and the sale of a fine pearl could take hours, with tea and talk until a price was settled. A good merchant's reputation depended on his hospitality and his honesty.

The room itself shows how Gulf architecture manages without machines. Thick walls keep out the sun, high ceilings let heat rise, and openings are placed to catch the breeze. Carved plaster panels, called niches, frame the walls, and a few windows with wooden shutters are arranged so air can pass but direct sunlight cannot. The care taken over details such as the frames, the doors and the ceiling beams says something about the importance of the room.

Visiting is straightforward on foot, but the old quarter is awkward for bikes, with narrow lanes, parked cars and blind corners, so park on a wider road. Fuel is cheap and easy to find across Muharraq and Manama. Opening times for restored buildings vary, so check ahead rather than assuming. The cooler season, from November to March, is the best time, and late afternoon the best hour. If somebody offers you coffee, accept, take it with your right hand, and shake the cup when you have had enough.
