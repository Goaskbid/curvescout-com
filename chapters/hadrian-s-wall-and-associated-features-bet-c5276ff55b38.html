Hadrian's Wall and associated features bet (area 55.0,-2.3)

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

This is the stretch of Hadrian's Wall that everyone pictures: the stone curtain running along the crest of the Whin Sill, a hard dolerite ridge that ends in sheer north-facing crags, with lakes like Crag Lough and Greenlee Lough glinting below. Housesteads fort is close by, clinging to the hillside with its barracks, granaries and famous communal latrines, and the Wall dips and climbs over each crag in a way that seems almost reckless as engineering. The Sycamore Gap tree, which stood in a dip in the ridge for generations, was felled in an act of vandalism in 2023, and the loss is still felt locally.

The landscape here is open grazing land, big and windswept, with drystone walls, scattered farms and the dark line of Kielder Forest to the north. Skylarks sing over the rough grass in summer; in winter the place can be bleak and very beautiful. The Wall is a World Heritage Site, and between Chesters, Housesteads and Vindolanda, where the famous wooden writing tablets came out of the waterlogged ground, you could easily spend a whole day reading Roman life from the stones.

The B6318, the Military Road, runs just south of the Wall and is the main artery. It was built after the 1745 Jacobite rising so that government troops could move quickly between Newcastle and Carlisle, and in places its builders laid it right on top of the Wall's foundations. It is a fabulous road to ride, with long rolling straights over crests, dips that hide oncoming cars and a few sharp bends. Its reputation for accidents is well earned, so treat every crest as a blind corner. Farm vehicles and tourists pulling into car parks add to the fun.

Haydon Bridge and Haltwhistle are the nearest villages with fuel, and Hexham, a handsome abbey town, is the main centre about fourteen kilometres away. The Twice Brewed area on the Military Road has a pub and visitor centre at The Sill, which is useful for coffee, toilets and information. Weather moves fast up on the Sill: plan for sunshine and squalls in the same hour, and keep a layer handy even in summer.
