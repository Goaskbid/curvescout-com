Gaucín

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Gaucín clings to the side of a hill in the Serranía de Ronda, a white village of steep lanes that seems to be pouring down the slope toward the Genal valley. Above it stands the Castillo del Águila, the Eagle Castle, a Moorish fortress, perched on a rock. On a clear day the view from up there takes in the Rock of Gibraltar and, beyond the strait, the hills of Morocco. It is the sort of vista that makes you stop talking and just look for a while.

The village has a long and rough frontier history. It lay on the border between Muslim Granada and Christian Castile for generations, and the surrounding hills hide a number of watchtowers and old fortifications. Later, the area became notorious for smugglers carrying tobacco and other goods up from Gibraltar by mule trail, and bandits and contrabandists are part of the local legend. Today the place is calm, popular with foreign residents, and still very Andalusian, with bars full of old men and a Sunday market atmosphere that never quite goes away.

The riding in and out is a joy. The A-369 runs between Ronda and the coast, and the stretch through Gaucín is one of the great moto roads of southern Spain: a long run of medium and tight bends climbing and descending through cork oak woodland and open pasture, with the Genal valley dropping away beneath you. Cork oaks, with their stripped trunks, are everywhere, and in spring the hills are green and covered in wildflowers. Summers are hot and dry, so mornings and evenings are the best time, and a sudden cloud bank can drift up from the Strait in winter.

Practical matters are modest. Gaucín is a village of under two thousand people, with a handful of bars and restaurants and a couple of small shops. The nearest bigger town is Jimena de la Frontera, around fifteen kilometres away, and Ronda is the larger service town in the other direction, so plan your fuel and a proper meal around those. If you stop, a coffee on the terrace near the main square and a short wander up the lanes is time well spent. The local specialities are hearty mountain dishes, cured meats, and the strong local wines.
