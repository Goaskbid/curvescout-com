Salgesch

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Salgesch is the wine village at the end of the German-speaking world, or at least at its edge in the Rhône valley. Just downstream, in Sierre, people speak French. In Salgesch, they speak a Valais German dialect and make some of the best Pinot Noir in Switzerland, and the language border runs between them like an invisible fence that nobody has ever bothered to build. The vines grow on steep south-facing terraces above the village, with the Rhône flat and silvery below and the peaks of the Bernese Alps on the other side.

The valley here is one of the driest in the Alps. Rain clouds struggle to get past the mountains on either side, and the sun beats down on the slopes, which is why the vineyards flourish and why the surrounding hills have a steppe-like look, with dry grassland and rocky outcrops. The Pfynwald, a rare natural forest of Scots pine on a glacial rockslide, lies a short distance upstream and is a quiet detour if you want to see a piece of wild Valais.

For riding, the vineyard roads above Salgesch are a gentle pleasure: narrow, winding lanes between stone walls, with a handful of tight bends and long views. They are not fast, but they are a real alternative to the valley road. You will meet cyclists and walkers on the vineyard tracks, and the surface can be gritty in places. Harvest time in autumn brings tractors and a good deal of activity.

The village has a wine museum and several wine cellars where you can taste, and the locals are proud of their local grape varieties, including Cornalin, Humagne Rouge and Pinot Noir. If you plan to taste, a pause for a meal and a coffee is wise, and the village has a few places for it. A couple of cellars will wrap a bottle in a way that survives a tank bag, though riders should think about weight.

Fuel is easy: Sierre, just a few kilometres away, has pumps and supermarkets, and Salgesch itself is on the main road. Choose September for the colour, when the vineyards turn red and gold and the light is soft.
