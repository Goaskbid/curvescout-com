Tran Hai Fortress

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Trấn Hải Thành sits at the sea mouth of the Perfume River's lagoon system, a low old fort looking out over the beach at Thuận An, about twelve kilometres from the centre of Huế. It was built in the early nineteenth century under Emperor Gia Long, the founder of the Nguyễn dynasty, and rebuilt and strengthened under his son Minh Mạng, to guard the one waterway by which a hostile fleet could reach the imperial capital. In the middle of the century French ships showed what that fear was worth, and the Nguyễn defences fell in 1883 when the French attacked the estuary forts.

What you find today is quieter than the history. A square-ish brick and stone enclosure, partly restored, rises from sandy ground with casuarina trees nearby, and from the walls you can see the lagoon on one side and the open South China Sea on the other. Fishing boats pull up on the beach, and in the afternoon locals come out to sit under the shade and eat. It is not a polished attraction, and that is the charm.

The ride out from Huế is a pleasure by Vietnamese standards. Follow the river and then the long causeway-like road across the paddy and lagoon landscape, past tomb-like family graves, fish farms and rows of stilted houses. The surface is mostly good, but you share it with scooters, buses and the occasional water buffalo, so keep your speed honest and your eyes up. The Tam Giang lagoon, the largest in Southeast Asia, lies to the north and gives the whole area a wide, hazy light, especially at dawn.

Timing matters. Typhoons and heavy rain hit the coast most seriously between roughly September and November, and the beach road can be washed with sand after a storm. Between February and May, mornings are mild and the sky tends to be clear. Huế has plenty of fuel and food, including bún bò Huế, the spicy beef noodle soup that the city insists is the real thing. After the fort, return along the same road toward the citadel and the tombs of the Nguyễn emperors that fill the hills south of town.
