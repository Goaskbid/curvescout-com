Near Towcester, on the way back

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Silverstone is six kilometres away, and on Grand Prix weekend you will know it: the roads around Towcester clog with traffic, the sky fills with helicopters and the whole district hums with engines. On other days, this is quiet south Northamptonshire, a landscape of rolling fields, stone villages and woods, with the old Roman road of Watling Street running straight through Towcester.

The town was the Roman settlement of Lactodorum, a staging post on Watling Street, and later a coaching town, with old inns along its main street. Charles Dickens stayed at one of them and wrote it into The Pickwick Papers. Easton Neston, a grand house designed by Nicholas Hawksmoor, stands in parkland on the edge of the town.

East of Towcester, the Grand Union Canal passes through Stoke Bruerne, a canal village with a museum, a flight of locks and the southern portal of the Blisworth tunnel, one of the longest navigable canal tunnels in Britain. It is a lovely spot for a drink by the water, watching narrowboats work through the locks.

For a swim, there is little here. The canal is not for bathing, and the rivers are small. The honest answer is a pool in Towcester or Northampton.

The riding around Towcester is good, with lanes winding over the hills and through the villages, and the A43 and A5 offering faster routes. Traffic is heavy on race days and on summer weekends. Towcester covers fuel. On a quiet evening, though, make the short hop to Stoke Bruerne instead and sit by the top lock, where the only engines are narrowboat diesels chugging at walking pace.
