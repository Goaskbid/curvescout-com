Kasuga Village and Sacred Places in Hirado (area 33.4,129.5)

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Hirado is an island at the far northwest of Kyushu, joined to the mainland by a bridge, and it has a stranger history than most. In 1609 the Dutch East India Company opened a trading post here, and for a while Hirado was the only place in Japan where Europeans traded openly, before the shogunate shifted them to the artificial island of Dejima in Nagasaki in 1641. The town still has a harbour and a castle on the hill, and the sea is never far from any road.

Kasuga village, a few kilometres from the town, is something else entirely. It is part of the group of sites recognised by UNESCO in 2018 as relics of Japan's hidden Christians, the communities who kept their faith alive in secret through more than two centuries of persecution after Christianity was banned in the early seventeenth century. In places like this, believers disguised their devotions as Buddhist or Shinto customs and venerated mountains, including the sacred mountain of Yasumandake, whose peak rises behind the village. Rice terraces climb the slopes in small steps, and the old fields and stone walls feel unchanged.

The riding is slow and attentive. The coastal road around Hirado is narrow in places, with farms, small harbours and frequent bends, and in the village itself the lanes are tight, so go quietly. This is a working farming community, and the polite approach is a quiet engine and a wave to anyone you meet. Light traffic, good views over the water to the islets, and a feeling of rural calm make for a rewarding day, though one that rewards patience more than speed.

For fuel and meals, Hirado town, about eight kilometres away, has what you need, and it is a small but proper town with seafood restaurants and the sort of coffee shops where you can sit for an hour. Sasebo, a larger port and naval city, lies some thirty kilometres further south and has everything a rider could want, including the famous Sasebo burger.

Spring brings fresh green on the terraces and autumn gives clear, soft light; the summer is hot and humid, and typhoon season can disrupt things. If you can, visit on a weekday morning when the village is quiet.
