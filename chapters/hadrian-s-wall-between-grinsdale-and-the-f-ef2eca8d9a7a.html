Hadrian's Wall between Grinsdale and the f

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

West of Carlisle the Eden slows and starts to meander, swinging in wide loops through flat meadows on its way to the Solway, and here at Grinsdale Hadrian's Wall ran along the high ground above the river. It is a gentle, almost secret stretch of countryside, only a few miles from the city but feeling much further. The tiny hamlet of Grinsdale has a church dedicated to St Kentigern, an early saint associated with this region, and a scatter of farms. The Wall has gone, robbed for building stone centuries ago, but the line it followed is clear in the field boundaries and the lie of the land.

The Hadrian's Wall Path, a long-distance walking trail that follows the frontier from coast to coast, passes through here, and you will often see walkers with backpacks heading west towards the end of their journey. They are usually tired and happy, and some will tell you they have walked the whole thing. The path follows the riverbank for a while, with views of the water and the meadows, and there is a pleasant stretch through woodland.

The river itself is beautiful, with herons, kingfishers and the odd otter, if you are lucky. In spring and summer the meadows are full of wildflowers, and in winter the floodplain can turn into a lake when the river overflows. The Eden is notorious for flooding; Carlisle suffered badly in 2005 and again in 2015, and the flood defences along the river are a reminder of how quickly things can change.

Riding here is about narrow lanes and quiet countryside. The roads around Grinsdale, Kirkandrews-on-Eden and Beaumont are single-track in places, with passing places, hedges and blind bends. They are pleasant for an easy pace but not for speed, and you will meet tractors, cyclists and the odd walker. The B5307 west towards Kirkbride is a little wider and faster, with long views across the Solway plain.

Carlisle is the place for fuel and supplies; Grinsdale and its neighbours have little in the way of services. There are pubs in some of the nearby villages, but opening hours vary. Weather in this part of Cumbria is changeable, with rain often sweeping in from the west. Find a spot by the river, switch off the engine, and listen to the water; it is a lovely place to pause.
