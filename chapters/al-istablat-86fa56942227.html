Al Istablat

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Al-Istablat is one of the outlying sites of the Abbasid capital at Samarra, and its name, which echoes the Arabic for stables, hints at the scale of a court that kept thousands of horses and cavalrymen.

The ground here is flat and fertile near the Tigris, with fields of wheat, barley and vegetables fed by irrigation canals, some of them descended from Abbasid works, and the river itself is wide and brown, with reeds and willows along the bank. A little way back from the water the land becomes dry steppe, with scrubby bushes and patches of salt. In winter, flocks of ducks and waders feed on the wetlands, and storks pass through on migration. Further out, the desert begins in earnest, and distances look much shorter than they are.

For a rider, this belongs to the long, flat road between Baghdad and Samarra, about 125 kilometres in all, along Highway 1, through Balad and past farms, roadside stalls and army checkpoints. The surface is generally good but heavily used by lorries, and local drivers overtake in the face of oncoming traffic, so keep your distance and stay alert. Midsummer heat is intense, and the road shimmers, so start early and carry plenty of water. Dust storms appear in spring and early summer, and rain in winter can turn dirt tracks to mud.

Samarra is the nearest large town, with services including fuel, food and mechanics, and Balad, about twenty kilometres away, also has facilities. Beyond them the choices thin, so fill up early. The tea in a roadside stall is strong, served in small glasses with plenty of sugar, and bread and kebabs are the usual meal. Security conditions in Iraq can change quickly, so check official travel advice and talk to local people about the safest routes before you set out.
