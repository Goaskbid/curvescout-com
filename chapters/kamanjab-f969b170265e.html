Kamanjab

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Kamanjab is a farm-service town on the edge of Damaraland, a place of dust, cattle trucks and big skies that serves as the practical heart of north-western Namibia. It sits at a junction where the tarred road from Outjo and Etosha meets the gravel routes heading west into the empty country of Kunene, and for travellers it is the last place to buy proper supplies for a long way. The surroundings are a mix of commercial cattle farms and scrub-covered hills, dry for most of the year and briefly green after rain.

Practicalities come first. The town has fuel, a supermarket, a few small guesthouses and a couple of simple places to eat, and it is also the sensible spot to check tyres, fill water bottles and buy snacks before going west. The roads out of town are tar in places and gravel in others, with the C40 heading toward the Grootberg Pass, where the gravel drops steeply off a plateau in a series of tight bends with wide views of the valley below. The surface is usually good but can be corrugated, and the road is often shared with cattle, donkeys and goats. The best time to ride is the dry season, from May to October, when the days are warm and the nights cold.

The town's history is typical of this country. It began as a farming settlement, and it has long been the home of Herero communities, as well as German-descended and Afrikaner farmers whose families came a century ago. Further north, Himba people live in scattered settlements, and you may see their distinctive appearance in the market or by the road. The region's earlier story involves San hunter-gatherers and later waves of herders, and rock engravings can be found on some farms, though visiting them requires local guidance and permission.

Western Etosha is a natural next step. The park's western areas, with fewer visitors and drier landscapes, are known for desert-adapted wildlife and big waterholes, and the approach from here is quiet and scenic. Entry fees apply, and park rules on speed and conduct are strict, with gates closing at sunset. The land around Kamanjab also holds small private reserves offering game drives and, in some cases, cheetah and carnivore centres.

The scenery has a stark beauty: flat-topped hills, termite mounds like gravestones, and acacia trees with umbrella crowns. The air smells of warm dust and dry grass, and sunsets come in a blaze of orange.

Fill the tank here, carry plenty of water and plan to arrive before dark, since animals are hard to see at night.
