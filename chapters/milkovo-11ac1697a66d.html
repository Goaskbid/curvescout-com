Milkovo

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Milkovo is the quiet crossroads of central Kamchatka, a village of around eight thousand people on the Kamchatka River, where the long road from the capital, Petropavlovsk, forks toward the hot-spring country of Esso and toward the great volcano group around Klyuchi. There is no grand sight in the village itself. It exists because someone has to sell fuel, bread and tinned fish to everyone passing, and because the broad river valley is flat enough to farm.

The riding here is of the open, high-latitude kind. The central road runs north through long straights, birch and larch woods, boggy clearings and low rolling hills, with snow-topped volcanoes appearing now and then on the horizon like rumours. The tarmac can be good for stretches and then break up into frost-heaved patches, and distances between settlements are large, so top up whenever you pass a pump. The season is short. Summer, roughly late June to early September, brings long daylight, mosquitoes, and bear-watching opportunities, and by October the first snow is already on the ground. Bad weather can arrive quickly from the Pacific or the Sea of Okhotsk.

Kamchatka is a Ring of Fire peninsula, and the volcanic nature of the land shapes everything. The Klyuchevskaya group to the north includes Klyuchevskaya Sopka, at around 4,750 metres the tallest active volcano in Eurasia, often wearing a plume of steam. Hot springs bubble from the ground in many valleys, and the soil is dark with volcanic ash. The Kamchatka River itself is one of the great salmon rivers of the Pacific, and in summer and autumn the fish run upstream in huge numbers, followed by brown bears, eagles and fishermen, in about that order of seriousness.

The region's culture is a mix of Russian settlers, descended from Cossacks and later arrivals, and indigenous peoples, notably the Itelmen and Koryak, whose traditions of fishing and reindeer herding survive in some villages. In Milkovo, you will find a few shops, small cafes serving fish soup and dumplings, and basic accommodation.

Fill the tank here, buy food and water, and keep spare supplies for the road. Treat bears seriously, keep food sealed and do not wander off into the brush.
