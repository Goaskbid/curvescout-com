Wild Atlantic Way point 8

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Malin Head is the northernmost point of the island of Ireland, and it feels like it: a bare, wind-scoured headland of rock, heather and grass, with the Atlantic stretching away to the horizon in three directions. A signal tower from the Napoleonic era stands on the top, and nearby, laid out in white stones on the ground, is the word EIRE and the number 80, a wartime marker placed there to tell pilots that they were over neutral Ireland. Carndonagh and Buncrana are the nearest towns, though neither is close.

The ride to Malin Head is part of the Inishowen 100, a loop of the Inishowen Peninsula, and the final stretch is a quiet road through farmland with a few bends and a view that opens suddenly at the end. The surface is generally good but narrow, and in places the road is edged by stone walls and fuchsia. The weather is nearly always windy, and in winter the Atlantic storms send spray over the cliffs, so a clear, calm day is a gift. Banba's Crown, the tower at the tip, is the usual objective.

The headland has been a lookout for centuries, and the weather station here has been broadcasting shipping forecasts for ships in the Atlantic, with Malin among the sea areas named in the BBC's shipping forecast. The scenery has also appeared on film, as the setting for scenes in a recent Star Wars movie, which drew new visitors. Nearby, a sea-cut chasm known as Hell's Hole boils with surf in rough weather. The cliffs are home to seabirds, and on a clear winter night the northern lights are sometimes visible.

Fuel is available in Carndonagh, about sixteen kilometres away, and Malin village has a few shops and pubs. There are no services at the head itself, so bring what you need. The roads are open all year, but the exposed headland is best visited in calm weather, and the long summer evenings from May to August give the best light. Pack a windproof layer, since the wind here is no joke even in July.
