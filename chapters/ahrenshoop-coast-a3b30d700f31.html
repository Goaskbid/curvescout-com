Ahrenshoop coast

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Ahrenshoop sits on the narrow neck of land where the Fischland meets the Darß, a thin strip of dune, forest and reed between the Baltic Sea and the Bodden lagoons. It was a fishing village until the 1890s, when painters arrived, drawn by the light and the quiet, and settled to form an artists' colony that has lasted more than a century. The reed-thatched cottages, the small galleries and the Kunstkaten, little art studios in old houses, are the legacy, and the village still has an easy, creative feel. On a grey day the sea and sky merge into silver, and on a clear one the horizon is a hard, bright line.

The coast here is dynamic. The sea eats at the cliff, called the Hohes Ufer, a bluff of sand and clay several metres high, and every winter storm takes a little more. Behind the beach, the land narrows so much that you can see both the Baltic and the lagoon from one spot. On the lagoon side, the Bodden are shallow and sheltered, with reed beds that hold cranes, herons and sea eagles, and in autumn thousands of cranes gather in the area to rest on their way south, one of the great bird spectacles of northern Germany.

On the road, Ahrenshoop is the point where the long ride through the Fischland and Darß finds its rhythm. The road is a single main route, flat and gently curving through pine woods and fields, and there are few alternatives, so traffic can build up on summer weekends. A steady pace and patience are the keys. Surface quality is good, and bends are mild, so this is a ride for scenery rather than speed. Wind off the sea can be strong, and crosswinds on the exposed stretches need attention. Early morning and evening are the best times.

Parking near the beach is limited and charged, so look for the official lots and leave valuables with you. Fuel is not abundant in the village itself, and the nearest town of real size is Ribnitz-Damgarten, about fifteen kilometres away, which has stations, supermarkets and a good amber museum. Born and Wustrow, smaller villages nearby, have cafés and fish stalls.

Try smoked fish from a stand, particularly herring or eel, with fresh bread, and eat it on the dune or the harbour wall. The air tastes of salt and pine, and the gulls watch you closely.
