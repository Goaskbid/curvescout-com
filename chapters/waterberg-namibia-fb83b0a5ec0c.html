Waterberg Namibia

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The Waterberg is a wall of red sandstone that rises about two hundred metres out of the flat bushveld of central Namibia, running for roughly fifty kilometres. Its flat top, fed by springs that seep from the rock, supports woodland and wildlife unlike anything on the plains below, and the escarpment, in rust and gold, glows at sunrise and sunset. The plateau is a national park, home to rare species such as sable and roan antelope, white and black rhinos, and a colony of Cape vultures that nests on the cliffs.

It is also a place of heavy history. In August 1904, at the foot of the plateau, German colonial troops defeated a large Herero force in the Battle of Waterberg, and the survivors were driven into the Omaheke desert, where thousands died of thirst. A genocide followed, and the memory remains a painful part of Namibia's story. A small cemetery near the rest camp holds the graves of German soldiers, and Herero people still come to the area to remember. It is a place to be quiet and respectful, and local guides tell the story with dignity.

The ride in takes you across a landscape of thorn scrub and cattle farms, where the straight road to the escarpment is lined by fences and acacia trees, and the plateau appears on the horizon like a ship. The final approach is on gravel or tar, depending on the route, and livestock and wildlife wander across, so keep your speed moderate. Heat in summer is intense, and the rains bring afternoon thunderstorms, so mornings are better. Winter nights are cold but the days are bright, and the dry season is the best time for wildlife.

Okakarara, about twenty-two kilometres away, is the nearest town of any size, with a population of about five thousand and basic fuel and supplies, while Otjiwarongo, farther on, has a wider range of services. Fill the tank before you reach the plateau and carry water. The rest camp offers accommodation, a restaurant and a pool, and a short walk leads to a viewpoint above the plains. Take binoculars, wear a hat, and be sure to book ahead in the holiday season.
