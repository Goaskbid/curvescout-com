Chuncheon

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Chuncheon is a lake city. The Bukhan and Soyang rivers meet here and have been dammed several times over, so the city of some two hundred thousand people sits in a basin ringed by green mountains and wrapped in long, still reservoirs: Uiam Lake right on its doorstep, Soyang Lake in the hills to the north-east, Chuncheon Lake upstream. Mist hangs on the water most autumn mornings, and the light here has a soft, washed quality you notice as soon as you come over the last ridge from Seoul.

You come to Chuncheon to eat, and specifically to eat two things. Dakgalbi is the city's own dish: chicken marinated in a fiery red pepper paste and stir-fried at your table on a huge round iron pan with cabbage, sweet potato, rice cakes and perilla leaves, and when it is nearly gone, the staff fry rice in what is left. There is a whole street of dakgalbi restaurants near Myeongdong in the city centre. The second is makguksu, buckwheat noodles served cold in a tangy broth with a little sugar, mustard and vinegar to adjust as you like, perfect after a hot ride.

Soyang Dam, finished in 1973, is the largest earth-fill dam in Korea, and it holds back a huge lake that winds through the mountains towards Inje, with ferries to remote temples like Cheongpyeongsa. Nami Island, in the river just south-west of the city, became famous across Asia after the television drama Winter Sonata was filmed among its rows of trees, and it is crowded with visitors, especially in autumn, so it is a stop for the curious rather than for peace.

The riding to and from Chuncheon is some of the best close to Seoul. Route 46 follows the Bukhan valley with long curves, river views and plenty of company on weekends, and the smaller roads north and east climb into the mountains of Gangwon, towards Hwacheon, Yanggu and Inje, with real passes, tight bends and thick forest. Remember that expressways are closed to motorcycles in Korea, so the national roads are your routes, and they can be busy around the lakes on Sundays. The best seasons are spring, for blossom, and October, when the hills go red and gold.

Fuel, convenience stores and motels are easy to find all over the city, and the lakeside paths are good for an evening walk. Order dakgalbi for two even if you are alone; nobody will mind, and you will need it for tomorrow.
