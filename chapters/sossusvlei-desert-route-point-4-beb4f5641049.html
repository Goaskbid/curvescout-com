Sossusvlei Desert Route point 4

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Of all the places in this part of the Namib, Deadvlei is the one that turns up on postcards, and the reason is simple: it looks wrong in the best way. A white clay pan, baked hard, sits between walls of orange dune, and from its surface stand black, skeletal camelthorn trees, long dead, still upright.

The explanation is a story of water. The Tsauchab once flooded this hollow often enough for camelthorn trees to take root along it. Then the climate dried and dunes migrated across the river's route, cutting off the flow. The trees died where they stood, and the desert air was so dry that the wood never rotted. Estimates of how long ago that happened, and how old the trees are, vary, but figures of several centuries up to nearly a thousand years are the ones usually quoted, and what you see is blackened by sun rather than burnt.

Getting here is a day's work. The tar road from Sesriem runs a long way into the dunes and stops, the final few kilometres are deep sand, and the usual approach is on foot or by the shuttle. A rider on a standard bike leaves the machine at the end of the tarmac, so the real exercise in this place is the walk. It is not far, but sand pulls at each step, and the heat makes distance deceptive. Wear light, breathable clothing, and take more water than you expect to drink.

Photographers know the trick: arrive at the first light, when the shadow line cuts a sharp diagonal across the pan and one side of the big dune is glowing while the other is still dark. After that the sun comes up quickly and the whole bowl turns into a reflector. By the middle of the day the crowds and the heat arrive together.

Walk quietly if you can. Besides the odd tourist, you may notice small tracks of beetles and lizards crossing the clay, and the occasional oryx on the dune slopes. Few places make desert life feel so improbable, and so stubbornly alive.
