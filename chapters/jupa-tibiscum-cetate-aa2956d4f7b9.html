Jupa - Tibiscum / Cetate

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Jupa is a village in the Banat, just beside the site of Tibiscum, the Roman town that grew where the Timiș and the Bistra meet at the foot of the Southern Carpathians. Today it has an archaeological museum and a field of excavations, with the remains of the fort, the baths and the civilian houses that once stood here. The Romans took over the Dacian lands in the early second century, and Tibiscum, sitting on a main road to the Danube, became a busy place of soldiers, merchants, craftsmen and priests. Some of the finds from the town show how mixed it was, with Greek, Roman and local gods all being worshipped.

The land is gentle, with meadows, willow-lined streams and low hills that lead toward the mountains of Țarcu, which stand to the south on clear days. It is a place for a slow walk among the stones rather than a long visit, and the field is open and unfenced in many parts. Roman bricks and fragments still turn up in the ploughed fields around, a sign that the town stretched much farther than the area now excavated. The grass is full of butterflies in summer, and storks nest on the village poles.

On a motorbike, Jupa is a calm stop on a longer day. The roads in the valley are good, with the main route to Caransebeș busy with trucks and cars, but side roads through Păltiniș and Obreja are quiet, with gentle bends and the odd tractor. A ride along the valley toward the mountains in spring, when the orchards are in blossom and the snow still shines on the high ridges, is one of the most pleasant in the region. Autumn is just as lovely, though cooler in the mornings.

The closest large town is Caransebeș, a few kilometres away, with fuel, restaurants and shops; Păltiniș and Obreja are smaller, so do not count on a station in either. The ride heads toward Sarmizegetusa, the first capital of Roman Dacia, founded by Trajan after the conquest and named Colonia Ulpia Traiana Augusta Dacica Sarmizegetusa. It stood in the Hațeg basin, far from the Dacian mountain fortress of the same name. If you are curious about the difference between the two, ask at the museum, where the staff love to talk about it.
