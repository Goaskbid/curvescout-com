Near Rømskog, on the way back

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Out where Norway runs into Sweden along a line of forest and water, Rømskog sits on the shore of Rømsjøen, a long lake surrounded by spruce and pine. Until a recent merger it was one of the smallest municipalities in the country, and it still feels like the end of the road: a few farms, a church, a school and endless trees.

This stop lies a few kilometres from the village, in country where lakes outnumber people. Swimming is easy to find: any lake shore with a rock or a sandy patch will do, and Norway's right to roam, allemannsretten, allows you to swim and walk almost anywhere, provided you respect private gardens and farmland. The water is clear, brown-tinged from the peat, and in July it can be surprisingly warm on the surface, though it chills fast below.

The forest is home to moose, deer, foxes and the occasional lynx, and in the early morning and evening, moose crossing the road are a real danger. Take it slowly at dawn and dusk, especially on the narrow gravel roads that branch off the main routes.

The riding is quiet and gentle, with narrow roads winding through forest and along lakes. The surface can be rough, with frost heaves, gravel and potholes, and some side roads are unpaved. Traffic is minimal, and you may ride for long stretches without seeing another vehicle.

Practicalities matter here. Fuel is scarce, and the nearest reliable stations are in Bjørkelangen or Mysen, so plan accordingly. Food is limited to small shops in the villages, so carry snacks and water. In summer the light lasts late into the evening, and a swim in a still lake with the forest around you is one of the simplest pleasures Scandinavia has to offer.
