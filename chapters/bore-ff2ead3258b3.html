Bore

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Bore sits on the coast of Jæren, the flat, wind-brushed farmland south of Stavanger, and the landscape here is unlike the rest of Norway. There are no fjords or high mountains: the land is low and open, with fields of grass and potatoes, stone walls, and a long beach of pale sand backed by dunes. The farmers of Jæren are some of the most productive in the country, and the region is known for its dairy farms and vegetable fields. Bryne, about nine kilometres away, is a market town of around eight thousand, and Sandnes is the larger city, so fuel and supplies are not a problem.

The beaches are the draw. Bore, Hellestø and Orrestranda are long stretches of sand, popular with surfers, who ride the Atlantic swell in thick wetsuits all year round. The dunes are protected, and the wind is nearly constant, making the coast a place for kites and windsurfers. The surf scene is surprisingly lively, and cafes by the beaches serve coffee and waffles. On a warm day in summer, the sea may be inviting, though it is never warm.

The riding on the Jæren route is relaxed, with easy curves, flat terrain and wide views of the sea. The road follows the coast, passing through farmland and small villages, with an occasional glimpse of the North Sea. Wind is a major factor, and it can be strong enough to push the bike across the lane, so keep a firm grip. The surface is generally good, and traffic is light, apart from weekends in summer. In winter, storms can be fierce, and the road may be icy.

The area is rich in history. Standing stones and burial mounds from the Bronze Age and Viking Age dot the fields, and the Hå Gamle Prestegard, an old vicarage, has been turned into a cultural centre. Hå also has one of the best preserved lighthouses on the coast. Locals celebrate the cheese and the local waffles, and strawberries from the farms in June are a delight. The light on the flat land is clear and bright, and in the evening, the sunset over the North Sea lingers.
