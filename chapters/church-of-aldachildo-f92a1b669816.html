Church of Aldachildo

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Aldachildo is another of the small wooden churches on Lemuy, and it asks you to think about how the Chilote churches were actually used. They were not built for tourists, or even mainly for Sundays. When Jesuit missionaries began to organise the islands in the seventeenth century, they did not have enough priests to be everywhere. So they trained local laymen to look after chapels, lead prayers and baptise, and the missionaries themselves sailed round the circuit once a year, on a kind of pilgrimage that the communities still remember with an annual festival. After the Jesuits were expelled in the eighteenth century, those lay guardians kept the system alive.

The result is a landscape of chapels placed where people lived: on a headland, by a beach, at the edge of a hamlet. Aldachildo's church fits this pattern, in a settlement of farms and fishing, on an island of rolling pasture and wind-bent trees. The nearest large town is Chonchi, about 14 km away across the water, and Castro, the provincial capital, is a bit farther off. Lemuy has little in the way of shops and services, so bring cash, snacks and fuel.

The ride to the church is the kind that suits patience. Gravel sections wind between fences and stone walls, and the road rises and falls in short bursts, with views down toward calm bays where oyster and mussel farms float in rows. In rain the surface turns to mud, and the wind can come in hard from the Pacific side. If the sky is clear, you can see volcanoes on the mainland across the Gulf of Corcovado, snowy cones far to the east.

Take the time to look closely if the doors are open. The interior is usually painted in soft blues and whites, with a ceiling shaped like a ship's hull, and the wooden saints show decades of patient repair. The whole island culture revolves around the minga, a communal work party, and once even whole houses were dragged across the water on floats by teams of oxen and neighbours. The church stands as a record of that cooperative habit, and it is worth leaving a small donation if there is a box.
