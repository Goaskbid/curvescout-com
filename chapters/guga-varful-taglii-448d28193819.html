Guga - Vârful Țâglii

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Rugășești, Vad and Poiana Blenchii are the kind of villages most people drive past on the way to somewhere else, and that is precisely the charm of the hills that rise between them. The point marked as Guga, Vârful Țâglii, lies on this rolling country north of the Someș, roughly a dozen kilometres from Dej, in the band of land where Rome drew the northern edge of Dacia. A summit called Țâglii is the sort of place where a watchtower would have looked out across the Someș corridor toward the free tribes beyond, and the archaeological lists record a frontier site here.

On the bike, think in terms of slow pleasures. County roads around Vad and Rugășești wind through low hills of wheat, sunflower and pasture, with wooden gates carved in the Transylvanian manner and church towers popping up behind every crest. Tarmac quality changes without warning, and the final approach to any hilltop is likely to be a field track, so leave the heavy tourer at the bottom if the ground is wet. The light is best in the early evening, when the long shadows pick out old earthworks you would never see at noon.

Dej itself is a proper town with fuel, supermarkets and a market, and it has a long story of its own: salt from the nearby Ocna Dejului mines was shipped down the Someș for centuries, and the Reformed church in the centre is one of the finest Gothic buildings in this corner of Transylvania. The town is a sensible place for coffee and a meal before heading into the hills, and the riverside road along the Someș gives an easy, flowing ride either upstream toward Beclean or west toward Gâlgău.

Nature here is gentle rather than dramatic. Buzzards circle over the hay, storks nest on electricity poles in the villages, and in late summer the verges smell of wild thyme and dust. Ride respectfully: these are working farms, tracks belong to the people who use them, and a wave to the man on the tractor goes a long way. If the ride brings you back the same way, the return gives you the valley from the other side, which feels like a different landscape entirely.
