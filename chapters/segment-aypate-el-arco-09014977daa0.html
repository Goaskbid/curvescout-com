Segment Aypate - El Arco

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

High above the dry coast of northern Peru, the Andes rise into a cold, wet world of páramo and cloud forest, and in the highlands near Ayabaca sits Aypate, an Inca administrative centre that few visitors ever see. It was one of the major stops on the Inca road network in this part of the country, linked to the great highway that ran north toward Quito, and the Qhapaq Ñan road system was listed by UNESCO in 2014. The stone terraces, plazas and buildings lie scattered on a windy hilltop, with the mist drifting through, and the sense of remoteness is extraordinary.

The segment named Aypate to El Arco is a rural stretch in the Ayabaca district of the Piura region. Roads here are rough, steep and often unpaved, and conditions change with the weather. In the dry months, roughly from May to October, tracks are firmer, and in the rainy season landslides and mud are a real risk. This is a place for a capable bike, a patient pace and local advice, and for riders who are happy to turn back if the weather turns. Temperatures at altitude can be cool even when the lowlands are hot.

Ayabaca, about eighteen kilometres away, is a town of around five thousand and is the nearest place for supplies, food and a bed. Fuel in a small highland town is not always certain, so carry what you need, and plan to refill in Sullana or Piura on the way up. Each October the town hosts one of the largest religious pilgrimages in northern Peru, devoted to a figure known as the Señor Cautivo, and thousands of people walk up from the coast.

The mornings can be luminous, with the whole landscape clear and sharp, before the cloud thickens. Take a good map, tell someone your route, and give yourself time to sit and look at the stones, because the Inca did not choose this site by accident.
