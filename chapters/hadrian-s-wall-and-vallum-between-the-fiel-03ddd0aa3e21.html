Hadrian's Wall and vallum between the fiel (area 54.9,-2.9)

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Carlisle was a Roman town before it was an English one. Luguvalium grew beside the Eden, and just across the river at Stanwix stood the largest fort on Hadrian's Wall, home to a cavalry regiment of a thousand men. The wall itself ran along the north side of today's city, and though houses and roads cover it, its line can be traced by plaques and fragments, and the wall and its Vallum are part of the UNESCO listing. A stretch of the ditch and the foundations of milecastles are visible beyond the suburbs.

The medieval city was a border stronghold, and its red sandstone castle, begun in the eleventh century, saw sieges, raids and prisoners, among them Mary Queen of Scots in 1568. The cathedral, founded in the twelfth century, is one of the smaller English cathedrals, with a magnificent east window. Border reivers, the cattle-raiding families of the Anglo-Scottish frontier, terrorised the region in the sixteenth century, and their names still survive in local families and place names.

Carlisle has about seventy-eight thousand people, with fuel, supermarkets, pubs and restaurants everywhere, and a pleasant centre of old streets and a market hall. For a rider, it is a hinge between landscapes: the Lake District to the southwest, the Pennines to the southeast, the Scottish Borders to the north. The A686 over Hartside, with its summit café, is a favourite motorcycle road, climbing from the Eden valley across the North Pennines with long, flowing bends and enormous views toward the Lakeland fells.

The M6 and the A69 give fast access, but quieter lanes in the Eden valley are more rewarding. Weather can change rapidly, and the high roads can be icy in winter. Spring and early autumn are good seasons, with clear light and fewer crowds. Take a few minutes to find a section of the wall in the suburbs, a modest line of stone beside a road, and imagine the soldiers who patrolled this ridge two thousand years ago.
