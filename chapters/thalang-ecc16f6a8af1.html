Thalang

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Thalang is a crossing point on the Thakhek Loop, a place where a few houses, a handful of shops and a patch of rice field mark the spot where the road swings toward the great limestone country. It lies in Khammouane province, in central Laos, in the zone where the Hinboun river and its reservoir cut through towering karst, and the fields in front of the cliffs are green in the wet season and golden after harvest. The label may be modest, but the setting is anything but.

The geology is the story. The Phou Hin Poun protected area, a vast plateau of limestone that has been dissolved by rain into spires, caves, sinkholes and underground rivers, covers much of the region, and the road passes beside it, with wall after wall of grey rock draped in forest. Mist often clings to the tops in the morning. It is a landscape of the kind you find in northern Vietnam, but with far fewer people and a gentler pace. Caves with streams, swimming holes and viewpoints appear along the way if you are willing to follow a side track.

The riding is easy and delightful. The road wanders through villages and fields with long, flowing bends, a smooth surface for the most part, and a constant rotation of buffalo, bicycles and women carrying baskets. In the heat of the day, the air is thick, and the shade of a roadside shelter is welcome. The cooler dry season, from November to February, is the best time, while the rainy months can bring flash floods, mud and leeches on the trails.

As for services, Thalang is a small place, so do not rely on a full range. Fuel is sometimes sold from drums or bottles at roadside shops, which is a practical, if unscientific, arrangement, and it is best to arrive with a good tank. Food stalls serve noodle soup, grilled chicken and sticky rice, and you can usually buy cold drinks and water. Simple guesthouses are nearby. Make time to stop, take off your helmet and listen: in the quiet of the afternoon you will hear roosters, cicadas and the faint thrum of a distant motorbike.
