W National Park of Niger

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The W National Park gets its name from the shape of the Niger river, which makes a double bend here that looks like a letter W on the map. The park straddles the borders of Niger, Benin and Burkina Faso, and with neighbouring Arly and Pendjari it forms the largest protected savanna area in West Africa. This is the northern edge of the Sudanian savanna, where grassland and dry woodland meet the Sahel, and the river is the lifeline that holds everything together.

Wildlife is the main story. West African elephants, one of the last substantial populations, live in the park, along with hippos, buffalo, baboons, warthogs, roan antelope and a few lions. The Niger itself hosts hippo pods and, in theory, the rare West African manatee. Birdlife is rich along the river, with fish eagles, storks and bee-eaters. The rains run from roughly June to September, when tracks turn to mud and the grass grows tall, and the dry season from November to May is the time when animals gather at water and are easier to see.

I should be honest about the riding. This is not a place for casual travel. Tracks are rough, distances are long, and fuel and spare parts are scarce. Security in parts of the wider region has been unstable for years, and governments regularly advise against travel to areas near the borders. Any plan to come here needs current official advice, a local guide who knows the situation and permission from the park authorities.

If you do visit, the best season is the dry months, when the harmattan haze sometimes softens the horizon and the temperature is bearable in the morning. Go early, carry far more water than you think you need, and expect to share the track with cattle, goats and the occasional elephant. The park is one of the great wild places of West Africa and deserves informed care.
