Lake Crescent

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Lake Crescent is the kind of lake that makes you slow down without being asked. It fills a deep glacial trough in the north of Olympic National Park, roughly six hundred feet at its deepest, and the water is so clear and blue that on a calm day it looks lit from below. Steep forested ridges drop straight into it, with Storm King rising above the south shore. Port Angeles lies about twenty-seven kilometres east, and that is where you find fuel and a proper meal.

Highway 101 follows the lake's southern edge in a series of curves that hug the shoreline, with the forest pressing close on the uphill side and the water glittering through trees on the other. It is a narrow road with no real shoulders and a firm speed limit, shared with logging trucks, motorhomes and the occasional cyclist, so this is a ride to take at a gentle pace, watching the road and enjoying the scenery in the straights. In the morning the lake is often glassy and the light comes in at a low angle; by midday the shoreline fills with visitors.

The lake has a history of its own. Lake Crescent Lodge, a cluster of wooden buildings on the south shore, dates from the early twentieth century, and Franklin Roosevelt stayed in the area in 1937 on the trip that helped shape the national park. The lake is also home to a trout found nowhere else, the Beardslee, a quirk of isolation after the glaciers retreated. A short walk from the road leads to Marymere Falls through old-growth forest of fir, hemlock and cedar draped with moss.

Weather here is changeable. The park sits on the edge of one of the wettest regions in the lower forty-eight, and rain can arrive in minutes, making the road slick, especially under the trees where leaves and needles gather. A fleece and a rain shell make sense even in summer. The best months are July to September, though weekends are busy. Take your time, stop at a turn-out, and let the lake have your attention.
