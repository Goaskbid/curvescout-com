Segment Socoroma Sur

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Socoroma is a village high above the desert, and the road to it is a climb from the coast into another world. Starting from the sea at Arica, the road rises through the arid foothills, past cactus and rocky ridges, and up into the Andean altiplano, passing from sea level to the better part of three thousand metres in a couple of hours. The change is obvious: the air thins, the temperature drops, the light becomes harder and the sky turns a deeper blue. Socoroma sits on the old route between the coast and the high plateau, on terraced slopes cultivated for centuries.

The village is a working Aymara community, with a small church, stone houses with straw roofs and terraces where oregano, potatoes and maize grow. The terraces are far older than the Spanish, and the llamas that drift across the path are not for show. It is a place where local life continues at its own pace, and visitors are expected to be quiet and respectful. Mules and llamas once carried silver and goods along the trails here, and the stone-paved tracks survive in places.

The riding is where the segment earns its name. A road of long gradients, hairpins and fast sweepers climbs steadily, with the surface mostly good but often broken at the edges, and with trucks heading to the mines and the Bolivian border. Weather is changeable: clear mornings, afternoon cloud, and a chill at dusk, and in the rainy season, from December to March, thunderstorms and flash floods can close roads. Altitude is a real concern; a ride that starts at the sea and ends at 3,000 metres can cause headaches and breathlessness, so take it slowly and drink water.

Plan your fuel carefully. Arica is the last reliable place on the coast, and while there is fuel at Putre and along the main road, small villages like Socoroma have no stations. Cold nights are normal at this height, so carry warm clothing even in summer. In the village, there may be a small shop with water and snacks, but do not rely on it. The best seasons are the dry months from April to November, and the best hours are the morning and early afternoon, before the cloud builds and the wind rises.
