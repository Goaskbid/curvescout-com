There are stations at the edge of town, supermarkets, and a handful of bakeries, and the A10 motorway is close enough that the town gets its share of passing trade. From here, the roads south and west leave the flat farmland and climb gently into a country of old castles and quiet villages.

The castles are the point of this ride. West of here, on a wooded hill above Bad Belzig, stands Burg Eisenhardt, a medieval fortress with a tall keep that you can climb for a view across the Fläming. A few kilometres away, Burg Rabenstein, a restored hilltop castle, hosts falconry displays and has a forest setting that looks as though it came from a fairy tale. Nearer, at Ziesar, lies a bishop's castle, now a museum. The Fläming never had grand princes, and its castles are modest but atmospheric, standing in woods and old villages, with a sense of having been forgotten.

The Fläming is also a landscape of unusual quiet. It is a glacial ridge, rising to about two hundred metres at its highest point, covered in pine, beech and heath, with long views from the heights. The villages have stone churches, thatched roofs in places and few visitors. The roads are among the best empty ones in the region, with gentle gradients, long curves, and tree-lined stretches.

Before leaving, it is worth taking time to walk around the old centre of Beelitz, which has a Gothic church and a few remaining parts of its walls. Then fill up, buy water, and head out. Services in the Fläming are limited, with small villages offering little more than a bakery or an inn, and some closed on Mondays. The best months are May to October, with golden autumn light making the castles look their best.
