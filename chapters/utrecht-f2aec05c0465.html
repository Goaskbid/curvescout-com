Utrecht

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Utrecht is the middle of the Netherlands in a very literal way, and it feels it: trains from everywhere come through the station, and everything else is arranged around a canal that sits lower than the street. The Oudegracht has wharves and cellars cut into its banks, once warehouses for goods unloaded straight from boats, now cafes with tables at water level. Above it all stands the Dom Tower, a little over a hundred metres of Gothic stone, which you will see from the ring road long before you arrive.

The city began as a Roman fort on the Rhine and became the seat of a bishop, and for centuries it was the religious capital of the Low Countries. In 1579 the northern provinces signed the Union of Utrecht here, the first step toward the Dutch Republic. A more modern sort of fame belongs to Gerrit Rietveld, whose Schröder House, all crisp planes and primary colours, still looks like next year rather than the 1920s. Dick Bruna, who drew Miffy, was born in the city, and the rabbit is on the pavements and in the museum.

For a rider, Utrecht is a launch pad rather than a destination. The ring and the motorways are busy, and you want to be out of the built-up belt quickly. Heading east, the ground rises a little into the Utrechtse Heuvelrug, a wooded ridge of sand and gravel left by ice, where small roads wind through pine and heath and past country houses. To the north-west the river Vecht meanders on a narrow dike road lined with eighteenth-century estates, slow and pretty, with a speed limit that deserves respect. This is also where the old Dutch water defences run: forts and flooded fields built to protect Amsterdam, a theme you will meet again on the road toward the Pampus fort.

Weather is the usual Dutch mix, so a layer in the top box is sensible even on a warm day, and wind across the polders can be stronger than the trees suggest. Fuel is simple, with stations all around the edge of town and along every main road out. The best hour to roll out is early on a weekend, before the cycle lanes and the market stalls fill the city and the traffic lights start to bunch up.
