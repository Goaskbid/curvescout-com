Seward Highway point 5

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Cooper Landing is the sort of place you reach with a pleasant surprise. The road leaves the high passes and drops towards the Kenai River, which runs a startling milky turquoise, the colour of glacier flour suspended in cold water. The river flows out of Kenai Lake and on through the village and the canyon below, and it is one of the best-known salmon rivers in Alaska. In July the red salmon run brings anglers shoulder to shoulder along the banks, and a bear will occasionally walk through.

The surroundings are mountain and forest, with spruce and birch below and bare slopes above. The Kenai Mountains here are part of a national wildlife refuge, and black and brown bears, moose and Dall sheep all live in the area. Sit quietly at a pullout and the odds are good that something will appear. If you stop for food, keep it sealed, and don't walk off into the bush with sandwiches.

The ride around the lake is lovely, with gentle curves, views over the water, and a road that runs through the forest. It is generally in good condition, though the narrow shoulders and the many fishing and camping traffic movements mean you need to be alert. Summer weekends can be slow, as campers and trailers crowd the route, and construction can create delays, so build in some slack.

There are no big towns near this stop, so this is a place to use sparingly: a few small settlements have fuel, food and lodging, but hours are limited and prices high. Fill up before you set off from the larger centres. The weather is cool and often damp even in midsummer, and the light on the lake in the evening is wonderful. If you are lucky, you will smell woodsmoke from the cabins and see the mist lift off the river as the sun climbs, a good moment for a hot drink and a few photographs.
