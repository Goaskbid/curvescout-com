Wachusett Mountain

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Wachusett is the mountain you see from half of central Massachusetts, and it earns the attention by standing alone. Rising to about six hundred metres out of rolling, wooded country, it is a monadnock, a single resistant mass of old rock left standing when the land around it wore down, and its name comes from an Algonquian phrase usually translated as near the mountain. In the summer of 1842 Henry David Thoreau walked here from Concord, and his essay on the trip, A Walk to Wachusett, still reads like the work of a man who loved a day out.

A paved road climbs to the summit, and it is one of the few places in the eastern part of the state where you can ride to the top of something. It switches back through oak and maple, then through spruce and fir near the top, and its tight bends and steep pitches demand slow, careful riding, with a barrier-free edge in places and leaf litter in the corners. It is closed in winter and during bad weather, and sometimes on busy days, so check before you set out. At the top, a clearing with a fire tower gives a view east toward the Boston skyline on a clear day and north to the peaks of southern New Hampshire.

The mountain is also a hawk watch. In September and October, thousands of raptors ride the thermals along the ridge on their way south, and watchers gather with binoculars to count broad-winged hawks, kestrels and the occasional eagle. In winter the slopes belong to skiers, and the ski area is one of the busiest in the state for its size. The whole thing is a protected state reservation, with a network of trails running through mature forest, and a few small ponds and meadows.

Princeton, about five kilometres away, is the village at the foot, a rural town with a pretty common and a handful of shops, but fuel is better done in Westminster, seven kilometres off, where there are stations and diners along Route 2A. Weekends in October are packed, so the best time is a weekday morning, when the air is crisp, the car park is half empty and the leaves are just turning.
