Socotra b

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Halfway along Socotra's north coast, between Hadibu and the fishing town of Qalansiya, the island shows its gentler face. The coastal plain is narrow here, a strip of pale sand and scrub squeezed between the sea and limestone plateaus that climb steeply inland, and the road runs along it for most of the way west. Both towns lie roughly thirty kilometres away in opposite directions, which makes the place a natural midpoint on the island's main east–west journey.

What makes the north coast special is the view up. Look inland and the plateaus are scattered with bottle trees and cucumber trees, while beyond them the jagged granite of the Haggier range catches cloud most afternoons. In the right season the wadis carry clear water down to the shore, and you can stop by a pool where small fish dart around your boots while Egyptian vultures watch from the rocks. The birdlife is outstanding, with several species found only on the archipelago.

People have lived here for a very long time, trading frankincense and aloe with Greek, Indian and Arab sailors. The islanders speak Soqotri, an ancient South Arabian language that has no traditional written form and that linguists are working hard to record. Life still turns on goats, dates and the sea, and the courtesy shown to visitors is old-fashioned and sincere. Dress modestly, ask before photographing people and accept the tea when it is offered.

Practicalities are simple and stark. Yemen remains under travel warnings, so visits usually come through organised trips with local guides, and private vehicles are rare. The coast road is the best on the island, but sand blows across it and goats wander onto it at random. Fuel exists in Hadibu and, less reliably, in Qalansiya; nothing in between. The northeast monsoon months from October to April bring the calmest seas and the most comfortable temperatures, and outside that window the summer wind can make even standing upright on the beach a challenge.
