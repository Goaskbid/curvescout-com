Viñales Valley

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The Viñales Valley is one of those places where the landscape looks like a stage set. Flat red fields lie between steep, rounded limestone hills called mogotes, sheer-sided and covered in vegetation, standing like green loaves on a tablecloth. They are the remains of an ancient cave system whose roof collapsed over millions of years, and the surrounding karst is among the oldest in the Caribbean. The valley sits in the western province of Pinar del Río, and UNESCO recognised it as a cultural landscape in 1999.

What makes it cultural, not merely beautiful, is the way people still farm. This is Cuba's tobacco country, and the red earth is planted with the leaf that goes into the island's cigars, grown in small plots and often ploughed with oxen. Farmers dry their harvest in thatched barns, and visitors are often invited in to see how it is done and to taste the result. Coffee, beans and maize grow in between, and the little town of Viñales itself, about a kilometre away, is a street of low, colourful houses with deep porches.

Getting here means a long ride west from Havana, first on the broad, often empty national motorway and then on the older roads through Pinar del Río. The surfaces vary from good to bad without warning, and you should watch for potholes, animals, bicycles and horse-drawn carts, especially near villages. The valley road itself is gently winding, with views opening onto the mogotes at nearly every bend, and the best light is in the early morning, when mist hangs low between the hills, and again in the late afternoon.

Viñales has places to eat and drink, many of them in family homes, and the food is simple, hearty and plentiful. Pinar del Río, about twenty kilometres away, is the larger town. Fuel in Cuba is unpredictable, so fill up whenever you can and do not count on a station being open. Leave time to turn off the engine and just sit; the valley rewards stillness.
