Selent lake shore

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Selent is a village of about thirteen hundred people, and its lake, the Selenter See, is the second largest in Schleswig-Holstein, a long glacial basin of roughly twenty-two square kilometres. Nothing here is grand. The shores are low and wooded, reed beds line the edges, and there are good places for birdwatching, with grebes, swans and cormorants common. It is a quiet bit of Holstein Switzerland where the main events are the arrival of the wind, the colour of the water and the occasional sailing dinghy.

The village itself is small but has a real history. Selent has an old manor, Gut Selent, and the surrounding estates were part of the dense network of noble properties that shaped the landscape of eastern Holstein for centuries. The big country houses, the long lime avenues leading up to them, and the red-brick farm buildings all belong to the same story. The area is largely agricultural, with wide fields of rapeseed, wheat and maize under big skies. The Baltic is not far to the north, and on a clear day the air has a tang of salt that makes the place feel more open than inland Germany.

For motorcyclists, the roads around the lake are an easy pleasure. They are mostly narrow, with gentle bends, short climbs and shady tunnels of trees, and traffic is light except at weekends in high summer. Watch for farm vehicles and cyclists, and slow down in the villages, where speed checks are common. The best riding is in the early morning or late afternoon when the sun is low and the shadows long. Spring and autumn have fewer visitors, and the light on the water is excellent then.

There is no big fuel infrastructure in Selent, which is a village, so fill up beforehand. The nearest town with a good choice of stations and supermarkets is Lütjenburg, about ten kilometres away, with around fifty-eight hundred inhabitants and a pretty old centre. Hohwacht, on the Baltic coast beyond it, is a seaside resort with beach cafes and fish rolls, a natural next stop. In Selent itself you can usually find a bakery and a cafe on a good day, though hours vary, so do not plan your lunch around it. The lake is best enjoyed with a flask of coffee and an unhurried ten minutes on the shore.
