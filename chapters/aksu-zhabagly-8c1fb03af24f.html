Aksu-Zhabagly

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Aksu-Zhabagly is Kazakhstan's oldest nature reserve, founded in 1926, and it sits where the Western Tian Shan meets the end of the Talas Alatau. The protected area covers a wide altitude range, from steppe foothills to glaciers, and the result is a startling variety of habitats in a small space: juniper forest, meadow, scree, high snowfields and deep canyons carved by rivers with names like Aksu, meaning white water. Wildlife includes ibex, argali, brown bear, lynx and, in the high country, snow leopard, though your chances of seeing the cat are close to zero.

Spring is when the area earns its reputation. In April and May, the lower hills fill with wild tulips, including species that grew here long before the flower became a Dutch obsession, and meadows turn red, yellow and white for a short season. The Karabastau canyon has a collection of ancient rock carvings, and in the surrounding valleys you can find old burial mounds and other traces of the herders who lived here for millennia. Nearby, in the countryside around Zhabagly village, is a shrine to Arystan Bab, a revered teacher said to have taught Khoja Ahmed Yasawi.

The ride from Shymkent heads east on fast tarmac, then turns into the hills on narrower roads that pass farms, orchards and small villages. Corners become more frequent as the land rises, and the last stretch to the reserve can be bumpy and unpaved in places. Livestock roams freely, and in spring there is often mud. The air is clean and cooler than the plain, and the light on the snow peaks is often superb, particularly in the early morning.

You need to plan carefully. Entry into the reserve usually requires a permit, and it is easiest to arrange in advance through the reserve office or a local guesthouse. Fuel is not available near the reserve; Tyulkubas is the closest larger town, and Shymkent is the safe place to fill up completely. In the villages you will find simple guesthouses that serve warm bread, soup, honey and tea. Come in spring for the tulips, or in early autumn when the air is crisp and the hills turn gold.
