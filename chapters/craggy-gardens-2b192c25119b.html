Craggy Gardens

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Craggy Gardens is a high, bald ridge on the Blue Ridge Parkway at about milepost 364, where the forest gives way to heath and thickets of Catawba rhododendron, and in the second half of June the slopes burst into purple blooms. The elevation is about 5,500 feet, high enough that the trees are twisted and stunted by wind and ice, and the view from the trails near the visitor centre stretches across the mountains of western North Carolina. A short, steep walk leads to a rocky outcrop called Craggy Pinnacle, from which the whole ridge can be seen.

The mountains here are among the oldest on Earth, worn down over hundreds of millions of years, and the Black Mountains, a few miles north, include Mount Mitchell, at 6,684 feet the highest peak east of the Mississippi River. A spur road from the Parkway climbs to its summit. The nearby towns of Black Mountain and Weaverville are the gateways to the area, and Asheville, about twenty-four kilometres to the south-west, has a lively downtown, the Biltmore estate and a reputation for craft beer and music.

The Parkway runs for 469 miles along the Appalachian crest, with a maximum speed of 45 miles an hour, and no commercial traffic. The riding is relaxed and absorbing, with long, graceful curves, tunnels and viewpoints, and the road is almost always clean and smooth. Fog is common at this elevation, and can come down in minutes, and the temperature can be ten degrees colder than in the valley. Deer and bears may cross the road. In September 2024, Hurricane Helene caused severe damage to the Parkway around Asheville, and sections have been closed for repair, so check the National Park Service for the current status before you go.

The nearest stations are in Black Mountain and Weaverville, a short ride down the access roads, and Asheville has everything. The visitor centre at Craggy Gardens has a small shop and toilets, but no restaurant, so bring a lunch.

The best time to come is mid-June for the rhododendrons, or October for the colours, and mornings are less crowded. Stand on the ridge, feel the wind and enjoy the sense of space.
