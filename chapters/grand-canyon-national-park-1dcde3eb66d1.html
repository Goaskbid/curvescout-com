Grand Canyon National Park

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

The first thing the Grand Canyon does is refuse to make sense. You walk to the South Rim, a flat forest of pine and juniper at about two thousand one hundred metres, and the ground simply ends. Below is a gorge up to sixteen kilometres wide and a mile deep, a stack of red, buff and violet rock layers that record nearly two billion years, with the Colorado River a thin brown thread at the bottom. Photos flatten it, and nothing prepares you for the scale.

The rim road system is what a rider wants. The drive east to Desert View follows the canyon for about forty kilometres, with a string of pull-offs, and ends at the Watchtower, a stone tower designed by the architect Mary Colter and finished in 1932, built to look like an ancestral Puebloan structure. West of the village, Hermit Road is closed to private vehicles for much of the year and run by shuttle. Surfaces are good and speed limits low, and tourist traffic and elk on the road are bigger hazards than curves.

The park is also a living place. Ravens and ground squirrels work the viewpoints, and California condors, reintroduced after near extinction, sometimes ride the updrafts at the rim. Dawn and dusk are the best light, when the rock glows from within, and summer afternoons often bring storms that lace the canyon with rain and lightning. In winter the rim can be snowy and icy, so check conditions.

Practical points matter here. Fuel inside the park is limited, and the town of Tusayan, a few kilometres south of the entrance, has more services and motels, with Flagstaff, about ninety minutes away, as the main base. An entrance fee applies, and it is valid for several days. Water fountains are available near the village, and you will need it, because the dry air at this altitude takes a lot out of you. Leave plenty of time, since the main viewpoints are tempting enough to eat a whole afternoon.
