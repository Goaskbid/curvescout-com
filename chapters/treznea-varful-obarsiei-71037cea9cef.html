Treznea-Vârful Obârșiei

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Treznea is a small village that appears in Romanian history books for a bad reason. In August 1940, the Second Vienna Award, imposed by Germany and Italy, handed northern Transylvania to Hungary, and in the weeks that followed, Hungarian troops entered the area. On 9 September, in Treznea and the neighbouring village of Ip, dozens of Romanian civilians were killed in reprisals, and a memorial in Treznea recalls them. The territory returned to Romania in 1944, but the memory of those years still shapes local life.

The landscape is not dramatic, which is part of the contrast. The village lies on the southern flank of the Meseș Mountains, in the county of Sălaj, among rolling hills of pasture, orchard and beech wood, and Vârful Obârșiei, the hilltop that gives this point its name, is a quiet summit about six kilometres from Meseșenii de Jos. The word obârșie means source or origin, and in Romanian usage it can mean the head of a stream. Clear small streams run off the ridges here and feed the rivers of the Crasna valley below.

Mixed communities of Romanians, Hungarians and Roma live across this region, and the older houses and churches reflect all three traditions, with Orthodox, Greek Catholic and Reformed churches sometimes in the same valley. That makes the area a lesson in how complicated borders can be, and a reminder to be respectful and curious when you talk to the people you meet.

For riders the roads are quiet and winding, with plenty of bends, short climbs and views from the ridges. Surfaces range from decent asphalt to rough patches and gravel, so ride within your sight, and watch for horses, carts, geese and stray dogs. Weather in the Meseș is changeable, with fog in cooler months and heavy summer storms. Fuel is limited in the villages, so fill up in larger towns, and Zalău is the reliable choice. Small shops in the villages sell coffee, bread and simple food.

An unhurried visit in spring or autumn, with an hour spent at the memorial, adds a sober but meaningful layer to the ride.
