High point near Spreenhagen

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Spreenhagen is a scattered rural municipality south-east of Berlin, where the River Spree winds through forests and meadows between Fürstenwalde and the capital. The villages here are small and quiet, with old churches, farmhouses and modern homes for commuters, and the landscape is a typical Brandenburg mix of pine forests, wet meadows and lakes.

The Spree is the river of Berlin, flowing through the heart of the city, but here it is a quieter stream, meandering through a broad valley with alder woods and reeds. The forests around Spreenhagen are part of a large wooded area that stretches south towards the Dahme lakes and east towards the Scharmützelsee, and they are where deer, boar and many birds. In summer, the lakes are popular for swimming and boating, and the air smells of pine and warm sand.

The roads are mostly flat, running dead straight through pine forest and only bending where a lake or a village gets in the way. Traffic is light, apart from the main roads near the motorway, which can be busy with commuters and trucks. The A12 motorway to Frankfurt an der Oder and Poland runs nearby, providing a fast route east or back to Berlin. Watch for wildlife, especially at dawn and dusk, and for sand on the road at forest edges.

Fuel and supplies are most reliably found in Fürstenwalde, the nearest larger town, a few minutes away. Fürstenwalde has a cathedral and a pleasant centre on the Spree. Spreenhagen itself works well for a rest in the forest before heading south towards the Lusatian lakes, where former opencast mines have been flooded to create a new landscape.
