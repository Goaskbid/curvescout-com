Bielsa

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Bielsa sits at the point where two Pyrenean rivers, the Cinca and the Barrosa, meet, and it feels like the last village before the mountains close in. Fewer than five hundred people live here, which is hard to believe when you see how many walkers and riders pass through in summer. Behind the town the Pineta valley climbs towards the Monte Perdido massif, one of the great limestone ranges of the Pyrenees, and a short ride up that side road brings you under walls of grey rock with a glacial amphitheatre at the end.

The town you see today is mostly younger than it looks. Bielsa was heavily damaged in the Spanish Civil War and rebuilt in the 1940s by the state reconstruction service, so the old stone houses on the main square sit beside a plainer, tidier layer of postwar buildings. Locals still hold a fire festival called the Trangas, in which men carry burning pine bundles through the streets. It is said to be very old, and it is certainly one of the stranger things you can see in Aragon.

The riding here is about contrast. Coming up from the south along the A-138 you follow a gorge-cut valley with long, rhythmic bends, tunnels cut straight through the rock, and a surface that is generally good but patched by winter. North of the village the road runs to the Bielsa tunnel, which has linked Aragon with the French side of the range since the 1970s and saves you a high pass. Mornings are cool and clear; by late afternoon in July and August the valley fills with day-trippers and the odd slow campervan, so an early start pays off.

The nature is the real reason to stop. Chamois and griffon vultures are regular sights, beech and fir forest crowds the slopes, and the air smells of cut grass and cold stone. This is also the border country of the Ordesa and Monte Perdido park, so conditions can change fast: a clear morning can turn into afternoon cloud with a sharp drop in temperature, and snow lingers at height well into spring.

For practical matters, this is a small place. There are bars and a few places to eat around the square, and a coffee here is a good use of twenty minutes. Aínsa, a good way down the valley, is the sensible place to be sure of a full tank before heading up into the quiet end of the range.
