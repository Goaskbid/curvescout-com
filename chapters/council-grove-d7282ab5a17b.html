Council Grove

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Wagon trains did their last serious shopping in Council Grove. In the nineteenth century, this was the final reliable stop on the Santa Fe Trail, the place where traders headed for New Mexico cut timber for spare axles, stocked up and waited for enough company to travel safely onward. A treaty with the Osage was signed here in 1825 under a great oak, and the Kaw, or Kansa people, were later settled nearby, so the place has a long record as a meeting point, which is what the name means.

Today the town has a little over two thousand residents and a main street that still looks like a Western film set without trying. The Hays House claims to be the oldest continuously operating restaurant west of the Mississippi, dating its start to the 1850s, and it is a decent bet for a hearty lunch. The Neosho River winds through town, and the Council Oak stump, the Post Office Oak and several old stone buildings are easy to find on foot after you park the bike. Council Grove Lake just outside the town gives the water a different mood, with grass shores and broad skies.

The riding in and out is classic Flint Hills: long, easy bends over rolling grass, hardly a tree except along the creeks, and views that carry for miles. Surfaces are generally good on the main highways, the secondary roads can be chip-sealed or gravel, so check before you commit. Expect cattle trucks, farm pickups and drivers who will wave as they pass. A south wind can blow steadily through the summer, and spring brings smoke from the pasture burns, which look dramatic and sometimes drift over the road.

Council Grove has the services a rider needs, with fuel, a few places to eat and a motel or two, so it works well as a proper stop rather than a quick photo. Cottonwood Falls and its limestone courthouse lie about thirty kilometres south, and Herington to the northwest has fuel as well. Come in the evening, when the grass catches the light and the town goes quiet.
