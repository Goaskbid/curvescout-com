Sary Tash (area 39.7,73.3)

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Sary-Tash is a handful of low houses and a few guesthouses at a crossroads in southern Kyrgyzstan, at about 3,100 metres, and it has a lot of importance for a place so small. Here the road from Osh, coming south over the passes, meets the route that turns east to China at Irkeshtam and the one that turns south to the Pamir Highway and Tajikistan. In the old days, caravans on the Silk Road passed through, and the Soviet Union later built the road that is still the main artery of the region.

The setting is magnificent. To the south, the Alay valley opens out into a broad, high plain of pale grass, with the snowy wall of the Trans-Alay range beyond, the northern edge of the Pamirs. On clear days, Peak Lenin, at more than seven thousand metres, shows above the ridge, and the view is among the great mountain panoramas of Central Asia. The air is thin, dry and cold, with a clean, grassy smell, and the wind can be fierce.

Riding in this region is a serious proposition. The road from Osh climbs over the Taldyk pass to get here, and the Kyzyl-Art pass lies farther south on the way toward Tajikistan; the surface is partly paved, partly rough, with potholes, landslides and sections of gravel. Weather changes fast, with snow possible in any month, and the high passes are closed or dangerous in winter. Cars and trucks are few, and livestock and shepherds on the road are common. Nights are cold, and even in summer the temperature can drop below freezing. The best time to come is roughly June to September, and altitude can bring headaches and fatigue.

There are limited services. Sary-Tash has a few guesthouses and homestays that offer tea, bread, soup and a bed, and a couple of simple shops, and fuel is available in small quantities from roadside sellers and a basic station, though quality and supply can be uncertain. It is wise to carry extra fuel, water and food, and to fill whenever you can. Mobile signal comes and goes, and border permits may be needed for the routes toward Tajikistan and China, so check requirements in advance.

Warm tea with bread and honey in a guesthouse is the right welcome after the cold ride, and the generosity of the Kyrgyz herders and hosts is one of the best things about the region.
