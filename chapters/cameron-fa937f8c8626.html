Cameron

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Cameron is a small town with a very large story. It sits on the Louisiana coast where the Calcasieu Ship Channel reaches the Gulf, and the first thing most visitors do is cross the water on the free ferry, a short, slightly comic ride that carries cars, trucks, and motorcycles over to the east bank. On a clear day you can see the shipping lanes and the long, low shape of the marsh, and a pelican usually appears as if on cue.

The parish named for the town is one of the least populated and most storm-battered in the state. In 1957 Hurricane Audrey devastated the area, killing hundreds of people, and Rita in 2005 and Laura in 2020 flattened much of what had been rebuilt. Locals have a quiet, practical attitude toward this, and the town's modest size today is the result of repeated loss rather than lack of interest. Fishing, shrimping, oil and gas, and gas export terminals are the economic mainstays, and the water on both sides of the road is among the most productive in the country.

The riding is the Creole Nature Trail's flat, open run along the marsh. Highway 27 and Highway 82 meet in this area, and the road can feel remote even when other vehicles are about. The wind is the thing to respect, and the heat and humidity of summer are real. Alligators are common in the roadside ditches, and in winter the wetlands hold huge flocks of ducks and geese. The Sabine National Wildlife Refuge is nearby, with a boardwalk and a walking trail, and it is a fine place to watch birds and gators from a safe distance.

Services in Cameron are limited, and since the storms they have been unpredictable. Treat the town as somewhere to cross the ferry and perhaps eat a plate of fried shrimp, rather than as a place to rely on for fuel. Fill the tank in Sulphur or Lake Charles beforehand, and carry water and a rain jacket. The ferry can pause for fog or high winds, so allow some slack in your schedule. Late autumn and spring are the comfortable times; the heat of high summer and the hurricane season are best avoided if you can.
