Husoy

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Husøy is a fishing village on a small island off the coast of Senja, tied to the larger island by road, and the lanes of its wooden houses are painted in the traditional reds, yellows and whites of the north. It is a working harbour first of all: boats at the quay, nets and crates on the wharf, and the air heavy with the sharp smell of salt, diesel and fish. Racks of drying cod stand in places, the old method of preserving the catch that built the economy of the Norwegian north. Stockfish, as it is called, was shipped to southern Europe for centuries, and the trade shaped places like this.

The ride to Husøy is a gentle one. The road follows the coast, passing small bays and farms, and the island comes into view as a low, green hump against the grey sea. It is a good place to slow down, park, and walk. The lanes are narrow and the speed is low, and there is no hurry. In summer, the light lasts all night, and the village has a calm, outward-looking feel, while in autumn the sea can be rough, with waves thundering on the rocks at the outer edge. A strong wind is common, and a rider on a lightweight bike should be ready for gusts when crossing exposed stretches.

Small places like this live on a few things: fish, tourism in summer, and the stubborn love of those who stay. If you meet someone mending nets on the quay, you may get a story or two, but remember that people are at work. Check what is open before you come, as cafés and shops are few and may keep short hours. Seabirds nest on the cliffs nearby, and sea eagles, with their huge pale wings, sometimes circle over the harbour.

Finnsnes, back on the mainland side, is the nearest town for fuel, supermarkets and repairs, and it is some distance away, so top up before you set out along the coast. Gryllefjord, a small place farther out, is closer, but it offers few services. The best time to be here is a still evening in summer, when the water is calm, and the whole village glows in the long low sun.
