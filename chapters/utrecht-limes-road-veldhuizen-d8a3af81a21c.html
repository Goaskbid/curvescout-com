Utrecht-Limes road | Veldhuizen

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

This is a ride along an old frontier that is now a residential suburb. When the Romans held the lower Rhine, the river itself was the border of the empire, and a chain of forts, watchtowers and roads ran along its south bank. One of the forts stood where Utrecht is now, under the name Traiectum, and another, with a fine reconstruction, lies in the modern district of Leidsche Rijn. The whole frontier, the Lower German Limes, was added to the World Heritage list in 2021. Today it is hard to see anything of it, because the Rhine silted up and shifted, and the land is mostly polder, pasture and new housing estate.

The Utrecht-Limes road follows the line of the old river, now called the Oude Rijn, past Veldhuizen, Vleuten and De Meern. Here you find dyke roads, a neat line of willows, long narrow fields reclaimed from the peat, and in the distance the Dom Tower of Utrecht standing alone above the flat. A handful of finds have come to light as the district was built, including a Roman barge that was dug out of the mud nearby, and it is worth looking in a local museum if you want to see the real things.

The riding is flat, quiet, and entirely under the thumb of the Dutch traffic police. Speed limits are low, cameras are common, and cyclists use every road. A motorbike wins a quiet kind of pleasure here, in the smell of cut grass, the wind off the polders, and the low light on the water. Wind is the main weather issue, because there is nothing to stop it, and rain showers arrive suddenly from the west.

Veldhuizen and Vleuten have fuel and supermarkets, and Utrecht is only a few minutes away if you want something more substantial, with its canal wharves and cafes at water level. The pleasure is in taking it slowly and imagining the legions on the far bank, looking into the unconquered north.
