Częstochowa

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

For Poles, Częstochowa means one thing, the Jasna Góra monastery, and about a quarter of a million people live around it in a city that exists largely to serve that fact. The monastery stands on a limestone ridge, a fortified complex of walls, bastions and a tall baroque tower, and within it hangs the icon of the Black Madonna, a dark painted image of the Virgin and Child, with two scars across her cheek. Several million pilgrims arrive every year, many on foot, in groups that walk for days from every corner of the country.

The monastery was founded by Paulite monks in 1382, and the icon's history is wrapped in legend. In 1655, during the Swedish invasion known as the Deluge, a small garrison here held out against a much larger army, and the defence became a turning point in national morale, the proof, for Poles, of divine protection. Pope John Paul II visited several times, and the place became a symbol of resistance during the communist years. The treasury and military museum, with votive offerings from soldiers and pilgrims, are worth a visit, and the long avenue leading up to the monastery is always busy.

For a rider the real draw lies in the surroundings. The Kraków-Częstochowa Upland, also known as the Jura, is a limestone country of white cliffs, caves and the ruins of small medieval castles, the Eagles' Nests, built in the fourteenth century by King Casimir the Great to guard the border. The route follows lanes through forests and farmland, with gentle bends, short climbs and views of pale rock towers rising above the trees. Roads are usually good, though sometimes narrow, and traffic is heavy in the city and around holidays.

Services in Częstochowa are comprehensive, with stations, workshops, supermarkets and plenty of cafes serving soup, dumplings and cake.

If you can, arrive in the late afternoon, when the crowds are thinner and the sun lights the tower. Walk along the ramparts, and be respectful, since this is a living place of worship and not only a monument.
