Ustrzyki Gorne

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Ustrzyki Górne is where Poland starts to run out, and that is the whole charm. The village sits in the Bieszczady, the far south-eastern corner of the country, in a landscape of open grassy ridges called połoniny, with the Ukrainian border only a short distance away. The road comes up from Lutowiska through beech forest and valley meadows, and the last villages feel more like the edge of a frontier than a tourist area. You arrive at a small settlement with a handful of guesthouses, trailheads for the hills, and the sensation of having left the busy Poland behind.

The reason the countryside looks so empty is history. Before the Second World War this was a mixed region of Boyko and Lemko villages with wooden churches, orchards and small farms. After the war, forced resettlement in the late 1940s and border changes emptied whole valleys, and forest grew over the old village sites. Walk even a short way off the road and you may find an old orchard, a cemetery gone to moss, or the stone foundations of a church. It gives the place a quiet, thoughtful atmosphere that sits well with the wide ridges.

Nature has taken the space back. The Bieszczady National Park around here is one of the wildest areas in Poland, home to brown bears, wolves, lynx and a herd of European bison, and in the evening it is not unusual to see deer along the roadside. Riders should keep speeds moderate at dusk, because animals appear without warning, and the narrow roads have blind bends and occasional gravel. The beech forests turn gold in October, which is perhaps the best time to come, though early summer with wildflowers on the ridges is a close second.

Services are thin. There are small shops and simple restaurants serving pierogi, żurek and trout, but fuel should be organised beforehand: Lesko and Ustrzyki Dolne to the north are the sensible places to fill the tank, and Lutowiska behind you has only a little. Weather turns quickly in the hills, so pack a rain layer, and be aware that the border zone means you should carry your documents.

Most people come to ride the Bieszczady loop and then walk up one of the big meadows for the view. Do both if you can, and leave time for a slow lunch.
