Statue of Lao Tze

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

North of Quanzhou, where the city fades into granite hills and orchards, the road runs up to the foot of Qingyuan Mountain and a figure that stops most visitors for a moment. The Lao Tze statue is a seated old man with a long beard, carved out of a natural boulder, and it has been there since the Song dynasty, roughly a thousand years. It is among the largest carved Taoist figures in China, and because the stone was never moved, the whole thing feels grown rather than built.

Lao Tze, or Laozi, is the semi-legendary author of the Tao Te Ching, a short book about flowing with the world instead of forcing it. The story goes that he rode away on a water buffalo toward the western pass and wrote the book for a guard who asked him to leave something behind. It is hard not to like a philosopher who is remembered mainly for saying less. The hillside around the statue is wooded, with paths up to viewpoints and small temples, and the rock itself is a lesson in patience, weathered into smooth grey folds.

The ride up from town is short and ordinary: urban sprawl, then orchards, then a modest hill road with little traffic on weekdays. Weekends and holidays bring crowds of locals. The nicest hours are morning, before haze builds, and the cooler months from October to March, when the slopes are clear and the air smells of camphor and wet earth. In summer the humidity is heavy and afternoon thunderstorms are common.

Quanzhou, four kilometres south, covers everything you need: fuel, food, hotels. Fengzhou, a smaller town closer to the statue, has local places for noodles and tea. The statue is a free-standing public monument in a park-like setting, so treat it as a calm half-hour rather than a day out, and keep the helmet with you if you leave the bike near the car park. A reminder too that foreign riders face strict licensing rules in China, so confirm your paperwork before you plan.
