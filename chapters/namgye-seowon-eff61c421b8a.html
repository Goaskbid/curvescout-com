Namgye-seowon

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Hamyang is a quiet county in the mountains of South Gyeongsang, on the eastern edge of Jirisan, the great massif in the south of the Korean peninsula, and Namgye-seowon stands on a gentle slope a few kilometres from the county town. Founded in 1552 to honour the Neo-Confucian scholar Jeong Yeo-chang, it was one of the earliest private academies in Korea and served as a model for many others. Its layout set a pattern: the lecture hall at the front, dormitories on either side, and the shrine for the rites at the top of the slope, so that the buildings climb the hill in a hierarchy of importance. Unusually, the dormitories have small raised pavilions overlooking ponds, where students could rest and contemplate the landscape. Namgye is part of the UNESCO listing of the Seowon, Korean Neo-Confucian Academies.

Hamyang has another historic treasure. The Sangnim forest, along the river in the county town, was planted more than a thousand years ago by Choe Chi-won, a great scholar of the Silla kingdom, to protect the town from floods. It is one of the oldest man-made forests in Korea, and its old oaks and hornbeams form a cool, shady walk, especially beautiful in autumn.

Jirisan, the second-highest mountain in South Korea, dominates the region. Its national park, the first in the country, covers a vast area of forested ridges, deep valleys and Buddhist temples, and its peaks are often in the clouds. The roads around it are among the best in Korea for motorcycles. The road from Hamyang over the Odo pass towards Jirisan climbs in a beautiful series of hairpins, with views over the ridges, and the roads through the valleys on the eastern side of the massif are winding and scenic, with temples and hot springs. The surface is excellent, the traffic light on weekdays, and the speed limits enforced by cameras. Watch for wild boar and deer.

Hamyang has fuel, shops and restaurants, though it is not listed among the larger towns nearby. The region is famous for mountain herbs and wild ginseng, and Hamyang hosts a festival dedicated to wild ginseng. Try sanchae bibimbap, rice with mountain vegetables, in a restaurant near the park.

The best seasons are spring and autumn; summer is hot and humid, with monsoon rains, and winter brings snow to the mountains.
