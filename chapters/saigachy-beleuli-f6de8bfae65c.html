Saigachy-Beleuli

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

This is a place where the map offers few names and even fewer roads. What I can describe is the landscape, and it is one of the most austere in the world: pale, stony ground, scattered tufts of saltwort and wormwood, and a horizon as flat as a table, broken only by the edge of the plateau where it drops in chalk cliffs known locally as chinks.

The name Saigachy comes from the saiga, the strange, big-nosed antelope that once roamed the steppes in millions. It is a creature that looks half ancient, with a bulbous snout that filters dust and warms the winter air. The herds migrate across the Ustyurt, and their numbers fell disastrously in the 1990s and again in 2015, when disease killed hundreds of thousands in a few weeks. Conservation efforts have helped some populations recover. Goitered gazelles, wild asses called kulan, and wolves also live on the plateau, and eagles hunt over the open ground.

For riders, the plateau is a dream and a danger at the same time. The main roads that cross it, linking Kazakhstan and Karakalpakstan, are long tarmac lines with enormous distances between settlements, and the side tracks are sand and gravel that can swallow a bike. Summer temperatures are brutal, often above forty degrees, and winters bring ice and fierce winds. Spring, when the steppe briefly flowers, and early autumn are the best times.

There are no reliable services off the main road. Carry fuel for far more than you expect to need, water for days, tools, a satellite messenger if possible, and a local contact who knows the tracks. The nearest real towns are a long way off, and the ones that exist are small. Border formalities in this region can be complicated, so check what permits are needed. The landscape reward is silence, and a sky that seems to go on forever.
