Snoqualmie and Yakima Canyon food stop

This is an existing place description, not a separately verified motorbike route. Seasonal, access and service information may have changed.

Snoqualmie Falls drops two hundred and sixty-eight feet over a granite ledge, about a hundred feet farther than Niagara, and it sounds like a train. The Snoqualmie River pours into the gorge in a column of white, and a pale mist rises from the pool below, drifting over the viewing platform. For the Snoqualmie people, the falls are sacred, a place where the mist rising from the water connects the earth to the sky, and they still hold ceremonies there. The Salish Lodge, a handsome hotel perched at the lip, has become a landmark in its own right, and its restaurant has long been popular for breakfast, though bookings help.

The town of Snoqualmie, with thirteen thousand people, is close to the falls and has a bit of the logging-town charm that it was founded on. The Northwest Railway Museum, in the old depot, keeps a collection of historic locomotives and runs excursion trains on a stretch of the former Great Northern line in summer. Next door at North Bend, a few miles up the valley, Twede's Cafe and the surrounding hills were used for the filming of Twin Peaks, the cult television series, and fans still come to find the diner and the cherry pie, and Mount Si, the jagged peak behind the town, looks every bit the part.

For a rider, this is the doorstep of the Cascades. Interstate 90 runs up the valley to Snoqualmie Pass, about three thousand feet, and the older roads on the valley floor offer a quieter way with long curves and forest. The surface is mostly good, but rain makes it slick, and traffic builds on weekends when hikers head for Mount Si and the trails. Look out for cyclists in the valley, and for fog in the morning.

The town has fuel stations, supermarkets and many places to eat, and the area around the falls has a large car park and a short walk to the viewing platform. A trail leads to the base of the falls, about half a mile, and is steep on the way back. The falls are at their best in winter and spring, when the river is full, and in summer they thin to a pale veil. Rain is likely, so take waterproofs. In winter, ice sometimes forms on the cliffs beside the falls, and the whole scene is silent and white.
