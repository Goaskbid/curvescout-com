Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Area of Low-dam on the Plain-Causeway in F

    Five thousand years ago, a society on the Yangtze delta built dams. The Liangzhu culture, which flourished around 3300 to 2300 BC in what is now Zhejiang province, built a huge water management system in the hills and plain northwest of modern Hangzhou, and UNESCO listed it in 2019 together with the ruins of the Liangzhu city. The system included high dams in the mountains, low dams on the plain and a network of canals and reservoirs, and it is among the oldest large-scale hydraulic engineering works known.

  2. 2. Jiaxing-Hangzhou Section of Jiangnan Canal

    The Grand Canal of China is the longest artificial waterway in the world, a chain of canals and rivers linking Beijing with Hangzhou that was begun in sections well over two thousand years ago and unified under the Sui dynasty in the seventh century. Its southernmost stretch, the Jiangnan Canal, crosses the flat, watery plain between Jiaxing and Hangzhou, and this section is part of the UNESCO World Heritage site. Grain, silk and salt moved north along it for centuries, and the towns that line its banks grew rich on that traffic.

  3. 3. Hangzhou Xiaoshan - Shaoxing Section of Zh

    Shaoxing is a city built on water. Its canals, bridges and narrow stone-paved lanes have made it one of the most photogenic towns in eastern China, and the Zhedong Canal, which links Hangzhou, Shaoxing and Ningbo, is its great artery. The canal belongs to the Grand Canal system, the longest artificial waterway in the world, which UNESCO listed in 2014. The Zhedong section carried grain, silk, tea and travellers for more than a thousand years, and in Shaoxing you can still see black-awninged boats, arched stone bridges and old warehouses along the banks.
