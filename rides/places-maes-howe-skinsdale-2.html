Why this ride

A 449 km round trip from Stromness - roughly 10 h in the saddle. The stops in between: Munsary & Shielton, East Halladale, Skinsdale and back through East Halladale. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Maes Howe

    Start here.

    Maeshowe is a grass-covered mound in a field beside Loch Harray, about thirty-five metres across and encircled by a ditch, and inside it is the finest chambered tomb in northern Europe. Built around 2800 BC, it is entered by a long, low passage of huge single slabs, aligned so that around midwinter the setting sun shines straight down it and lights the back wall. The chamber itself is high and square, with corbelled walls that narrow towards the roof and side cells where the bones of the dead were laid. The precision of the masonry, with no mortar, is astonishing, and the stone slabs in the passage weigh several tonnes.

    Read the complete Maes Howe write-up.

  2. 2. Munsary & Shielton

    102 km in.

    Inland from Wick, in the far north-east of Scotland, the land flattens into a vast, rolling expanse of peat bog, brown and gold, dotted with pools and lochans that reflect the sky. Munsary and Shielton are areas of this blanket bog, part of the Flow Country, which was added to the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2024 as the largest expanse of blanket bog in Europe. Here, sphagnum mosses have built up peat over thousands of years, storing vast amounts of carbon, and the bog teems with life: insect-eating sundews, cotton grass waving white in summer, and birds such as golden plover, greenshank and dunlin nesting among the hummocks.

    Read the complete Munsary & Shielton write-up.

  3. 3. East Halladale

    132 km in. The ride passes about 10 km from it.

    Strath Halladale is one of the emptiest valleys in mainland Britain, and East Halladale is a scatter of crofts and sheep fields along it. The A897 runs up the strath from Helmsdale on the east coast to Melvich on the north coast, following the Halladale River for much of the way, and for long stretches you will see more deer than cars. It is single track with passing places, so the rules are simple: pull in to let faster traffic by, wave, and never park in a passing place.

    Read the complete East Halladale write-up.

  4. 4. Skinsdale

    225 km in. The ride passes about 6 km from it.

    Nobody lives in Skinsdale to speak of, which is more or less the point. The glen lies in the east of Sutherland, an empty fold of moorland and burn between the coast at Brora and Helmsdale and the great open bog country to the north-west, and the nearest towns of any size, Golspie, Brora and Dornoch, are all over twenty kilometres away. A road passes through this part of the country, but the glen itself is a place you look into rather than ride through.
