Why this ride

A 169 km round trip from Kronstadt - roughly 3 h in the saddle. It goes by way of The Park «Nearest Dubki», The I.Repin Estate «The Penates», The E.I.Lopukhina's and back through The I.Repin Estate «The Penates». The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

From Fort «Paul I» to The Park «Nearest Dubki» is 26 km, mostly on КАД. An easy profile, never above 13 m. Watch for sand and spray near the water.

The stops

  1. 1. Fort «Paul I»

    Start here.

    To the Russian navy, Kronstadt is the fortress island of Kotlin in the eastern Gulf of Finland, and the forts scattered across the water around it were built to stop any enemy from approaching St Petersburg by sea.

    Read the complete Fort «Paul I» write-up.

  2. 2. The Park «Nearest Dubki»

    26 km in.

    Dubki simply means little oaks, a common name for a patch of woodland, and the ride titles connect this spot with the Oranienbaum Springboard, which is the south shore, across the water, where a Soviet bridgehead held out through the siege of Leningrad from 1941 to 1944 while the city itself was encircled.

    Read the complete The Park «Nearest Dubki» write-up.

  3. 3. The I.Repin Estate «The Penates»

    50 km in.

    In 1903 the painter Ilya Repin, the most famous Russian artist of his day, moved to a wooden house in the village of Kuokkala on the Gulf of Finland, which he named the Penates, after the Roman household gods. He lived and worked there until his death in 1930, and after Finnish independence in 1917, he found himself living abroad, refusing invitations to return to the Soviet Union. The house, with its glass-roofed studio and eccentric architecture, was destroyed in the Second World War and rebuilt afterwards, and it is now a museum with many of his paintings and possessions.

    Read the complete The I.Repin Estate «The Penates» write-up.

  4. 4. The E.I.Lopukhina's

    82 km in.

    North of Saint Petersburg's centre, the city thins out into a mix of suburbs, dachas, pine woods and the long marshy shores of the Karelian Isthmus.

    Read the complete The E.I.Lopukhina's write-up.
