  1. 1. Medicine Hat

    Rudyard Kipling passed through Medicine Hat in 1907 and described it as having all hell for a basement, a reference to the huge reserves of natural gas beneath the town. The gas was so plentiful that the city kept its street lamps burning day and night for years because it was cheaper than paying someone to turn them off, and it still calls itself the Gas City. Today Medicine Hat is a busy prairie city of more than sixty thousand people in the valley of the South Saskatchewan River, surrounded by dry grassland and deep coulees.

  2. 2. Maple Creek

    They call Maple Creek the Old Cow Town, and the nickname fits. This small Saskatchewan town of about two thousand people grew up in the 1880s as a ranching centre when the Canadian Pacific Railway came through, and cattle are still the backbone of the local economy. The main street has old brick buildings, a western-wear store and a museum full of ranching history, and on a quiet afternoon it feels like the place has not changed much in a century.
