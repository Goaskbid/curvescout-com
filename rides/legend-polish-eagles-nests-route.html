Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kraków

    Kraków has the kind of old town that makes you slow down on foot, and it deserves it. At the centre is the Rynek Główny, the main market square, one of the largest medieval squares in Europe, with the Cloth Hall in the middle and the two uneven towers of St Mary's Basilica at the corner. Each hour a trumpeter plays a bugle call from the taller tower, and the tune stops abruptly in mid-phrase, in memory of a watchman said to have been shot in the throat by a Tatar arrow while sounding the alarm.

  4. 4. Near Ogrodzieniec

    Ogrodzieniec Castle is the biggest ruin on the Eagles' Nests Trail, and the first sight of it is a proper surprise: a long, ragged line of walls and towers sitting along a limestone ridge above the village of Podzamcze, with strange pale rocks poking out of the woods all around it. It looks like a film set, and in fact it has been used as one. The Jura upland here is full of such outcrops, which makes the whole hilltop seem to grow out of the stone.

  5. 5. Częstochowa

    For Poles, Częstochowa means one thing, the Jasna Góra monastery, and about a quarter of a million people live around it in a city that exists largely to serve that fact. The monastery stands on a limestone ridge, a fortified complex of walls, bastions and a tall baroque tower, and within it hangs the icon of the Black Madonna, a dark painted image of the Virgin and Child, with two scars across her cheek. Several million pilgrims arrive every year, many on foot, in groups that walk for days from every corner of the country.
