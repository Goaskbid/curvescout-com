Why this ride

Out of Papeete and back again: 105 km, roughly 1 h 42 in the saddle. On the way it passes Pointe Venus, Tiarei waterfalls, Teahupoo and Taravao. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Papeete

    Papeete is not the Tahiti of the posters, and that is rather its charm. It is a busy, slightly shabby, humid Pacific capital of some twenty-six thousand people, with another few hundred thousand in the sprawl around it on the north-west coast of the island, and it works hard at being a normal French town. Scooters weave through the traffic, bakeries sell baguettes at dawn, and the harbour is full of ferries heading for Moorea, which lies like a jagged green wall on the horizon.

  2. 2. Pointe Venus

    Pointe Venus is a low finger of black volcanic sand poking into the Pacific just north of Papeete, and it is the gentlest start you could ask for on a lap of Tahiti. The coast road, the RDO as locals call it, runs straight through Mahina, and a short side lane leads out past the casuarina trees to a car park by the beach. Arue and Pirae, the suburbs of the capital, are a few minutes behind you, so you arrive here still half in town traffic and with the whole island ahead.

  3. 3. Tiarei waterfalls

    Past Mahina the coast road swings away from the lagoon and starts to feel like proper east Tahiti, with the mountains standing straight up on your right and a roar of surf on your left. Tiarei sits in that long strip of narrow plain, and the waterfalls above it are the reason to pull over. They are the Faarumai falls, reached by a short drive inland from the main road and then a walk through dense, wet forest of tree ferns, hibiscus and tall mape trees. The falls drop from the high ridges that hold the rain coming in from the sea, so the water is rarely absent, and in the wet months from November to April they thunder.

  4. 4. Teahupoo

    The road to Teahupoo ends rather than finishes. You run down the south side of Tahiti Iti, the small round peninsula hanging off the main island like a second thought, through villages with names that sound like a drumbeat, and at the end the tarmac simply gives up at a river mouth and a patch of grass. Beyond is the bush, a footpath and then the cliffs; nobody drives around the far side.

  5. 5. Taravao

    Taravao is a neck of land, and you notice it the moment you arrive. Tahiti is two volcanoes joined by a narrow isthmus, a figure of eight lying on its side, and this is the waist. The coast road from Papeete splits here: one arm heads on around Tahiti Nui toward Teahupoo, the other climbs to the plateau and the viewpoint over the isthmus. For a rider it is the island's junction, supply point and breathing space all at once.
