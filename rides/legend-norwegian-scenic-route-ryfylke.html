Why this ride

Norwegian Scenic Route. fjords, waterfalls, mountains. 117 km from Forsand to the finish, about 2 h 08 of riding. On the way it passes Hjelmelandsvågen, Sand and Sauda. The highest point is about 1,250 m. The line favours views over speed.

  2. 2. Hjelmelandsvågen

    Hjelmelandsvågen is a small village on the shore of the Jøsenfjord, in the heart of Ryfylke, the fjord and mountain district northeast of Stavanger. It is the main settlement of Hjelmeland municipality, with a harbour, a ferry quay, a church, a few shops and a cluster of houses on the slope above the water, and it is a quiet place where the main event of the day is often the arrival of the ferry across the fjord to Nesvik.

  3. 3. Near Sand

    The Sandsfjord is one of the quieter arms of the great Boknafjord system, a long strip of dark water between steep, wooded mountains, and the village of Sand sits near its head where the Suldalslågen river comes down from the hills. Around here Ryfylke loses its coastal softness. The slopes get higher, the farms cling to whatever flat ground the ice left behind, and waterfalls hang off the cliffs after rain, which in this part of Norway means most days.

  4. 4. Sauda

    Sauda sits at the head of the Saudafjord, in a deep valley surrounded by high mountains, and it is a town built on industry rather than tourism. Hydropower was the foundation: the rivers that fall from the mountains drove turbines, and the power in turn fed smelters, which gave work to thousands in the twentieth century. The town's history is also one of mining. At Allmannajuvet, a gorge a short way outside the town, zinc was mined in the early twentieth century, and today the remains of the mine have been turned into a museum, with a striking modern building designed by the Swiss architect Peter Zumthor, part of the Norwegian scenic route programme. It is a rare place where the story of work is told with such care.

  5. 5. Norwegian Scenic Route Ryfylke point 5

    Røldal is a village that most riders pass through without realising how much sits behind the petrol-station-and-bakery impression. It lies on the western rim of the Hardangervidda plateau, beside a long lake, with the road to Haukeli climbing steeply out of its eastern edge. Odda lies to the north-west, the larger town for any serious errands, and Sauda is the other neighbour in the opposite direction.
