Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Brattleboro

    Brattleboro sits in the southeast corner of Vermont, on the Connecticut River, and it is a place with real character, a town of about seven thousand people that feels bigger because of its energy. It was the site of the first permanent English settlement in Vermont, at Fort Dummer, built in 1724 as a frontier defence against raids from the north. The town grew as a trading point on the river, and in the nineteenth century it became known for the Estey Organ Company, which at its peak was one of the largest producers of reed organs in the world, shipping instruments to parlours across America.

    Read the complete Brattleboro write-up.

  2. 2. Stockbridge

    The long Main Street runs between old maples and white porches, with the Red Lion Inn, a sprawling clapboard hotel with a rocking-chair porch, anchoring the middle. Norman Rockwell lived and worked here from the 1950s until he died in 1978, and his painting of this very street, wrapped in snow and Christmas lights, is among his best-loved pictures. The museum that carries his name sits on a hillside just outside the village.

  5. 5. Bennington

    Bennington is the southwestern gateway to Vermont, a college and craft town of around nine thousand, set in a valley between the Green Mountains and the Taconics. The big thing you see is the Bennington Battle Monument, a tall pale tower of dolomite finished in 1891 that stands on the hill above town and can be seen from miles away. It commemorates the 1777 battle, which was actually fought a few miles west across the line in New York, when American militia defeated a British detachment going after supplies. It is a rare monument you can go up inside, with a lift to the top, and the view across three states is wide.
