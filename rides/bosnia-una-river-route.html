Why this ride

148 km from Bihać to Drvar, about 2 h of riding. The stops in between: Strbacki Buk and Martin Brod. The highest point is about 1,000 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bihac

    Bihać sits on a bend of the Una River in the far north-west corner of Bosnia and Herzegovina, hemmed in by Croatia on three sides, and the river is the reason to come. The Una is a famously clear, turquoise-green water that tumbles over travertine steps and small waterfalls, and rafters and kayakers come from across Europe to run it. The town itself is a modest, friendly place with a pleasant promenade along the water, a handful of cafés, and a few relics of its long and complicated history.

  2. 2. Strbacki Buk

    The Una River in western Bosnia is one of the clearest rivers in Europe, an extraordinary emerald-green stream that runs through limestone country in a succession of pools, rapids and waterfalls. Štrbački Buk is the most dramatic of these, a broad staircase of cascades where the river drops over a wide barrier of travertine, the mineral deposit built up over thousands of years by the water itself. The roar is audible long before you arrive, and the spray cools the air.

  3. 3. Martin Brod

    Martin Brod is a quiet village at the upper end of the Una, close to where Bosnia meets Croatia, and it is the place where the river first shows what it can do. Here the Una flows through a short canyon, tumbles over a series of waterfalls and passes the remains of old watermills, built beside the water so that the stream could turn the stones. Some of the mills are still standing, with wooden wheels and moss-covered roofs, and a few are in use.

  4. 4. Drvar

    Drvar sits in a high green bowl of western Bosnia, circled by forested mountains and close to the Dinara range, which marks the border with Croatia. It is a small town, remote and quiet, and for a rider following the Una River route it is a place where the road runs out of towns for a while, so it makes sense to top up when you can. The larger centres are Bosansko Grahovo, about twenty kilometres away, and Sanica, though neither is large by European standards.
