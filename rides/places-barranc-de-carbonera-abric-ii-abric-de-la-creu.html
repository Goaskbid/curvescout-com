Why this ride

From Beniatjar it runs 71 km to Beniatjar; allow about 1 h 13 of riding. On the way it passes Barranc de mastec, Abric de la Creu and back through Barranc de mastec. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc de Carbonera- Abric II

    Between the mountain ranges of northern Alicante and the plains of Valencia lies the Vall d'Albaida, a quiet and little-visited district of small towns, vineyards and orchards, and the shelter at Barranc de Carbonera, Abric II, is on its edge. It lies about two and a half kilometres from Otos, a village of five hundred people, and close to Ráfol de Salem and Bèlgida, which are about the same size. It carries painting in the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain, and the ride links it to the Cova de la Catxupa, an even smaller site in the same area.

  2. 2. Barranc de mastec

    Between Cocentaina and Alcoy, in the hilly interior of Alicante province, a small ravine called the Barranc de Mastec holds rock shelters with prehistoric paintings, part of the UNESCO listing of rock art of the Mediterranean Basin. This corner of Spain is particularly rich in it, with styles ranging from the naturalistic Levantine figures of hunters and animals to the strange, abstract macro-schematic art that seems unique to these valleys. The ravine is surrounded by terraced olive and almond groves, pine woods and limestone crags.

  3. 3. Abric de la Creu

    Ontinyent and Bocairent sit close together in the southern part of Valencia province, in the upper valley of the Clariano river, surrounded by the mountains of the Serra de Mariola and the Serra Grossa. Ontinyent is a textile town with a fine old quarter on a hill above the river, crowned by the tall bell tower of the church of Santa Maria. Bocairent, a few kilometres away, is a medieval village perched on a rocky outcrop above a ravine. The Abric de la Creu, the shelter of the cross, lies in the hills between the two.
