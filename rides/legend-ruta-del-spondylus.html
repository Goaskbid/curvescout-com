Why this ride

curated scenic road. Pacific coast, beaches, fishing towns. From Guayaquil it runs 262 km to Manta; allow about 4 h 46 of riding. The stops in between: Salinas and Ruta del Spondylus. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Near Guayaquil

    Guayaquil is the biggest city in Ecuador, a hot, noisy and energetic port on the Guayas river, a few degrees south of the equator, and the place where a rider's journey up the coast begins. It is a commercial city, and proud of it. In the nineteenth century, cocoa made fortunes here, and the grand houses and the old customs building along the river recall that boom. The waterfront walk, the Malecón, runs for more than two kilometres along the river with gardens, fountains and a lighthouse, and on a warm evening it is full of families.

  2. 2. Salinas

    Salinas is where the Ecuadorian coast runs out of land. The town sits on the tip of the Santa Elena peninsula, on a dry, scrubby headland that sticks out into the Pacific west of Guayaquil, and it is the most polished beach resort in the country, with a curving seafront boulevard, a row of tall apartment blocks, a yacht club and a busy fishing harbour. It lies side by side with La Libertad and Santa Elena, which are bigger and much rougher, and the three towns blur into one built-up strip. For a rider this is the start of the Ruta del Spondylus, and the town makes a good place to prepare for the miles ahead.

  5. 5. Manta

    Manta is the busiest port on the coast of Ecuador, and a fishing town to its marrow. It lies on the western tip of a peninsula in the province of Manabí, and its harbour is crowded with fishing boats, tuna trawlers and cargo ships, so that the whole waterfront smells of salt, diesel and frying fish. Tuna canning has been the city's big business for decades, and the town sits on a coast where the cold Humboldt Current meets the warm tropical water, a meeting that makes the sea rich.
