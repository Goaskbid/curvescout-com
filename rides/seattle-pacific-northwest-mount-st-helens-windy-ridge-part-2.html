Why this ride

A 493 km one-way ride from near Sequim, about 10 h 07 of riding. On the way it passes Seattle / Pacific Northwest, near Gold Bar and Manchester. The highest point is about 1,600 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Photo stop near Sequim

    Sequim sits on the north side of the Olympic Peninsula, on a broad plain between the Olympic Mountains and the Strait of Juan de Fuca. It is famous for its climate: lying in the rain shadow of the Olympics, it receives far less rain than Seattle or the rainforests on the west side of the peninsula. That dry, sunny microclimate made it a farming centre, and today it is known for its lavender fields, which bloom in July and fill the air with fragrance.

  4. 4. Near Manchester

    Across Puget Sound from Seattle, on the eastern shore of the Kitsap Peninsula, the little community of Manchester looks back at the city skyline from a beach of pebbles and driftwood. On a clear evening the view is remarkable: the towers of downtown Seattle across the water, the Space Needle, ferries crossing in the dusk and, on a good day, Mount Rainier floating above it all. It is a quiet, unassuming place, with houses scattered along the shore and in the woods behind.

  5. 5. Back to Mount St

    Bonney Lake sits on a plateau east of Puget Sound, at the edge of the suburban sprawl that spreads out from Tacoma and Seattle, and on a clear day Mount Rainier fills the horizon as a vast white wall. The mountain, at over four thousand metres, is such a presence that it seems closer than it is, and locals check it each morning the way other people check the weather. It is a very large volcano, officially active, and the city is built in the path of its mudflows, a fact that the county takes seriously.
