Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Almaty

    Almaty means place of apples, or near enough, and the apples are not just a name. Wild apple forests, the ancestors of many of the world's cultivated varieties, still grow on the slopes of the Tian Shan mountains above the city, and botanists believe the fruit began its long journey to the world's orchards somewhere in this region. For most of the twentieth century Almaty was the capital of Kazakhstan, until the government moved to Astana in 1997, and it remains the country's biggest and most relaxed city, with wide tree-lined avenues, Soviet blocks and a skyline of snow peaks to the south.

  2. 2. Medeu

    Medeu is a big, white, somewhat surreal sports arena in a mountain valley. At about 1,700 metres above sea level, in the Zailiysky Alatau gorge just above Almaty, it holds one of the highest outdoor speed-skating rinks in the world, opened in the Soviet era in the early 1950s. Thin air and hard, fast ice made the rink famous, and many world records were set here over the decades. A long staircase climbs beside the arena, a good test for riders who have been sitting down all day.

  3. 3. Shymbulak

    Shymbulak is the ski resort Almaty keeps above itself like a spare room. The base sits at around 2,200 metres in the Zailiysky Alatau, reached by a steep road above Medeu or by a gondola lift, and from there chairlifts and cable cars carry skiers higher, to slopes at over three thousand metres. In winter it fills with skiers and snowboarders from across Kazakhstan; in summer it is quieter, with hikers, mountain bikers and people simply escaping the heat of the city.
