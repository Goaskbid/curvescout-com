Madagascar RN7: Ranomafana to Toliara

Why this ride

1155 km from Ifanadiana to Toliara, about 15 h 50 of riding. The stops in between: Fianarantsoa and Isalo. The highest point is about 1,250 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ranomafana

    Ranomafana means hot water in Malagasy, and the name is a clue to the place. The village sits in a steep, forested valley on the escarpment that drops from Madagascar's central highlands toward the east coast, where the Namorona river drops over rapids and hot springs seep from the rock. The surrounding national park, created in 1991, protects some of the island's most important rainforest, and in 2007 it was added to a UNESCO listing of the rainforests of the Atsinanana. Mist hangs in the trees, orchids cling to the branches, and the air is warm and damp.

  2. 2. Fianarantsoa

    Fianarantsoa sits in the middle of Madagascar's southern highlands, and the name is usually translated as the place where one learns what is good. It is the capital of the Betsileo people, a population of rice farmers whose terraced hillsides you will have been riding through for the last hundred kilometres, and the old upper town, perched on a ridge above the modern one, still has the stone houses and church spires that the nineteenth century left behind. About 167,000 people live here, which makes it one of the larger cities on the RN7, and it is the proper stop for fuel, cash and a real meal before the road runs south toward the drier country.

  3. 3. Isalo

    Isalo is the end of the long southbound haul down the RN7, and the landscape changes abruptly to mark it. After the green terraces of the highlands the road enters a country of dry grass, scattered palms and then, suddenly, a sandstone massif that rises like a ruined wall out of the plain. The Isalo range is a mass of Jurassic sandstone that wind and rain have carved into canyons, buttresses and bare orange ridges, with narrow green oases hidden between them where water still runs all year.

  4. 4. Toliara

    The RN7 ends at Toliara, a flat, sandy, sun-struck town on the south-west coast of Madagascar, where the highlands have been left far behind and the land is dry, thorny and bright. The town, sometimes still called Tulear, has about a hundred and fifteen thousand people, and it sits on the Mozambique Channel with wide streets, dusty pavements, handcarts and the pousse-pousse rickshaws that carry people everywhere. Nights are warm, days are hot and the sea breeze is a mercy.
