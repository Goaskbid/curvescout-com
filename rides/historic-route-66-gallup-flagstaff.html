Historic Route 66: Gallup to Flagstaff

Why this ride

A one-way ride of 704 km, Gallup to Flagstaff - roughly 9 h 42 in the saddle. It goes by way of Holbrook and Winslow. The highest point is about 2,350 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Gallup

    Gallup is a long, thin town squeezed between red sandstone cliffs and the railroad tracks, on the old line of Route 66 in northwestern New Mexico. It sits at about 2,000 metres, with a dry, high-desert climate and a landscape of mesas and pale grassland, and it is known as the Indian Capital of the World, since it serves as a trading centre for the Navajo, Zuni and Hopi nations. Galleries and trading posts in town sell turquoise jewellery, woven rugs and pottery, and the quality of the work is high, though prices reflect that.

  2. 2. Holbrook

    Holbrook is a small town on Interstate 40 in northern Arizona, a place that still looks like the postcard for old Route 66. About five thousand people live here, among sunbaked streets, neon signs and dusty sidewalks. The Wigwam Motel, a ring of concrete teepees with vintage cars parked outside, opened in the 1950s and is a favourite photo stop. Across the street, curio shops sell turquoise jewellery and rubber tomahawks, and the diners offer big breakfasts and tall milkshakes.

  3. 3. Winslow

    Winslow, Arizona, is famous for a song. In 1972, the Eagles released Take It Easy, with the line about standing on a corner in Winslow, Arizona, and today a small park on the main street has a statue and a mural, and tourists line up to take their picture on the corner. The town sits on the old Route 66, on the high plateau of northern Arizona, beside the Little Colorado River, with a population of about nine and a half thousand, and a long railway history that shaped it.

  4. 4. Flagstaff

    Flagstaff stands at about 2,100 metres, higher than most Alpine villages, and it feels it. The air is thin and dry, the sky is a deep blue, and the town is wrapped in the largest ponderosa pine forest in North America, with the San Francisco Peaks rising to the north. The tallest of them, Humphreys Peak, is 3,851 metres, the highest point in Arizona, and it holds snow into early summer. The peaks are volcanic, the remains of a huge cone that blew out long ago, and they are sacred to many Native nations, including the Hopi and Navajo.
