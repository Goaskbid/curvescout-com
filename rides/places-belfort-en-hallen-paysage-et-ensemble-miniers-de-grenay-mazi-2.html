Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Belfort en Hallen

    Bruges grew rich in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries as a cloth and trading port, and the belfry on its market square is the proudest sign of that wealth. Rising about eighty-three metres above the Markt, with an octagonal upper stage, few towers in Belgium are better known. The Hallen, the old market halls at its base, once held the town's cloth trade, and the belfry stored the town's charters and its bells, so that merchants and citizens could be called together and warned of fire or attack. It is also a part of the group of belfries of Belgium and France on the World Heritage list.

  2. 2. Belfort met Lakenhal

    Every evening at eight o'clock, traffic stops under the Menin Gate in Ieper and buglers of the local fire brigade play the Last Post for the soldiers of the British Empire who died in the Ypres Salient and have no known grave. The ceremony has been held almost every evening since 1928, interrupted only during the German occupation in the Second World War. Walk from the gate to the Grote Markt and you come to the Cloth Hall and its belfry, a huge Gothic building that dominated medieval Flanders, when Ypres was one of the great cloth towns of Europe.

  3. 3. Paysage et ensemble miniers de Grenay-Mazi

    Take any road across the Artois plain around Grenay and you will see the shape of the mining basin on the skyline: conical slag heaps, called terrils, rising out of the farmland and cités, and tall brick pit-head buildings that once housed the winding gear. This area lies between Lens and Béthune, in a district called the Gohelle, and it was mined intensively from the nineteenth century until the last pit closed in 1990. The Grenay and Mazingarbe sites are part of the UNESCO-listed mining basin, which recognises both the buildings and the landscape, and the way company housing, railways and spoil heaps shaped one another.
