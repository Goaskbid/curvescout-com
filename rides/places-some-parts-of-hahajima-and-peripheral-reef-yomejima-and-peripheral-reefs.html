Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Some parts of Hahajima and peripheral reef

    The Ogasawara Islands lie about a thousand kilometres south of Tokyo, in the Pacific, and getting there takes a full day on a ferry, which is a good start to understanding why they have remained so special. The Bonin Islands, as they are also known, have never been connected to a continent, and as a result the plants and animals that reached them evolved in isolation, giving rise to a wealth of species found nowhere else. UNESCO listed the islands in 2011, and they are often called the Galapagos of the East.

  2. 2. Anejima and peripheral reefs

    Anejima is a speck of an island in the Ogasawara group, the Bonin Islands, scattered in the Pacific about a thousand kilometres south of Tokyo. Nobody rides a motorcycle to it. The only practical way to reach the group is the ferry from Tokyo to Chichijima, a trip of roughly a day, and the smaller islands around Chichijima are reached by boat or not at all. So this stop is really about the scenery of a reef and the story of the place, and if you are planning a ride here, plan it around Chichijima's little roads.

  3. 3. Minamijima and peripheral reefs

    A thousand kilometres south of Tokyo, in the Pacific Ocean, the Ogasawara Islands rise from the sea, a scattered chain of volcanic islands that have never been connected to a continent. Minamijima, a small uninhabited island just off Chichijima, is one of their jewels: a ring of jagged limestone around a sheltered cove, the Ogiura, with white sand, turquoise water and a sinkhole beach that looks like something from a dream. The island and its reefs are part of the Ogasawara Islands World Heritage site, listed by UNESCO in 2011.

  4. 4. Ototojima and peripheral reefs

    Let us be honest from the start: you will not ride a motorcycle on Ototojima. This small, uninhabited island lies in the Ogasawara archipelago, about a thousand kilometres south of Tokyo in the western Pacific, and it has no roads, no people and strict rules about who can land. What it does have is a remarkable natural world. The Ogasawara Islands, also known as the Bonin Islands, have never been connected to a continent, and their plants and animals evolved in isolation, producing many species found nowhere else. The archipelago was listed by UNESCO in 2011, and Ototojima is one of its most precious parts.
