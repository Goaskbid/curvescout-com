Why this ride

From Ljubljana it runs 253 km to Ljubljana; allow about 4 h 22 of riding. It goes by way of Vojsko - Idrijca Water Barrier, City Fortress of Palmanova and back through Vojsko - Idrijca Water Barrier. The highest point is about 900 m. It crosses from Slovenia into Italy - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. The works of Jože Plečnik in Ljubljana - H

    Joze Plecnik is the architect who gave Ljubljana its face. Born in the city in 1872, he trained in Vienna and worked in Prague, where he remodelled the castle for the new Czechoslovak republic, and in 1921 he returned to his home town as a professor. Over the next three decades he reshaped the capital with bridges, markets, squares, colonnades and a library, and he did it with a classical sensibility that mixed Greek and Roman forms with local materials and an unmistakable wit. His works in Ljubljana are on the UNESCO list.

  2. 2. Vojsko - Idrijca Water Barrier

    The water barriers of the Idrijca are among the strangest industrial monuments in Europe: massive stone and timber dams built across a narrow mountain river not to store water for drinking or power, but to float logs. Locals call them klavže, and the biggest have been nicknamed the Slovenian pyramids for their huge, carefully cut blocks. They lie in the deep, wooded gorge below the Vojsko plateau, a short ride from the town of Idrija.

  3. 3. City Fortress of Palmanova

    From the air Palmanova is a perfect nine-pointed star, and even from the saddle you can feel the geometry. The roads come in through monumental gates, cross the moats and ramparts, and run dead straight to a vast hexagonal piazza in the middle, where the whole town seems to stop and look at itself. It is one of the purest examples of an ideal Renaissance city ever built, and it sits on the flat Friulian plain between Udine and the Adriatic like a diagram that somebody forgot to take back.
