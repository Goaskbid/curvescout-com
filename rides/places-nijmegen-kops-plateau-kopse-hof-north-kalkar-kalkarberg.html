Why this ride

From Hengstdal it runs 183 km to Hengstdal; allow about 3 h 09 of riding. The stops in between: Till, Fort Pannerden, Kalkar-Kalkarberg and back through Fort Pannerden. It crosses from Netherlands into Germany - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Nijmegen-Kops Plateau | Kopse Hof North

    Kopse Hof is the name given to this northern corner of the plateau, and the first thing to say is that the Roman layer is under your feet, not in front of you. For the last few decades Dutch archaeologists have dug across this ridge ahead of every new street and housing block, and what they found was astonishing: ditches, postholes, the outlines of timber barracks, pits and graves, pottery, coins, tools and shoe soles preserved in the wet. A city sits on top of a legionary camp, and the modern streets of Nijmegen run over the old Roman roads.

  2. 2. Till

    The Lower Rhine around Kalkar and Kleve is wide, flat country: green polders, willows along drainage ditches, brick farmhouses and church spires visible for miles. Till is a small village here, part of Bedburg-Hau, just off the Rhine embankments between Kalkar and Kleve, with Emmerich across the river. It lies close to the line of the Roman frontier, the Lower German Limes, which followed the Rhine to the North Sea.

  3. 3. Fort Pannerden

    Right where the Rhine enters the Netherlands and splits in two, the Dutch put a fort. Fort Pannerden was built around 1870 at the point where the river divides into the Waal, flowing west toward Nijmegen and Rotterdam, and the Pannerdens Kanaal, which feeds the Nederrijn and the IJssel. Control the split, and you control how much water goes where, which in a country defended by deliberate flooding mattered enormously. The fort, a low, heavy brick and earth structure surrounded by a moat, guarded the dykes and sluices that could send water into the defensive lines further west. It is counted among the Dutch Water Defence Lines listed by UNESCO.

  4. 4. Kalkar-Kalkarberg

    Near the Dutch border, where the Rhine flows through wide meadows and dyked farmland, the small town of Kalkar holds a surprising amount of history. The Kalkarberg is a low rise near Altkalkar, and on it stood the Roman fort of Burginatium, one of the posts along the Lower German Limes, now part of the UNESCO-listed Frontiers of the Roman Empire. The rise would have given a valuable view over the river plain, and the fort controlled the road between Xanten and Nijmegen. Today it is fields and trees, but the shape of the land still hints at the old fortification.
