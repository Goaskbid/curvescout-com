Why this ride

A 162 km round trip from Zalău - roughly 4 h in the saddle. It goes by way of Treznea-Coasta Ciungii 2, Hurez - Între Dealul Mare și Arsură and back through Treznea-Coasta Ciungii 2.

The roads

Porolissum → Treznea-Coasta Ciungii 2

It is 38 km from Porolissum to Treznea-Coasta Ciungii 2, mostly on DC73. It climbs 470 m to the top at 553 m and drops 330 m again. Water alongside for much of it - pick one good pull-out and stop.

The stops

  1. 1. Porolissum

    Start here.

    On a hill above the village of Moigrad, the Roman town of Porolissum guarded a key pass through the Meseș mountains, the gateway to Roman Dacia from the north-west. Founded as a military camp after the conquest in 106, it grew into a town of thousands, with a large fort, an amphitheatre, temples, baths and a customs post, and it became a municipium, a self-governing city, under the emperor Septimius Severus. The ruins are among the most impressive Roman remains in Romania, with a reconstructed fort gate, walls and parts of the town, and they are part of the frontier sites of Roman Dacia.

    Read the complete Porolissum write-up.

  2. 2. Treznea-Coasta Ciungii 2

    38 km in.

    Treznea is a small village in the hills of Sălaj county, eleven kilometres east of Zalău, and its name carries a heavy weight in Romanian history. In September 1940, after northern Transylvania was transferred to Hungary by the Second Vienna Award, Hungarian troops entering the region killed scores of villagers here, Romanians and Jews, in one of the worst atrocities of that time. A memorial in the village remembers the victims. It is a sobering reminder that these quiet hills have seen terrible violence, and a short, respectful stop at the memorial is worth more than any viewpoint on the ride.

    Read the complete Treznea-Coasta Ciungii 2 write-up.

  3. 3. Hurez - Între Dealul Mare și Arsură

    81 km in.

    Somewhere between Dealul Mare and Arsură, two wooded hills above the village of Hurez in Sălaj county, the Roman frontier of Dacia ran along the heights, marked by watchtowers and small earthworks that are now almost hidden by forest. Together with the towers further north on the Meseș ridge, they formed a line that looked west towards the lands beyond the empire, and they were included in the World Heritage listing of the Dacian frontier in 2024. Finding the remains requires a map and some determination; the reward is a sense of how thinly Rome stretched its soldiers across these hills.
