Why this ride

curated scenic road. lake, waterfalls, Bernese Alps. A one-way ride of 34 km, Thun to Lauterbrunnen - roughly 37 min in the saddle. The stops in between: Spiez and Interlaken. The highest point is about 2,150 m. The line favours views over speed.

Slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did. Water arrives beside the road and stays for 9 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Thun

    Thun is a town that looks as if someone designed it for a postcard and then forgot to charge for it. The Aare river flows out of the lake through the middle of town in a fast, clear, turquoise channel, past wooden covered bridges and old mills, and above it all stands the castle, a twelfth-century keep with four corner turrets built by the Zähringen dukes, its tall tower visible from many angles. At the foot of the castle hill is the Obere Hauptgasse, a street with raised pavements above the shops, which gives the town centre a quirky, two-level look. With over forty thousand people, Thun has all the services you could want.

  2. 2. Spiez

    Spiez occupies a promontory on the south shore of Lake Thun, and it has the kind of position that makes you think the founders knew exactly what they were doing. A castle stands on a spur above the water, with a Romanesque church beside it, vineyards on the slope behind, and a bay below with sailing boats, a harbour and a lakeside promenade. Beyond the lake, the pyramid of the Niesen rises to the south, and the great white mass of the Bernese Alps fills the horizon to the east.

  3. 3. Interlaken

    Interlaken means between the lakes, which is exactly where it sits: a flat strip of land on the Aare, wedged between Lake Thun and Lake Brienz, with the Bernese Oberland piled up behind. On a clear day the Jungfrau fills the end of the main avenue, and visitors line up to take pictures of that view across the green lawn of the Höhematte, where paragliders land.

  4. 4. Lauterbrunnen

    The first thing about Lauterbrunnen is the walls. The valley floor is flat and green, a little under a kilometre wide, and on both sides the limestone rises almost vertically for several hundred metres, as if a giant had cut the mountain with a spoon. That is what a glacier does, and this one did it thoroughly. Water falls off the cliffs in dozens of places, which gives the valley its name, meaning loud fountains, and there are said to be some seventy-two waterfalls within its length.
