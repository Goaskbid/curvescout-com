Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Haifa Bahá’í Cemetery

    Mount Carmel rises straight from the Mediterranean, and Haifa climbs its slope in tiers, the port at the bottom, the German Colony halfway up, and the leafy residential districts along the crest. On the hillside stands the golden-domed Shrine of the Báb, surrounded by terraced gardens of nineteen levels, the best-known sight in the city and the centre of the Bahá'í faith. The cemetery on Carmel, in quieter gardens nearby, holds the graves of members of the family of Bahá'u'lláh, and is a place of stillness among cypress and lawn. The Bahá'í holy places in Haifa and western Galilee are listed by UNESCO.

  3. 3. Necropolis of Bet She’arim: A Landmark of

    In the second century AD, after the failed revolts against Rome, the centre of Jewish life moved to Galilee, and the Sanhedrin, the supreme rabbinical council, settled for a time at Bet She'arim, on a hill at the western edge of the Jezreel valley. Rabbi Judah ha-Nasi, the patriarch who compiled the Mishnah, the first great codification of Jewish law, lived here, and when he was buried in the necropolis, Jews from all over the diaspora wanted to be buried near him. The result is a vast network of catacombs carved into the soft limestone, listed by UNESCO in 2015.
