Why this ride

From Oviedo it runs 709 km to Oviedo; allow about 15 h 23 of riding. On the way it passes Tito Bustillo, Hayedos de Picos de Europa - Cuesta Fria, Covaciella and Hayedos de Picos de Europa - Canal de Asot. It tops out around 1,350 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

The stops

  1. 1. Oviedo

    Start here.

    Oviedo is a small and handsome capital that sits a few valleys back from the sea, in a green country that the Spanish call the natural paradise. The old kingdom of Asturias was born here, in the eighth century, when a little group of Christian nobles held out in the mountains against the Muslim armies that had swept up the peninsula. The kings who followed, Alfonso II and Ramiro I among them, built churches on the hills above the city in a unique style that art historians call Asturian pre-Romanesque. Santa María del Naranco and San Miguel de Lillo, both from the ninth century, are among the oldest and most graceful of them, and UNESCO has listed them together with other Asturian monuments.

    Read the complete Oviedo write-up.

  2. 2. Tito Bustillo

    80 km in.

    In 1968, a group of young cavers from Ribadesella found their way into a cave system beneath the Ardines hill, just outside the town, and stumbled upon an extraordinary gallery of Palaeolithic art: horses, deer and reindeer, painted in red and black, along with strange red symbols that some interpret as female figures. One of the group, Celestino Fernández Bustillo, known as Tito, died in a mountain accident a few days later, and the cave was named in his honour. Tito Bustillo is one of the most important painted caves in Europe, and it is part of the UNESCO listing of Cave of Altamira and Palaeolithic Cave Art of Northern Spain. Visits are limited in number and season to protect the art, and booking in advance is essential.

    Read the complete Tito Bustillo write-up.

  3. 3. Hayedos de Picos de Europa - Cuesta Fria

    170 km in.

    The beech forests of Cuesta Fría cling to the north-facing slopes of the Valdeón valley, on the León side of the Picos de Europa, where the limestone mountains rise abruptly above the green of the Cantabrian coast. They are part of a group of old beech woods across Europe that UNESCO recognised for their natural value, because the trees here have grown with little human interference for a very long time. Posada de Valdeón, a village of a few hundred people, lies about five kilometres away.

    Read the complete Hayedos de Picos de Europa - Cuesta Fria write-up.

  4. 4. Covaciella

    261 km in.

    Between the coastal town of Llanes and the high limestone walls of the Picos de Europa, a small cave near Carreña de Cabrales holds images that have been in the dark for something like fifteen thousand years. Covaciella was only found in the 1990s, and its paintings of bison, horses and deer, in black and red, are among the finest examples of Palaeolithic art in Asturias. It was added to the UNESCO listing of the Cave of Altamira and Paleolithic Cave Art of Northern Spain in 2008. The cave is closed to the public to protect the paintings, but the nearby interpretation centre explains what is inside and why it matters.

    Read the complete Covaciella write-up.

  5. 5. Hayedos de Picos de Europa - Canal de Asot

    349 km in.

    Valdeón is the valley people forget to mention when they talk about the Picos de Europa, and that is a mistake. On the southern side of the range, in the province of León, it is a deep green bowl ringed by limestone peaks, with the village of Posada de Valdeón at its heart. The beech forests on its slopes, including those in the side valley known as the Canal de Asotín, are part of the UNESCO site of the Ancient and Primeval Beech Forests of the Carpathians and Other Regions of Europe, a transnational listing that grew to include several forests in Spain. Walking in them in autumn, when the leaves turn copper and gold and the ground is soft with moss, is unforgettable.

    Read the complete Hayedos de Picos de Europa - Canal de Asot write-up.
