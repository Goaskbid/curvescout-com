Why this ride

358 km from Old City to Old City, about 5 h 37 of riding. It goes by way of Ancient Jericho/Tell es-Sultan, As-Salt - The Place of Tolerance and Urban, Um er-Rasas and back through As-Salt - The Place of Tolerance and Urban. It crosses from Palestinian Territories into Jordan - carry ID and check the toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Old City of Jerusalem and its Walls

    Start here.

    Few square kilometres on earth carry as much weight as these. The Old City of Jerusalem holds sites sacred to Judaism, Christianity and Islam, packed into a maze of stone lanes inside walls built by the Ottoman sultan Suleiman the Magnificent in the 1530s. The Western Wall, the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, the Dome of the Rock and the al-Aqsa Mosque lie within a few minutes' walk of each other, and the city's four traditional quarters, Jewish, Christian, Muslim and Armenian, each have their own character, markets and rhythms. The Old City and its walls were added to the UNESCO list in 1981.

    Read the complete Old City of Jerusalem and its Walls write-up.

  2. 2. Ancient Jericho/Tell es-Sultan

    35 km in.

    Jericho is one of the oldest continuously inhabited towns on earth, and at Tell es-Sultan, on its northern edge, archaeologists have dug down through layers of settlement more than ten thousand years old. Among the finds is a great stone tower, built in the Neolithic, around eleven thousand years ago, long before writing, metal or the wheel, which is often called the oldest such tower in the world. The tell, with its trenches and mud brick walls, was listed as a World Heritage Site in 2023.

    Read the complete Ancient Jericho/Tell es-Sultan write-up.

  3. 3. As-Salt - The Place of Tolerance and Urban

    80 km in.

    As-Salt climbs three steep hills in the Balqa highlands north-west of Amman, a town of honey-coloured limestone houses with arched windows, terraced streets and long staircases. In the late Ottoman period, from the 1860s to the 1920s, it was the most important town in the region east of the Jordan, a trading hub where merchants from Nablus, Damascus and Beirut settled and built grand houses in a mix of local and European styles. Muslims and Christians lived side by side, sharing guesthouses and charitable traditions, and the town was inscribed on the UNESCO list in 2021 as As-Salt, the Place of Tolerance and Urban Hospitality.

    Read the complete As-Salt - The Place of Tolerance and Urban write-up.

  4. 4. Um er-Rasas

    177 km in.

    The most surprising thing at Umm ar-Rasas is a tower. It stands alone in the fields about a kilometre north of the old town, a tall square column of stone, about fifteen metres high, with no door and a small chamber at the top, and it was built for a stylite, one of those early Christian ascetics who lived on top of pillars to be closer to heaven and further from the world. Stylites became famous in Syria in the fifth century, and this tower, which dates from the Byzantine period, is one of very few that survive in the region, and it may have been used as late as the ninth century. A monk lived in that little room, and pilgrims came to see him.
