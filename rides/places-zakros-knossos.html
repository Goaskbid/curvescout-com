Why this ride

278 km from Palekastro to Palekastro, about 4 h 48 of riding. It goes by way of Malia, Knossos and back through Malia. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Zakros

    The far east of Crete runs out of road at Zakros, and that is exactly its charm. The village sits on the very edge of the island, below the Sitia mountains, and the way here from Sitia, about nineteen kilometres, leaves the tourist belt behind and climbs through olive terraces and rocky hills that smell of thyme and dust.

  2. 2. Malia

    Malia is two things in Crete at once: a beach resort known for loud nights, and the site of a Minoan palace that is nearly four thousand years old. The first palace was built around 1900 BC, destroyed by an earthquake or invaders, rebuilt about 1650 BC and then destroyed again around 1450 BC, the same wave of disasters that ended Minoan power across the island. It was excavated by French archaeologists from the 1920s, after a local scholar, Joseph Hatzidakis, had begun work in 1915. It is the third in size of Crete's great palaces, after Knossos and Phaistos.

  3. 3. Knossos

    Five kilometres south of Heraklion, on a low hill above the Kairatos stream, lie the ruins that gave Europe its oldest legend of a labyrinth. Knossos was the greatest centre of Minoan Crete, a sprawling palace complex of courtyards, storerooms, staircases and shrines that flourished in the second millennium BC. Its confusing plan, with hundreds of interlocking rooms on several levels, may well have inspired the Greek myth of King Minos, the craftsman Daedalus and the Minotaur imprisoned in the maze, waiting for Theseus.
