Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Lysebotn

    Lysebotn lies at the inner end of the Lysefjord, a long, narrow fjord in Rogaland, and it is at the bottom of one of the most famous hairpin roads in Europe. The fjord is about forty kilometres long, with cliffs rising nearly a thousand metres on each side, and the village is tiny, with a few houses, a harbour and a hotel. Jørpeland, thirty-five kilometres away by water, is the nearest sizeable settlement, and there are no pumps in Lysebotn, so arrive with a full tank. Ferries from Stavanger call here in summer.

  2. 2. Lysevegen hairpins

    The Lysevegen climbs out of the Lysefjord's inner end at Lysebotn, and it does so in twenty-seven hairpins, one after another, with a short spiral tunnel thrown in near the top for good measure. The bends are tight, the gradient is steep, and the walls are rock on one side and a long drop on the other, and from the bottom you can look up and see the road stacked in zigzags on the cliff. It was completed in the 1980s and, with its blend of engineering and wild country, has become one of the best-known mountain roads in Norway. Riders who like switchbacks arrive here as if on a pilgrimage.

  3. 3. Oygardstol

    Øygardstøl is a small mountain station at the top of the Lysevegen, on a bare plateau about six hundred metres above the sea, and its main purpose is to look at what you have just climbed. A café and a car park stand at the rim, and from the terrace the view drops straight down into the Lysefjord, with the road you rode zigzagging below and the water a long, dark, narrow strip far beneath. On a clear day it is striking. When cloud fills the fjord you look down into milk, with only the sound of falling water to tell you what is below.

  4. 4. Sirdal highlands

    The Sirdal highlands are the high plateau above the Lysefjord, a vast, treeless upland of granite, lakes and bog at about 900 metres above sea level. When you come over the top of the Lysevegen hairpins, the landscape changes completely: instead of steep fjord walls, you find a wide, open tableland, with low hills, scattered boulders and shining water. The vegetation is heather, dwarf birch and lichen, and the weather feels more like the Arctic than the south coast. There are no towns here, only farms and summer cabins, and the nearest services are some distance away.
