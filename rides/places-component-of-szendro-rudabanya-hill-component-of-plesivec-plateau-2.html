Why this ride

A 86 km round trip from Rudabánya - roughly 1 h 29 in the saddle. On the way it passes Caves of Aggtelek Karst and Slovak Karst, Component of Plešivec plateau and back through Caves of Aggtelek Karst and Slovak Karst. It crosses from Hungary into Slovakia - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. Open plateau country, wide and exposed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Component of Szendrő-Rudabánya Hill

    The Szendrő-Rudabánya hills are a modest ridge of low wooded hills in northeastern Hungary, between the Bódva and Sajó valleys, and they are not on any tourist itinerary. That is a large part of their charm. This is the quiet borderland near Slovakia, with small villages, orchards, vineyards and forests of oak and hornbeam, where the roads are empty and the pace slows to that of a cart.

  2. 2. Caves of Aggtelek Karst and Slovak Karst

    Under the hills on the border of Hungary and Slovakia lies one of the largest cave systems in Europe. The Baradla cave, entered from Aggtelek on the Hungarian side and from Domica on the Slovak side, runs for some twenty-five kilometres through limestone, a gallery of stalactites, stalagmites and underground rivers, and parts of it are open to visitors. It is the heart of the Aggtelek and Slovak Karst, a cross-border UNESCO site of more than seven hundred caves, listed in 1995 and extended in 2000. Concerts are held in the largest chamber, where the acoustics are excellent, and the temperature underground stays at a constant cool level, a relief in summer.

  3. 3. Component of Plešivec plateau

    The Slovak Karst is a world of limestone plateaus and deep gorges in southern Slovakia, along the Hungarian border, and the Plešivec plateau is one of its great tablelands, a high, flat-topped block of rock rising steeply above the valleys of the Slaná and the Štítnik rivers. Its surface is a rolling steppe of grass and scrub, pitted with sinkholes, and beneath it lies a maze of caves and abysses, part of the UNESCO site of the Caves of Aggtelek Karst and Slovak Karst.
