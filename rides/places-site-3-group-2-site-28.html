Why this ride

From Muang Phônsavan it runs 190 km to Muang Phônsavan; allow about 3 h 52 of riding. The stops in between: Site 21, Site 28 and back through Site 21. It tops out around 1,250 m. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Site 3 - Group 2

    Start here.

    Group 2 gives you the sense of how the jars sit within working farmland. The cluster lies near a village, with fields running right up to the edge of the cleared path, and as you walk you pass rice stubble, a small irrigation channel and perhaps a woman carrying a load of firewood. The jars here are of medium size, some pushed over, some partly buried, and they are mixed with outcrops of natural rock. It all looks quite casual, as if the landscape had not noticed it was famous.

    Read the complete Site 3 - Group 2 write-up.

  2. 2. Site 21

    42 km in.

    The Plain of Jars is one of Southeast Asia's great riddles: across the Xieng Khouang plateau in northern Laos, thousands of huge stone jars lie scattered over hillsides, in groups of a few or a few hundred, some taller than a person. They were carved from sandstone and other rock during the Iron Age, roughly between 500 BC and AD 500, and the most widely accepted theory is that they were linked to funerary practices, perhaps used to let bodies decompose before final burial. Local legend has a happier explanation: the jars were made to brew rice wine for a victory celebration of a mythical king. The sites were added to the World Heritage list in 2019, and Site 21 is one of the many numbered groups in the countryside around Phonsavan.

    Read the complete Site 21 write-up.

  3. 3. Site 28

    87 km in.

    The Plain of Jars is one of the great archaeological puzzles of Southeast Asia: hundreds of carved stone jars, some taller than a person and weighing several tonnes, scattered across hillsides and meadows of the Xieng Khouang plateau in northern Laos.
