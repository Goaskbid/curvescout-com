Why this ride

A 162 km round trip from Moratalla - roughly 2 h 48 in the saddle. On the way it passes Cavidad V, Arroyo de la fuente de las Zorras, Letreros de los mártires and back through Arroyo de la fuente de las Zorras. The highest point is about 1,400 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. La Risca II

    Some of the best meals in the Segura uplands are served in places that have no sign. A bar in a Murcian hill village will put out a plate of migas, the fried breadcrumbs with garlic and chorizo that shepherds have eaten for centuries, or a stew of lamb with potatoes and peppers, and it will be a great deal better than the price suggests. La Risca, in the municipality of Moratalla, is a hamlet of scattered farms twelve kilometres from the town, and the shelter lies among the fields in a pocket of the hills, where the cortijos, the old farmhouses, stand in clumps of almond and olive.

  2. 2. Cavidad V

    Nerpio is about as far from anywhere as you can get in mainland Spain. This village of around sixteen hundred people sits in the Sierra del Segura, in the far south-west of Albacete province, close to the borders of Murcia and Andalusia, and the surrounding hills are dotted with shelters containing prehistoric paintings. The numbered sites around here, Cavidad V among them, are part of the UNESCO-listed Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin, and the Nerpio area is one of the richest concentrations of Levantine art in Spain, with hunting scenes, archers and animals painted thousands of years ago.

  3. 3. Arroyo de la fuente de las Zorras

    The name means the stream of the foxes' spring, which is the kind of place name that only survives in country where people still walk. The ravines around Nerpio hold dozens of painted rock shelters, with Levantine and Schematic paintings of hunters, animals and symbols, and they form part of the UNESCO listing of rock art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula. Visits to many of them need to be arranged through the local interpretation centre.

  4. 4. Letreros de los mártires

    Out in the high north-east of Granada province, Huéscar feels a long way from the Alhambra and the beaches of the Costa Tropical. This is the altiplano, a broad upland of wheat fields, almond trees and pale badlands at around a thousand metres, ringed by mountains, with the great limestone hump of La Sagra rising to the north. The rock shelter called Letreros de los Mártires lies in this country, a dozen or so kilometres from the town.
