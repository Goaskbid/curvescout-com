Why this ride

378 km from Hermigua to Hermigua, about 6 h 31 of riding. It goes by way of Teide National Park, San Cristóbal de La Laguna, back through San Cristóbal de La Laguna and back through Teide National Park. The highest point is about 1,100 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Garajonay National Park

    Mist is the main character in this forest. On La Gomera, one of the smallest of the Canary Islands, the trade winds blow clouds against the mountains in the centre of the island, and the trees comb the moisture out of the air so effectively that a damp forest stays damp all year. The Garajonay National Park, which covers the high ground at around 1,400 metres, is one of the best remaining stands of laurisilva, a type of subtropical cloud forest that once covered much of southern Europe before the ice ages. Moss hangs from the branches, lichen covers the trunks, and the light has a green, underwater quality. UNESCO listed the park in 1986.

  2. 2. Teide National Park

    Spain's highest mountain is a volcano standing in the middle of an island in the Atlantic, and that alone makes Teide worth the ride. It rises to 3,715 metres above sea level, and from the ocean floor it is one of the tallest volcanic structures on Earth. The national park around it, listed by UNESCO in 2007, is a high desert of pumice, lava and strange rock towers, set in an ancient caldera, the Cañadas, and the road across it is among the most extraordinary on any motorcycle map. The Roques de García, a cluster of twisted, wind-carved pillars, stand in front of the mountain like a stage set.

  3. 3. San Cristóbal de La Laguna

    La Laguna was the first Spanish colonial town not built behind walls, and its plan, a grid of straight streets around open squares, became a model for towns in the Americas. It was founded in the late fifteenth century, after the Spanish conquest of Tenerife, and for centuries it was the island's capital, with a cathedral, convents, a university from the eighteenth century, and tall houses with carved wooden balconies. UNESCO listed the old town in 1999. The city is cool and often misty, because it sits at about five hundred metres on a plateau where the trade-wind clouds gather, and the fine drizzle that drifts in from the north-east is a local fixture.
