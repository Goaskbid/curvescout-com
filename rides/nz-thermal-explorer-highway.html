  1. 1. Hamilton

    Hamilton straddles the Waikato River, New Zealand's longest, in the middle of some of the richest dairy country on earth. It is the country's fourth-largest city, a university and agricultural research town, and it is often treated by passing travellers as somewhere to get through on the way to somewhere else. That is a little unfair, but on a ride to Taupō it is mostly what it is: a place to fill the tank, eat something and get back on the road.

  2. 2. Rotorua

    Rotorua smells of eggs, and everyone is soon used to it. The city sits in the crater of an ancient volcano, on the shore of a lake, and the ground beneath it is alive: steam rises from roadside drains, mud pools bubble in parks, and in the thermal reserve of Te Puia the Pohutu geyser erupts many times a day, throwing a column of boiling water twenty metres or more into the air. The sulphur is the price of living on a hot spot, and the nickname of Rotovegas, half affection and half mockery, covers the tourist bustle.

  3. 3. Taupo

    The lake at Taupō is so big and so calm on a still morning that it is hard to believe what it actually is: the flooded crater of one of the most violent volcanoes on the planet. Around 25,500 years ago, the Oruanui eruption blew out an enormous volume of rock and ash, and the collapse formed much of the basin the lake now fills. Then, roughly 1,800 years ago, the Hatepe eruption sent ash and pumice across the North Island and, according to some accounts, coloured sunsets as far away as Rome and China. Lake Taupō, the largest lake in New Zealand, sits in that scar.
