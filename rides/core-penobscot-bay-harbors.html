  1. 1. Belfast

  2. 2. Camden

    Camden is the harbor town that makes Maine look as good as it does in the brochures. The Camden Hills rise right behind the town, with Mount Battie, about eight hundred feet high, standing over the inner harbor, and Penobscot Bay lies in front, dotted with islands and sailing vessels. Windjammers, the old-style schooners that take passengers on multi-day cruises, fill the waterfront in summer, their masts and rigging a forest against the sky.

  3. 3. Rockland

  4. 4. Damariscotta

    Damariscotta is oyster country, and it has been for a very long time. Along the banks of the Damariscotta River, huge heaps of discarded oyster shells, some of them thirty feet deep, mark the places where Native peoples feasted for over two thousand years, and these middens are among the largest on the Atlantic coast. Today the river is home to a thriving aquaculture industry, and the oysters are among the best in the country, served on the half shell in a half-dozen restaurants.
