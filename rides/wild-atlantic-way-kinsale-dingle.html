Wild Atlantic Way: Kinsale to Dingle

Why this ride

From Kinsale it runs 389 km to Dingle; allow about 5 h 29 of riding. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kinsale

    Kinsale is a small harbour town on the south coast of County Cork, and it has been around long enough to have seen plenty of drama. The Battle of Kinsale in 1601 ended in defeat for the Irish lords and their Spanish allies and is often treated as the real end of the old Gaelic order. The star-shaped Charles Fort, built later in the seventeenth century, still guards the harbour mouth, and you can walk its ramparts for a view across the water.

  2. 2. Mizen Head

    Ireland runs out here, and it does so with a flourish. Mizen Head is the south-western tip of the island, a ragged headland of dark rock and heather above a sea that is rarely calm, and the road ends at the point where there is nothing between you and Newfoundland but ocean. From the clifftop you can see the Fastnet Rock far out to the south-west, a lonely pinnacle with a lighthouse, which was the last sight of Ireland for many emigrants and is often called Ireland's Teardrop.

  3. 3. Dingle

    Dingle is a small harbour town at the western end of County Kerry's Dingle Peninsula, with fewer than two thousand people but about as many pubs as you could want. The streets slope toward the water, shops and houses are painted in strong colours, and the harbour is full of fishing boats. This is a Gaeltacht area, where Irish is still the everyday language for many families, so you may see signs in Irish first, and the town's Irish name, An Daingean, appears on the road markers.
