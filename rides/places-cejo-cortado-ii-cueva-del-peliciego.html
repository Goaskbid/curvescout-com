Why this ride

A 187 km round trip from Mula - roughly 3 h 13 in the saddle. It goes by way of Cueva-sima de la Serreta, Abrigo de los Cortijos, Cueva del Peliciego and back through Abrigo de los Cortijos. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cejo Cortado II

    Mula does not announce itself to motorcyclists, which is a pity, because the roads around it are among the most enjoyable in inland Murcia. The town of nearly seventeen thousand sits on the river that bears its name, below the long grey wall of the Sierra Espuña to the south, and its old quarter climbs a hill capped by a castle. Cejo Cortado, seven kilometres from the centre, is a small crag in dry hill country where a shelter holds prehistoric paintings, the kind of place you find by following a farm track and reading the contours.

  2. 2. Cueva-sima de la Serreta

    Calasparra is rice country, which surprises people who think of Murcia as parched and brown. The rice grows in terraced paddies fed by the clear, cold waters of the Segura, and the short-grain variety grown here has protected status and a reputation among Spanish cooks as the best for paella and slow rice dishes. Just upstream, the river squeezes through the Almadenes gorge, a narrow cleft between limestone walls where vultures soar and the water runs deep green. The Cueva-sima de la Serreta lies in the dry hills above, near the border with Cieza, in a land of rock and pine.

  3. 3. Abrigo de los Cortijos

    Hellín is a town in the south-east of Albacete province, on the edge of the dry plains of La Mancha, where the landscape breaks into hills and ravines on its way down to the Segura river. Its old town climbs a hill crowned by a castle, and its streets are lined with houses from the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. The Abrigo de los Cortijos lies in the hills about nine kilometres from the town, toward Albatana and Tobarra.

  4. 4. Cueva del Peliciego

    Jumilla is wine. This town in the north of Murcia sits in a high, dry valley surrounded by bare mountains, and its vineyards, planted with the Monastrell grape, produce powerful red wines under the Jumilla designation of origin, one of the oldest in Spain. The Cueva del Peliciego, a rock shelter in the hills near the town, holds prehistoric paintings in the Levantine style, and it has been known since the early twentieth century, when it was one of the first rock art sites studied in the region.
