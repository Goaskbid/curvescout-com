Why this ride

A 339 km round trip from Clairvaux-les-Lacs - roughly 6 h in the saddle. It goes by way of From the Great Saltworks of Salins-les-Bai, Fortifications of Vauban, Le Locle and back through Fortifications of Vauban. It tops out around 900 m. It crosses from France into Switzerland - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Le Grand Lac de Clairvaux → From the Great Saltworks of Salins-les-Bai

It is 52 km from Le Grand Lac de Clairvaux to From the Great Saltworks of Salins-les-Bai, mostly on D 27. Downhill for the most part, 380 m in all, never above 652 m. Best in the early light, before the traffic.

The stops

  1. 1. Le Grand Lac de Clairvaux

    Start here.

    The Jura plateau behind Clairvaux-les-Lacs is a country of limestone, cows and lakes, and the Grand Lac de Clairvaux is the largest of the lakes that give the town its name. Prehistoric people lived on its shore on pilings, in villages that rose and fell for centuries, and the waterlogged mud preserved their wood and tools. The sites on Clairvaux's lakes are among the pile-dwelling places inscribed on the UNESCO list in 2011. The lake today is a quiet blue sheet, ringed by reeds and low wooded hills, with a beach where families swim in July.

    Read the complete Le Grand Lac de Clairvaux write-up.

  2. 2. From the Great Saltworks of Salins-les-Bai

    52 km in.

    Salins-les-Bains lies at the bottom of a deep, narrow valley in the Jura, squeezed between two forested ridges, each crowned by a fort, and its wealth for most of history came from beneath its streets. Deep underground, salty water flows through layers of rock salt, and for over a thousand years the people of Salins pumped it up and boiled it in great pans to make salt, a precious commodity before refrigeration, used to preserve food and taxed heavily by the crown. The Grande Saline, the great saltworks, is now a museum, and you can walk through its underground galleries, where a huge hydraulic wheel and pump from the nineteenth century still turn, lifting brine from the depths.

    Read the complete From the Great Saltworks of Salins-les-Bai write-up.

  3. 3. Fortifications of Vauban

    94 km in.

    Victor Hugo was born in Besançon in 1802, and so were the Lumière brothers, the pioneers of cinema, a generation later. The capital of Franche-Comté sits in a tight loop of the river Doubs, with the old town on the peninsula inside the loop and the citadel on a rock at the neck, closing the only side not protected by water. The citadel was built by Vauban, Louis XIV's great military engineer, after the French conquest of the region in the late seventeenth century, and it is one of the twelve groups of Fortifications of Vauban listed by UNESCO in 2008.

    Read the complete Fortifications of Vauban write-up.

  4. 4. Le Locle

    170 km in.

    Le Locle, high in the Swiss Jura near the French border, is a town built for making watches. After a series of fires in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, it was rebuilt on a grid of parallel streets, with houses and workshops designed to let maximum light into the rooms where watchmakers worked at their benches. The town, with neighbouring La Chaux-de-Fonds, was listed by UNESCO in 2009 as an example of urban planning shaped entirely by a single industry. Tissot was founded here in 1853, Zenith in 1865, and many smaller firms continue the tradition. Daniel JeanRichard, a goldsmith who is said to have founded the Jura watch industry, worked here in the eighteenth century.
