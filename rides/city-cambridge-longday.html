Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cambridge

    Cambridge is flat, and for a motorcyclist that is both its weakness and its secret. The city sits where the chalk of the Gog Magog hills runs out into the fens, and the River Cam wanders through its heart past the colleges, between lawns and willows, under little bridges where punts jostle on summer afternoons. King's College Chapel, begun in the fifteenth century and finished under the early Tudors, has a fan-vaulted ceiling that looks as if it were carved from lace, and its choir sings evensong most days of term. More than 158,000 people live here, so fuel, food and a bed are never in doubt.

  2. 2. Tower of London

    The Tower of London is the oldest and most layered fortress in the capital. William the Conqueror began the White Tower in the 1070s, a massive keep of Caen stone meant to show the English who was in charge. Over nine centuries it was a palace, a mint, an armoury, a menagerie and a prison, and some of the people who went in did not come out: Anne Boleyn and Lady Jane Grey were executed here, and the two young princes of the fifteenth century vanished within its walls. Today the Crown Jewels sit in the Waterloo Block and the Yeoman Warders, known as Beefeaters, lead tours with a mix of grim history and well-polished jokes.

  4. 4. Palace of Westminster and Westminster Abbe

    Few views in the world are photographed more often, and few are harder to enjoy from the saddle. The Palace of Westminster, rebuilt after the fire of 1834 by Charles Barry and Augustus Pugin in a Gothic style that deliberately looked backward, stands on the north bank of the Thames, with its famous clock tower at one end. The tower is properly called the Elizabeth Tower, and Big Ben is the name of the great bell inside it. Across the road is Westminster Abbey, where English and British monarchs have been crowned since William the Conqueror in 1066, and where many of them are buried, along with poets, scientists and the Unknown Warrior.
