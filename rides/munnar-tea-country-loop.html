Why this ride

A 235 km loop that starts and ends near Cochin, about 3 h 47 of riding. It goes by way of Adimali, Munnar, Top Station and Marayoor. The highest point is about 1,550 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Munnar Tea Country Loop

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 14 km this is Munnar Tea Country Loop, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kochi

    Kochi is the old spice port of Kerala, and it still has the look of a place where traders from many countries have come and gone. Arab, Chinese, Portuguese, Dutch and British merchants all passed through, and the old quarter around Fort Kochi keeps traces of them: Chinese fishing nets on the waterfront, a Portuguese-era church, a synagogue in Jew Town and colonial houses facing the water. The city itself is spread across islands and the mainland, tied together by bridges and ferries, with a lot of water around.

  2. 2. Adimali

    Adimali is a working town in the cardamom hills of Kerala, on the road that climbs from the plains near Kochi into the high country around Munnar. It is not a tourist town, and nobody comes here for the sights, but it is a handy, lively place, a trading centre for spices, rubber, tea and cardamom, with a crowded bazaar, a lot of buses and the constant beep of horns. It is also the last proper town before the road begins its steepest climb.

  3. 3. Munnar

    Munnar is tea country on a grand scale, a high town in the Western Ghats of Kerala, at about fifteen hundred metres, surrounded by steep green hills that are covered, row on row, in a close-clipped carpet of tea bushes. The slopes are so regular and so bright that they look painted, and the mist moves through them in the morning, turning the valley into a soft, silvery landscape. It is cool here, even when the plains below are baking, and the air has a faint smell of tea leaves.

  4. 4. Top Station

    Top Station is the high point of the road between Munnar and the Tamil Nadu border, a viewpoint at about seventeen hundred metres, where the Kerala hills fall away abruptly to the plains of Tamil Nadu far below. The name comes from the days when a ropeway carried tea down from the estates to the railhead at Kottagudi, and this was the topmost station. Today, the ropeway is long gone, but the name has stuck, and the place is a favourite stop for travellers going between Kerala and Tamil Nadu.

  5. 5. Marayoor

    Marayoor lies on the dry, rain-shadow side of the Western Ghats, a small town in the Anamalai hills of Kerala where the lush tea country around Munnar gives way to a landscape of sugarcane fields, coconut palms and rocky, scrub-covered hills. It is a different world from the damp green slopes just over the ridge, hotter, drier and quieter, and the change happens within a few kilometres. The people here are mostly farmers, and the pace of life is slow.
