Why this ride

From Seascale it runs 227 km to Seascale; allow about 3 h 51 of riding. On the way it passes Low Mire, Hadrian's Wall & vallum from A6071 to The and back through Low Mire. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Ravenglass Roman fort

    Start here.

    Ravenglass is the only coastal village in the Lake District National Park, a small huddle of whitewashed houses along a single street facing a tidal estuary where three rivers, the Esk, the Irt and the Mite, meet the Irish Sea. At low tide the estuary becomes a vast expanse of mud, sand and channels, with boats lying aslant on the shingle, and at high tide the water laps almost at the cottages' doorsteps. The Romans saw the same harbour and built a fort here, Glannoventa, as a supply base on the Cumbrian coast, linked to the line of coastal forts and towers that extended from Hadrian's Wall down the coast.

    Read the complete Ravenglass Roman fort write-up.

  2. 2. Low Mire

    57 km in.

    Low Mire lies in the quiet farmland behind the Cumbrian coast, near Dearham and Maryport, where the land slopes gently down to the Solway Firth and the hills of southern Scotland rise across the water. It is an area of small fields, hedges and scattered farms, with the Lake District fells visible to the south-east on clear days.

    Read the complete Low Mire write-up.

  3. 3. Hadrian's Wall & vallum from A6071 to The

    113 km in.

    Do not expect a great stone wall here. Near Brampton, on the stretch running out toward the A6071, Hadrian's Wall is mostly a matter of earthworks and imagination: a line in a field, a softened ditch, the long parallel banks of the vallum to the south. In this western sector the wall was originally built of turf and timber before being rebuilt in stone, and centuries of farmers and builders carried off the dressed stones for barns and churches. What survives is listed as part of the UNESCO Frontiers of the Roman Empire, and once your eye tunes in, you start seeing the line everywhere.
