Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Roman Bridge over Albarregas River

    Mérida is built on two rivers. The big one is the Guadiana, crossed by the famous long bridge, and the small one is the Albarregas, a stream that curves around the northern and western edge of the old town and has been bridged since Roman times. The Roman bridge over the Albarregas is a modest structure of granite arches, nothing like the length of its big cousin, but it did a job: it carried the road out of the colony toward the west and the north, and it still stands. A few steps from it the old streets give way to a park with trees, a place where locals walk dogs and children ride bikes.

  2. 2. Old Town of Cáceres

    Cáceres has an old town so complete that film crews treat it as a ready-made set, and walking its streets at dusk, when the stone glows amber and the crowds have gone, you can see why. Inside the walls, built largely by the Almohads in the twelfth century, are dozens of fortified mansions, towers, churches and palaces, many built by noble families who made their fortunes in the conquest of the Americas. The towers once had battlements, but Queen Isabella ordered most of them cut down to curb the nobles' power. UNESCO listed the old town in 1986.
