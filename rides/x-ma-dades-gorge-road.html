Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Boumalne Dades

    The road to Boumalne Dadès runs through country that looks like another planet. The town sits at about fifteen hundred metres on the edge of the High Atlas, on the old route known as the Road of a Thousand Kasbahs, and from here the Dadès river cuts a gorge into red rock that is among the finest in Morocco. The settlement itself is small and dusty, a stop on the highway with a market, cafes and a handful of hotels, but it is the gateway to some serious riding.

  2. 2. Dades Gorge

    Where the Dadès river has carved its way into the southern flank of the High Atlas, the red and orange cliffs close in around a ribbon of green fields and palm groves. The Dadès Gorge is part of the long valley sometimes called the Road of a Thousand Kasbahs, a string of fortified mud-brick houses and villages built along the water by Berber families, who farmed the narrow strip of irrigated land and defended it from raiders. Above Boumalne Dadès, the valley narrows and the rock turns to strange formations, including the weathered pillars known as the monkey fingers.
