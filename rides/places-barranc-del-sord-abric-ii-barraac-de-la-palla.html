Why this ride

From Famorca it runs 72 km to Famorca; allow about 1 h 14 of riding. The stops in between: Al Patró, Barraac de la Palla and back through Al Patró. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc del Sord-Abric II

    Behind the busy Costa Blanca, only half an hour from high-rise beach towns, there is a country of terraces and ravines that feels like another century. The Barranc del Sord, the deaf ravine, is one of many dry gullies cutting the limestone mountains of the Marina Baixa, and the shelters in its walls hold paintings made by prehistoric people, part of the Levantine art that stretches along Spain's Mediterranean side. The shelters are protected, and many have fencing or require local guidance.

  2. 2. Al Patró

    The Vall d'Ebo is one of those valleys that history has emptied. In 1609 the Spanish crown ordered the expulsion of the Moriscos, the descendants of the region's Muslim population, and in the mountains of the Marina Alta, many villages lost almost everyone overnight. Some Moriscos resisted in the high cliffs of the Muela de Cortes before the end came. Vall d'Ebo today has barely three hundred inhabitants, and its terraced fields, built by Morisco farmers for irrigation, are still visible as stone steps climbing the hillsides.

  3. 3. Barraac de la Palla

    Tormos and Sagra are tiny villages at the foot of the mountains in the north of Alicante province, at the point where the plain of the Marina Alta meets the hills. They are surrounded by orange groves and orchards, with the mountains of the Vall de Laguar and the Serra del Migdia rising steeply behind. The Barranc de la Palla, the ravine of the straw, cuts into the hills near Tormos, close also to Orba and the town of Pego.
