Why this ride

From Donji Vakuf it runs 391 km to Donji Vakuf; allow about 8 h of riding. It goes by way of Dugo polje at Blidinje, Grčka glavica in the village of Biskup and back through Dugo polje at Blidinje. It tops out around 1,250 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

The roads

Bosnia Mostar to Sarajevo Mountain Route

It is 89 km from Grčka glavica in the village of Biskup to back through Dugo polje at Blidinje. It climbs 1330 m to the top at 1,245 m and drops 480 m again. Take a break before the climb, not halfway up.

The stops

  1. 1. Prašuma Janj

    Start here.

    Prašuma means primeval forest, and Janj is one of the few places in Europe where that word is literally true. In the hills above the Janj river, between Šipovo and Donji Vakuf in central Bosnia, a block of mixed beech, fir and spruce forest has survived without logging, its oldest trees reaching enormous heights and girths. Since 2021 it has been part of the World Heritage site of ancient beech forests of Europe, joining a chain of reserves from the Carpathians to the Pyrenees.

    Read the complete Prašuma Janj write-up.

  2. 2. Dugo polje at Blidinje

    109 km in.

    High on a karst plateau in Herzegovina, between the massive mountains of Čvrsnica and Vran, lies Blidinje, a wide upland valley of meadows, limestone outcrops and black pine forests at around twelve hundred metres. At Dugo polje, the long field, a cluster of medieval tombstones stands in the grass, carved with crosses, spirals, rosettes, hunting scenes and human figures. These are stećci, the distinctive gravestones of the medieval Balkans, made between the twelfth and sixteenth centuries, and Dugo polje is one of the most impressive groups, part of the collection of stećci sites listed by UNESCO in 2016 across Bosnia and Herzegovina, Croatia, Montenegro and Serbia.

    Read the complete Dugo polje at Blidinje write-up.

  3. 3. Grčka glavica in the village of Biskup

    194 km in.

    The Greek hillock, Grčka glavica, is a misleading name: the medieval tombstones that crown it have nothing to do with Greece. In Bosnia and Herzegovina, folk tradition often credited the stećci, the great carved medieval tombstones of the region, to Greeks, giants or other mysterious ancestors, and the names have stuck. The necropolis near the village of Biskup, in the hills south of Konjic, is a group of these stones, some decorated with reliefs of crosses, rosettes, hunting scenes or figures, and it is one of the sites in the UNESCO listing of stećci medieval tombstone graveyards, listed in 2016.

    Read the complete Grčka glavica in the village of Biskup write-up.
