Marin Headlands and Point Reyes

Why this ride

Leave the city behind and spend the day on roads with texture. Out of Fairview and back again: 408 km, roughly 7 h 25 in the saddle. It goes by way of Winters, in Kenwood, Bolinas and Daly City. near Portola Valley is the high point.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fuel stop near Fairview

    The hills behind Hayward rise steeply from the flat bay plain, and Fairview sits right on top of them, a semi-rural community of ranches, winding lanes and houses with long views across San Francisco Bay. On a clear day, you can see the bridges, the city skyline and the Peninsula hills beyond. It is a peaceful spot above one of the busiest urban areas in the country.

  2. 2. Near Winters, on the way back

    West of Winters, the Sacramento Valley ends abruptly at the Vaca Mountains, and Putah Creek cuts a narrow canyon through them up to Monticello Dam and Lake Berryessa. This spot lies in that rugged, dry country a short way above Winters, where the valley floor of orchards and fields gives way to steep hills of oak, grey pine and golden grass, burned brown by summer and green after the winter rains.

  3. 3. Food stop in Kenwood

    Kenwood sits in the Valley of the Moon, the name Jack London gave to the Sonoma Valley, where he built his ranch and his ill-fated dream house, Wolf House, which burned before he could move in. The ruins and his grave lie in Jack London State Historic Park in nearby Glen Ellen, and the valley around them remains one of the most beautiful in California, with vineyards, oak woodland and the Mayacamas Mountains rising to the east.

  4. 4. Bolinas

    Bolinas does not want to be found, and it has made that clear for decades. Locals have a long tradition of removing the road sign that points to the town from Highway 1, so you may have to watch for the unmarked turnoff at the end of Bolinas Lagoon. The reward is a small, eccentric, salt-weathered village at the tip of a mesa on the Marin coast, with a few streets of old wooden buildings, a beach, surfers and an atmosphere that feels decades removed from the Bay Area just over the hill.

  5. 5. Near Daly City

    South of San Francisco's city limits, the land runs out abruptly. Daly City's houses, the ones with the colourful matching boxes that inspired the song Little Boxes, cover the hills right up to the edge of high, crumbling sea cliffs, and below them the Pacific pounds a long empty beach.

  6. 6. Half Moon Bay

    Half Moon Bay is where the Peninsula's mountains finally meet the Pacific. The town sits on a wide coastal plain, backed by the green flank of the Santa Cruz Mountains and fronted by a long crescent of sand, and it feels like a different state from the busy bay side only twenty minutes away over Highway 92. Fog arrives most summer afternoons, so mornings are your best bet for sun.

  8. 8. Back to Marin Headlands

    That tells you something about how this ride ends: after a long day on the coast the route finishes on the far side of the water, among the suburbs that fill the shelf between the bay and the dry brown ridges behind. Fairview and Castro Valley are a few minutes away, Hayward a little further, and the whole district sits directly on the Hayward Fault, which is a fact most locals have made peace with.
