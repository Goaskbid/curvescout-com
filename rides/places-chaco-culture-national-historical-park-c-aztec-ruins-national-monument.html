Why this ride

It goes by way of Pierre's site, Twin Angels, Aztec Ruins National Monument and back through Twin Angels. The highest point is about 2,050 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Chaco Culture National Historical Park - C

    Chaco is a place where you have to earn the silence. The way in is a long dirt road, many miles of corrugations and dust, that leaves the paved highway in the high desert of north-west New Mexico and runs across open range before dropping into a wide canyon, and by the time you arrive, you have been shaken and quiet for a good while. Then you see the walls. Between about the middle of the ninth century and the twelfth, the Ancestral Puebloan people built a dozen huge stone buildings here, called great houses, of which Pueblo Bonito is the largest, with hundreds of rooms in a D-shaped plan, stacked several storeys high, and a vast number of ceiling timbers carried in from forests far away.

  2. 2. Pierre's site

    North of Chaco Canyon, the Ancestral Puebloans built something that still puzzles archaeologists: a road. Not a trail, but a wide, carefully engineered path, about nine metres wide in places, running almost dead straight north across the desert for some fifty kilometres. It is known as the Great North Road, and Pierre's site sits right on it. This is a cluster of small structures built on and around a prominent sandstone outcrop, including what may have been signalling stations or shrines on top of the hill, and from its height you can see far across the San Juan Basin. Like Kin Ya'a to the south, it is protected as one of the Chaco outliers on the World Heritage list.

  3. 3. Twin Angels

    South of Bloomfield, on the edge of the badlands of Kutz Canyon, a small Chacoan great house sits on a ridge with views over the San Juan Basin. Twin Angels Pueblo is one of the outliers of Chaco Culture, a stone building with rooms and a kiva, built around a thousand years ago by people connected with the great centre at Chaco Canyon, about eighty kilometres to the south. It lies near the end of the Great North Road, the long, straight Chacoan road running north from the canyon, and it is part of the UNESCO-listed Chaco Culture. It is managed by the Bureau of Land Management, and reached by a rough dirt road and a short hike.

  4. 4. Aztec Ruins National Monument

    The Aztecs never came anywhere near Aztec, New Mexico. The ruins here were named by nineteenth-century settlers who assumed, wrongly, that such impressive buildings must have been made by the famous people of central Mexico. In fact, they were built by Ancestral Puebloans between about 1100 and 1300, as part of the great Chacoan world centred on Chaco Canyon, sixty miles to the south, and they are included with Chaco in the UNESCO listing of the Chaco Culture.
