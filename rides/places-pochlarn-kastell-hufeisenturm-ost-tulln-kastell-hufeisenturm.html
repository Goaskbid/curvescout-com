  1. 1. Pöchlarn - Kastell Hufeisenturm Ost

    The word Hufeisenturm means horseshoe tower, and it is the sort of detail that tells you about the Roman world when it was under pressure. In the fourth century, with raiders crossing the Danube more often, the Romans rebuilt many of their frontier forts with a new style of defence. Instead of square corner towers, they built tall towers in a U shape that projected out from the walls, giving archers and artillery a wider field of fire. The east tower at Pochlarn, the Roman fort of Arelape, belongs to that period.

  2. 2. St. Johann im Mauerthale - Wachtturm

    Above the Wachau, in the forested hills on the south bank, a watchtower stood in the Roman period, and its stones are a quiet reminder that this valley was once the border of an empire. The Danube was the northern frontier of the province of Noricum, and the Romans guarded it with forts and signal towers every few kilometres, a chain that now forms part of the UNESCO listing for the Danube Limes. The tower near St Johann im Mauerthale was one of these, placed to see along the river and warn of anyone crossing from the north. Not much remains but the foundations, and they sit in a place with a long view.

  3. 3. Traismauer - Kastell südwestlicher Fächert

    Traismauer is a small wine town on the south side of the Danube, just where the Traisen river comes down from the Alpine foothills to meet the big river, and it has been a fortified place for something like eighteen centuries. The Romans called their fort here Augustianis, one link in the chain of camps and watchtowers that guarded the frontier of the province of Noricum. In 2021 the Danube Limes was added to the UNESCO list, and Traismauer is one of the places where you can still see the old walls doing a job.

  4. 4. Tulln - Kastell Hufeisenturm

    Egon Schiele was born in Tulln in 1890, in the station building where his father was stationmaster, and the town that produced one of the most provocative painters of Viennese modernism is today a quiet, prosperous place on the Danube, known as Austria's garden town. Long before that, it was the Roman fort of Comagena, part of the frontier along the Danube that protected the Roman province of Noricum. One of the fort's towers has survived remarkably well: the so-called Salzturm, a horseshoe-shaped tower on the riverbank, one of the best-preserved Roman structures in Austria. It is part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire – The Danube Limes, listed by UNESCO in 2021.
