  1. 1. Victoria

    Victoria sits at the southern tip of Vancouver Island, the capital of British Columbia and the very British city that nobody expects on the Pacific coast. The Inner Harbour is the center of it, with the Parliament Buildings outlined in lights at night and the grand old Fairmont Empress hotel facing across the water, and ferries, seaplanes and little harbor taxis crowd the dock. The Hudson's Bay Company built a fort here in 1843, and the town grew from it, with a Chinatown that is among the oldest in Canada.

  2. 2. Sooke

    Sooke is the last real town on Vancouver Island's southwest coast before the road narrows and the forest takes over. West of Victoria, Highway 14 passes through the growing suburbs of Langford and then reaches this community of about six thousand people on Sooke Harbour and Basin, a sheltered inlet protected from the open sea by the long curving sandbar of Whiffin Spit. On a clear day, across the Strait of Juan de Fuca, the snowy Olympic Mountains of Washington State fill the southern horizon.

  3. 3. Lake Cowichan

    At the eastern end of a long, deep lake in the mountains of southern Vancouver Island, the small town of Lake Cowichan marks the point where the Cowichan River begins its run to the sea at Cowichan Bay. The lake stretches about thirty kilometres west into the hills, surrounded by forest and logged slopes, and the river flowing out of it is famous for its steelhead and trout fishing and, on hot summer days, for tubing, as hundreds of people float lazily downstream from town.
