Why this ride

From Teulada it runs 153 km to Teulada; allow about 2 h 38 of riding. The stops in between: Racó de la Cova dels Llidoners, Coves Roges- Abric I, Barranc de Bolulla and back through Coves Roges- Abric I.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cova de la Catxupa- Abric III

    Behind Teulada the land rises sharply, and the third shelter in the Catxupa series sits where the first foothills begin. It is not an obvious place for an ancient painted site. The coast is only a few kilometres away, and the view toward the sea is partly built over. But the Levantine rock art tradition ran right down to this edge of the Mediterranean, and the overhangs of the hills here were used by people long before anyone built a villa.

  2. 2. Racó de la Cova dels Llidoners

    Somewhere up in these limestone folds, thousands of years ago, someone painted little figures on the rock that are still there. The Racó de la Cova dels Llidoners is one of many painted shelters in the Marina Alta, the north-eastern corner of the province of Alicante, which form part of the UNESCO-listed rock art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula. The shelters hold Levantine, Schematic and older Macroschematic paintings, showing hunters, animals and abstract shapes, and many are protected by fences and only visitable with a guide. The valleys around Murla, Orba and Tormos are full of them.

  3. 3. Coves Roges- Abric I

    Planes looks like a village that has been arguing with gravity for a thousand years and winning. Its houses climb a hill crowned by the remains of a Moorish castle, and the whole place overlooks a valley of olive and almond terraces that falls toward the Beniarrés reservoir. The Coves Roges, the red caves, lie in the broken limestone country around here, and the first of their shelters carries prehistoric paintings, part of the scattered rock art of the Valencian mountains. The colour in the name is not poetry: the rock itself is stained a warm iron red.

  4. 4. Barranc de Bolulla

    Bolulla is barely a village, a few hundred people tucked into a valley of the Marina Baixa behind Benidorm, and the ravine that carries its name cuts into the limestone hills just outside it. The mountains here are steep and close together, terraced for centuries with olives, almonds and loquats, and their caves and shelters hold traces of prehistoric painting that place this corner of Alicante within the wider family of Mediterranean rock art. What you see on the ground is a dry, rocky gully scented with pine and wild herbs, with the ruined castle of Bolulla perched on a ridge above, a reminder of the Moorish centuries when these valleys were densely settled.
