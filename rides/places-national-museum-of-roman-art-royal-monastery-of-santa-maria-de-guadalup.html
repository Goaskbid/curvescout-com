Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. National Museum of Roman Art

    Rafael Moneo's museum in Mérida is a building that makes Roman engineering look contemporary. Opened in 1986, it is built of thin Roman-style bricks in a set of tall arches that echo the arcades of the aqueducts, and as you walk inside the great central nave you feel as though you have wandered into an imaginary basilica. The architect won the Pritzker Prize in 1996, and this building is among the works that made his name. It stands on the site of Roman Emerita Augusta, and below it lie the remains of streets and houses, which you can visit on a lower level.

  2. 2. Old Town of Cáceres

    Cáceres has an old town so complete that film crews treat it as a ready-made set, and walking its streets at dusk, when the stone glows amber and the crowds have gone, you can see why. Inside the walls, built largely by the Almohads in the twelfth century, are dozens of fortified mansions, towers, churches and palaces, many built by noble families who made their fortunes in the conquest of the Americas. The towers once had battlements, but Queen Isabella ordered most of them cut down to curb the nobles' power. UNESCO listed the old town in 1986.
