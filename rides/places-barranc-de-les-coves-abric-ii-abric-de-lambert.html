Why this ride

272 km from Ibi to Ibi, about 4 h 41 of riding. On the way it passes Cova del mansano, Abric de Lambert and back through Cova del mansano. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc de les Coves- Abric II

    Ibi is the first thing to understand here, because the town sits so close that the rock shelter feels like its back garden. Ibi is a place that makes toys, and has done for generations; the whole Hoya de Castalla and the neighbouring town of Onil grew a tinplate-and-plastic industry that once filled children's bedrooms across Spain. Before the factories, Ibi was known for something colder: snow and ice gathered from the high ground of the Sierra de Mariola and stored in pits for the summer. A town that lives on cold in a region that bakes is a good story to carry up the hill.

  2. 2. Cova del mansano

    Every cyclist in Europe seems to know the Coll de Rates, and that is a good hint for motorcyclists too. The climb starts in Parcent, a few kilometres from the Cova del Mansano, and winds up the mountainside in a long sequence of smooth, well surfaced bends to a pass with a view over the whole Vall de Pop and the sea beyond. The rock shelter itself sits in the hills around Jalón, Alcalalí and Parcent, a short walk from the road, and its paintings belong to the same World Heritage group as the other Levantine shelters of the coast.

  3. 3. Abric de Lambert

    Cullera is not where most people would look for prehistory. It is a busy beach town at the mouth of the Júcar, with high-rise apartments along the sand, a lighthouse on the point and rice fields stretching inland toward the Albufera lagoon. But the town has a mountain at its back, a limestone outcrop crowned by a castle and a sanctuary, and in that rock there are shelters, including the one known as the Abric de Lambert.
