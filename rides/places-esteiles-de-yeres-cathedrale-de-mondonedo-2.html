  1. 1. Estéiles de Yeres

    Some places are best understood by a rider who has had to stop and wait. At Yeres, in the upland between the Sil and the Teleno range, a farm tractor or a herd of cattle will often hold you up on the road, and it is a good moment to look at the hillside and notice that the slope is not natural. The estéiles here, the heaps of Roman mining waste, are folded into the landscape and overgrown with oak, broom and heather.

  2. 2. Roman Walls of Lugo

    Lugo is the only city in the world still completely surrounded by intact Roman walls. Built in the late third century, when the Roman Empire was under pressure from invasions, the walls run for more than two kilometres around the old town, with dozens of semicircular towers and ten gates, some Roman and some added later. They are wide enough at the top for a walkway, and walking the full circuit, with views over the rooftops of the old town on one side and the modern city on the other, is the best way to get a sense of the place. The walls were listed by UNESCO in 2000.

  3. 3. Cathedrale de Mondonedo

    Mondoñedo is a small cathedral city in the north of Galicia, tucked into a green valley of the Masma river between the Cantabrian coast and the hills of the interior. With barely five thousand people it is one of the smallest places in Spain to have a cathedral, and the building is the reason the town exists. It was begun in the late twelfth century and finished over several centuries, with a Romanesque core, Gothic vaults and a Baroque west front with two towers. Inside, there are medieval wall paintings in the cloister and a fine stone choir, and the whole is built from the grey local granite, which turns green with lichen and gold in the evening sun.
