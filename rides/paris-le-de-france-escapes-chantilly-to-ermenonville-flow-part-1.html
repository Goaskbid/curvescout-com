Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fuel stop near Lagny-sur-Marne

    Lagny-sur-Marne sits on the river Marne about thirty kilometres east of Paris, a busy town with an old centre, a riverside quay and a large, unfinished Gothic church, Notre-Dame-des-Ardents, all that was completed of an ambitious abbey church begun in the thirteenth century. The town was an important market in the Middle Ages, part of the network of Champagne fairs, and Joan of Arc fought near here in 1430, shortly before her capture at Compiègne.

  2. 2. Food stop near Crépy-en-Valois

    Crépy-en-Valois was once the capital of the Valois, the old county whose name passed to a whole dynasty of French kings. Today it is a busy market town of some fifteen thousand people on the plateau north-east of Paris, a natural place to refuel before heading on to Laon and the Aisne valley.

  3. 3. High point near Creil

    Creil is an industrial town on the Oise, about fifty kilometres north of Paris, and it would be easy to pass it by. Yet within a few kilometres lie some of the finest forests and châteaux in northern France, and the high ground around the town gives you a good sense of how the river has carved its way through the chalk and limestone plateaux of the Île-de-France.

  4. 4. Scenic stretch near Meulan-en-Yvelines

    Meulan-en-Yvelines sits on a bend of the Seine, about forty kilometres north-west of Paris, where the river divides around an island. The old town climbs steeply up the hillside from the water, and from the top you get a fine view over the river, the bridges and the valley. Across the water, Les Mureaux has a long aerospace history, and parts of the Ariane rockets are made there.

  5. 5. Paris / Île-de-France escapes

    Ézy-sur-Eure is a modest town of three thousand on the river Eure in Normandy, close to the border of the Eure and Eure-et-Loir, and its neighbour, Saussay, is a hamlet. The place is a few kilometres from Anet, a small town with one of the most celebrated châteaux of the French Renaissance, built in the 1540s by the architect Philibert de l'Orme for Diane de Poitiers, the favourite of King Henri II. The château, with its elegant courtyard and its famous carved entrance gate, is open to the public, and the story of Diane, who was about twenty years older than the king and ruled his heart for decades, is told in the rooms.

    Read the complete Ézy-sur-Eure, on the way back write-up.
