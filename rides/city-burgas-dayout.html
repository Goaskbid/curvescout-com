Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Burgas

    Burgas is the working heart of Bulgaria's southern Black Sea coast, a port city that has never quite decided whether it is industrial or seaside, and the answer is a little of both. The long Sea Garden, laid out in the late nineteenth century, runs along the cliff above the beach and is lined with statues, flowerbeds and cafes, and the pier at the end juts out into the bay like an invitation. The old railway station, with its fine early twentieth-century frontage, is a reminder of the city's role as a trading hub.

  2. 2. Madara Rider

    Above the village of Madara, on a vertical cliff about a hundred metres up, a horseman is carved into the rock, thrusting a spear at a lion that lies under his horse, while a dog runs behind. The Madara Rider is the only relief of its kind in Europe, made in the early eighth century by the first Bulgarian state, probably to celebrate a victory by Khan Tervel, and it is a rare relic of the pre-Christian era of the country. UNESCO listed it in 1979. Beside it are Greek inscriptions recording the deeds of Bulgarian khans, among the oldest written documents in the country.
