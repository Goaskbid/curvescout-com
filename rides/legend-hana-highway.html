Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kahului

    Kahului is where most people land on Maui, and for a rider it is the last flat, tidy, six-lane stretch of the day. The town sits on the isthmus between the two volcanoes, with Haleakalā's enormous grey-green shoulder rising to the south and the older, jagged West Maui Mountains to the west. It is a planned town, laid out by the sugar company in the middle of the last century to house plantation workers, which is why it feels more like a grid of shopping centres and bungalows than a postcard. That is fine. You are here for the practical things.

  2. 2. Paia

    Paia is where the Hana Highway properly starts, even though the road has been climbing out of the Kahului plain for some time already. It is an old sugar and plantation town on Maui's north shore, a short row of weathered wooden storefronts, surf shops, and cafes with a very relaxed idea of the clock. Windsurfers and kitesurfers gather at the beaches nearby, and the trade winds that make them happy are the same ones that keep the air here cool and slightly salty.

  4. 4. Near Hana

    By the time you reach the Hana end of the Hana Highway, you have earned it. The road from Kahului winds along the north-east coast of Maui for about eighty kilometres, with hundreds of bends and dozens of narrow bridges, most of them only one lane wide, through rainforest, past waterfalls and above black lava cliffs. Hana itself is a small, quiet town of about twelve hundred people, set around a bay with a red cinder hill, Ka'uiki, guarding its southern end.

  5. 5. Hana

    There is a particular kind of satisfaction in arriving at Hana, which has little to do with the town itself. Hana is small, a post office and a scattering of houses, a bay, a few shops, and the sense that the world has been politely asked to stay outside. You come here because the ride was long and the road gave you no choice. A little over fifty miles, ridden at the honest pace the highway demands, takes a good part of a morning, and the pleasure is in having done it.
