Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Daugavpils

    Daugavpils is Latvia's second city, standing on the Daugava river in the south-east, closer to Belarus and Lithuania than to Riga. About a hundred thousand people live here, and the mixture is unusual: most are Russian speakers, with Latvians, Poles and Belarusians among them, and Old Believers, followers of a seventeenth-century Russian church tradition, have their own communities in the region of Latgale. The city's temperament is quieter and more provincial than the capital's.

  2. 2. KARISCHKI Gireišiai

    This stop sits in north-eastern Lithuania, in the Aukštaitija highlands near the town of Rokiškis, about twelve kilometres away, and not far from the Latvian border. The name on the map is a mixture of an old German-era spelling and a local village, and the specific site is hard to pin down, so the useful thing is to describe the country around it, which is quiet, gently rolling and full of lakes and woods.

  3. 3. JACOBSTADT Jekabpils

    Jēkabpils lies on the Daugava, Latvia's great river, in the region called Sēlija, and its older German name, Jacobstadt, tells you who founded it. Duke Jacob Kettler of Courland, who ruled this land in the seventeenth century, planted a town here in 1670 and gave it his name, in the same decade he was trying to found colonies in Tobago and Gambia. The duchy was a small, wealthy state that punched above its weight. Across the river sits Krustpils, an older settlement with a castle, and the two towns grew together over the centuries and were merged in 1962.
