Why this ride

A real weekend escape from the flat city grid. 1208 km round from Azul - plan on about 20 h 50 of riding. The stops in between: Sierra de la Ventana, Cerro Ventana view and Tornquist hills. It keeps to quiet secondary roads.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Azul fuel

    Azul is a quiet city of fifty-four thousand on the open pampas of Buenos Aires province, about three hundred kilometres south-west of the capital, and it begins with a fort. In 1832 a frontier outpost was built on the banks of the Arroyo Azul, the Blue Stream, to guard settlers against raids and to push the line of European settlement farther into the plains. The town grew around it, and the grid of streets, wide plazas and low houses still has a frontier feel.

  2. 2. Sierra de la Ventana

    The Sierra de la Ventana is a surprise in the middle of the pampas. After hundreds of kilometres of flat grassland, cattle and wind, the horizon in the south-west of Buenos Aires province lifts into a chain of folded quartzite ridges, the Sierras Australes, that rise to just over a thousand metres, grey and bare on top, and green with streams and groves of poplar below. The range is named for a natural window, the ventana, a gap in the crest of Cerro de la Ventana that frames the sky, and a steep path up the mountain leads to it for those who like walking.

  3. 3. Cerro Ventana view

    The Pampas are famously flat, which is why the Sierra de la Ventana looks so improbable, a long wall of quartzite ridges erupting from the grassland in the south of Buenos Aires province. Cerro de la Ventana is the one with the hole in it. Near the crest, at a little over a thousand metres, erosion has punched a natural window through the rock, and on a clear day from the plain you can see the sky through it like a small bright coin. The trail to it takes a few hours of steady uphill walking, and it is a rite of passage for people from Bahía Blanca and the capital.

  4. 4. Tornquist hills

    West of the Cerro de la Ventana, the hills crowd in around the town of Tornquist and its provincial park, and the country becomes more rugged and more varied. Ernesto Tornquist, a banker and landowner of the late nineteenth century, owned these lands, and the park that bears his name protects a stretch of the Sierras Australes with some of the best walking in the province. Cerro Tres Picos, the highest summit in Buenos Aires province at about 1,239 metres, is nearby, and the surrounding hills carry names like Napostá Grande and Bonete.
