Why this ride

355 km from Luzern to Luzern, about 6 h of riding. It goes by way of Swiss Alps Jungfrau-Aletsch, Old City of Berne, back through Old City of Berne and back through Swiss Alps Jungfrau-Aletsch. It tops out around 2,350 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

The stops

  1. 1. Lucerne

    Start here.

    Lucerne is where central Switzerland likes to show off. The town sits at the end of its lake, with the Reuss river sliding out through the middle, old houses with painted gables along the quay, and mountains on every side, Pilatus to the south and the Rigi to the east. The best-known sight is the Kapellbrücke, the Chapel Bridge, a covered wooden footbridge from the fourteenth century with a stone water tower beside it, and painted panels under the roof. A fire in 1993 burned much of it, and the bridge was rebuilt, so the paintings you see are mostly reconstructions, but the shape is old. Nearby the Lion Monument, carved into a sandstone cliff in 1821, commemorates Swiss guards who died in Paris in 1792, and Mark Twain called it the saddest piece of stone in the world.

  2. 2. Swiss Alps Jungfrau-Aletsch

    About 88 km in.

    No road comes anywhere near. This is a landscape of ice, rock and sky, the first Alpine area to be listed as a World Heritage site, and the riding is all around it rather than through it.

    Read the complete Swiss Alps Jungfrau-Aletsch write-up.

  3. 4. Old City of Berne

    About 178 km in.

    Bern does not shout. The Swiss capital sits on a sandstone spur in a tight loop of the green Aare, with six kilometres of arcaded streets that let you walk the whole old town in the rain without getting wet. It was founded in 1191 by Duke Berthold V of Zähringen, and legend says he named it after the first animal he killed hunting nearby, a bear. The bears have been the town's mascots ever since, and a few still live in the BärenPark down by the river at the end of the Nydeggbrücke.
