Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Tulln - Kastell Hufeisenturm

    Egon Schiele was born in Tulln in 1890, in the station building where his father was stationmaster, and the town that produced one of the most provocative painters of Viennese modernism is today a quiet, prosperous place on the Danube, known as Austria's garden town. Long before that, it was the Roman fort of Comagena, part of the frontier along the Danube that protected the Roman province of Noricum. One of the fort's towers has survived remarkably well: the so-called Salzturm, a horseshoe-shaped tower on the riverbank, one of the best-preserved Roman structures in Austria. It is part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire – The Danube Limes, listed by UNESCO in 2021.

  2. 2. Bacharnsdorf - Wachtturm

    The Wachau is a stretch of the Danube between Melk and Krems where the river runs through a gorge, with terraced vineyards on the slopes and small towns of white walls and baroque towers beside the water. UNESCO listed the cultural landscape in 2000, and it is one of the most beautiful stretches of the river. Bacharnsdorf is a tiny village near Spitz, the town at the heart of the valley, and Weissenkirchen is only a few minutes away.

  3. 3. Semmering Railway

    Carl von Ghega's railway over the Semmering was a bold idea in the 1840s, when many engineers doubted that a conventional locomotive could haul a train over a mountain pass. Built between 1848 and 1854, the line climbs from Gloggnitz to the Semmering summit through fourteen tunnels and across sixteen viaducts, some with two tiers of stone arches, curving around the flanks of the mountains on ledges cut into the rock. It became the first mountain railway inscribed on the UNESCO list, in 1998, and it still carries trains between Vienna and the south, with the new Semmering Base Tunnel under construction beneath it to take over the heavy traffic.
