Why this ride

157 km from Cizer to Cizer, about 2 h 42 of riding. On the way it passes Românași - Largiana / Cetate, Tihău - Grădiște, Rogna - La Bontauă and back through Tihău - Grădiște. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sângeorgiu de Meseș

    Saint George is the patron of this village, and on 23 April, his feast day, Sângeorgiu de Meseș comes to life. Romanians call the day Sângeorz or Sfântul Gheorghe, and in rural Transylvania it has long marked the start of the pastoral year, the day when sheep are taken to the hills and the shepherds make their contracts for the season. Green branches are hung on gates, fires are lit, and people make sure to eat lamb. The village sits in the western Meseș, about six kilometres from Cizer, a village of some two and a half thousand.

  2. 2. Românași - Largiana / Cetate

    At Românași, in the valley of the Agrij in Sălaj county, the outline of a Roman fort called Largiana can still be traced on a low rise above the village, its rectangle of ramparts and ditches softened by centuries of ploughing. It stood on the road that ran from Porolissum, the great frontier stronghold to the north, down towards Napoca, today's Cluj, and it housed an auxiliary unit guarding the approach to the Meseș mountains. Archaeologists have found inscriptions, coins and pottery here, and the site is part of the Dacian frontier listed by UNESCO in 2024.

  3. 3. Tihău - Grădiște

    The Someș river makes its way through the hills of north-western Romania between Cluj and Baia Mare, and near the small town of Jibou, in Sălaj county, it passes through a narrow valley guarded on its slopes by the remains of a Roman fort. The site, known locally as Grădiște, which simply means old fort, lies above the village of Tihău, close to Surduc and Gâlgău Almașului.

  4. 4. Rogna - La Bontauă

    Rogna is a scatter of farms in the hills of northern Sălaj, close to the Someș valley and the border with Maramureș, and La Bontauă is the name the locals give to a piece of high ground where a Roman frontier site has been recorded. The northern edge of Roman Dacia ran through these hills, and the Romans placed watchtowers and small forts on high points to keep an eye on the river valleys and the free Dacians to the north. Today there is little to see apart from the shape of the land, but the view over the rolling hills makes it easy to imagine a sentry looking out across the valley.
