Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Parc National de Masoala

    Masoala is the biggest national park in Madagascar, a huge peninsula of rainforest, mangrove and coral reef in the north-east of the island, and it is listed by UNESCO as part of the Rainforests of the Atsinanana, a group of eastern reserves that hold some of the richest wildlife on earth. Rain falls here all year, and the forest is thick, dark and loud with life. Red ruffed lemurs, found nowhere else, move high in the canopy, and the aye-aye, the strange nocturnal lemur with long fingers, lives in the trees. Chameleons, tomato frogs, leaf-tailed geckos and a dazzling collection of birds fill the undergrowth.

  2. 2. Parc National de Masoala

    Masoala is the great green thumb of Madagascar, a peninsula pushing into the Indian Ocean in the northeast of the island, and the national park that fills it is the biggest protected area in the country. The rainforest here comes down to the beach, and the border between trees and sea is almost nonexistent, so you can stand on white sand with palms behind you and coral in front. Antongil Bay, on the western side, is deep and wide, and humpback whales come there to breed between about July and September.

  3. 3. Parc National de Masoala

    Masoala is the largest national park in Madagascar, covering more than two thousand square kilometers of rainforest on a rugged peninsula in the northeast, and this point lies on its western side, facing Antongil Bay. The bay is wide, deep and sheltered, one of the best places in the Indian Ocean to see humpback whales, which come to calve between about July and September. The town of Maroantsetra, at the head of the bay, is the usual gateway, a quiet, humid place with a river, a market and boats waiting at the shore.
