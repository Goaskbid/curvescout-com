Why this ride

It goes by way of Segment Paredones - Paredones, Segment Mamamag - Mamamag, Historic Centre of Santa Ana de los Ríos d and back through Segment Mamamag - Mamamag. It tops out around 3,750 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Avenue of the Volcanoes

It is 32 km from Segment Mamamag - Mamamag to Historic Centre of Santa Ana de los Ríos d on Avenida Ordoñez Lasso. You lose around 1300 m along the way; the top is 3,859 m. Snow can close it outside summer, so check before you go.

The stops

  1. 1. Segment Santa Martha - Santa Martha

    Start here.

    If Mamamag is the top of the climb, Santa Martha is the other end of the story: the foothills where the Andes tip into the coastal plain, thirty-odd kilometres from Naranjal and a long way from anywhere that feels like highland. The air is thicker and wetter here, and the vegetation changes from tussock grass to ferns, bamboo and the dense green of the western slopes.

    Read the complete Segment Santa Martha - Santa Martha write-up.

  2. 2. Segment Paredones - Paredones

    68 km in.

    West of Cuenca the Andes fall away towards the Pacific in one of the most dramatic descents on the continent, from the cold lakes and páramo of the Cajas massif to the hot banana plantations of the coastal plain. Paredones, a small Inca site in the Molleturo area on this western flank, takes its name from the stone walls, paredones, that survive among the grass: remains of buildings, enclosures and a stretch of the Qhapaq Ñan that crossed these mountains between the highlands and the coast. It is part of the Andean road system inscribed as a World Heritage Site in 2014.

    Read the complete Segment Paredones - Paredones write-up.

  3. 3. Segment Mamamag - Mamamag

    118 km in.

    West of Cuenca the Andes lift quickly, and the Mamamag label sits on the edge of that rise, about twenty-four kilometres from the city, where the cultivated valleys give way to cold, open páramo. The nearest real town is Cuenca itself, a university city of cobbled colonial streets at roughly two and a half thousand metres, and anything you need, from fuel to a decent soup, you should sort out there before you climb.

    Read the complete Segment Mamamag - Mamamag write-up.

  4. 4. Historic Centre of Santa Ana de los Ríos d

    150 km in.

    High in the southern Ecuadorian Andes, at about 2,500 metres, Cuenca was founded by the Spanish in 1557 on a grid plan, on the site of an Inca city called Tomebamba, which was itself built over an earlier Cañari settlement. The colonial centre, with its cobbled streets, whitewashed houses with wooden balconies, churches and plazas, was inscribed by UNESCO in 1999, and the blue-tiled domes of the new cathedral are the city's landmark. The Tomebamba river runs through the city, lined with parks and old houses perched on the bank.

    Read the complete Historic Centre of Santa Ana de los Ríos d write-up.
