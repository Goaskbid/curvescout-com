Why this ride

324 km from Córdoba to Córdoba, about 5 h 35 of riding. The stops in between: Cueva del Encajero, Baeza and back through Cueva del Encajero. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Historic Centre of Cordoba

    Córdoba was once the biggest city in Europe, or so people say, and it is easy to believe when you stand inside the Mezquita. The great mosque was begun in 785 by Abd al-Rahman I, on the site of a Visigothic church, and it grew over two centuries until it held a forest of columns, hundreds of them, in red-and-white striped arches that seem to run on forever. In the sixteenth century a Christian cathedral was built in the middle of it, a clumsy and strangely moving collision of two worlds, and the emperor who approved it is said to have regretted it when he saw the result.

  2. 2. Cueva del Encajero

    Olives, and then more olives. Riding west out of Jaén towards Torredonjimeno you cross one of the most single-minded agricultural landscapes in Europe, a rolling grid of grey-green trees running over every hill to the horizon, broken only by white farmhouses and the occasional oil mill. Somewhere among these low hills near Villardompardo, the Cueva del Encajero is a small rock shelter with traces of prehistoric painting, one of the many scattered sites that make up the World Heritage listing of rock art in Spain's Mediterranean basin.

  3. 3. Baeza

    Baeza is a town of honey-coloured stone set on a ridge above a sea of olive trees. From its edge, the view drops over the Guadalquivir valley to the dark line of the Sierra Mágina, and in every direction the hills are covered with orderly rows of olives, millions of them, the source of more olive oil than almost anywhere on earth. The town's Renaissance palaces, churches and squares, together with those of neighbouring Úbeda, make up a World Heritage site.
