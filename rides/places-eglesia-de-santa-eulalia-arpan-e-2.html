Why this ride

From Vielha it runs 384 km to Vielha; allow about 7 h 18 of riding. It goes by way of Cueva de Revilla, Arpán E 2 and back through Cueva de Revilla. It tops out around 2,000 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

The roads

Pyrenees N260 Route

Cueva de Revilla to Arpán E 2: 72 km, mostly on A-2205. A proper up-and-over: 400 m of climbing, 1,122 m at the top, 710 m down. This is the bit the ride is built around.

The stops

  1. 1. Eglésia de Santa Eulàlia

    Start here.

    The little Romanesque church of Santa Eulàlia stands at Erill la Vall, one of the villages of the Vall de Boí in the Catalan Pyrenees, and its tall, slim bell tower is a landmark from the road. Together with ten other churches and hermitages in the valley, it was listed by UNESCO in 2000, and the whole group is a small marvel. They were built in the early twelfth century, in a style borrowed from Lombardy, with stout stone walls, round arches and bell towers decorated with blind arcades. Inside, the frescoes that once covered the walls were taken down in the early twentieth century and are now in the museum in Barcelona, while replicas have been placed on site.

  2. 2. Cueva de Revilla

    115 km in.

    Revilla is one of the most spectacularly placed hamlets in the Spanish Pyrenees, perched on a ledge above the Garganta de Escuaín, a deep gorge cut into the limestone of the Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park. It looks down into the canyon from a terrace of meadows, with the high peaks behind. The Cueva de Revilla is a cave in the cliffs near the hamlet, and the area between Bielsa, Plan and Boltaña is some of the wildest country in Aragón.

    Read the complete Cueva de Revilla write-up.

  3. 3. Arpán E 2

    187 km in.

    The Río Vero is not a big river, but it has spent a very long time doing something clever with the limestone of the Sierra de Guara. Above Alquézar it has cut a gorge that narrows to a few metres in places and widens again into pools the colour of bottle glass, and the plateau on both sides is a dry country of box, holm oak and aromatic scrub that bears almost no relation to the water below. Arpán is a small side-valley three kilometres from the village, a short walk off the road, and its shelter is one of several in the Vero canyon system that carry painted figures from the ancient hunters of the Levantine tradition.
