  1. 1. Yerevan

    Yerevan is a pink city. The stone that built it is volcanic tuff, in shades from rose to apricot to grey, and when the evening light hits the apartment blocks and the opera house it glows as if lit from inside. It is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, founded as the fortress of Erebuni in 782 BC, and yet most of what you see was laid out in the Soviet years by the architect Alexander Tamanian, in a ring plan around a great central square. A little over a million people live here, about a third of the country, and the capital feels like the engine room of Armenia.

  2. 2. Archaeological site of Zvartnots with ruin

    Zvartnots is a ruin with a great deal of ghost left in it. The name means roughly the temple of the celestial angels, and it was built in the seventh century by the Armenian Catholicos Nerses III, who wanted a church of astonishing size and shape: a round, three-storey building rising from a cross-shaped plan, with a high central dome and a ring of columns. For a few centuries it dominated this plain between the Hrazdan river and the old capital of Vagharshapat. Then it collapsed, probably in an earthquake some time before the tenth century, and the stones lay forgotten under a mound until archaeologists dug them out in the early twentieth century.

  3. 3. Saint Hripsimeh Church and St. Shoghakat C

    This stop is for the stone itself. Hripsime church is one of the oldest buildings in Armenia still standing as it was built: a compact, square-planned church topped by a drum and a conical dome, finished in 618 under the Catholicos Komitas, and so well proportioned that architects have been copying the plan for fourteen centuries. It sits in a green enclosure on the western side of Vagharshapat, a short ride from the cathedral at Echmiatsin, and its tuff walls have gone the colour of dark honey.

  5. 5. Cathedral and Churches of Echmiatsin and t

    Armenians call this the Mother See, and the phrase is meant literally: Echmiatsin is the spiritual centre of the Armenian Apostolic Church, the seat of the Catholicos, the place the faith is governed from. The cathedral stands on the edge of Vagharshapat, a flat town of about forty thousand on the Ararat plain west of Yerevan. Tradition holds that Gregory the Illuminator, after the king converted around the year 301, saw a vision of Christ descending with a golden hammer to mark where the church should rise. That vision gave the place its name: Echmiatsin means the descent of the only-begotten. The present building has been rebuilt and added to many times, but its bones date to the fifth century and the core to even earlier.
