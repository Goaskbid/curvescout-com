Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Near Thonon-les-Bains

    The Route des Grandes Alpes officially begins on the shore of Lake Geneva at Thonon-les-Bains and ends some seven hundred kilometres later on the Mediterranean at Menton. Standing here at the start, with the lake spread out flat and silver in front of you and the Swiss shore hazy on the far side, it is hard to believe that within an hour you will be climbing into proper mountains. That contrast is the whole romance of the thing.

  2. 2. Near Modane

    This spot lies in the Maurienne, the long valley of the river Arc that runs from the high Alps down toward Chambéry, and the town of Modane sits at the heart of it. For centuries this was the way across the mountains to Italy, via the Mont Cenis pass, and in the nineteenth century engineers drove a railway tunnel under the Fréjus massif, which opened in 1871 and gave the town its role as a border station. A road tunnel followed in 1980. Today Modane is quiet and a bit industrial, but its position makes it a natural stopping place.

  3. 3. Waypoint 3

    The Route des Grandes Alpes is a French invention of the early twentieth century, a line drawn from Lake Geneva to the Mediterranean to link the high passes, and at this point on it you are in the quiet southern part, Haute-Provence, where the Alps run out of height and begin to smell of thyme.
