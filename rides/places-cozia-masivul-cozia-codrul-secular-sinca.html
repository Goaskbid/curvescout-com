Why this ride

A 343 km round trip from Brezoi - roughly 8 h 51 in the saddle. On the way it passes Câmpulung - Jidova 1, Codrul secular Șinca and back through Câmpulung - Jidova 1. It tops out around 1,200 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

The roads

Transfagarasan Highway

Câmpulung - Jidova 1 is 82 km from Cozia - Masivul Cozia on Strada Valea Iașului. You lose around 1290 m along the way; the top is 1,586 m. This is the bit the ride is built around.

The stops

  1. 1. Cozia - Masivul Cozia

    Start here.

    The Olt is one of the few rivers in Romania that cut straight through the Carpathians instead of going around them, and one of the places where it does it is the foot of the Cozia massif. A narrow gorge, a few kilometres long, runs between steep wooded walls, with the river dark and fast at the bottom, a railway and a road squeezed in beside it, and the ridge of Cozia towering on one side. The mountain itself is a rounded, forested block of crystalline rock, high enough to hold snow into spring, and much of it is protected as a national park, with beech, fir and oak, chamois on the high crags, bears in the forest and a good scattering of rare plants.

  2. 2. Câmpulung - Jidova 1

    83 km in.

    Câmpulung Muscel lies at the foot of the Carpathians in northern Argeș county, and it claims the title of the first capital of Wallachia. Tradition credits its founding as a princely seat to the legendary Negru Vodă in the thirteenth century, and the old princely court and the Negru Vodă monastery in the town centre recall those early days of the Romanian principality. The town spreads along the valley of the Râul Târgului, with the mountains of Iezer and Piatra Craiului rising steeply to the north.

    Read the complete Câmpulung - Jidova 1 write-up.

  3. 3. Codrul secular Șinca

    171 km in.

    Codru is an old Romanian word for a deep forest, and the Codrul Secular Șinca, the ancient forest of Șinca, is exactly that: a stand of old-growth beech and fir on the northern slopes of the Făgăraș mountains in Brașov county, where trees have grown, died and fallen for centuries without being cut. It is part of the UNESCO listing of the ancient and primeval beech forests of Europe, a chain of protected forests across many countries that together tell the story of how beech spread across the continent after the last ice age. Walking among trees that are hundreds of years old, with fallen trunks covered in moss and fungi and the canopy closing high above, is a humbling experience.
