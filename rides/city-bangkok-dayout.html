Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bangkok

    Bangkok is a city you meet with all your senses at once: the smell of charcoal grills and jasmine garlands, the sound of tuk-tuks and boat engines on the river, the heat that reaches you before you step out of the hotel lobby. About five million people live in the city proper and many more in the sprawl around it, and the Chao Phraya river is the thread that still holds the old centre together. The Grand Palace, Wat Pho with its huge reclining Buddha, and the temples of the old quarter sit along its eastern bank, and ferries cross the water for a few baht.

  2. 2. Historic City of Ayutthaya

    For more than four hundred years Ayutthaya was one of the richest cities in Asia, founded in the mid-fourteenth century on an island where three rivers meet, and visited by Portuguese, Dutch, French, Japanese and Persian traders who all had their own quarters. Then in 1767 a Burmese army took it after a long siege and burned most of it to the ground. The Thai court moved south, eventually to Bangkok, and what remained became a field of brick ruins, the prangs and chedis standing above the grass. UNESCO listed the site in 1991.
