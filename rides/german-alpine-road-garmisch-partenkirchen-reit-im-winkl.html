Why this ride

Out of Garmisch-Partenkirchen, one way: 156 km, roughly 2 h 33 in the saddle. It goes by way of Walchensee and Tegernsee. The highest point is about 1,550 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Garmisch Partenkirchen

    Garmisch-Partenkirchen lies in a wide Alpine valley in Bavaria, close to the Austrian border, with the Wetterstein range rising behind it and the Zugspitze, at 2,962 metres the highest mountain in Germany, standing at the end of the valley. The town is really two, Garmisch and Partenkirchen, joined together for the 1936 Winter Olympics, and each still has its own character. Partenkirchen, the older part, has a Roman history, as it stood on the road across the Alps, and its main street, with painted houses, is the more charming.

  2. 2. Walchensee

    Walchensee is one of the deepest and clearest alpine lakes in Germany, lying at about eight hundred metres in the Bavarian Pre-Alps, with a surface the colour of turquoise glass on a good day and dark green on a stormy one. The lake is surrounded by forested mountains, with the Herzogstand and the Heimgarten rising to the north, and it has a calm, almost Scandinavian look in the early morning.

  3. 3. Tegernsee

    Tegernsee is a lake town in the Bavarian foothills, at the eastern edge of the Tegernsee valley, with the lake in front and steep, forested mountains behind. The Benedictine monastery that gave the place its name was founded in the eighth century, and its church and cloister still dominate the shore, though the buildings are now partly a secondary school and partly a brewery tavern, the Bräustüberl, where locals and visitors share long tables and big pretzels in the courtyard. It is a lively place on a sunny afternoon, with a crowd of beer drinkers, cyclists and bikers.

  4. 4. Reit im Winkl

    Reit im Winkl is the snow-sure village at the end of the Chiemgau Alps, a pocket of Bavaria wedged into the corner where Germany meets Austria. At about 700 metres, it sits in a high, green bowl among steep peaks, with the Kaisergebirge, the jagged limestone range of the Tyrolean Kaiser mountains, standing across the border to the south. The snow here is dependable in winter, and the place has produced a number of Olympic skiers.
