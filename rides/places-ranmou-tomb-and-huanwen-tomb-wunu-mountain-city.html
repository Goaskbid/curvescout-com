  2. 2. Wandu Mountain City

    Wandu is the mountain fortress that Koguryo's rulers built to retreat to when their lowland capital was threatened, and it still stands above the Yalu valley, a long line of stone walls following the contours of a steep ridge. Its builders chose a site where cliffs and slopes made natural defences, and the gates, towers, lookout platforms and palace foundations are visible in the forest. It is a place of long silences and big views, the sort of site that makes the strategy of the old kingdom obvious.

  3. 3. Changchuan Tomb No. 1

    Changchuan Tomb No. 1 is among the painted tombs of the ancient Koguryo kingdom, a state that ruled northern Korea and much of Manchuria from the first centuries BC until 668. The tomb itself lies on the Chinese bank, in a valley of fields and low hills where the Koguryo kings had one of their capitals.

  4. 4. Capital Cities and Tombs of the Ancient Ko

    Few places show the ambition of a lost kingdom as clearly as the valley of the Yalu at Ji'an, where Koguryo built three cities and left forty tombs that UNESCO listed in 2004. The kingdom began in the first century BC, grew into a regional power and fought off Chinese dynasties for centuries, and at its height it controlled territory from Manchuria to the middle of the Korean peninsula. Its warriors rode armoured horses, its builders cut huge blocks of granite, and its artists painted the walls of tombs with scenes that still glow.

  5. 5. Wunu Mountain City

    Wunu Mountain City is a ruined fortress on a flat-topped crag in Liaoning province, in the green hills of northeastern China, and it was probably the first capital of the Goguryeo kingdom. Before that kingdom grew into a power that rivalled Chinese dynasties, its founders chose a mountain stronghold above a river for safety. Cliffs shelter the plateau on almost every side, and the builders closed the few gaps with stone walls. Inside, there are traces of buildings, a spring, a soldier's quarters and a platform that may have served as a lookout. It is part of the Capital Cities and Tombs of the Ancient Koguryo Kingdom, listed by UNESCO in 2004, which takes in sites around Huanren and Ji'an.
