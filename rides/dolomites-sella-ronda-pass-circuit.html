Why this ride

Four iconic passes, rock walls and pure alpine theatre. 118 km round from Selva - plan on about 2 h 16 of riding. The climbs: Sella Pass, Pordoi Pass and Campolongo Pass. It tops out at about 2,250 m. It is a high-mountain day.

Scenic road chapters

Great Dolomites Road

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 26 km this is Great Dolomites Road, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Gardena Pass

    Gardena Pass, or Passo Gardena in Italian and Grödner Joch in German, climbs to 2,121 meters in the heart of the Dolomites, linking the Val Gardena with the Val Badia. The road crosses a high saddle between the huge grey cliffs of the Sella massif and the Puez-Odle range, and the view from the top is one of those scenes that make people stop and fall silent. The Dolomites were inscribed by UNESCO in 2009, valued for their pale limestone towers and for the way the rock changes color from white to pink to deep red at sunrise and sunset, a glow the local people call enrosadira.

  2. 2. Sella Pass

    The Sella Pass is the one the Dolomites are famous for, even if the locals would argue that every pass here is. At 2,244 metres, it connects the Val Gardena with the Val di Fassa, running between the sheer grey towers of the Sassolungo, or Langkofel, on one side and the great fortress mass of the Sella group on the other. The rock is dolomite, a pale limestone that glows pink and orange at sunset, an effect the Ladin people call enrosadira, and the setting is the sort of place where cyclists weep and motorcyclists forget to ride.

  3. 3. Pordoi Pass

    Pordoi is the high point of the Sella Ronda, a pass of 2,239 metres between the Fassa valley and Arabba, and for many people the one with the best view in the Dolomites. The road climbs from Canazei in about thirty hairpins to the saddle, where a big car park, a few restaurants and the cable car to Sass Pordoi cluster beneath the pale cliffs of the Sella massif. On a clear day, the rock turns pink and gold at dusk, the effect known locally as the enrosadira.

  4. 4. Campolongo Pass

    Campolongo is the quiet pass in a family of loud ones. At 1,875 metres it links Corvara in Alta Badia with Arabba and the upper valley of Livinallongo, and on the Sella Ronda it is the stretch that most riders ride without thinking, between the more famous climbs of Pordoi and Gardena. That undersells it. The road winds up through pasture and scattered spruce from Corvara with a long, relaxed run of hairpins, and from the top the whole of the Sella massif stands over you, a pale, castellated block of limestone that looks as if it was built rather than eroded.

    Read the complete Campolongo Pass write-up.

  5. 5. Arabba overnight base

    Arabba is a small village in the Livinallongo valley, at about sixteen hundred metres, and for a motorcyclist it is one of the best places in the Dolomites to sleep. It lies at the foot of the Pordoi, Campolongo and Falzarego passes, close enough to the Marmolada, the highest peak in the range at about 3,340 metres, to see the glacier on a clear day. Cable cars climb from the village in winter and summer, the slopes behind are green with meadows and grazing cows, and when the day-trippers leave the passes in the evening, the place settles into quiet.

Pordoi Pass to Campolongo Pass

This Pordoi Pass → Campolongo Pass stretch is for rhythm and scenery. Keep your head up, let the view breathe, and resist turning every open bend into a target. Stop once properly rather than half-stopping everywhere.
