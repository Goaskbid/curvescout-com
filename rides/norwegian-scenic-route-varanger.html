Why this ride

A one-way ride of 238 km, Vuonnabahta to Vardø - roughly 3 h 13 in the saddle. The stops in between: Vadso and Ekkeroy. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Varangerbotn

    Varangerbotn lies at the innermost point of the Varangerfjord in Finnmark, and it is a small place with a large role as the capital of the Sami culture of the east. The fjord is wide and cold, opening into the Barents Sea, and the landscape around is open tundra, low hills and scattered birch. The village has a Sami museum, the Várjjat Sámi Musea, which tells the story of the Sea Sami, who lived by fishing on the coast. Services are modest, and Kirkenes, to the east, and Vadsø, to the north, are the larger towns in the region.

  2. 2. Vadso

    Vadsø is the main town of the Varanger peninsula, and it feels like it knows it is on the edge of things. About five thousand people live here, on a low, treeless shore facing a fjord that opens straight into the Barents Sea, and the light off that water in summer is flat, silvery and long. It is the administrative centre of Finnmark, which explains the solid public buildings, and it has a harbour, a main street, a church and the sort of supermarket you start to appreciate when you have been riding the empty tundra for a day or two.

  3. 3. Ekkeroy

    Ekkerøy is a small peninsula sticking out into the Varangerfjord, joined to the mainland by a narrow causeway, and it is one of the best places in the Arctic to watch seabirds from the road. Thousands of kittiwakes nest on the cliffs in summer, packed on every ledge, and the noise and smell are overwhelming in the best way. The birds wheel and shriek and drop off the cliff in clouds, and on a calm day you can sit with a sandwich and watch for as long as you like. Eider ducks, shags and gulls share the shore, and out on the water you may see more.

  4. 4. Vardo

    Vardø is the easternmost town in Norway, on a small island in the Barents Sea, and it is reached by an undersea tunnel, the Vardø tunnel, built in the 1980s. The town has about two thousand people, wooden houses painted in soft colours, a harbour and a fortress. The Vardøhus Fortress, finished in 1738, is the northernmost fortress in the world, and it stands guard over the entrance to the Arctic. The town has been a fishing port for centuries, and in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, it was the site of a terrible witch hunt, in which about ninety people, mostly women, were burned at the stake. A memorial by the artist Louise Bourgeois and the architect Peter Zumthor, called the Steilneset Memorial, stands on the shore, with a long wooden hall lit by one bulb for each victim.
