Why this ride

A 158 km round trip from Andernach - roughly 3 h 14 in the saddle. It goes by way of Watchtowers WP 1/53 to 1/57, Limessection north of Arzbach, Limessection between watchtowers WP 2/13 a and Upper Middle Rhine Valley.

The roads

Moselle Wine Route

The 18 km between Watchtowers WP 1/23 to 1/24 and Watchtowers WP 1/53 to 1/57, mostly on B 42. An easy profile, never above 288 m. Take it at your own pace.

The stops

  1. 1. Watchtowers WP 1/23 to 1/24

    Start here.

    The Upper German-Raetian Limes began at the Rhine, at Rheinbrohl, and climbed into the hills on the east bank, and towers WP 1/23 and 1/24, near the wine village of Leutesdorf, are among the first along its route. They are now marked by foundations or mounds in the forest above the river, and they are part of the World Heritage listing. From here the frontier ran for more than five hundred kilometres to the Danube, and it is fascinating to stand at the very beginning of such an enormous undertaking.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 1/23 to 1/24 write-up.

  2. 2. Watchtowers WP 1/53 to 1/57

    18 km in.

    This stretch of the Limes sits close to Bendorf and Stromberg, on the plateau above the Rhine near Koblenz, and the watchtowers numbered here, five in all, follow the line as it climbs from the river. The frontier was a controlled boundary: a palisade or bank, a ditch, and a series of towers within sight of each other, with a patrol path behind. Traces survive as low banks in forest and field, and some positions have been marked or partly reconstructed, which makes this a good place to understand how the system worked.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 1/53 to 1/57 write-up.

  3. 3. Limessection north of Arzbach

    37 km in.

    North of Arzbach the land lifts out of the Lahn valley and into the Westerwald, and the Roman frontier did the same. The Limes here is a section rather than a single tower, a line where a ditch, bank and a string of watchtowers once ran across the ridge. It is a faint thing now, a low earthwork under trees, but you can follow it on foot if you know where to look, and the local Limes trail marks the way. The nearest villages, Arzbach and Kadenbach, are small and quiet, with only a couple of thousand people between them.

    Read the complete Limessection north of Arzbach write-up.

  4. 4. Limessection between watchtowers WP 2/13 a

    62 km in.

    Up on the plateau of the Hintertaunus, north of the Rhine gorge, the Roman frontier crossed a quiet landscape of fields and forest near Singhofen, and a section of it between two watchtowers survives here as a faint bank and ditch running through the woods. This is the Upper German Limes, which ran for more than five hundred kilometres from the Rhine to the Danube, marking the boundary between the Roman Empire and the free Germanic tribes from the second century until the Romans withdrew in the third. It was not a great wall like Hadrian's in Britain, but a line of watchtowers, forts and a timber palisade, later strengthened with a ditch and bank. Together with the Raetian Limes, it was listed by UNESCO in 2005.

    Read the complete Limessection between watchtowers WP 2/13 a write-up.

  5. 5. Upper Middle Rhine Valley

    79 km in.

    Between Bingen and Koblenz, the Rhine cuts a deep, winding gorge through the slate hills of the Rhenish Massif, and for centuries this has been one of the great landscapes of Europe: steep slopes covered with vineyards, castles on every crag, villages of half-timbered houses along the river, and barges gliding past. The Upper Middle Rhine Valley was listed by UNESCO in 2002, and Sankt Goarshausen sits at its heart, at the foot of the most famous rock on the river.

    Read the complete Upper Middle Rhine Valley write-up.
