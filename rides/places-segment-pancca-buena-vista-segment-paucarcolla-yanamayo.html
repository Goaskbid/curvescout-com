Why this ride

A 392 km round trip from Santa Rosa - roughly 6 h 46 in the saddle. The stops in between: Segment Q’omer Moqo-Nicasio, Segment Paucarcolla-Yanamayo and back through Segment Q’omer Moqo-Nicasio. The highest point is about 4,350 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Segment Pancca-Buena Vista

    A section of the old Inca road, the Qhapaq Ñan, runs through the area, part of the huge network that the Incas built to link Cusco with the far reaches of their empire, now recognised as a World Heritage site. In places the road survives as a paved or stone-edged track that crosses pasture and peat, a reminder of how far a runner or llama train once travelled.

  2. 2. Segment Q’omer Moqo-Nicasio

    Lampa, twenty-four kilometres away, is known in Peru as the Pink City, and it gives you a place to orient this high, windswept stretch of the altiplano north of Lake Titicaca. The Inca road passed through this country as part of the Qhapaq Ñan, tying Cusco to the Collao plateau, and the segment from Q'omer Moqo, a name that means something like green hillock in Quechua, to Nicasio crosses plains of tall grass, small farms and broad skies.

  3. 3. Segment Paucarcolla-Yanamayo

    The Qhapaq Nan is the great road system of the Inca, a web of paths, causeways and stairways that ran for more than thirty thousand kilometres across the Andes, and a stretch near Puno is part of it. The segment between Paucarcolla and Yanamayo follows the plateau around the northwest shore of Lake Titicaca, where the Inca built a road to link the Collao, the great high plain, with the heart of the empire at Cusco. The route is now part of the UNESCO listing of the Andean Road System, which was granted in 2014 and spans six countries.
