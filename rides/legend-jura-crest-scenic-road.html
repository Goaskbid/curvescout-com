Why this ride

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Nyon

    Nyon was a Roman town before most of Europe had names, founded by Julius Caesar around 45 BC as Colonia Iulia Equestris, a settlement for veteran cavalry on the shore of Lake Geneva. Columns from the Roman forum still stand by the lake, and a small museum in the old basilica shows what remains. Today it is a pleasant town of about seventeen thousand people with a castle on the hill, a waterfront promenade and a market, and it has the quiet, wealthy air of a place that has been well looked after for two thousand years.

  5. 5. Sainte-Croix

    Sainte-Croix lies on a sunny shelf of the Vaud Jura, with the Chasseron ridge rising behind it and the Swiss plateau stretched out far below. The town is about four thousand people, but it punches above its weight in one very specific area: mechanical things that make music. For more than a century this was the capital of Swiss music boxes and automata, and the industry grew into a wider tradition of precision engineering. The Paillard company, makers of Bolex cameras and Hermes typewriters, was founded here, and the town's wealth shows in the solid old buildings along the main street.
