Why this ride

A 89 km round trip from Frankfurt am Main - roughly 1 h 32 in the saddle. It goes by way of Watchtower WP 3/23*, Watchtowers WP 3/23, Watchtower WP 3/19* to 3/21* and Watchtowers WP 3/20 to 3/22.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Frankfurt

    Frankfurt is the one German city with a skyline, and the locals have a nickname for it: Mainhattan. A cluster of glass towers rises from the north bank of the river Main, the banking district, and you can see it from the Taunus hills on clear days. The rest of the city is more modest than the towers suggest. The old centre was heavily bombed in the war and has been rebuilt in places, with the Römer, a medieval town hall with stepped gables, restored on its square and the Cathedral of St Bartholomew, where emperors were crowned, still standing by the river.

  2. 2. Watchtower WP 3/23*

    On the wooded ridges near Idstein, the Taunus Limes crossed some of its highest and most remote ground, and Watchtower WP 3/23 is one of the many sites along this stretch. The asterisk in the official label suggests a site whose exact location is uncertain or reconstructed from survey work, which is common along the frontier, where many towers survive only as faint mounds in the forest. Still, walking the Limes trail here, with the bank and ditch running through the beech woods, gives a real sense of the Roman border.

  3. 3. Watchtowers WP 3/23

    Idstein sits in a bowl of the Taunus hills north of Wiesbaden, and its centre is among the best-kept half-timbered towns in Hesse. The Killinger House, a tall timber-framed building with elaborate carving, is the showpiece, and the Witches' Tower, a stone remnant of the old castle, stands above the old town as a reminder of a darker chapter. The Roman Limes passes through the hills just to the north, and Watchtower 3/23 is one of many positions on the ridge, part of the line that ran for about five hundred kilometres across Germany.

  4. 4. Watchtower WP 3/19* to 3/21*

    The Taunus does not shout. It is a rounded, forested upland north of Wiesbaden and Frankfurt, and the Roman frontier crossed it on its way from the Rhine to the Main. These numbered watchtowers near Idstein belong to the Upper Germanic frontier, which in this part of Hesse ran through woods and clearings along the high ground. Archaeologists have recorded the tower positions along the line, and the asterisks in the label are their way of marking sites that are uncertain or only partly known. In practice you find woodland, a trail and the faint trace of an old bank.

  5. 5. Watchtowers WP 3/20 to 3/22

    The Taunus is the low, wooded range north of Frankfurt, and the Romans chose it as the place to draw their line. The Limes crossed the ridge at a height that gave good views over both sides, and watchtowers placed close together made it possible to send signals by fire and smoke from tower to tower. The numbers, such as WP 3/20, are from the survey carried out by German archaeologists at the end of the nineteenth century, and they refer to the third section of the line. The Upper Germanic-Raetian Limes was listed by UNESCO in 2005.
