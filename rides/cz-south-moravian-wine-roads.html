Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Brno

    Brno is the second city of the Czech Republic, but it behaves like the first city of Moravia, with its own dialect, its own pride and its own very good wine. About 370,000 people live here, a lot of them students, and the centre has the mixture of old and new you would expect: a medieval core below a hilltop castle, baroque churches, and a serious collection of twentieth-century modern architecture.

  2. 2. Mikulov

    Mikulov stands beneath a white limestone ridge, the Pálava hills, and from a distance its castle on the rock and the pale church tower on the Holy Hill look like a stage set. It is the southernmost town in Moravia, a few kilometres from the Austrian border, and it has been a crossroads for centuries, a place where Czech, German and Jewish cultures lived together. In German it was Nikolsburg, and for a long period it was one of the most important Jewish communities in central Europe.

  3. 3. Lednice

    Lednice is a palace, a park and a surprising piece of luxury in the south of Moravia. The palace belonged to the princes of Liechtenstein, who ruled these lands for centuries and turned them into a vast landscaped estate, and the building that stands at the end of the village today is a neo-Gothic confection of towers and pinnacles, rebuilt in the nineteenth century in the English style. Around it lies a huge park with ponds, avenues and follies, and together with the neighbouring Valtice, the whole area is a UNESCO World Heritage site, listed in 1996 as the Lednice-Valtice cultural landscape.

    Read the complete Lednice write-up.

  4. 4. Valtice

    Valtice is the wine capital of the Czech Republic, and it looks the part. A large baroque palace dominates the town, built by the Liechtenstein family, who held the estate for centuries and made it a showpiece of their wealth. The palace has a grand courtyard, a riding hall and enormous wine cellars beneath it, and part of the cellars now houses the National Wine Centre, where visitors can taste a hundred of the best Czech wines, chosen in an annual competition. For anyone who enjoys wine, this is the place to learn about the country's vineyards.
