Why this ride

A 398 km round trip from Yverdon-les-Bains - roughly 6 h 22 in the saddle. On the way it passes Dorfstation, Chapelle Notre-Dame-du-Haut de Ronchamp and back through Dorfstation. It crosses from Switzerland into France - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Jura Crest Scenic Road

Dorfstation to back through Dorfstation: 280 km, mostly on N 19. Over the top at 1,316 m - roughly 1850 m up and 1820 m down. Fill the tank before the climb.

The stops

  1. 1. Baie de Clendy

    Start here.

    At the southern tip of Lake Neuchâtel, just east of Yverdon-les-Bains, the shore curves into a shallow bay fringed with reeds, poplars and a beach where local families swim in summer. Behind it, in a meadow, stands a double row of tall grey stones. These are the menhirs of Clendy, raised by Neolithic farmers more than five thousand years ago and re-erected in the twentieth century after they were found toppled in the ground. Offshore and along the bay lie the remains of lakeside settlements of the same era, part of the UNESCO listing of prehistoric pile dwellings around the Alps.

    Read the complete Baie de Clendy write-up.

  2. 2. Dorfstation

    59 km in.

    The south shore of Lake Biel is a gentle place of reeds, vineyards on the far bank and farmland stretching back towards the Seeland plain, and at Lüscherz, one of the small lakeside villages, the remains of a prehistoric pile-dwelling settlement lie in the shallows. Dorfstation, as the site is known, is part of the transnational World Heritage Site of Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps, inscribed in 2011. Neolithic and Bronze Age farmers built their houses on wooden piles along the shore, and the waterlogged ground preserved timbers, tools, textiles and food remains for thousands of years.

    Read the complete Dorfstation write-up.

  3. 3. Chapelle Notre-Dame-du-Haut de Ronchamp

    203 km in.

    Notre-Dame-du-Haut sits on a hill above Ronchamp, at the foot of the Vosges, and it is one of the strangest and most beautiful buildings of the twentieth century. Le Corbusier designed it in the early 1950s and it was consecrated in 1955. The white walls curve and lean, the roof sweeps up like a hull or a hat, and the thick walls are pierced by small deep windows filled with coloured glass. Inside, light falls in coloured pools on the floor, and the whole room feels carved rather than built. There has been a pilgrim chapel on this hill for centuries, and the earlier church was destroyed in the war, which is what led to the commission.

    Read the complete Chapelle Notre-Dame-du-Haut de Ronchamp write-up.
