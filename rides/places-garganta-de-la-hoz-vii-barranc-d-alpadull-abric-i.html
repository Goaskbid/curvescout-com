Why this ride

A 281 km round trip from Villajoyosa - roughly 4 h 51 in the saddle. On the way it passes Cueva de las Arañas del Carabassi, Palmeral of Elche, Barranc d'Alpadull- Abric I and back through Palmeral of Elche. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Garganta de la Hoz VII

    Everyone in Spain associates the hills north of Alicante with one thing: turrón. The town of Xixona, in the valley behind the coast, has been making the almond-and-honey nougat for centuries, and the soft, oily version, turrón blando, and the hard, brittle one, turrón duro, are shipped across the country at Christmas. Almonds grow all over these slopes, and you will pass many groves on a ride. Whether you pick up a box from a factory shop or a roadside stall, it is the right souvenir for a rider with a pannier.

  2. 2. Cueva de las Arañas del Carabassi

    The Carabassí is one of the last stretches of wild-ish coast between Alicante and Santa Pola: a line of dunes held together by pine and marram grass, a long beach, and the sea glittering under the flight path of Alicante airport. In the low rocky ground behind it lies a small cave known for painted marks, the Cueva de las Arañas del Carabassí, close to the village of El Altet and a short hop from Santa Pola.

  3. 3. Palmeral of Elche

    Riding into Elche from the dry hills of southern Alicante, the sudden forest of date palms feels like a mistake of geography, as if a piece of North Africa had been set down in Spain. In a sense it was. The palm groves were laid out in their present form during the Islamic period, when Arab settlers brought an irrigation system of channels fed by the Vinalopó river and arranged the orchards in walled plots, with palms on the edges and crops beneath. Tens of thousands of palms remain today in and around the city, the largest palm grove in Europe, and the Palmeral was added to the UNESCO list in 2000.

  4. 4. Barranc d'Alpadull- Abric I

    The painted shelter of Barranc d'Alpadull sits in the folds of the Serra de Mariola, the limestone massif that rises between Bocairent, Alfafara and Agres in the inland corner where Valencia, Alicante and the old kingdom's hill country all meet. It is one of the many small overhangs in eastern Spain that carry Levantine rock art, the group of sites inscribed together by UNESCO as the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin. Do not expect a museum. These are faint, reddish figures under a lip of stone, usually fenced, often hard to read unless the light is low and raking, and part of the pleasure is simply knowing that hunters drew here thousands of years before anyone thought of building a road up this valley.
