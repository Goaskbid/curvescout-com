Why this ride

248 km from Alpera to Alpera, about 4 h 17 of riding. On the way it passes Cueva moma, Abric de les finestres IV and back through Cueva moma. The highest point is about 850 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abrigo II

    Alpera is a small town on the plateau between Albacete and the Valencian coast, in the flat, windy country where La Mancha begins to tilt towards the sea. It is best known among archaeologists for Cueva de la Vieja, a shelter with one of the largest collections of Levantine paintings in the interior, and Abrigo II is part of the same group. The cluster of figures includes human forms, animals and symbols, painted in red and black, and the whole group is part of the UNESCO listing for the Mediterranean basin.

  2. 2. Cueva moma

    Bicorp's name appears on the map of rock art mainly because of its famous spider cave, but the Cueva Moma sits a few kilometres south-west of the village and has its own quiet claim to attention. It is a Levantine painted site in the dry highlands of the Canal de Navarrés, in the middle of inland Valencia, where the hills are steep, the villages small and the roads wander.

  3. 3. Abric de les finestres IV

    Bocairent is a village built on a rock, its old houses clinging to a limestone spur above a ravine in the Serra de Mariola, in the south of the province of Valencia. Across the ravine, in the cliff face, a row of small square openings looks out like windows: these are the Covetes dels Moros, a series of artificial caves cut into the rock, connected by internal passages, whose purpose is still debated. They may have been granaries, refuges or even a monastery, and their name means the little caves of the Moors.
