Why this ride

From Bellinzona it runs 219 km to Bellinzona; allow about 3 h 47 of riding. It goes by way of Sacri Monti of Piedmont and Lombardy, Monte San Giorgio, Bodio centrale o delle Monete and back through Bodio centrale o delle Monete. The highest point is about 1,550 m. It crosses from Switzerland into Italy - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Three Castles

    Bellinzona is a small city in a very big position. It stands where the Ticino valley opens into the Magadino plain, at the point where the roads from the San Gottardo and San Bernardino passes both come down from the Alps, and whoever held it controlled the way between Milan and the north. That is why three castles crown the rocky hills above the town, linked by a long wall that once barred the whole valley. Castelgrande, the oldest, stands on a rock in the centre, Montebello on a spur above, and Sasso Corbaro on a high hill farther back, and together they were listed by UNESCO in 2000 as outstanding examples of medieval fortification.

  2. 2. Sacri Monti of Piedmont and Lombardy

    On the western shore of Lake Como, above the village of Ossuccio and facing the little Isola Comacina, a cobbled path climbs the hillside through olive groves and woods, past fourteen small chapels, to a sanctuary at the top. This is the Sacro Monte of Ossuccio, one of nine sacred mountains in Piedmont and Lombardy listed together by UNESCO. Built in the seventeenth century, the chapels illustrate the Mysteries of the Rosary with life-size terracotta and plaster figures arranged in dramatic scenes, each one a small piece of theatre.

  3. 3. Monte San Giorgio

    Monte San Giorgio is a wooded, pyramid-shaped mountain on the shores of Lake Lugano, straddling the border between Switzerland and Italy. To the eye, it is a pleasant green hill with villages on its slopes. To palaeontologists, it is one of the most important fossil sites in the world. About two hundred and forty million years ago, during the Middle Triassic, this area was a tropical lagoon, and its sediments preserved an extraordinary range of marine reptiles, fish, invertebrates and plants. The mountain was added to the UNESCO list in 2003 on the Swiss side, and in 2010 on the Italian side.

  4. 4. Bodio centrale o delle Monete

    Gavirate, on the northern shore of the Lago di Varese in Lombardy, has a sweet claim to fame: it is the home of the brutti ma buoni, ugly but good, crunchy biscuits made with hazelnuts and egg whites, which were reportedly invented here in the nineteenth century and are still sold in local pastry shops. The lake itself is a small, shallow lake surrounded by low hills, and its shores were home to some of the earliest pile-dwelling villages in Italy. The site of Bodio Centrale, also known as delle Monete, on the southern shore near Bodio Lomnago, is part of the Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps, listed by UNESCO in 2011.
