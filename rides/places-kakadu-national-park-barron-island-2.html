Why this ride

It goes by way of Kakadu National Park, Barron Island and back through Kakadu National Park. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kakadu National Park

    Kakadu is a vast area in the Top End of the Northern Territory, about twenty thousand square kilometres of floodplains, escarpments, billabongs and savanna woodland, and it is one of the few places in the world that has been given World Heritage status for both its natural and cultural values. The name is said to derive from Gagudju, a language spoken by Aboriginal people in the northern part of the park. Aboriginal people have lived here for tens of thousands of years, and the park is owned by its Aboriginal traditional owners and leased to the national parks service.

  2. 2. Kakadu National Park

    Kakadu is big in a way that is hard to take in until you have driven in it. The national park covers nearly twenty thousand square kilometres of the Northern Territory, about the size of Wales, and holds a whole series of landscapes: sandstone escarpments, floodplains, billabongs, monsoon forest, tidal rivers and open woodland. It is one of Australia's few World Heritage Areas listed for both natural and cultural values, and it is owned by its Aboriginal traditional owners, who lease it to the government and manage it jointly.

  3. 3. Barron Island

    It is country that is more water than land for half the year, a mosaic of mangroves, mudflats, paperbark swamps and open grassy plains cut by creeks that rise and fall with the monsoon.
