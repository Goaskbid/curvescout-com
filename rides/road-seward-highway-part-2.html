  1. 1. Moose Pass

    Moose Pass is a small cluster of cabins, a general store and a roadhouse beside a turquoise lake, and the speed limit through it is low for good reason. The village lies on the Kenai Peninsula in south-central Alaska, about forty kilometres north of Seward on the Seward Highway, and perhaps two hundred people live here. Trail Lake, in front of the village, is fed by glacial meltwater, and the water has the milky blue-green colour of ground rock, which makes the whole valley glow. Mountains still holding patches of snow rise on both sides, and spruce forest covers the lower slopes.

  2. 2. Seward

    Alaska's Seward Highway earns its reputation from beginning to end, and Seward is where it runs out of road. The town sits at the head of Resurrection Bay, a fjord on the Kenai Peninsula ringed by steep, snow-streaked mountains, and it has a working harbour full of fishing boats, tour vessels and the occasional cruise ship. It was founded in 1903 as the ocean terminus for a railroad to the interior, and it is also mile zero of the Iditarod Trail, the dog-sledding route to Nome. The 1964 Good Friday earthquake, the most powerful ever recorded in North America, destroyed much of the waterfront here, and the town has rebuilt itself since.
