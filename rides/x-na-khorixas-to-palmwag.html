  1. 1. Khorixas

    Khorixas is not a place most people plan to see, but it is where the dry heart of Damaraland has its administrative office, a straggle of low buildings, shops and a petrol station on the gravel and tar roads of Kunene. The town, with about twelve thousand people, was laid out in the 1970s as the capital of the old Damaraland homeland, and while it lacks the charm of a colonial town, it serves a vast area and is a lively place, full of minibus taxis, market stalls and people who have come from distant farms to buy supplies.

  2. 2. Palmwag

    Palmwag is a small oasis in the dry, stony north-west of Namibia, named for the palm trees that cluster around a spring on the Uniab River, and it is one of the few places in Kunene where you can find shade, cold water and a bed. The surroundings are a huge concession of desert and rocky hills, a wilderness of red earth and pale grass where animals roam freely, and the lodge sits at the edge of it like a small green island.
