  1. 1. Lachute

    Lachute takes its name from the falls on the North River, the Rivière du Nord, that tumble through the middle of town: la chute, the fall. It lies at the southern edge of the Laurentians, in the county of Argenteuil, on the plain north of the Ottawa River, and it is a working town of about ten thousand with a long industrial past in paper and textiles. Most people speak French, though an English-speaking community, descended from Scottish and Irish settlers, has been here since the nineteenth century.

  2. 2. Mont-Tremblant

    The trembling mountain, as the Algonquin are said to have called it, rises to about 875 metres, the highest point in the Laurentians north of Montreal. A spirit lived in it, the story goes, and the earth shook when people disturbed it. These days what disturbs it are skiers, mountain bikers and a resort village of brightly painted, steep-roofed buildings at its foot, laid out like a Quebec version of an alpine town. Mont-Tremblant, the municipality of nearly nine thousand that includes the old village, the resort and the town centre on Route 117, has grown into one of the busiest holiday areas in Canada.
