  1. 1. East London

    East London is the only river port in South Africa, and the Buffalo River that runs through it has been the heart of the city since the nineteenth century. It began as a landing point for British troops during the frontier wars, grew into a harbour town, and today has nearly half a million people, a busy port, and a motor industry, with a large Mercedes-Benz plant. The city sits on the Indian Ocean coast of the Eastern Cape, with long, empty beaches such as Nahoon and Orient on its doorstep, and surf that draws bodyboarders and surfers all year.

  2. 2. Coffee Bay

    Coffee Bay gets its name from a shipwreck, or so the story goes. In the nineteenth century, a vessel carrying coffee beans is said to have gone down off this coast, and the beans washed ashore and sprouted on the beach, giving the place its name. Whatever the truth, the village is a small, laid-back settlement on the Wild Coast of the Eastern Cape, in the heart of the Xhosa homeland, with a crescent of sand, green hills and round thatched huts dotted over the slopes.
