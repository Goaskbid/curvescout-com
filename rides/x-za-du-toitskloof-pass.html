  1. 1. Paarl

    Paarl is named after a rock. In 1657, an explorer saw a huge granite dome glistening after rain and called it the Pearl, and the town grew up beneath it, along the Berg River at the heart of the Cape winelands. The Paarl Rock, which locals say is one of the largest granite outcrops in the world, rises above the valley in a smooth grey whaleback, and you can walk or drive up to its base for views over vineyards and the distant peaks of the Drakenstein range.

  2. 2. Du Toitskloof Pass

    For decades, every vehicle crossing from Cape Town to the interior had to climb the Du Toitskloof Pass, and there are drivers who still talk of the queues and the fog. In the late 1980s, a long tunnel was bored under the mountain on the N1 highway, and the old road was given up to riders, cyclists and anyone prepared to spend a little more time. It is now one of the quietest great roads in the Cape, with the traffic thundering through the tunnel below.

  3. 3. Worcester

    Worcester, in the Western Cape, is the largest town in the Breede River Valley and the traditional beginning of the Route 62 for many travellers, a place of about a hundred and twenty-seven thousand people at the foot of the Hex River Mountains. It sits at the edge of the Cape winelands and it feels it. Vineyards climb the slopes behind the town, irrigated fields spread across the valley floor, and the peaks above turn pink and purple at sunset. In winter they carry snow, a surprise to anyone who thinks of South Africa as permanently warm.
