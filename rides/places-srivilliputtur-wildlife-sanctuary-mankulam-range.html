Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Srivilliputtur Wildlife Sanctuary

    Srivilliputtur sits at the foot of the Western Ghats in the dry south of Tamil Nadu, and its great gift to the world is a tower. The temple of Andal, a female saint of the Vaishnava tradition, has a gopuram of eleven tiers that rises above the town and is used as the emblem of the Tamil Nadu government. Inside, the rituals continue much as they have for centuries, and the town's other famous product is palkova, a sweet milk fudge, said to be better here than anywhere else. A bite of it and a glass of strong tea is a good break on a hot day.

  2. 2. Eravikulam National Park

    Anamudi is the highest mountain in India south of the Himalaya, and it rises from the Eravikulam plateau like a dark tooth. The national park, in the high Western Ghats near Munnar in Kerala, protects a landscape of rolling grassland, thick shola forest in the folds of the hills, and open skies, at heights above two thousand metres. It forms part of the Western Ghats World Heritage Site, inscribed by UNESCO in 2012, and it is the best place in the world to see the Nilgiri tahr, a stocky wild goat that lives on steep, grassy slopes.

  3. 3. Mankulam Range

    The Mankulam range lies in the high country of Idukki district in Kerala, in the Munnar uplands that form the highest part of the Western Ghats south of the Nilgiris. The mountains here are enormous, rolling green downs of grass dotted with dark patches of shola, the stunted evergreen forest that fills the folds between the ridges, and the entire landscape is part of a protected block that includes Anamudi, at 2,695 metres the highest peak in India south of the Himalaya. The Grass Hills National Park sits here, with its sweeping slopes and strange, windswept look.
