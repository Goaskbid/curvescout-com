  1. 1. Apache Junction

    Apache Junction is the last big town on the eastern edge of the Phoenix sprawl, a desert city of about thirty-eight thousand people at the foot of the Superstition Mountains. The mountains rise abruptly behind the suburban streets, a wall of reddish volcanic rock with a jagged crest, and they are one of the most famous landmarks of the American Southwest, soaked in legend and mystery. It is a place of RV parks, rock shops and diners, but also the starting point of one of Arizona's most dramatic roads.

  2. 2. Apache Trail point 2

    Canyon Lake is a surprise: a long, narrow reservoir of deep turquoise water, tucked among red cliffs and saguaro-covered slopes in the middle of the Sonoran Desert. It is one of a chain of lakes on the Salt River, created by dams built in the 1920s to supply water and power to the Phoenix area, and it has the look of a fjord in a desert. The road runs along its shore with a series of bends that give a view across the water to the dark cliffs of the Superstitions.

  3. 3. Apache Trail point 3

    Tortilla Flat is a town that is mostly a saloon. The population is usually given as six, and the entire community consists of a restaurant, a general store, a gift shop and an ice cream parlour, all in old wooden buildings in a little valley between rocky hills. It began as a stagecoach stop on the road to Roosevelt Dam, and later served as a camp for construction workers, and it has been catering to travellers ever since.

  4. 4. Apache Trail point 4

    Fish Creek Hill is the part of the Apache Trail that people remember, and some wish they had not. The road, by this stage a graded dirt track, drops down the side of a canyon in a series of steep, narrow switchbacks, with a very steep grade, loose gravel and a sheer drop on the outer edge. There are no guardrails to speak of. The colours of the rock are extraordinary, with bands of red, orange, yellow and white, and from the overlook at the top you can see the canyon winding below.

  5. 5. Apache Trail point 5

    Theodore Roosevelt Dam is a stone fortress in a desert gorge, and when it was finished in 1911 it was the tallest masonry dam in the world. It holds back the Salt River to form Roosevelt Lake, a vast sheet of blue water in the dry hills of the Tonto Basin, and it was the first major project of the new US Reclamation Service, built to supply water to the farms and cities of the Phoenix valley. The dam was named for the president who had pushed through the Reclamation Act, and who came to dedicate it himself.
