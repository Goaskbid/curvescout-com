  1. 1. Fuel stop near Yashio

    Yashio is a flat, workaday city in south-eastern Saitama, just north of Tokyo's Adachi ward, sandwiched between the Nakagawa and Ayase rivers. It is a landscape of small factories, warehouses, apartment blocks and the occasional field of vegetables, crossed by expressways and railway lines. It will not feature in any guidebook, and it does not pretend otherwise.

  2. 2. Near Kumagaya, on the way back

    In July 2018 Kumagaya recorded 41.1 degrees Celsius, then the highest temperature ever measured in Japan, and the city wears the title of hottest town with something like pride. Banners proclaim it, shops sell heat-themed souvenirs, and in summer the town fills with misting stations. If you are riding through in July or August, take the heat seriously: drink constantly and avoid the middle of the day.

  3. 3. Food stop near Itsukaichi

    Itsukaichi takes its name from the market that was once held here on days ending in five, a trading point where charcoal, timber and silk from the mountains of western Tokyo met merchants from the plains. It is now part of the city of Akiruno, a quiet town at the mouth of the Akigawa valley, where the suburbs of Tokyo finally give way to forested hills.
