  1. 1. Turangi

    Turangi is a fishing town that was built almost overnight. In the 1960s, when the Tongariro Power Scheme needed workers to divert the rivers of the central plateau into hydroelectric stations, a new town was laid out beside the Tongariro River at the southern end of Lake Taupō. About three and a half thousand people live there now, and the place has settled into an easy life based on trout, tourism and the great volcanic national park behind it.

  2. 2. Waiouru

    Waiouru is a military town on the edge of a desert, which sounds like a joke but is simply geography. The settlement sits at about 800 metres on the central plateau of the North Island, in the rain shadow of the three great volcanoes, and the surrounding land of tussock, gravel and wind-bent scrub gets the name Rangipo Desert because of its dry, stony look. In truth it receives plenty of rain, but the soil drains instantly and little grows.
