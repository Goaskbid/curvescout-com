Why this ride

curated scenic road. Atlantic coast, flysch cliffs, fishing towns. 92 km from Bilbao to Donostia / San Sebastián, about 1 h 40 of riding. It goes by way of Bermeo, Lekeitio and Zumaia. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Basque Coast Route

Slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did. Water arrives beside the road and stays for 24 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bilbao

    Bilbao was an industrial city for most of its life, an iron-ore and shipyard town on the Nervión river, and then in 1997 it reinvented itself with a titanium-clad museum that changed how cities think about culture. Frank Gehry's Guggenheim sits right on the riverbank, curved and glittering like a ship that has run aground, and its success gave the whole place a new confidence. You will notice that the older parts of town, the Casco Viejo with its seven original streets, are still very Basque, loud and unpretentious, with bars full of locals at lunchtime and counters stacked with pintxos.

  2. 2. Near Bermeo

    Smell it before you see it: Bermeo announces itself with diesel, salt and grilled fish. This is one of the main fishing ports on the Basque coast, a town of about seventeen thousand people whose harbour is still full of working boats, with tall old houses stacked behind and the fish market and the old fishermen's quarter in plain view. The Basque fleet has gone out from here for centuries, and the town takes its anchovies and bonito very seriously. If you see marmitako, the tuna and potato stew, on a menu, order it.

    Read the complete Bermeo write-up.

  3. 3. Lekeitio

    Lekeitio is the kind of fishing harbour that makes you slow down whether you meant to or not. It sits on the Bay of Biscay coast of Bizkaia, behind a sheltered cove, and it has a town centre of about seven thousand people packed between the sea and a ring of green hills. The first thing you notice is the island, San Nicolás, which lies just offshore and joins the mainland by a causeway at low tide, when the beach opens up and the whole town seems to walk out over the sand.

  4. 4. Zumaia

    Zumaia is where you go to look at time. On the cliffs just beside the town, at Itzurun beach, the sea has cut through layer after layer of tilted sedimentary rock known as flysch, thin bands of limestone and shale that stack up like the pages of a book and run into the water in long ridges. The rocks span a huge stretch of geological history, and geologists come from all over the world to study the layer that records the end of the Cretaceous, the period when the dinosaurs disappeared. That makes this one of the great natural clocks in Europe, and it is part of the Basque Coast Geopark.

    Read the complete Zumaia write-up.
