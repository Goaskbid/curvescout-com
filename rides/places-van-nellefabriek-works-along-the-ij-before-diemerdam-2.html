Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Van Nellefabriek

    The Van Nelle factory, on the edge of Rotterdam, is one of the most beautiful industrial buildings ever made. Built between 1925 and 1931 for a company that processed coffee, tea and tobacco, it is a long, light building of glass and steel, with curtain walls that let daylight flood into the workspaces. The designers, Johannes Brinkman and Leendert van der Vlugt, created what became an icon of modernist architecture, a factory of light and air for its workers. It was added to the UNESCO list in 2014.

  2. 2. Corbulo's canal | Rozenrust

    In the year 47, a Roman general with time on his hands and a restless army decided to dig a canal. The Roman historian Tacitus records that Gnaeus Domitius Corbulo, the commander on the Rhine frontier, kept his legions busy by having them cut a waterway of about twenty-three miles between the Rhine and the Meuse, so that ships could avoid the dangerous open sea. The Fossa Corbulonis, as it is known, probably linked the Old Rhine near Leiden with the Maas estuary, and its line seems to follow the later course of the Vliet, a waterway that still runs between Leiden and Delft. Scholars debate the exact route, but this area, between Voorburg and the Rozenrust district, is a natural candidate.

  3. 3. Fort near Heemstede

    Between Haarlem and the bulb fields of the Dutch coast, near the town of Heemstede and the hamlet of De Glip, lies a squat, grassy fort surrounded by a moat. This is the Fort near Heemstede, built around the turn of the twentieth century as part of the Defence Line of Amsterdam, a ring of forts, dykes and flooding zones around the city, and now part of the UNESCO-listed Dutch Water Defence Lines.

  4. 4. Works along the IJ before Diemerdam

    Just east of Amsterdam, where the city's suburbs give way to water and polders, a narrow strip of land runs out along the old shore of the IJ and the IJmeer toward Muiden. The Diemerdam is an old dike, and the works along the IJ before it are part of the Dutch Water Defence Lines, the system of forts, dikes, sluices and inundation fields that the Dutch built over centuries to defend their cities by flooding the land around them. The Defence Line of Amsterdam, a ring of forts around the capital, was listed by UNESCO in 1996 and later joined with the New Dutch Waterline into a single site.
