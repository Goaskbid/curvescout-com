Why this ride

From Białystok it runs 195 km to Białystok; allow about 3 h 22 of riding. On the way it passes Bialowieza Forest, Białowieża Forest, back through Białowieża Forest and back through Bialowieza Forest. It crosses from Poland into Belarus - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bialystok

    Białystok is the capital of Podlasie, the north-eastern corner of Poland, and a city with a surprisingly cosmopolitan past. Around 290,000 people live here now, but the historical mix was far richer, with Poles, Jews, Belarusians, Lithuanians, Tatars and Russians all contributing to the character of the town. In the nineteenth century it grew into a textile centre, and before the Second World War, Jews made up a majority of its population, a history that ended in the Holocaust and is remembered in several memorials around the city.

  2. 2. Bialowieza Forest

    There is nowhere else in lowland Europe quite like Białowieża. The forest straddles the border between Poland and Belarus, and it is the last large remnant of the primeval woodland that once covered the plains of the continent. Some of its trees are centuries old, towering oaks, limes, hornbeams and spruces, and the ground is littered with fallen trunks, which are left to rot as they would be in nature, and which feed an astonishing variety of fungi, beetles and birds. UNESCO listed it in 1979 and later extended the listing to include the Belarusian side.

  3. 3. Białowieża Forest

    Here the forest gets closer to the border, and the history in the air gets heavier. For centuries this was a royal hunting ground, preserved by Polish kings and Lithuanian grand dukes and later by the Russian tsars, who made it one of the best-protected game reserves in Europe, and it was this protection that kept the forest alive.
