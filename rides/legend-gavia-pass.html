Why this ride

2,621 m, narrow, unlit tunnel, and quieter than the Stelvio next door. A one-way ride of 28 km, Bormio to Ponte di Legno - roughly 31 min in the saddle. The highest point is about 2,600 m. Gavia Pass is open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bormio

    Bormio lies in a high valley of Lombardy, a town of narrow lanes, stone arches and church bells at around 1,200 metres, with mountains rising steeply on every side. It has been a spa since Roman times, and the warm springs that bubble up from deep in the rock still feed thermal baths where you can soak after a long day. In winter it is a ski resort with a long tradition, and the famously steep Stelvio downhill run, used for World Cup races, starts above the town.

  3. 3. Near Ponte di Legno

    Ponte di Legno takes its name from the wooden bridge that once crossed the Oglio river here, and it is the last proper village before the road leaves the Val Camonica and heads up into the high mountains. It sits at about 1,260 metres, and it is surrounded by the peaks of the Adamello and Ortles-Cevedale groups, with the Tonale Pass just to the east and the Gavia pass to the north. The village is a ski and walking resort, and it keeps a handsome old centre of stone houses beside a modern strip of hotels and cafes. The nearby hamlets of Temù and Vezza d'Oglio are smaller still.
