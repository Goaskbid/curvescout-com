Why this ride

505 km from Balmain to Balmain, about 9 h 46 of riding. On the way it passes Australian Convict Sites, Greater Blue Mountains Area and back through Australian Convict Sites. It tops out around 1,050 m. Expect gradients and long views.

  2. 2. Australian Convict Sites

    70 km in.

    North of Sydney, where the Hawkesbury River winds between sandstone cliffs and forested ridges, there is a road that convicts built by hand. The Old Great North Road, begun in the 1820s, was meant to link Sydney with the Hunter Valley, and it was cut and paved by gangs of prisoners working with picks, hammers and iron bars, often in chains. It runs for over two hundred kilometres, and several sections survive, with walls of fitted sandstone, culverts, drains and cuttings that still look neat after two centuries. Together with other sites it is part of the UNESCO-listed Australian Convict Sites, which record the transportation of about 160,000 men, women and children from Britain.

    Read the complete Australian Convict Sites write-up.

  3. 3. Greater Blue Mountains Area

    251 km in.

    The Blue Mountains are named for a haze. On a warm day, fine droplets of oil released by the eucalyptus forest scatter the blue end of the light and the whole plateau seems to float in a pale mist. The Greater Blue Mountains Area covers about a million hectares of sandstone plateau, deep gorges and sheer cliffs, split into eight protected areas, and UNESCO lists it for the astonishing variety of eucalypts, more than a hundred species, which have adapted to every kind of soil and slope.

    Read the complete Greater Blue Mountains Area write-up.
