Why this ride

A 664 km round trip from Tashkent - roughly 15 h in the saddle. The stops in between: The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve, Besh-Aral State Nature Reserve - main part and back through The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve. It tops out around 2,700 m. It crosses from Uzbekistan into Kyrgyzstan - carry ID and check the toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Chorsu Market

    Start here.

    The blue dome of Chorsu is the best landmark in old Tashkent. The great market under it, a huge circular hall roofed with a single sky-blue cupola, stands on the site of a crossroads whose name means four streams or four roads, and the bazaar here has been the heart of the old city for centuries, long before the Soviet engineers built the dome. Around it, in the surrounding lanes, stalls spill into the streets, and the noise, smell and colour are overwhelming: pyramids of spice, mounds of dried apricots, raisins and nuts, piles of round, patterned breads, hanging carcasses at the butchers' hall, and women in bright dresses calling out prices.

    Read the complete Chorsu Market write-up.

  2. 2. The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve

    118 km in. The ride passes about 8 km from it.

    East of Tashkent, the land rises steeply into the western ranges of the Tian Shan, and the Chatkal range forms the border with Kyrgyzstan. The Chatkal State Biosphere Reserve, established in the Soviet era, protects a stretch of these mountains, with juniper forests, walnut and wild fruit trees, alpine meadows and rocky peaks.

    Read the complete The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve write-up.

  3. 3. Besh-Aral State Nature Reserve - main part

    332 km in. The ride passes about 19 km from it.

    Far up the Chatkal valley in western Kyrgyzstan, where the river runs between the Chatkal and Sandalash ranges of the Western Tien Shan, lies the Besh-Aral State Nature Reserve. It protects mountain steppe, juniper woodland, walnut and apple groves and rocky high country, and it is part of the transnational World Heritage Site of the Western Tien Shan, inscribed in 2016 and shared with Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan. The reserve was created in particular to protect Menzbier's marmot, a small marmot found only in this part of Central Asia.
