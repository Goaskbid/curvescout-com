Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Marvão

    Marvão looks like something that should not be possible: a whole village on a granite crag, circled by walls, with a castle at the top and a drop on every side. It stands at around eight hundred and sixty metres on the Serra de São Mamede, looking out over the plains of the Alentejo to the west and Spain to the east, and it has been used as a lookout since long before the border existed. The name is said to come from Ibn Marwan, a ninth-century Muslim leader who made the rock his stronghold.

  2. 2. Estremoz

    Estremoz is built on marble, and you see it everywhere. The stone is quarried in the hills around the town, and it is used for paving, windowsills, fountains, and even the occasional doorstep, giving the white town a gleam in the sun. The quarries around Estremoz and the nearby towns of Borba and Vila Viçosa are among the most important marble sources in the world, and from the road you can see huge pale scars in the hills where the stone is cut out in blocks.

  3. 3. Évora

    Alentejo is the part of Portugal that looks as if someone had ironed it. South and east of the Tagus, the land rolls out in low swells of wheat, olive and cork oak, with white villages on the hills and storks nesting on the chimneys, and Évora sits in the middle of it, about 130 kilometres east of Lisbon. It is a walled city of some fifty-five thousand, and for riders it is the natural pause in a long day of empty road.
