Why this ride

A 94 km round trip from Madīnat Ḩamad - roughly 1 h 42 in the saddle. On the way it passes Al-Alawi House, Madinat Hamad 2 Burial Mound Field and back through Al-Alawi House. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Madinat Hamad 2 Burial Mound Field

    Flat, pale and apparently empty, the central plain of Bahrain hides one of the oldest cemeteries on earth. Some four thousand years ago the island of Dilmun, as it was known to the Sumerians, buried its dead under thousands upon thousands of low stone mounds, and in places like Madinat Hamad and A'ali the mounds still stand in dense, grey fields. At one time there may have been several hundred thousand of them, which would have made Dilmun a famous necropolis in its day, and many have since been flattened by roads, housing and quarrying.

  2. 2. Al-Alawi House

    Muharraq's most famous export after pearls is probably football, and the Al-Alawi house stands in a quarter where every wall seems to have a team scarf hanging from it. The Muharraq club is one of the most successful in the country, with a loyal following, and on match days the cafés and lanes of the old town are noisy with argument and song. The people who live in the old merchant houses today are often the grandchildren of men who dived for pearls or traded them, and the town keeps a stubborn local identity separate from Manama, the capital across the water.
