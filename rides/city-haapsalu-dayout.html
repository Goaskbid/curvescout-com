Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Haapsalu

    Haapsalu is a small seaside town on the west coast of Estonia with a surprising amount of history and an odd fame for lace and mud. The town grew around an episcopal castle built in the thirteenth century by the bishops of Ösel-Wiek, a big fortress with a cathedral inside its walls, now a romantic ruin on the edge of the bay. Legend tells of the White Lady, a woman said to have been walled up alive for loving a choirboy, whose ghost appears at the cathedral window on August full moons; the town celebrates the story with a festival and a lot of theatrical flair.

  2. 2. Historic Centre

    Tallinn's old town is the best-preserved medieval city centre in northern Europe, and it has been protected by a ring of walls and towers that still stand for most of their length. UNESCO listed it in 1997. The city grew rich as a Hanseatic port, and its merchants built tall stone houses with steep roofs, guild halls and churches that are still in use. The town is divided into two parts, the Upper Town on the limestone hill of Toompea, where the rulers lived, and the Lower Town below, where the merchants and craftsmen did business, and for centuries the two had separate governments and sometimes separate laws.
