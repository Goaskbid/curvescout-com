Why this ride

A 84 km one-way ride from near Sari-Solenzara, about 1 h 26 of riding. It goes by way of Col de Bavella and Zonza. The highest point is about 1,200 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Corsica Bavella Route

The ground tilts up and stays tilted for 32 km. 1218 m of it, taken steadily rather than in one wall. A steady rhythm suits this better than bursts. The height arrives whether you hurry or not.

Rider note

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Solenzara

    The Solenzara river rises in the granite heart of southern Corsica and falls to the east coast in a series of pools so clear and green that they look lit from below, and it is here, at the mouth, that the Bavella road begins. The coastal village of Sari-Solenzara is small and workaday, with a harbour, a long beach and a handful of restaurants, and it also has a military air base nearby whose jets occasionally tear across the sky. It is a quiet place by Corsican standards, a spot to fill the tank, buy a bottle of water and have a coffee before the mountains.

  2. 2. Col de Bavella

    The Aiguilles de Bavella are the teeth of southern Corsica, a ridge of jagged granite needles that rise above the pass like the spines of some enormous stone creature. The Col de Bavella, at 1,218 metres, lies in their shadow, a saddle of dark pine forest and boulders where the road from Zonza crosses the range on its way to the east coast. A small chapel stands at the pass, with a statue of the Madonna of the Snows, and in the early morning light, with mist in the valleys, the sight is almost unreal.

  3. 3. Zonza

    The road from Porto-Vecchio to Zonza climbs out of the humid coast into another Corsica, one of chestnut and pine, granite and cold streams. Zonza lies at about eight hundred metres in the Alta Rocca, a small village of fewer than two thousand people scattered across a ridge, with the great granite needles of Bavella standing over it to the north. The coast is only twenty kilometres away, but the altitude, the air and the sense of space make it feel much further.

  4. 4. Porto Vecchio

    Porto-Vecchio sits on a deep bay in the south-east of Corsica, and it is one of the island's most prosperous and cheerful towns. The Genoese founded it in 1539 on a hill above the sea, building a walled citadel to defend a harbour whose marshy shores bred malaria, and for centuries, it was a lonely, unhealthy outpost. Now the swamps are drained, the old town is lively with restaurants and shops, and the surrounding coast has some of the best beaches in the Mediterranean. Palombaggia and Santa Giulia, a short ride south, have white sand, turquoise water and pine trees, and they get very crowded in July and August.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Solenzara to Col de Bavella

Solenzara → Col de Bavella climbs about 1218 m in 20 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Col de Bavella to Zonza

Col de Bavella → Zonza drops about 1218 m over 9 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Zonza to Porto Vecchio

Zonza → Porto Vecchio is 25 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
