  1. 1. Bealach na Ba Road point 1

    Tornapress is not much more than a handful of houses at the head of Loch Kishorn, but it marks the start of one of the great roads of Britain. The Bealach na Bà, the Pass of the Cattle, climbs from sea level to over six hundred metres in a few miles, the steepest ascent of any road in the country, and the first sight of it, a thin grey line zigzagging up the hillside into the cloud, is enough to make you check your brake pads.

  2. 2. Bealach na Ba Road point 2

    At the summit of the Bealach na Bà the world opens like a door. You have climbed through hairpins and past rocky gullies to a bare plateau at about six hundred and twenty-six metres, with a car park, a sign and, on a clear day, a view west across the Inner Sound to the islands of Raasay and Skye, with the Cuillin ridge jagged beyond. On a bad day you can see your front wheel. Both versions are worth having.

  3. 3. Bealach na Ba Road point 3

    Applecross sits on the western edge of the peninsula, facing the Isle of Skye across a narrow strait, and it is the kind of place that the long road to reach it makes you appreciate. The village is a scatter of white cottages along a sheltered bay, with a church, a small shop, a walled garden and, famously, an inn on the shore that serves seafood to riders who have just survived the pass.
