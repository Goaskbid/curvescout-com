  1. 1. Ponta Delgada

    Ponta Delgada is the main town of the Azores, on the south coast of São Miguel, a green volcanic island in the middle of the Atlantic. The town is laid out along the sea, with black basalt and white limestone pavements in patterns that make the streets look like a drawing, and three big arches, the Portas da Cidade, mark the old entrance from the harbour. Fishing boats, whale-watching boats and ferries come and go, and the island's wealth once came from oranges, then pineapples, which are still grown in glasshouses and are sold in the market with a sweetness you will not forget.

  2. 2. Near Mosteiros

    Mosteiros sits at the very western tip of São Miguel, where the road runs out of island and the Atlantic takes over. It is a small village of low houses, a harbour, a church and an old basalt shoreline, and the reason people come is the group of sea stacks standing just off the coast. They are black, jagged and covered with the green of lichen and salt-hardy plants, and they look like the ruins of something. The name means monasteries, and one story says that the stacks resemble the shapes of monks' cowls, though the origin of the name is not entirely clear.
