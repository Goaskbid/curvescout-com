Why this ride

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Jindabyne

    Jindabyne has an odd secret: the town you drive through is not the original one. The old Jindabyne lies underwater beneath Lake Jindabyne, drowned in the 1960s when the Snowy Mountains Scheme dammed the Snowy River, and the whole community was moved uphill to its present spot. On a very dry year when the lake falls, you can sometimes see the old bridge piers. The lake itself is now the town's centrepiece, a big, blue, cold sheet of water backed by the Snowy Mountains and used for trout fishing, sailing and sitting on a bench with a pie.

  2. 2. Alpine Way point 2

    Thredbo is a ski village in a steep valley under the highest peaks of the Australian mainland, and it has a very different feel from the open lake country around Jindabyne. The village sits beside the Thredbo River, with chalets and lodges climbing the slopes, and in winter it fills with skiers. In summer, the chairlift carries walkers and mountain bikers up the slope, and the track to the summit of Mount Kosciuszko, at 2,228 metres the highest point in Australia, starts nearby.

  3. 3. Alpine Way point 3

    Beyond Thredbo, the Alpine Way climbs to its high point at Dead Horse Gap, at about 1,580 metres, and enters a landscape of snow gums, granite boulders and open heath. This is the wild heart of Kosciuszko National Park, and the road crosses the main range here, with long views to the west toward the Murray valley and the plains of Victoria. Mist is common, and so are sudden changes in temperature.

  4. 4. Alpine Way point 4

    Where the Alpine Way ends, Khancoban begins, and the contrast is one of the best things about the ride. After the cold, bare heights of the Main Range, the road drops steadily into the valley of the Swampy Plain River, the air warms, and the slopes turn from snow gum to dry forest and then to farmland and pasture. The town is small and tidy, a line of low buildings around a green, and it exists because of the Snowy Scheme, having been built in the 1950s to house the engineers and workers who constructed the nearby power stations.
