  2. 2. Photo stop near Nanaimo

    Ask a Canadian about Nanaimo and the first thing you will hear about is the Nanaimo bar, a no-bake square of crumb base, custard buttercream and chocolate that turns up in cafes across the country and is unapologetically sweet. The city itself, with about eighty-five thousand people, is the second largest on Vancouver Island and the main ferry hub to the mainland, with boats to Horseshoe Bay and Tsawwassen near Vancouver. For many riders it is where the island begins or ends.

  3. 3. Near Richmond

    The point lies on the western edge of Richmond, out where the city gives way to the mudflats and marshes of the Fraser River delta. Richmond is built almost entirely on flat delta land, protected by dykes from the river and the sea, and the western shore, along Sturgeon Bank, is a wide expanse of mud, eelgrass and tidal channels, important for migrating birds. On a clear day, the view across the Strait of Georgia to the mountains of Vancouver Island is wide and calming, and the sunsets are often spectacular.
