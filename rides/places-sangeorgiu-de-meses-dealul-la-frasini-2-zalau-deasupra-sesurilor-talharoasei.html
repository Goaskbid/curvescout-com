Why this ride

From Stârciu it runs 102 km to Stârciu; allow about 1 h 46 of riding. The stops in between: Vânători - Dealul Cocinilor, Zalău - Deasupra Șesurilor Tâlhăroasei and back through Vânători - Dealul Cocinilor. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sângeorgiu de Meseș -Dealul La Frasini 2

    A watchman on a Roman tower had a simple job and a lonely one. He stood on a wooden platform on a hill, looked along the frontier and waited. If he saw a raiding party coming, he lit a signal fire or beat an alarm, and the next tower along the line repeated it. The men who did this were not Romans from Italy but auxiliaries, soldiers recruited in the provinces, from Gaul, Syria, Thrace or Britain, who served twenty-five years and received citizenship at the end. The second of the two sites at Dealul La Frasini in the Mes hills belongs to that system on the Dacian frontier, and it was recognised by UNESCO in 2024 as part of the Dacia Porolissensis frontier.

  2. 2. Vânători - Dealul Cocinilor

    The hill of Dealul Cocinilor rises above the valley of the Crișul Repede near Ciucea, on the western edge of Cluj county, and it marks another point on the Roman frontier of Dacia, this time in the Meseș mountains, where the limes ran along the hills protecting the province of Dacia Porolissensis. The watchtowers here guarded the approaches from the west and north, and from the hilltop you can still see why: the valley below is a natural corridor, used by traders, armies and travellers for thousands of years.

  3. 3. Zalău - Deasupra Șesurilor Tâlhăroasei

    The Meseș mountains rise to the west of Zalău like a long green wall, and they formed the natural western boundary of Roman Dacia in this region. Along their crest and on the slopes above the plains, the Romans built a line of watchtowers, and the site above the fields with the evocative name of Șesurile Tâlhăroasei, roughly the robbers' flats, near the villages of Treznea and Aghireș, is one of them. It is part of the Dacian frontier on the World Heritage list. Up in the forest the remains are modest, but the position, high on a slope with a view over the valley, shows how the frontier used every advantage of the terrain.
