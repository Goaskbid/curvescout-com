Why this ride

A one-way ride of 283 km, La Grande to Baker City - roughly 4 h 10 in the saddle. It goes by way of Joseph Oregon and Hells Canyon Overlook. The highest point is about 2,000 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. La Grande Oregon

    La Grande lies flat as a table in the middle of the Grande Ronde Valley, a wide bowl of farmland in north-eastern Oregon ringed by the Blue Mountains to the west and the Wallowas to the east. It is a railroad town of about thirteen thousand people, with a college, a brick downtown and a long, wide main street, and it feels more like Idaho or Montana than the rainy Oregon of the coast. Wheat, hay and cattle fill the valley, and the air is dry and clear.

  2. 2. Joseph Oregon

    Joseph is a small town at the foot of a very large mountain range. The Wallowa Mountains, sometimes called the Alps of Oregon, rise sharply behind it, with snow on the high summits for much of the year and a long glacial lake, Wallowa Lake, tucked into the valley mouth just south of town. It is a place that feels a long way from anywhere, in the far northeastern corner of the state, at the end of a long road that runs up through Enterprise and has no real continuation.

  3. 3. Hells Canyon Overlook

    Hells Canyon is the deepest river gorge in North America, cut by the Snake River between Oregon and Idaho, and it is deeper than the Grand Canyon, though far less visited and far less famous. Across the gulf, the Seven Devils range in Idaho tilts up to more than 2,400 metres, nearly eight thousand feet above the river. What strikes you is the lack of a crowd. A couple of other vehicles in the car park and a lot of silence is normal.

  4. 4. Baker City

    Baker City looks across a wide, dry valley at the Elkhorn Mountains, a jagged line of peaks that stay snowy well into the summer, and the contrast between sage-covered flats and white summits is the first thing you notice. It is a town of about ten thousand people in the far east of Oregon, on the old Oregon Trail, and its brick downtown is better preserved than most in the West. The Geiser Grand Hotel, a grand 1889 building with a stained-glass ceiling in the dining room, recalls the gold boom that made the town rich.
