Why this ride

311 km from Albocàsser to Albocàsser, about 5 h 22 of riding. On the way it passes La Roca del migdia, Castell de Villafamés, Cova de Gargán and La Joquera. The highest point is about 1,300 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Coves del Civil o Ribasals- Abric III

    To the north of Alcalà de Xivert, in the hills that carry the Maestrat toward the Ports, the Coves del Civil, also called Ribasals, are a group of overhangs in a limestone gorge close to the village of Albocàsser. Abric III is the third in the series, a bit under six kilometres from the village, which has about fourteen hundred people. The paintings of the region belong to the northern extension of the Levantine art of the Spanish Mediterranean, protected by UNESCO since 1998, and the nearby Valltorta ravine is one of the most celebrated sites.

  2. 2. La Roca del migdia

    The inland hills of Castellón are some of the emptiest in eastern Spain. Behind the orange groves and resorts of the coast the land rises into the Maestrat, a high, stony country of plateaus, gorges and stone-walled fields, where villages of a few hundred people cluster around fortified churches and the horizon is crowned by the bulk of Penyagolosa, nearly 1,800 metres high. Villafranca del Cid, Culla and Benassal lie in this landscape, and the Roca del Migdia, the Noon Rock, is a shelter in a limestone face that was painted in prehistory.

  3. 3. Castell de Villafamés

    Villafamés announces itself from a long way off: a village of white and ochre houses stacked up a hill of reddish rock, with the remains of a castle crowning the top. It stands a little inland from the coast of Castellón, roughly between Borriol, Cabanes and the beach town of Benicàssim, close enough to the sea that you can smell salt on a westerly breeze turning, yet already in that rumpled hill country that leads up into the Maestrat.

  4. 4. Cova de Gargán

    High in the interior of Castellón, where the coastal orange groves are long forgotten and the land climbs toward the Penyagolosa massif, the shelter known as Cova de Gargán sits in a country of limestone ledges, holm oak and dry-stone terraces. Vistabella del Maestrazgo is the nearest village, a handful of kilometres away on its high plateau, with Villahermosa del Río down in its valley to the other side and Lucena del Cid a little further south. It is quiet up here in a way that the coast never is.

  5. 5. La Joquera

    Just behind Castelló de la Plana, where the coastal plain meets the first hills, the ravine of La Joquera cuts into the limestone near the village of Borriol. Its shelters hold prehistoric paintings, part of the UNESCO-listed rock art of the Mediterranean basin, and the area has been studied for its archaeology for decades. It is unusual to find such ancient art so close to a large modern city, and the contrast between the busy plain below, with its orange groves, factories and motorways, and the quiet hills above is striking.
