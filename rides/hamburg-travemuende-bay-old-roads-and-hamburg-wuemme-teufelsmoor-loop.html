Why this ride

A one-way ride of 463 km, Farmsen-Berne to Ottensen - roughly 8 h in the saddle. On the way it passes Ahrensburg clean exit, Lübeck old road, Travemünde bay stop and Eutin lake road.

The stops

  1. 1. Hamburg north-east edge

    Start here.

    Hamburg's north-eastern fringe, where the city gives way to fields, is a district of long avenues, brick housing blocks and green corridors. The Wandse, a small river that runs through the area, has been tamed into a chain of ponds and parks, and the path along it is popular with walkers and cyclists. Farmsen-Berne and Steilshoop, the two large districts nearby, are residential neighbourhoods built mostly in the twentieth century, with rows of apartment buildings, shopping centres and tree-lined streets. Wandsbek, a little further south, is the big local hub.

    Read the complete Hamburg north-east edge write-up.

  2. 2. Ahrensburg clean exit

    14 km in. Ahrensburg clean is the fuel stop for this leg.

    Ahrensburg is where Hamburg lets go of you. Northeast of the city, on the edge of the Stormarn district, the urban sprawl thins out into villas and fields, and the town acts as a final pocket of services before the roads open into proper countryside. It is a prosperous commuter town of about thirty thousand people, with tidy streets, a good supply of fuel stations and a station on the S-Bahn line, and for riders leaving the city, it is a clean, simple place to top up and get your bearings.

    Read the complete Ahrensburg clean exit write-up.

  3. 3. Lübeck old road

    63 km in.

    Lübeck is the queen of the Hanseatic League, and from the old road in you can see why. The brick skyline of seven church spires rises from an island in the Trave, with the great Holstentor gate, built in the fifteenth century and so heavy that it has sunk into the soft ground, standing guard at the western entrance. The whole old town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and with over two hundred thousand people around it, the city has everything a rider could want: fuel, restaurants, shops and a lively old centre.

    Read the complete Lübeck old road write-up.

  4. 4. Travemünde bay stop

    81 km in.

    At the mouth of the Trave, where the river gives up and the Baltic begins, the beach at Travemünde runs in a long, pale curve beneath the promenade. It is a proper town beach, with striped wicker beach chairs, a pier, a casino and a long row of hotels, and in summer it is very busy. The water is shallow and gentle, warming quickly, and the beach slopes so gradually that small children can paddle a long way out. A small lighthouse on the waterfront is claimed to be the oldest in Germany, dating from the sixteenth century, and it is a pleasant landmark to meet at.

    Read the complete Travemünde bay stop write-up.

  5. 5. Eutin lake road

    115 km in.

    Eutin is a small town that carries itself like a larger one, thanks to a history of bishops and dukes. For centuries it was the seat of the prince-bishops of Lübeck, who made the place their country residence, and later it belonged to the Dukes of Oldenburg. Their legacy is a moated castle beside the Großer Eutiner See, with a landscaped park around it, and a town centre of cobbled lanes, a market square and brick gabled houses. The composer Carl Maria von Weber was born here in 1786, and every summer an opera festival is staged on the lake in his honour, with the castle gardens as a backdrop.

    Read the complete Eutin lake road write-up.

  6. 6. Plön water view

    131 km in.

    There is a particular bend of the path at the water's edge in Plön where the whole lake seems to open at once. The castle stands on its hill to one side, white against the trees, the great lake spreads out in front with its islands and reed beds, and the sky sits large over it all. In the morning, with a light mist on the surface and swans moving slowly along the margin, it is a picture that needs very little help from a camera.

    Read the complete Plön water view write-up.

  7. 7. Back to Hamburg

    219 km in.

    The Wandsbek and Farmsen end of Hamburg is the quiet side of the city, and it makes a gentle landing at the end of a day. After the traffic of the autobahn, the streets are slow and tree-lined, with low brick buildings and the soft glow of shop windows. The district is not on any tourist list, but it has the unhurried feel of a place where people actually live, with weekly markets, small cafés and corner bakeries, and a long stretch of park beside the Wandse where dog walkers and joggers pass in the evening.

    Read the complete Hamburg write-up.

  8. 8. Hamburg west edge

    238 km in.

    Altona was Danish until 1864, a fact that surprises many visitors, and the quarter still feels a little apart from the rest of Hamburg. The water is just ten metres or so below the street at this point. It is the part of Hamburg most closely tied to ships and the sea, and you can smell it: tar, diesel, wet rope and fish.

    Read the complete Hamburg west edge write-up.

  9. 9. Buxtehude exit gate

    268 km in.

    Buxtehude is the town of the hare and the hedgehog. In the Low German fairy tale collected by the Brothers Grimm, the hedgehog challenges the hare to a race, and wins by tricking him, with his wife waiting at the finish line looking just like him. The story is set on the heath near Buxtehude, and the town celebrates it with statues and a fountain. The name has also become a German byword for a remote place, as in the saying that someone is living in Buxtehude, which is a bit unfair to a lively Hanseatic town of nearly forty thousand.

    Read the complete Buxtehude exit gate write-up.

  10. 10. Zeven country roads

    303 km in.

    Zeven lies in the middle of the Geest, the sandy ridge of country that runs through the heart of Lower Saxony between the marshes of the coast and the lowlands of the south. The land is gently rolling, with pine woods, heath remnants, big farms and wet meadows in the valleys, and the villages are built around old churches and clusters of oaks. It is not a landscape that shouts, and it makes for easy riding, with long, quiet roads that curve between fields and woods and rarely see much traffic.

    Read the complete Zeven country roads write-up.

  11. 11. Worpswede artists’ village

    334 km in.

    Worpswede began as a rebellion. In 1889 a handful of young painters, tired of the academies and their studio light, moved to a village in the Teufelsmoor, the Devil's Moor, north of Bremen, to paint what they saw: black peat canals, birch trees, huge skies, and people who cut turf for a living. Fritz Mackensen, Otto Modersohn, Fritz Overbeck, Heinrich Vogeler and, a few years later, the painter Paula Modersohn-Becker made the village a household name among art lovers, and the colony they founded grew into something like a small, permanent festival of the arts.

    Read the complete Worpswede artists’ village write-up.

  12. 12. Ottersberg Wümme roads

    358 km in.

    The Wümme is one of the quieter rivers of northern Germany, a dark, slow stream that winds through meadows and moorland east of Bremen, and the roads that follow it near Ottersberg are made of long, soft curves. The land is flat and wet, with reeds, alders and pollard willows along the banks, and on still mornings the mist hangs low over the water and the cattle stand in it to the knees. It is the sort of landscape painters came to find in the late nineteenth century, and many of them stayed.

    Read the complete Ottersberg Wümme roads write-up.

  13. 13. Rotenburg return road

    378 km in.

    Rotenburg on the Wümme is the town you eat in on the way home. It is a calm, prosperous place of about twenty-two thousand people, set on a stretch of the Wümme where the river is wide enough for rowing boats, with a market square, a handful of old half-timbered houses and a surprising number of good bakeries. It lacks the tourist crowds of the better-known towns, and that is exactly what makes it a good place to sit down with a plate and a pot of coffee.

    Read the complete Rotenburg return road write-up.
