Why this ride

A 195 km round trip from Erlach - roughly 3 h 32 in the saddle. It goes by way of Pointe de Montbec I, La Chaux-de-Fonds / Le Locle, L’Abbaye 2 and back through La Chaux-de-Fonds / Le Locle. It tops out around 900 m.

The roads

Grand Tour of Switzerland

Pointe de Montbec I is 23 km from Dorfstation on Cudrefinstrasse. Fairly level - 426 to 484 m end to end. This is the bit the ride is built around.

The stops

  1. 1. Dorfstation

    Start here.

    The south shore of Lake Biel is a gentle place of reeds, vineyards on the far bank and farmland stretching back towards the Seeland plain, and at Lüscherz, one of the small lakeside villages, the remains of a prehistoric pile-dwelling settlement lie in the shallows. Dorfstation, as the site is known, is part of the transnational World Heritage Site of Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps, inscribed in 2011. Neolithic and Bronze Age farmers built their houses on wooden piles along the shore, and the waterlogged ground preserved timbers, tools, textiles and food remains for thousands of years.

    Read the complete Dorfstation write-up.

  2. 2. Pointe de Montbec I

    23 km in.

    The south shore of Lake Neuchâtel is a long, soft margin of reeds, shallow water and woodland, and it hides one of the richest archaeological records in Europe. Pointe de Montbec, near the villages along the Fribourg and Vaud shore opposite Neuchâtel, is one of the places where prehistoric lake dwellers built their villages on the water's edge thousands of years ago. Their houses stood on piles driven into the soft lake shore, and when lake levels rose, the remains were sealed under water and mud, preserving wood, textiles, seeds and tools in astonishing detail. The prehistoric pile dwellings around the Alps were inscribed by UNESCO in 2011 as a single serial site spanning six countries, and this shore contributes a good number of them.

    Read the complete Pointe de Montbec I write-up.

  3. 3. La Chaux-de-Fonds / Le Locle

    68 km in.

    At about a thousand metres up in the Jura, in a long, high valley where winter arrives early and leaves late, two towns spent two centuries making most of the world's watches. La Chaux-de-Fonds and Le Locle are strange, fascinating places: not pretty old towns, but industrial cities laid out on a grid, with long parallel streets of apartment blocks designed to let maximum light into workshops where generations of craftsmen assembled tiny gears by the window. Their urban planning, built entirely around one industry, is on the World Heritage list.

    Read the complete La Chaux-de-Fonds / Le Locle write-up.

  4. 4. L’Abbaye 2

    97 km in.

    The north shore of Lake Neuchâtel is a long, gentle slope of vineyards running down to the water, with villages of honey-coloured stone and the Jura rising behind. At Bevaix, on the shore below the village, archaeologists have found traces of prehistoric pile dwellings at a site known as L'Abbaye, named after a medieval priory that once stood nearby. It is one of the Swiss components of the Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps World Heritage site. Nothing is visible on the surface; the remains lie underwater or in the lake sediments. But the lakeshore is beautiful, and there is a pleasant path along the water.

    Read the complete L’Abbaye 2 write-up.
