Why this ride

320 km from Darfo Boario Terme to Darfo Boario Terme, about 6 h of riding. It goes by way of Parco Archeologico Nazionale dei Massi di, Parco Pluritematico, Parco Archeologico Comunale di Seradina-Be and Molina di Ledro. It tops out around 2,100 m.

The roads

Passo di Vivione

It is 26 km from Parco Comunale di Luine di Darfo-Boario Te to Parco Archeologico Nazionale dei Massi di , mostly on SS42. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 227 and 387 m. Take it at your own pace.

Rider note

Check the pass is open before you commit to it.

The stops

  1. 1. Parco Comunale di Luine di Darfo-Boario Te

    Start here.

    Valcamonica, the long valley of the Oglio river north of Lake Iseo, holds one of the largest collections of prehistoric rock carvings in the world, with hundreds of thousands of figures pecked into smooth glacier-polished rocks over thousands of years. It was the first site in Italy to be listed by UNESCO, in 1979. The Luine park, on a wooded hill above Darfo Boario Terme at the southern end of the valley, is one of the places where you can see these engravings up close, with paths leading between rock surfaces covered in figures of animals, people, weapons and mysterious symbols.

    Read the complete Parco Comunale di Luine di Darfo-Boario Te write-up.

  2. 2. Parco Archeologico Nazionale dei Massi di

    26 km in.

    Several thousand years of people have been scratching pictures onto the rocks of Val Camonica, and the oldest are thought to be roughly eight thousand years old. The valley runs north from Lake Iseo in Lombardy, following the Oglio river up to the Tonale pass, and its glacier-polished sandstone slabs turned out to be an ideal canvas. The Camuni, an Iron Age people, carved thousands of figures: hunters, deer, warriors, ploughs, huts, dancing figures and a swirling sun-like motif that is now the emblem of the Lombardy region, the Rosa Camuna. In 1979 this became Italy's first World Heritage site.

    Read the complete Parco Archeologico Nazionale dei Massi di write-up.

  3. 3. Parco Pluritematico

    46 km in.

    Valcamonica holds one of the largest collections of prehistoric rock carvings in the world, some three hundred thousand figures pecked into the smooth glacier-polished rocks of the valley over thousands of years, from hunters and deer to warriors, ploughs, houses and mysterious symbols. It was the first Italian site to be listed by UNESCO, in 1979. In the upper valley, near Sonico, a few kilometres south of Edolo, a multi-themed park around the rocks of Còren de le Fate, the fairies' rock, offers carvings, nature trails and an insight into the life of the valley.

    Read the complete Parco Pluritematico write-up.

  4. 4. Parco Archeologico Comunale di Seradina-Be

    64 km in.

    Seradina is the part of Valcamonica that shows how the Camuni told stories. On a broad slab of polished sandstone, shaped by a glacier and now shaded by oak and chestnut, a crowd of tiny figures has been pecked into the surface: men ploughing with oxen, hunters stalking deer, warriors with round shields, huts on stilts and fields marked out in squares. The scenes were carved over many centuries by people who lived by farming and herding, and they read like a diary of an Alpine community from the Bronze and Iron Ages. Nearby, the Bedolina rock holds a famous design often described as a map, a network of lines, dots and little houses that seems to record fields and paths.

    Read the complete Parco Archeologico Comunale di Seradina-Be write-up.

  5. 5. Molina di Ledro

    159 km in.

    In 1929, when the level of Lake Ledro was lowered to feed a hydroelectric plant, thousands of wooden posts emerged from the mud near Molina di Ledro. They were the remains of a Bronze Age pile-dwelling village, inhabited some four thousand years ago, and the finds included pottery, tools, weapons, and a canoe. Molina di Ledro is part of the UNESCO-listed Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps, and the Museo delle Palafitte, on the lake shore, displays the finds alongside reconstructed huts on stilts.

    Read the complete Molina di Ledro write-up.
