Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ushuaia

    Ushuaia calls itself the end of the world, and with a straight face: it is the southernmost city of any size on the planet, tucked between the Beagle Channel and a wall of jagged Andean peaks at the bottom of Tierra del Fuego. About fifty-eight thousand people live here, in a town that climbs the hillside in colourful tin-roofed houses, and the harbour is full of expedition ships bound for Antarctica. The ride you came for runs out of town to the east and west, and both directions are short, because the road simply stops.

  2. 2. Lago Fagnano

    Lago Fagnano is the great lake of Tierra del Fuego, about a hundred kilometres long and shared between Argentina and Chile, and it lies in a deep valley that splits the island from east to west, between the Fuegian Andes and the lower hills to the north. It was named for Giuseppe Fagnano, an Italian priest who worked as a missionary in the region in the nineteenth century, though the Selk'nam people who lived here before had their own name for it. The water is cold, dark and often whipped to white by the wind, and its shores are covered with thick forest of southern beech.

  3. 3. Lapataia

    Lapataia is where the road ends, and that is the whole point. Ruta 3, which begins in Buenos Aires, runs for more than three thousand kilometres to this quiet bay in Tierra del Fuego National Park, and a wooden sign by the water records the distance and invites you to have your photograph taken. The bay lies on the Beagle Channel, in a landscape of forest, peat bogs and snow-covered mountains, with the water dark and still and the opposite shore, belonging to Chile, only a short distance away.
