Why this ride

A one-way ride of 268 km, Mysore to Wellington - roughly 3 h 40 in the saddle. The stops in between: Bandipur, Gudalur and Ooty. The highest point is about 1,700 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Nilgiri Mountain Road

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 50 km this is Nilgiri Mountain Road, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mysuru

    Mysuru, still called Mysore in many mouths, is the former capital of the Wodeyar kings and one of the easiest big cities in India to like. With about nine hundred thousand people it is large, but it moves at a gentler pace than Bengaluru, and the centre is built around the Mysore Palace, a huge Indo-Saracenic building that is lit by about a hundred thousand bulbs on Sundays and during the Dasara festival. The wide avenues, old markets and sandalwood stalls give the city its character. Sandalwood soap and silk are local products, and Mysore pak, a sweet made of gram flour, ghee and sugar, was invented in the palace kitchens.

  2. 2. Bandipur

    The forest begins quite suddenly. One moment you are in the farmland around Gundlupet, with sunflower and marigold fields stretching to the horizon, and the next the road runs into dry deciduous woodland, and the signs warn you about elephants. Bandipur is a tiger reserve in southern Karnataka, joined along its border to the Mudumalai reserve in Tamil Nadu and the Wayanad forests in Kerala, and together they form one of the largest protected tracts of forest in India. The highway from Mysuru to Ooty cuts straight through it, which is why this ride feels more like a safari than a commute.

  3. 3. Gudalur

    Gudalur is the sort of place you pass through without noticing until you need it. It is a busy market town on the western edge of the Nilgiris, in Tamil Nadu, where roads from Mysuru, Ooty and the Kerala coast meet at a crowded junction of buses, jeeps and tea lorries. The surrounding hills are covered in plantations: tea on the slopes, coffee and pepper in the shade, with cardamom and rubber lower down. The air smells of damp earth and wood smoke, and the light has a green, filtered quality, especially in monsoon season when low cloud hangs over the ridges.

  4. 4. Ooty

    Ooty is the queen of the Nilgiris in the way that British hill stations liked to be queens, which is to say it is cool, slightly eccentric and wildly overcrowded at the weekend. Its official name is Udhagamandalam, and it sits at roughly 2,240 metres on a plateau in the Blue Mountains of Tamil Nadu. In the nineteenth century it was the summer capital of the Madras Presidency, and the colonial administrators built churches, clubs, cottages and a famous botanical garden in a landscape that reminded them of home. The hills were originally the territory of the Toda people, pastoralists with their own customs and beautiful barrel-vaulted huts, and some communities still live in the surrounding hills.

  5. 5. Coonoor

    Coonoor is the quieter sister of Ooty, and most riders who know both prefer it. It sits at about 1,800 metres on the Nilgiri plateau in Tamil Nadu, on the slopes above a deep valley, and it lives on tea. Plantations cover the hills around the town in neat green ridges, with pickers moving between the rows, and factories along the roads sell fresh leaf and chocolate. The army town of Wellington lies right next door, a little under two kilometres away, and Kotagiri is ten kilometres on. Coonoor itself has fuel pumps and everything else you need.
