Why this ride

curated scenic road. high alpine, lakes, hairpins. A one-way ride of 20 km, Davos to Zernez - roughly 22 min in the saddle. The highest point is about 2,400 m. Fluela Pass is subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Davos

    At about 1,560 metres, Davos is often called the highest town in Europe, and it is certainly one of the most unusual. It lies in a long, broad valley of Graubünden, with ski slopes on both sides, a lake, a main street that looks like a motorway, and a mix of grand old sanatoria, apartment blocks and hotels that tells the story of the town.

  2. 2. Near Davos

    Flüela tops out at 2,383 metres, a wide, bare saddle between the Schwarzhorn and the Weisshorn, and the first thing you notice when you get there is the silence. The road from Davos has climbed through the Flüela valley, first through pine and larch, then out onto open pasture, and now you are in a stony world of scree, snowfields and two small dark lakes beside the road. The old hospice at the top, a solid stone building, has served travellers for generations and still serves coffee, soup and cake to riders who arrive cold.

  3. 3. Near Zernez

    Zernez is a small village in the Lower Engadine, at about fifteen hundred metres, and the gateway to Switzerland's only national park. The Inn river runs through the valley, a cold green stream fed by glaciers, and the surrounding mountains are dark with larch and pine. The village has an unusual look for the Alps, with white houses decorated with sgraffito, patterns scratched in plaster, and small windows set deep into thick walls, typical of Engadine architecture. Most of the older buildings date from after 1872, when a fire destroyed much of the village, and the rebuilt streets are wider than most in the valley.
