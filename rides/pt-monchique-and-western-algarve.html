Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Lagos

    Lagos is an Algarve town with a past that is both glorious and uncomfortable. It was from this harbour in the fifteenth century that many of Prince Henry the Navigator's caravels set out down the African coast, and in 1434 Gil Eanes sailed from here to round Cape Bojador, a step that opened the sea route south. The same port saw, in 1444, the arrival of the first cargo of enslaved Africans brought to Portugal, and the old arcade where they were sold still stands on a small square, now a museum that tries to tell the story honestly. It is worth the time.

  2. 2. Monchique

    Monchique is the Algarve with the beach taken away. The town sits on the slope of the Serra de Monchique, a range of old volcanic rock that rises behind the coast and catches the rain and mist that the shore misses. The air is cooler, the slopes are thick with cork oak, chestnut and eucalyptus, and the whole mood is rural and quiet. The highest point, Fóia, reaches around nine hundred metres, the highest in the Algarve, and a road runs to the top for a wide view over the coast.

  3. 3. Silves

    Silves was once the capital of the Moorish Algarve, and its red sandstone castle still dominates the town. In the eleventh and twelfth centuries, when it was known as Xelb, it was a rich and cultured city, famed for its poets and its markets, and the river Arade carried ships right up to the foot of the walls. The castle, among the best-preserved Moorish fortifications in Portugal, has thick red walls, towers and a huge cistern, and it can be explored on foot with views over the rooftops and the orange groves.
