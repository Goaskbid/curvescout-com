Why this ride

A one-way ride of 218 km, Cusco to Santa Teresa - roughly 3 h 01 in the saddle. On the way it passes Pisac, Urubamba, Ollantaytambo and Abra Malaga. The highest point is about 4,300 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Sacred Valley Route

Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it. The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 4316 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cusco

    Cusco sits at about 3,400 metres in a high Andean valley, and the first thing it asks of you is patience. The air is thin, so even a short walk uphill can leave you breathless, and visitors from sea level often feel it as a headache or a strange tightness for a day or two. Locals will offer you coca tea, which helps a little; rest, water and a light dinner help more. On a motorcycle, thin air also saps engine power, a carburetted bike may run rich and a fuel-injected one will cope better, so do not be surprised if your machine feels a little tired.

  2. 2. Pisac

    Above the village of Pisac the Incas cut terraces so fine and so curved that the whole mountainside looks like a seashell. They run up the slope in great sweeping arcs, still green with crops, and above them on a spur of rock stands the citadel, a scatter of stone temples, baths and watchtowers that guarded the entrance to the Sacred Valley from the east. The village itself lies by the Urubamba river, at just under three thousand metres, thirty-odd kilometres from Cusco. Calca, the next town of any size, is another fifteen along the valley.

  3. 3. Urubamba

    Urubamba is the main town of the Sacred Valley of the Incas, a wide, fertile trough at about 2,900 metres, with the Vilcanota river running through it and snow peaks standing along the skyline. It is a place of farms, orchards and fields of maize, and the maize is a speciality, the big white-kernelled variety that is exported around the world and eaten in Cusco as a snack with cheese. The valley was the heartland of the Inca empire, with its fertile soil and mild climate, and terraces still climb the slopes, built to grow crops at different altitudes. The town sits in the middle of this, a busy, practical place of a few thousand people, with a market, a plaza and a lot of good restaurants.

  4. 4. Ollantaytambo

    Ollantaytambo is an Inca town in the Sacred Valley of Peru, about 2,800 metres above sea level, and unlike most Inca sites it is not a ruin: people live here in houses laid out on the original Inca street plan, and the streets, with their stone channels of running water, are some of the oldest continuously inhabited in South America. Above the town, a huge stepped terrace climbs the mountainside to a temple complex, built for the emperor Pachacuti in the fifteenth century, with enormous stones fitted so tightly that a knife blade will not pass between them.

  5. 5. Abra Malaga

    You cross Abra Málaga in one climate and descend in another. On the Urubamba side the valley is dry, dusty and Andean, with terraces, eucalyptus and the white shoulders of Nevado Verónica high above. Then the road tops out at 4,316 metres, a cold, windswept saddle of grass and rock, and plunges into the cloud forest on the eastern slope, where the air gets thick, wet and green and the temperature climbs a degree with every switchback. By the time you reach Santa María, a thousand-odd metres lower, you are in the edge of the Amazon basin, with orchids, bamboo, coffee bushes and mist hanging on every ridge.

  6. 6. Santa Teresa

    Santa Teresa is a village in the cloud forest of the Cusco region of Peru, in the valley of the Urubamba river, and it lies at the end of a long, rough road from the Sacred Valley. The village is small and rustic, set among coffee and fruit farms and surrounded by steep green mountains, and it is one of the main routes to Machu Picchu for those who do not want to take the train. Hot springs at Cocalmayo, a short distance from the village, are a popular place to relax, and the people here grow coffee, avocado and bananas. The nearest towns are Santa Ana and Ollantaytambo, a long way off.
