  1. 1. Ettelbruck

    Ettelbruck sits where the rivers Alzette and Sûre meet, in the middle of northern Luxembourg, and it has been a junction town for as long as there have been roads. People here call it the capital of the north, which is a bit of a stretch for a place of six thousand souls, but it does have the feel of a working crossroads: a railway station, a market, and a good number of cafes where farmers and commuters mix.

  2. 2. Vianden

    Vianden is one of those towns that looks like a scene from a children's book, and the castle is the reason. It stands on a rocky spur above the Our River, in the north-east of Luxembourg, a pile of towers, halls and chapels that grew over several centuries from the eleventh to the fourteenth, and was once the seat of the counts of Vianden. It fell into ruin in the nineteenth century and was restored from the 1970s, and today you can walk through its great halls, kitchens and armouries with a very good sense of how a medieval noble household worked.
