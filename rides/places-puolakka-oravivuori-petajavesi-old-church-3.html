Why this ride

161 km from Korpilahti to Korpilahti, about 2 h 47 of riding. On the way it passes Säynätsalo Town Hall, Petäjävesi Old Church and back through Säynätsalo Town Hall. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. PUOLAKKA Oravivuori

    Finland's lakeland is a country of water more than of land, and Päijänne, the second largest lake in the country, runs south from here for over a hundred kilometres in long, island-dotted fingers. Oravivuori, Squirrel Hill, is a forested rise near Puolakka in the Korpilahti district, now part of Jyväskylä, and the lookout on the hill gives a wide view over spruce, pine and the shining lake beyond. The label points to a hill rather than a monument, so think of it as a place to climb, look and breathe.

  2. 2. Säynätsalo Town Hall

    Alvar Aalto won the competition for this building in 1949, and it was finished in 1952, a village council hall on a small island in Lake Päijänne that has become a pilgrimage for architects. Säynätsalo sits just across a short bridge from the town of Muurame, with Jyväskylä, the city where Aalto spent so much of his working life, a few kilometres north; the island is now part of Jyväskylä itself. The town hall is a cluster of red brick and timber around a raised courtyard of grass, and the council chamber stands over it with its high roof, fan-shaped wooden trusses and a hush of slanted light.

  3. 3. Petäjävesi Old Church

    The lakeland of Central Finland is a place of low wooded ridges, narrow lakes and small farms, and Petäjävesi, a village of three and a half thousand people, lies on the shore of Lake Päijänne's northern arm, about twenty-five kilometres from Keuruu. Its old church, built entirely of pine logs, is one of the best-known wooden buildings in Europe and was inscribed by UNESCO in 1994.
