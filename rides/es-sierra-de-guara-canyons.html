  1. 1. Huesca

    Huesca is the capital of its province and the Pyrenees come as a promise on the northern horizon, a wall of blue ridges above a flat, farmed plain. It is not a tourist hotspot, and that is part of its charm: a real Aragonese town where people go about their day, with its old centre of narrow lanes and a cathedral whose Gothic facade hides a magnificent alabaster altarpiece carved by the sculptor Damián Forment in the early sixteenth century.

  2. 2. Alquézar

    Alquézar is a village built on a rock above a canyon, and every part of that sentence matters. The Vero river has cut a deep, narrow gorge through the limestone of the Sierra de Guara, and the village sits on a ridge above it, its yellow-brown houses climbing toward a hilltop collegiate church. That church, Santa María la Mayor, stands on the site of a Moorish fortress, which was taken by the Christian king Sancho Ramírez in the eleventh century, and the town grew in the shadow of its walls.
