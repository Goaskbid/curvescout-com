Gibb River Road

Why this ride

From Derby it runs 1336 km to Kununurra; allow about 18 h 01 of riding. It goes by way of Windjana Gorge, Bell Gorge, Mount Barnett and El Questro. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Derby

    Derby sits on a flat peninsula of mud and mangroves at the edge of King Sound, in the far north-west of Western Australia, and it is where the Gibb River Road starts. The tides here are among the biggest in the country, rising and falling about eleven metres, so the shoreline retreats for hundreds of metres twice a day and the water runs fast and brown. The town is small, with around four thousand people, and it has the dusty, relaxed air of a place that does a bit of everything: cattle, mining services, Aboriginal art and tourism.

  2. 2. Windjana Gorge

    Windjana Gorge is where the Lennard River slices through the Napier Range in the west Kimberley, and the gorge walls are the remains of a Devonian reef, built by algae and corals when this country lay under a warm sea about 350 million years ago. Walking beside those cliffs you can see fossils in the limestone, shells and marine creatures, and the sheer scale of the place is humbling. The walls rise some thirty metres or more above a sandy river bed, which in the dry season shrinks to a chain of green pools.

  3. 3. Bell Gorge

    Bell Gorge is one of the best-known swimming holes of the Kimberley, a series of falls tumbling over red sandstone into a deep green pool in the King Leopold Ranges in the north of Western Australia. It sits well off the Gibb River Road, reached by a rough track of around thirty kilometres from the main road to the Silent Grove campground, and then a walk of about a kilometre or so over rocks to the top of the falls. The dry season, roughly May to October, is the only time you can sensibly get here; in the wet, the road is cut by floods and closed.

  4. 4. Mount Barnett

    Mount Barnett is a cattle station in the middle of the Gibb River Road, and a roadhouse that every rider on that road ends up in, whether by plan or by thirst. It sits roughly halfway along the Kimberley's famous 660-kilometre track between Derby and Kununurra, and the Aboriginal-managed station runs the store, the campground and the fuel pumps that let you carry on in either direction.

  5. 5. El Questro

    El Questro began as a cattle station and became something else. This vast property in the East Kimberley, a short drive from the Gibb River Road's eastern end, was once a working pastoral lease, and it has been turned into a wilderness park, with gorges, hot springs and waterfalls set in a country of red cliffs and baobab trees. Emma Gorge, with its cool swimming hole beneath a high waterfall, and Zebedee Springs, a series of warm pools among palms, are the best known, and the walk into El Questro Gorge ends at a deep pool shaded by rock walls.

    Read the complete El Questro write-up.

  6. 6. Kununurra

    Kununurra is the Kimberley's tidy, green front door, a town built in the early 1960s to serve the Ord River Irrigation Scheme and set among red ranges and wide, flat farmland. For anyone finishing the Gibb River Road, it is the first place in hundreds of kilometres with a supermarket, a bakery and a hot shower that does not smell of bore water.
