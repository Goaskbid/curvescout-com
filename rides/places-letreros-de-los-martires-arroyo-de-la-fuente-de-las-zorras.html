Why this ride

267 km from Puebla de Don Fadrique to Puebla de Don Fadrique, about 6 h 33 of riding. The stops in between: Río frío, Cañada de la Cruz, Abrigo del Engarbo and Arroyo de la fuente de las Zorras. It tops out around 1,450 m.

The stops

  1. 1. Letreros de los mártires

    Start here.

    Out in the high north-east of Granada province, Huéscar feels a long way from the Alhambra and the beaches of the Costa Tropical. This is the altiplano, a broad upland of wheat fields, almond trees and pale badlands at around a thousand metres, ringed by mountains, with the great limestone hump of La Sagra rising to the north. The rock shelter called Letreros de los Mártires lies in this country, a dozen or so kilometres from the town.

    Read the complete Letreros de los mártires write-up.

  2. 2. Río frío

    43 km in.

    Cold water is a rare luxury in southern Spain, and a stream called Río Frío promises exactly that. It is a land of pine forests, limestone crags, deep river gorges and tiny villages, and the painted rock shelters scattered through these hills are part of the UNESCO listing of rock art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula.

    Read the complete Río frío write-up.

  3. 3. Cañada de la Cruz

    67 km in.

    The Cañada de la Cruz is a high valley or drovers' pasture in the Segura mountains; cañada is the word for the traditional livestock routes and grazing grounds used by shepherds moving their flocks between summer and winter pastures, and this part of Spain has a long history of transhumance.

    Read the complete Cañada de la Cruz write-up.

  4. 4. Abrigo del Engarbo

    92 km in.

    Deep in the Sierra de Segura in the northeast of Jaén province, close to where the Segura river is born, the Engarbo rock shelters hide a few small painted figures under limestone overhangs. They belong to the great serial UNESCO listing of Levantine rock art, prehistoric paintings found along the mountains of eastern Spain, with stylised humans and animals in red ochre that have survived thousands of years of sun, frost and goats. The shelters are modest and fragile, often fenced, and visits usually need to be arranged locally, so treat them as a reason to explore this remote corner rather than a guaranteed sight.

    Read the complete Abrigo del Engarbo write-up.

  5. 5. Arroyo de la fuente de las Zorras

    127 km in.

    The name means the stream of the foxes' spring, which is the kind of place name that only survives in country where people still walk. The ravines around Nerpio hold dozens of painted rock shelters, with Levantine and Schematic paintings of hunters, animals and symbols, and they form part of the UNESCO listing of rock art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula. Visits to many of them need to be arranged through the local interpretation centre.

    Read the complete Arroyo de la fuente de las Zorras write-up.
