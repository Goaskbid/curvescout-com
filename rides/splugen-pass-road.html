Why this ride

A 61 km one-way ride from near Hinterrhein, about 1 h 04 of riding. On the way it passes Splugen Pass and Montespluga. The highest point is about 2,200 m. Splugen Pass is subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. It crosses from Switzerland into Italy - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Passo dello Spluga

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2114 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance — cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Splugen

    Splügen is where the Rhine, still a young and rather modest river, runs through the high Rheinwald valley in Graubünden, and the village has been a waystation for as long as people have wanted to get over the Alps. Two passes leave from here: the Splügen itself, 2,113 metres, which drops to Italy and the Valle San Giacomo, and the San Bernardino, 2,066 metres, which heads south into Ticino. Choosing between them at the village fountain is one of the nicer decisions in the Alps.

  2. 2. Splugen Pass

    The Splügen Pass is the border between the Swiss canton of Graubünden and the Italian province of Sondrio, and at 2,114 metres it is one of the most beautiful ways to move between them. It has been in use since Roman times, and the road that crosses it today follows a route that was important for trade between Lombardy and the Rhine, with wine, grain and iron travelling north and salt and cattle south. Its character is bleak and open, with bare slopes, scattered boulders and a vast sky.

  3. 3. Montespluga

    Montespluga is a handful of buildings by a reservoir at about 1,900 metres, just below the Splügen Pass on the Italian side, and it feels like the end of the road, even though the road goes on. The village is at the head of the Valle Spluga, and it sits beside the dark blue water of Lake Montespluga, a long reservoir that was dammed in the twentieth century to generate electricity, and which covered part of the old village. The mountains rise from the shore in bare slopes of grey rock and tussock grass, and in the evening the light on the water is clear and cold.

  4. 4. Chiavenna

    Chiavenna is a town of about seven thousand people at the head of the Valchiavenna, where two great Alpine roads, the Splügen and the Maloja, split in different directions. The name is believed to come from the Latin clavis, a key, which is just what it was, the key to the passes between Lombardy and the north. Romans, medieval merchants and Habsburg troops all came this way, and the old town still has narrow streets, stone doorways and a small museum in a historic palace. The Mera river runs through the valley, cold and green.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Splugen to Splugen Pass

Splugen → Splugen Pass climbs about 2114 m in 6 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Splugen Pass to Montespluga

Splugen Pass → Montespluga drops about 2114 m over 3 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Montespluga to Chiavenna

Montespluga → Chiavenna is 24 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
