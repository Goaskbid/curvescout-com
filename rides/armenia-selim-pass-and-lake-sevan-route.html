Why this ride

Out of Yerevan, one way: 261 km, roughly 4 h 18 in the saddle. It goes by way of Noravank, Selim Pass and Orbelian Caravanserai. The highest point is about 2,400 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Armenia Selim Pass and Lake Sevan Route

The valley floor is behind you and the road starts working for its height at about 6.5%. Corners stack above one another where the ground allows nothing straighter — first and second gear more often than not. Look through each corner to the next one and let the engine do the holding.

Armenia Selim Pass and Lake Sevan Route

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2410 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance. Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Yerevan

    Yerevan is a pink city. The stone that built it is volcanic tuff, in shades from rose to apricot to grey, and when the evening light hits the apartment blocks and the opera house it glows as if lit from inside. It is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, founded as the fortress of Erebuni in 782 BC, and yet most of what you see was laid out in the Soviet years by the architect Alexander Tamanian, in a ring plan around a great central square. A little over a million people live here, about a third of the country, and the capital feels like the engine room of Armenia.

  2. 2. Noravank

    The red cliffs of the Amaghu gorge close in around you for the last few kilometres, and then Noravank appears, a small honey-coloured church perched against a wall of rust-coloured rock. The name means new monastery, though it was founded in the thirteenth century, and it stands in the Vayots Dzor province in southern Armenia, in a dry, rocky country of canyons, vineyards and apricot orchards. The setting is a large part of the effect. Crimson cliffs rise on both sides, the stream runs far below, and the church looks as though it grew from the stone.

  3. 3. Selim Pass

    At 2,410 metres, the Selim Pass is the high point of the old road between Lake Sevan and the Vayots Dzor valley, and it is a stern, beautiful place. The pass crosses the Vardenis range, a line of bare volcanic mountains, and the road climbs in long bends through rolling alpine meadows, where the grass is cropped short by sheep and the wind blows steadily. At the top, the scenery is open and empty, with no trees, only grass, rocks and sky, and the visibility on a clear day is enormous.

  4. 4. Orbelian Caravanserai

    Beside the road at the top of the pass, a squat building of dark basalt sits low in the grass, and it is one of the best-preserved caravanserais in Armenia. The Orbelian Caravanserai was built in 1332 by Prince Chesar Orbelian, who ruled the region, to give shelter to merchants crossing the mountains on the Silk Road. An inscription over the door records the date and the builder, in the Armenian of a prince who knew the value of being remembered. Travellers paid nothing, and the prince's purpose was as much pious as commercial.

  5. 5. Lake Sevan

    Lake Sevan lies at about nineteen hundred metres above sea level, a vast sheet of blue in a ring of bare mountains, and it is the heart of Armenia in every sense. It is one of the largest high-altitude freshwater lakes in the world, and the water, clear and cold, changes colour with the sky, from slate grey to turquoise to deep blue. Little rain falls on the shores, and the hills are brown for most of the year, which makes the lake seem even bluer.
