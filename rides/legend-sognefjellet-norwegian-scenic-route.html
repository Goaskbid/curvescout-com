  2. 2. Sognefjellet Norwegian Scenic Route point 2

    At the top of the Sognefjellet you are standing on the highest mountain road in northern Europe, and you will feel it. The point lies on the high plateau near the pass, at a little under fourteen hundred metres, where trees disappeared kilometres ago and the landscape is a mix of bare rock, tiny tarns, snow patches and low arctic vegetation. It feels more like the far north than southern Norway, and the weather matches: even in July, you can meet mist, sleet and a wind that cuts through a summer jacket.

  3. 3. Sognefjellet Norwegian Scenic Route point 3

    Beyond the high plateau the Sognefjellet starts to drop, and this point lies in the zone where the descent begins to feel serious. It is a stretch of the road in wild mountain country on the west side, near the Turtagrø area, which has been a climbers' base for well over a century and sits below the Hurrungane, a cluster of sharp peaks including Store Skagastølstind, one of the most coveted climbs in Norway. Even if you never intend to touch a rope, the setting is magnificent.

  4. 4. Gaupne

    Gaupne sits at the head of the Lustrafjord, which is the innermost arm of the Sognefjord, and it is a small village in a very large landscape. The fjord here is narrow and deep, with steep mountainsides rising straight from the water and farms perched on the shelves above, and from the village you can see glimpses of the Jostedalsbreen glacier plateau to the north. Gaupne has just over a thousand people and the municipal centre of Luster, with shops, a petrol station and a few places to eat, and Sogndal, about twenty kilometres away, is the larger town if you need more.
