Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Spermezeu - Sunătoare

    Sunatoare is the Romanian name for St John's wort, the yellow-flowered herb that people have gathered for centuries to make tea and oil, and the hill that bears the name above the village of Spermezeu must have been covered in it once. Spermezeu lies in the north of the Transylvanian plateau, in the hills that separate the valley of the Somes from the counties of Salaj and Bistrita-Nasaud, a landscape of rounded ridges, small streams, hayfields and woodland. Midsummer is the time when the yellow flowers open, and in the old custom of the region, people picked them around the feast of St John, on the twenty-fourth of June.

  2. 2. Church of the Holy Archangels

    The wooden church at Rogoz stands in the Lăpuș country, a quieter, lesser-known corner of Maramureș, south of the mountains and away from the main tourist circuits. Built in the seventeenth century and dedicated to the Holy Archangels Michael and Gabriel, it is unusual in shape: the roof is asymmetric and overhangs deeply on one side, and the eaves are supported by carved wooden brackets shaped like horse heads, a detail that seems to come straight out of folk tradition. Inside, the walls are painted with scenes from the Bible, including a vivid Last Judgement. It is one of the eight churches in the UNESCO listing of the Wooden Churches of Maramureș.

  3. 3. Gâlgău - Casa Popii

    The Someș river winds through northern Transylvania in a broad valley between rolling hills, and near Gâlgău and Poiana Blenchii, it forms a natural corridor between the Transylvanian plateau and the northwest. In Roman times, this valley marked the northern edge of the province of Dacia, and the frontier ran along the hills above it, with watchtowers and small forts. Casa Popii, the priest's house, near Gâlgău, is one of the sites of that frontier listed by UNESCO in 2024: probably a watchtower position on a ridge overlooking the valley. As with many of these sites, there is little to see above ground, but the views over the valley and the hills explain its purpose.

  4. 4. Surduc - Deasupra Văii Hrăii

    Surduc is a village in the Someș valley in Sălaj county, north-western Romania, where the river cuts a narrow path through hills on its way toward Baia Mare. Above the village, on a ridge overlooking the valley of the Hrăii stream, there are traces of a Roman watchtower, part of the frontier of the province of Dacia. The nearby villages of Tihău and Băbeni, and the town of Jibou, are all close by.

  5. 5. Zalău - Deasupra Șesurilor Tâlhăroasei

    The Meseș mountains rise to the west of Zalău like a long green wall, and they formed the natural western boundary of Roman Dacia in this region. Along their crest and on the slopes above the plains, the Romans built a line of watchtowers, and the site above the fields with the evocative name of Șesurile Tâlhăroasei, roughly the robbers' flats, near the villages of Treznea and Aghireș, is one of them. It is part of the Dacian frontier on the World Heritage list. Up in the forest the remains are modest, but the position, high on a slope with a view over the valley, shows how the frontier used every advantage of the terrain.
