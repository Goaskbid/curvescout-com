Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Talihina

    Talihina is a very small town in the Ouachita Mountains of southeastern Oklahoma, with just over a thousand people, and it is the western gate of one of the best ridge roads in the middle of the country. The name comes from the Choctaw language, and is often translated as iron road, a reference to the railroad that arrived here in the 1880s. This is Choctaw Nation land, and the tribe's presence is visible in the town signs, the language and the cultural centres. The Choctaw were forced here from Mississippi on the Trail of Tears in the 1830s, a history that gives the landscape a sombre depth.

  2. 2. Talimena Scenic Drive point 2

    Winding Stair Mountain lives up to its name. The ridge twists along the Oklahoma side of the border like a ribbon thrown on the ground, with the road keeping to its crest and offering one view after another across forested hills to the north and south. The mountain is part of a national recreation area, and the forest is a mix of shortleaf pine, oak and hickory, with an understory of dogwood and huckleberry. On a clear day you can see for forty miles.

  3. 3. Talimena Scenic Drive point 3

    Queen Wilhelmina State Park sits on the crest of Rich Mountain in western Arkansas, close to the Oklahoma line, and its lodge is one of the odd survivors of the Ouachitas. The name goes back to the 1890s, when Dutch investors backing a railroad called the Kansas City, Pittsburg and Gulf planned a resort here and named it for the young queen of the Netherlands. The first lodge was built in 1898, but the railroad's money ran out and the building was abandoned, and the current lodge, a mid-century rebuild, stands in its place.

  4. 4. Mena

    Mena is a small Arkansas town at the foot of the Ouachita Mountains, near the Oklahoma border, with a little over five thousand people and an old railroad main street. The story goes that Dutch investors who backed the railway named the town after the wife of one of their number, and the Dutch connection has stuck, so that the state park on the mountain above is named for Queen Wilhelmina of the Netherlands.
