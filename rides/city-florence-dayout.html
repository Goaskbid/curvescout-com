Why this ride

A 350 km round trip from Florence - roughly 6 h in the saddle. The stops in between: Villa de Cerreto Guidi, Historic Centre of San Gimignano, Montecatini Terme and back through Historic Centre of San Gimignano. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

The roads

Chiantigiana Wine Road

The 42 km between Florence and Villa de Cerreto Guidi on Strada di Grande Comunicazione Firenze-Pisa-Livorno. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 26 and 123 m.

The stops

  1. 1. Florence

    Start here.

    Florence was one of the great stops on the Mille Miglia, and it is easy to see why the race came through. Drivers arriving from Bologna had crossed the Raticosa and Futa passes in the Apennines, a long, twisting run of hairpins that tested both engines and nerves, then dropped down into the Arno valley to the city where Brunelleschi's red dome rises above a sea of terracotta roofs. The dome, completed in the fifteenth century over the cathedral, was a feat of engineering that was done without a conventional wooden frame, and it still defines the skyline.

    Read the complete Florence write-up.

  2. 2. Villa de Cerreto Guidi

    43 km in.

    Cosimo I de' Medici liked to hunt, and in the 1560s he had a villa built on a hilltop at Cerreto Guidi, on the edge of the marshes and woods west of Florence, where the game was plentiful. From the outside it is austere, a solid block of stone facing a square, but the approach is spectacular: a pair of great brick ramps, known as the Medici bridges, zigzag up to the entrance. The villa is part of the UNESCO listing of the Medici Villas and Gardens in Tuscany, and it holds a dark legend. Cosimo's daughter Isabella died here in 1576, and the story goes that she was murdered by her husband, Paolo Giordano Orsini, for her alleged infidelity. Whether it is true or not, it is told to every visitor.

    Read the complete Villa de Cerreto Guidi write-up.

  3. 3. Historic Centre of San Gimignano

    91 km in.

    Seen from the surrounding countryside, San Gimignano looks like a medieval Manhattan: a cluster of stone towers rising from a hilltop above vineyards and olive groves in the Val d'Elsa. In its heyday, in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, the town had dozens of them, built by rival families as symbols of power and wealth. Fourteen survive, enough to make it one of the most recognisable towns in Italy and a UNESCO World Heritage site since 1990.

    Read the complete Historic Centre of San Gimignano write-up.

  4. 5. Montecatini Terme

    162 km in.

    The water at Montecatini has been drunk for health since the Middle Ages, but it was in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries that the town became one of the great spa resorts of Europe, with grand thermal establishments built in neoclassical, Liberty and Art Deco styles, set in parks of plane trees and pines. The Tettuccio, with its marble colonnades and frescoes, is the most famous, a temple of water where visitors drink from taps along a counter. In 2021 Montecatini became part of the Great Spa Towns of Europe World Heritage Site, alongside Baden-Baden, Bath, Vichy and others.
