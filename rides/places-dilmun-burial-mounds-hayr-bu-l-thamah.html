Why this ride

913 km from Madīnat Ḩamad to Madīnat Ḩamad, about 16 h 36 of riding. On the way it passes Al Zubarah Archaeological Site and back through Al Zubarah Archaeological Site. It crosses from Bahrain into Qatar - carry ID and check the toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Dilmun Burial Mounds

    Start here.

    In the earliest written records of the Gulf, a land called Dilmun appears again and again. Sumerian tablets from the third millennium BC mention it as a trading partner, a source of copper and a place where certain gods were said to live. Scholars long argued over where it was, until excavations in Bahrain in the 1950s turned up a walled port city, temples and tens of thousands of graves. The match was convincing enough that Dilmun is now generally identified with Bahrain and the nearby coast of eastern Arabia.

  2. 2. Al Zubarah Archaeological Site

    About 457 km in.

    Out on the north-west coast of Qatar, where the desert slides into a shallow turquoise sea, stand the low ruins of Al Zubarah, once one of the busiest pearling and trading towns in the Gulf. In the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the town thrived on pearls dived from the oyster beds offshore, traded on to India, Persia and beyond. Then it declined, was abandoned in the early twentieth century, and the sand covered it. Archaeologists have since uncovered streets, houses, a souk, mosques and a long town wall running in an arc around the old harbour. UNESCO inscribed the site in 2013.
