Why this ride

From Bajram Curri it runs 610 km to Bajram Curri; allow about 16 h 05 of riding. It goes by way of Patriarchate of Peć Monastery, Stari Ras and Sopoćani, Studenica Monastery and back through Stari Ras and Sopoćani. The stored terrain estimate reaches about 1,500 m; a measured road-elevation profile is not available. It crosses Albania, Kosovo and Serbia - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Lumi i gashit

    Here the borders of Montenegro, Kosovo and Albania meet in the Prokletije, the Accursed Mountains, a rugged range of limestone peaks that rises above two thousand metres and is among the wildest country in the Balkans. The label uses the Albanian name for a stream, Lumi i Gashit, the river of Gashi, and the nearest places on the Montenegrin side are Plav and Gusinje, where Albanians and Bosniaks make up a large share of the population. Plav sits on a glacial lake, a cold, clear body of water ringed by high peaks, with a small mosque, an old Ottoman tower house and a quiet lakeshore promenade.

  2. 2. Patriarchate of Peć Monastery

    The Serbian Orthodox Church has had its seat at Pec since the thirteenth century, and the Patriarchate stands where the Rugova gorge opens onto the plain, with the walls of the mountains rising straight behind it. Three churches, joined by a shared porch, make up the complex, and inside them the walls are covered in frescoes that range from the thirteenth to the seventeenth centuries. The oldest, the Church of the Holy Apostles, was built under Archbishop Arsenije and has a serene, early painting style, while the later churches added by his successors show the growing wealth and ambition of the medieval Serbian state. UNESCO lists it together with Visoki Decani and other monasteries as the Medieval Monuments in Kosovo.

  3. 3. Stari Ras and Sopoćani

    In the hills of southwestern Serbia, near the town of Novi Pazar, lie the ruins of Ras, the first capital of the medieval Serbian state, and the monastery of Sopoćani, which holds some of the finest Byzantine painting in Europe. Stefan Nemanja, founder of the dynasty that ruled for two centuries, was born in this region, and the monuments here were the heart of his realm. Ras was a fortress on a hill above the river, and a short distance away the little church of St Peter, one of the oldest in Serbia, stands on a mound of an earlier cemetery with a round, early plan that suggests Byzantine and local roots.

  4. 4. Studenica Monastery

    The monastery of Studenica lies in a green gorge of the Golija mountains in central Serbia, where a clear river runs down through beech and oak woods toward the Ibar. Stefan Nemanja, the founder of the medieval Serbian state, began building it in the 1190s, and it became the model for all the monasteries that followed. Nemanja himself abdicated in 1196 and later became a monk, dying on Mount Athos in 1199. His son, Saint Sava, brought his father's remains back here, and they rest in the church today.
