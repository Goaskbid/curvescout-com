Why this ride

502 km from Lutowiska to Lutowiska, about 9 h 30 of riding. On the way it passes Wolosatka stream valley, Rožok, Ruská Bystrá and back through Rožok. It crosses Poland, Ukraine and Slovakia - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules.

The roads

Bieszczady Loop

It is 22 km from Polonina Wetlinska and Smerek to Wolosatka stream valley, mostly on road 897. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 685 and 824 m.

The stops

  1. 1. Polonina Wetlinska and Smerek

    Start here.

    In the far southeastern corner of Poland, where the country meets Slovakia and Ukraine, the Bieszczady mountains rise in long, rounded ridges covered with beech forest. Above the tree line, the summits are open meadows called połoniny, and Połonina Wetlińska is one of the most beautiful, a long grassy ridge with views over endless forested hills. Smerek, a peak at its western end, rises to over twelve hundred metres. On the ridge, the Chatka Puchatka, Winnie the Pooh's hut, a small mountain refuge, has been a beloved stop for hikers for decades. The walk up from Wetlina or the Przełęcz Wyżna pass takes a couple of hours, and the view from the top, especially at sunset, is unforgettable.

    Read the complete Polonina Wetlinska and Smerek write-up.

  2. 2. Wolosatka stream valley

    22 km in.

    The Wołosatka is a mountain stream in the far south-eastern corner of Poland, draining the slopes below Tarnica, at 1,346 metres the highest summit of the Polish Bieszczady. Its valley is part of Bieszczady National Park and of the World Heritage listing of primeval Carpathian beech forests: dark, mossy woods of beech and fir where dead trunks are left to fall and rot, and where bears, wolves, lynx and wildcats still move freely. Above the treeline the ridges open into the połoniny, high grass meadows that turn tawny in autumn and give the range its rolling, almost Scottish silhouette.

    Read the complete Wolosatka stream valley write-up.

  3. 3. Rožok

    192 km in.

    Rožok is a small patch of very old forest on the eastern edge of Slovakia, close to the borders with Poland and Ukraine, and it is one of the components of the Ancient and Primeval Beech Forests of the Carpathians and Other Regions of Europe, a World Heritage Site that stretches across many countries. Here, on a steep slope above the Starina reservoir, beech trees have grown undisturbed for centuries, some of them huge, with smooth grey trunks and wide canopies, and fallen trees lie rotting on the forest floor, feeding fungi, insects and birds.

    Read the complete Rožok write-up.

  4. 4. Ruská Bystrá

    251 km in.

    Hidden in a fold of the Vihorlat hills near the Ukrainian border, the little village of Ruská Bystrá has a wooden church built in the early eighteenth century, dedicated to Saint Nicholas, with a tower and a roof of wooden shingles and an iconostasis of painted icons inside. It is one of the Greek Catholic churches included in the World Heritage listing of the Wooden Churches of the Slovak Part of the Carpathian Mountain Area, inscribed in 2008, along with Catholic, Protestant and Orthodox churches across the country.

    Read the complete Ruská Bystrá write-up.
