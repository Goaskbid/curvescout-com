Cabot Trail

27 km that neither climbs nor drops much. The road settles into the ground rather than fighting it. This is where a day makes up time without anyone trying. Third and fourth, and very little else.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Baddeck

    Baddeck is a small town on the shore of the Bras d'Or Lake, in the middle of Cape Breton Island, and it has a surprising place in the history of flight. Alexander Graham Bell, the inventor of the telephone, fell in love with the area on a visit in the 1880s and built a summer estate on the headland across the water, which he called Beinn Bhreagh, Gaelic for beautiful mountain. He spent many summers there, and it is where his experiments in kites, hydrofoils and aircraft took place. In February 1909 the Silver Dart, designed by his young engineers, took off from the frozen surface of the lake near Baddeck and flew in the first powered flight in the British Empire.

  2. 2. Chéticamp

    Chéticamp is the heart of Acadian Cape Breton, a long, spread-out fishing village on the west coast where French is still the language of the kitchen and the church. The village sits at the foot of the Highlands, with the Gulf of St Lawrence in front and the steep, wooded mountains behind, and its tall steepled church of Saint-Pierre dominates the skyline. Acadian flags, the French tricolour with a gold star, fly from porches and lamp posts, and the houses are painted in bold colours, as if to defy the grey weather.

  3. 3. French Mountain

    French Mountain is the first great climb on the Cabot Trail as you come up from Chéticamp on the west coast of Cape Breton Island, and it rises to about 455 metres on the plateau of the Cape Breton Highlands. The road climbs steeply in a series of long, powerful bends, with the Gulf of St Lawrence dropping away behind you, and then flattens onto an open, windswept barren of stunted spruce, bogs and low scrub. On a clear day, the view back toward the sea is superb, and on a foggy one you can barely see the verge.

  4. 4. Pleasant Bay

    Pleasant Bay sits at the foot of a steep, forested slope on the western edge of Cape Breton Island, where the Cabot Trail climbs and dips along the Gulf of St. Lawrence. A small fishing village, it has a harbour, a few houses scattered along the shore and a view of the open water that is often full of whales. Pilot whales come close to the coast in summer, and boat trips from the harbour take visitors out to see them, with minke and sometimes fin whales as well.

Baddeck to Chéticamp

Baddeck → Chéticamp is 78 km of getting across. Ride it tidily and keep the enthusiasm for what comes after.

Chéticamp to French Mountain

Chéticamp → French Mountain climbs about 445 m in 23 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

French Mountain to Pleasant Bay

French Mountain → Pleasant Bay drops about 435 m over 13 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
