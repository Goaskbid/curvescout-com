Why this ride

Islamabad to Kashgar over the Khunjerab at 4,693 m. A one-way ride of 578 km, Islamabad to the finish - roughly 10 h 31 in the saddle. It goes by way of Abbottabad, Karakoram Highway, Chilas and Gilgit. The highest point is about 4,700 m. It crosses from Pakistan into China - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Scenic road chapters

Karakoram Highway

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 28 km this is Karakoram Highway, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Near Islamabad

    Islamabad is a planned city, and it looks it. Laid out in the early 1960s as a new capital for Pakistan to replace Karachi, it was designed by the Greek planner Constantinos Doxiadis on a grid of sectors, each with its own markets, schools and green spaces, and it sits at the foot of the Margalla Hills, the first wrinkles of the Himalayan foothills. Compared with the chaos of most South Asian cities, it is green, tidy and calm, with wide avenues and lots of trees. Next door, Rawalpindi, the old garrison town, is everything Islamabad is not: crowded, noisy, ancient and alive.

  2. 2. Abbottabad

    Abbottabad sits in a cool green bowl of pine-covered hills at about 1,250 metres, and for a rider coming up from the plains the first thing you notice is the air. After the heat of Islamabad and the Punjab plains, it feels like stepping into a different country. The town was founded in the 1850s by James Abbott, a British officer who was struck by the climate and the beauty of the area, and it kept its colonial bones: a cantonment of broad avenues, old schools, and a military academy at nearby Kakul. Today it is a city of around a hundred thousand people in the Hazara region, with a bustling bazaar and a university.

  3. 3. Karakoram Highway point 3

    Besham is a loud, low-slung bazaar town where the highway meets the Indus, and for many riders it is the place where the Karakoram Highway stops being a road and becomes an adventure. The river here has just come out of a deep gorge, brown with silt and fast enough to hear above the engine. Mountains close in on both sides, the town is jammed into a narrow bench of land beside the water, and trucks park at every angle outside tea stalls.

  4. 4. Chilas

    Chilas is a hot, dry town on the upper Indus, where the Karakoram Highway runs through one of the most dramatic gorges on earth. Here three of the world's great mountain ranges, the Himalaya, the Karakoram and the Hindu Kush, meet within a few dozen kilometres, and the Indus carves a trench between them that is deeper than the Grand Canyon in places. The stony hillsides above the town are bare and brown, which makes the snowy bulk of Nanga Parbat, at 8,126 metres the ninth highest mountain in the world, seem even more unlikely when it appears over the ridge.

  5. 5. Gilgit

    Gilgit sits in the place where three of the world's greatest mountain systems come together: the Karakoram, the Himalaya and the Hindu Kush. The town lies at roughly fifteen hundred metres in a broad, dry valley at the meeting of the Gilgit and Hunza rivers, with bare brown ridges close on every side, and on a clear day the snow of high peaks hangs over the roofs. Nanga Parbat, the ninth-highest mountain on earth, lies not far south, and from the Hunza side the huge ridge of Rakaposhi appears on the horizon.

  6. 6. Near Aliabad

    Hunza is the valley that makes people fall in love with northern Pakistan. Here, around Aliabad and Karimabad, the Karakoram Highway runs along a terrace above the Hunza River, and the mountains are on a scale that is hard to take in: Rakaposhi, at more than 7,700 metres, rises almost straight up from the valley floor to the south, and the peaks of Ultar Sar and Ladyfinger tower over Karimabad to the north. Below them, the valley is a green oasis of terraced fields, poplars and apricot orchards, watered by ancient irrigation channels that bring glacial meltwater down from the mountains.

  7. 7. Karakoram Highway point 7

    Passu is a few houses and a great deal of mountain. Sharp, serrated peaks known as the Passu Cones rise straight above the village like a row of dark teeth, and the Passu Glacier slides down the valley beside the road to end close to the Hunza River, a tongue of ice scattered with grey rubble. This is Upper Hunza, locally called Gojal, and everything here is bigger, drier and colder than in the lower valley. The altitude is around 2,400 metres.

  8. 8. Karakoram Highway point 8

    Khunjerab Pass, at 4,693 metres, is the highest paved international border crossing in the world, and it sits on the roof between Pakistan and China. The road climbs from Sost in a long series of gentle switchbacks, the tarmac smooth and wide, and the valley opens into a bleak, beautiful world of rounded brown mountains, snow patches and a sky that looks very close. A white gate marks the border and a modest stone sign says what you need to know.

  9. 9. Karakoram Highway point 9

    Tashkurgan lies on the Chinese side of the Pamir, a small town in a wide, high valley at about 3,100 metres, with bare mountains around it and a river flat and braided along the bottom. It is the capital of the Tashkurgan Tajik Autonomous County, and its people are Tajik in language and culture, related to the Persian-speaking peoples of the Pamirs rather than to the Han majority of China. Faces, clothes, and music reflect that, and so does the food.
