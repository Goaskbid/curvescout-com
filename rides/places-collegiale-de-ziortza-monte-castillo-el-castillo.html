Why this ride

From Ermua it runs 342 km to Ermua; allow about 5 h 24 of riding. On the way it passes Cathédrale de Saint Jacques Apôtre, Eglise Santa Maria de la Asuncion, La Garma and Monte Castillo - El Castillo. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Basque Coast Route

It is 35 km from Cathédrale de Saint Jacques Apôtre to Eglise Santa Maria de la Asuncion on Kantauriko autobia. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 13 and 117 m. Best in the early light, before the traffic.

The stops

  1. 1. Collégiale de Ziortza

    Start here.

    High on a green hillside above the village of Bolibar, in the Biscayan interior, the collegiate church of Ziortza looks out over a valley of meadows, farmhouses and eucalyptus and pine plantations. It is a tranquil, solid place, a church and cloister built of grey stone with a fortified look, and it stands on the Camino del Norte, the coastal route to Santiago, so you will often see pilgrims resting on the benches outside.

    Read the complete Collégiale de Ziortza write-up.

  2. 2. Cathédrale de Saint Jacques Apôtre

    51 km in.

    Down in the Casco Viejo of Bilbao, where the old streets run in a tight grid beside the Nervión, the cathedral of Santiago stands at the heart of the Siete Calles, the seven original streets of the medieval town. It is a Gothic church, rebuilt after a fire in the sixteenth century, with a neo-Gothic façade and spire added later, and it sits so snugly among the tall houses that you often see it only when you are standing right in front of it.

    Read the complete Cathédrale de Saint Jacques Apôtre write-up.

  3. 3. Eglise Santa Maria de la Asuncion

    86 km in.

    Castro-Urdiales has one of the best harbour fronts on the whole Cantabrian coast, and it knows it. The Gothic church of Santa María de la Asunción stands on a rocky headland above the old port, its buttresses and pinnacles silhouetted against the sea, flanked by the remains of a castle that now holds a lighthouse and by a medieval bridge leading to a little chapel on a rock. Built from the thirteenth century onward, the church is one of the finest Gothic buildings in northern Spain, and it lies on the coastal route of the Way of Saint James, which is part of a UNESCO listing that covers the pilgrimage roads of the north.

    Read the complete Eglise Santa Maria de la Asuncion write-up.

  4. 4. La Garma

    131 km in.

    La Garma is a low limestone hill a few kilometres inland from the Bay of Santander, green with meadows and oak scrub, the sort of rounded Cantabrian hump you might ride past without a second glance. Inside it lies one of the most important prehistoric caves in Europe. Found by chance in the 1990s, the cave of La Garma contains not just paintings but preserved living floors from the Palaeolithic: hearths, bones, tools and the outlines of shelters left exactly as they were when a rockfall sealed the entrance thousands of years ago. It forms part of the UNESCO site of the Cave of Altamira and Palaeolithic Cave Art of Northern Spain.

    Read the complete La Garma write-up.

  5. 5. Monte Castillo - El Castillo

    168 km in.

    Some of the oldest paintings in Europe sit in a hill above a village best known for its hot springs. The cave of El Castillo opens in the flank of Monte Castillo, right above Puente Viesgo in the Pas valley of Cantabria, and inside it are hand stencils and a red disc that scientists dated, in 2012, to more than forty thousand years ago. That makes them contenders for the oldest cave art known on the continent, and possibly older than any made by modern humans, since Neanderthals were around at the time. UNESCO lists the Cantabrian caves, and a visit means a guided group entering in small numbers, so book ahead and expect limits on the day.

    Read the complete Monte Castillo - El Castillo write-up.
