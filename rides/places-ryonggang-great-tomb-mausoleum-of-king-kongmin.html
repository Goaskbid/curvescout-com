Why this ride

From Yonggang-ŭp it runs 395 km to Yonggang-ŭp; allow about 6 h 49 of riding. It goes by way of Anak Tomb No. 3, Mausoleum of King Kongmin and back through Anak Tomb No. 3. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ryonggang Great Tomb

    Close to the Taedong river, on a plain that gently slopes down to the Yellow Sea, a low grassy mound hides a chamber that has survived for about fifteen hundred years. The Ryonggang Great Tomb, or Yonggang Taemyo, belongs to the Koguryo kingdom, and it is the largest of a group of tombs at Yonggang, near Nampo, that UNESCO listed in 2004 among the Complex of Koguryo Tombs. Its stone chambers are reached by a passage, and inside are painted walls, with the sort of scenes that make these tombs special: guardians, spirits, scenes from life, and the four great beasts of the cardinal directions, the Blue Dragon, White Tiger, Red Bird and the Black Tortoise.

  2. 2. Anak Tomb No. 3

    Some tombs hold their dead, and others hold a whole world. Anak Tomb No. 3 in South Hwanghae Province is one of the great painted tombs of the Goguryeo kingdom, which ruled much of Korea and Manchuria from the first century BC to 668 AD. The chamber, built of stone slabs and covered with plaster, is decorated with murals showing a kitchen, a stable, a smokehouse, musicians and a long procession of soldiers, officials and attendants, some two hundred and fifty figures by the usual count. An inscription dated to 357 AD names a man called Dong Shou, a refugee from the Chinese states, and for years that was taken to be the tomb's owner, though some scholars argue it may have belonged to a Goguryeo king.
