Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mannheim

    Mannheim is a city laid out like a chessboard, and that tells you most of what you need to know about it. The old centre was planned in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries by the Electors Palatine, who built a vast baroque palace on the flat ground where the Neckar meets the Rhine and then arranged the streets in squares with letters and numbers instead of names. Locals call it the Quadratestadt. You will find that nobody needs a satnav to cross the middle of town, which is a rare luxury for a rider starting a long day.

  2. 2. Heidelberg

    Heidelberg is the place that German Romantics made famous and Mark Twain made funny. The Neckar comes out of its wooded valley here and slips past a steep old town, a stone bridge with a gate, and above everything the red sandstone ruins of the castle. Visitors have been climbing up to look at it since the early nineteenth century, when poets and painters arrived and decided it was the most beautiful ruin in Germany. The damage was done by French troops in the 1680s and 1690s, in a war of succession, and a lightning strike finished the job later, so it was never a romantic wreck by design.

  3. 3. Rothenburg ob der Tauber

    Rothenburg ob der Tauber looks like a film set because, for a long time, it had no choice. The medieval town stands on a ridge above the Tauber valley in Franconia, enclosed by an almost complete ring of walls with towers and gates, and its timber houses, steep roofs and cobbled lanes have survived because the town lost its wealth after the Thirty Years War and could not afford to rebuild. It was too poor to modernise, and so it stayed old. Today it draws visitors in large numbers, and the main streets can be packed with tour groups by mid-morning.
