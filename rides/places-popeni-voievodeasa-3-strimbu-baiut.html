Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Popeni - Voievodeasa 3

    Voievodeasa means the voivode's wife, and the hill that carries the name is one of several summits that rise around Popeni, in the north-west of Sălaj. Who she was and which voivode she belonged to is not on any signpost, and local stories differ, so it is safest to say that the name belongs to the long age of Transylvanian princes and nobles whose estates, castles and quarrels once shaped these hills. The point lies about four kilometres from Creaca, a village of nearly three thousand.

  2. 2. Guga - Vârful Țâglii

    Rugășești, Vad and Poiana Blenchii are the kind of villages most people drive past on the way to somewhere else, and that is precisely the charm of the hills that rise between them. The point marked as Guga, Vârful Țâglii, lies on this rolling country north of the Someș, roughly a dozen kilometres from Dej, in the band of land where Rome drew the northern edge of Dacia. A summit called Țâglii is the sort of place where a watchtower would have looked out across the Someș corridor toward the free tribes beyond, and the archaeological lists record a frontier site here.

  3. 3. Strimbu Băiuț

    Maramureș is a part of Romania where wooden building became an art. The county in the far north, tucked against the Ukrainian border and ringed by forested mountains, has hundreds of timber churches, and eight of them were listed by UNESCO in 1999. The one at Strâmbu, in the Băiuț area, belongs to the Nativity of the Virgin and dates from the eighteenth century, built of oak logs with a steep, double-tiered shingle roof and a tall slender spire. It looks like a church that has learned to float, with a roof that sheds the heavy snow and a tower that reaches for the sky.
