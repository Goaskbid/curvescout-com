Why this ride

Norwegian Scenic Route. fjords, waterfalls, orchards. 126 km from Granvin to Odda, about 2 h 17 of riding. On the way it passes Eidfjord and Kinsarvik. The highest point is about 1,250 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Granvin

    Everything in Granvin is measured in apples and water. The village sits at the head of a short fjord arm off the Hardangerfjord, with a calm river, a lake and a ring of steep hills that hold the orchards in a warm pocket. Fewer than five hundred people live in the centre, so this is a place of a church, a few houses and a handful of farm shops rather than anything urban.

  2. 2. Near Eidfjord

    Eidfjord lies at the inner end of the Hardangerfjord, one of the longest fjords in Norway, and the village, with only a few hundred people, sits at the foot of steep cliffs, where waterfalls fall from the plateau above and the water below is dark green and still. The setting is dramatic. In the early morning, mist hangs on the fjord, and the mountains rise on every side, while orchards of apple and cherry trees on the lower slopes recall that the Hardanger region is Norway's fruit garden. In spring, when the trees blossom, the contrast with the snow on the peaks is striking.

  4. 4. Kinsarvik

    Kinsarvik is a quiet village on the east shore of the Sørfjord, a branch of the Hardangerfjord, and what matters about it is its position, between the fjord and the mountains, at the foot of a valley that leads to the plateau. A small stone church stands here that dates from the twelfth century, one of the oldest in Norway, and a visitor can still walk in and feel the age of the walls. The village used to be a trading post and a meeting point, and the stone church is a clue to how important it was. Rich farmers and the church once ruled this coast, and this is one of the places where you can feel it.

  5. 5. Near Odda

    South of Odda, in the narrow valley that leads toward Røldal, the road runs right past one of the most photographed waterfalls in Norway. Låtefossen falls in two great white streams that meet at the bottom and pass under an old stone bridge, so close to the road that spray can soak you as you stand at the railing. It is about 165 metres high, and in early summer, when the snow is melting, it thunders.

  6. 6. Odda

    Odda sits at the southern tip of the Sørfjord, a branch of the Hardangerfjord, and it is an industrial town in one of the most beautiful settings in Norway. About five thousand people live here, in a flat, green valley between steep mountains, with the Folgefonna glacier above and the Låtefossen waterfall nearby. The town grew in the early twentieth century around a carbide and fertiliser industry, using hydropower, and the factories left a mark on the landscape and the fjord. Today, the industry has shrunk, and the town is a base for outdoor activities, with a few cafes, hotels and shops.
