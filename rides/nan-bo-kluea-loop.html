Why this ride

A 131 km loop that starts and ends near Nan, about 2 h 07 of riding. It goes by way of Pua, Doi Phu Kha, Bo Kluea and Santikhiri road. The highest point is about 1,700 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Nan Bo Kluea Loop

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 1715 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance — cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Nan

    Nan is a small, calm provincial capital in a mountain-ringed valley in the far north of Thailand, close to the Laos border, and it has stayed quieter than Chiang Mai or Chiang Rai. The Nan river runs through the plain, and the old kingdom here was a separate Lanna state with its own princes until the early twentieth century. The temples keep the northern style, with steep roofs, carved gables and gilded flourishes, and the town has the unhurried air of a place that visitors are only beginning to find.

  2. 2. Pua

    Pua lies in a broad valley in the far north of Thailand, about sixty kilometres from the Laotian border, and in the rainy season the whole basin turns a vivid, almost electric green. The rice fields stretch up to the foothills, with farmhouses on stilts and buffalo grazing along the dykes, and at harvest in November they turn gold. Pua is the market town of the district, and the people include Thai Lue, Hmong and Mien, whose silver jewellery, indigo cloth and embroidered tunics fill the stalls on market days. Chiang Klang, the bigger town fourteen kilometres on, supplies most of the district's fuel and shops.

  3. 3. Doi Phu Kha

    Northern Nan province is the Thailand most people never see: long ridges folded one behind another, forest the colour of wet slate, and villages where the language, the clothes and the food change from one valley to the next. Doi Phu Kha sits in the middle of it, inside a national park that covers the highest mountains in the province and runs close to the Laos border.

  4. 4. Bo Kluea

    Bo Kluea means salt well, and the name is the whole story. In a narrow mountain valley close to the Laotian border, ancient springs push up brine from deep underground, and for centuries villagers have boiled it down in shallow pans over wood fires to make salt. They still do. The salt was once traded far into the hills and across the river to Laos, and the village was an important stop on the caravan routes. Today the brine wells sit beside the road, surrounded by stalls selling the grey-white crystals, along with local honey, dried mushrooms and woven cloth. Chiang Klang is the nearest large town, thirty-five kilometres back.

  5. 5. Santikhiri road

    The road threads through the mountains of Nan province near the Laotian frontier, and it is one of those routes that makes you slow down simply to look. What you can say honestly about this country is that it is wild, empty and beautiful, and that riding it is a pleasure in the old sense of the word.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Nan to Pua

Nan → Pua is 59 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Pua to Doi Phu Kha

Pua → Doi Phu Kha climbs about 1715 m in 22 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Doi Phu Kha to Bo Kluea

Doi Phu Kha → Bo Kluea drops about 1715 m over 11 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Bo Kluea to Santikhiri road
