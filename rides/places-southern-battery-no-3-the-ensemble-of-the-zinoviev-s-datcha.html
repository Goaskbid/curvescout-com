Why this ride

From Kronstadt it runs 155 km to Kronstadt; allow about 2 h 40 of riding. The stops in between: The Palace and Park of the Znamenskaya Dat, Volkhonskoe Highway, The Ensemble of the Zinoviev's Datcha and back through Volkhonskoe Highway. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

  1. 1. Southern battery No 3

    Sailors in the Gulf of Finland used to say that Kotlin Island was a long grey shape you saw only when you were already too close, and Southern Battery No. 3 is part of the reason the sailors came to respect it. It stands on the low, southern shore of the island, facing the channel that runs past Lomonosov on the mainland, one of a numbered line of coastal batteries that guarded the approach to Saint Petersburg from the sea. The batteries were built for guns and are now mostly quiet, grassy ruins of earth, brick and concrete.

  2. 2. The Palace and Park of the Znamenskaya Dat

    South-west of Saint Petersburg, along the shore of the Gulf of Finland, the land is a continuous belt of imperial estates. Peterhof is the best known, with its gilded fountains and long terraces, but behind it and around it is a countryside of parks, dachas and country palaces built by the imperial family and the aristocracy from the eighteenth century onward. Znamenka was one of these. The name comes from the Znamenie, the Sign, and the estate had a palace and a park that served as a summer residence, laid out in the landscape style that was fashionable in the nineteenth century.

  3. 3. Volkhonskoe Highway

    The Volkhonskoe Highway is not a scenic mountain road but a historic route on the southern edge of Saint Petersburg, running out from the city towards Gorelovo and the old road to Krasnoye Selo. It belongs to the network of eighteenth and nineteenth century highways that linked the capital with the imperial residences and military camps to the south and west, and it is included in the wider protected area of the historic centre of Saint Petersburg and its related monuments. What you see today is a mix of suburban growth, open fields and stretches of tree-lined road, with the ghosts of history underneath.

  4. 4. The Ensemble of the Zinoviev's Datcha

    Here the Neva is a working river, broad and fast, carrying barges and the outflow of Lake Ladoga past factories and housing blocks on its way to the city. Yet on the left bank near Ust-Izhora, among the industry, survive the traces of a much older riverside life: the country houses, datchas in the old aristocratic sense, that Saint Petersburg's wealthy families built along the Neva in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, with parks running down to the water. The ensemble of the Zinoviev datcha is one of them, included in the long list of estates, parks and buildings that make up the World Heritage Site around the historic centre of Saint Petersburg.
