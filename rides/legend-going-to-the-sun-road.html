Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Going-to-the-Sun Road point 1

    The road begins in a village that exists because of a railway. West Glacier and the neighbouring Apgar area sit at the southern foot of Lake McDonald, where the Great Northern Railway once dropped passengers to a string of park hotels, and the park's west entrance is still here. In the 1910s the railway company promoted the new national park as a kind of American Switzerland, building chalets across the mountains, and that image of alpine adventure still hangs over the place.

  2. 2. Going-to-the-Sun Road point 2

    Lake McDonald Lodge is a big log building with a deep porch, and it looks as though it belongs in a Swiss mountain story that someone transplanted to Montana. It went up in 1913 and 1914 as a hotel built by a local entrepreneur, and the Great Northern Railway later took it over and promoted the area for tourists. The architecture is Swiss chalet in style, with massive timbers, stone fireplaces and animal heads on the walls, and the interior is lovely and a little smoky, with a comfortable, slightly faded feel.

  3. 3. Going-to-the-Sun Road point 3

    Here the road stops following the valley and starts climbing the wall of it. The Loop is a single tight hairpin, a looping bend where the highway turns back on itself to gain height on the west face of the Garden Wall. It is the first of the dramatic engineering features of the route, and after the gentle lake shore, it sets the tone for what follows: the road is cut into the cliff, with a stone wall on the outside and a deep drop beyond.

  4. 4. Going-to-the-Sun Road point 4

    Logan Pass is the summit of the road, at 6,646 feet, and the only point where it crosses the Continental Divide. From here, water flows west toward the Pacific and east toward the Atlantic, and at Triple Divide Peak, a little to the south-east, a single raindrop can even end up in Hudson Bay. The pass sits in a high, rocky bowl under the sharp edge of the Garden Wall, and when you arrive, you feel as though you have reached the roof of the park.

  5. 5. Going-to-the-Sun Road point 5

    East of the divide, the whole landscape changes character. The road drops from the high pass and runs beside St. Mary Lake, a long, narrow expanse of deep blue water surrounded by steep peaks, and the air feels drier and windier. Forest is thinner and the mountains are more rugged. This is the rain shadow side of the park, where the clouds have dropped most of their moisture on the west, and the grasslands of the Great Plains lie just beyond.

  6. 6. Going-to-the-Sun Road point 6

    St. Mary is where the mountains end and the prairies begin, and the change is dramatic. Behind you, the peaks of Glacier National Park rise in a wall of rock and snow, and in front, the land opens into rolling grassland that stretches east for hundreds of miles. The little settlement at the park's eastern entrance is a handful of motels, a store and a few places to eat, but it sits in one of the most spectacular positions in Montana.
