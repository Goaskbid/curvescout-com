Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Paris / Île-de-France escapes

    Blérancourt is a village of about twelve hundred in the Aisne, in the green country between Soissons and Noyon, and it houses an unexpected museum: the Musée national de la Coopération franco-américaine, in the remains of a seventeenth-century château. It was founded by Anne Morgan, the daughter of the American banker J. P. Morgan, who came here after the First World War to help the villagers rebuild, and the museum tells the story of the friendship between France and the United States, from the American Revolution to the world wars. A fine garden and a pair of pavilions survive from the old château.

  2. 2. Vélizy-Villacoublay

    Vélizy-Villacoublay is a modern town on the plateau south-west of Paris, between the forests of Meudon and Verrières, best known for two very different things: one of the biggest shopping centres in the region and a military air base. The base at Villacoublay is where French leaders and foreign heads of state usually land when they come to Paris, and you may hear a government jet coming in low over the trees.

  3. 3. Back to Milly Forest

    Fouquet's château is on your doorstep here. Nicolas Fouquet, Louis XIV's finance minister, built it from 1656, with Le Vau as architect, Le Nôtre as garden designer and Le Brun as decorator, and in August 1661 he gave a lavish party there for the young king. Within weeks, Fouquet was arrested, and the king took the same team to build Versailles.
