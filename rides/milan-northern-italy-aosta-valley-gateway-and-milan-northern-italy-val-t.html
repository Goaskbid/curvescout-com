Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Milan

    Milan does not try to charm you, and that is part of its charm. It is Italy's business capital, a city of fashion, finance and design that works hard and dresses well. At the centre stands the Duomo, a Gothic cathedral begun in 1386 and not finished for about five centuries, a forest of white marble spires and statues, and you can climb onto the roof among them. Beside it, the glass-roofed Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II is a nineteenth-century shopping arcade, and beyond it La Scala opera house.

    Read the complete Milan write-up.

  2. 2. Roasio

    Roasio sits on the last low hills before the Alps, between Gattinara and Biella in northern Piedmont, a village of around two and a half thousand people spread across a few hamlets, with vineyards on the slopes above and the flat rice plain of Vercelli stretching south below. On clear days, Monte Rosa rises enormous and white to the north-west, and it is hard to concentrate on anything else.

    Read the complete Roasio write-up.

  3. 3. Aosta

    Aosta is a Roman town with the Alps standing around it like a stage set. The Romans founded it in 25 BC as Augusta Praetoria, after subduing the local Salassi tribe, and they laid it out on a neat grid that still shapes the old centre. The Arch of Augustus, the Porta Praetoria gate, and the remains of a theatre with a surviving wall nearly twenty metres high stand among cafes and shops, and snow-capped peaks rise at the end of every street. It has been called the Rome of the Alps.
