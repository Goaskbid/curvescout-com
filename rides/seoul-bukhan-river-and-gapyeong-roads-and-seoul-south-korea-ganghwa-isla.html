Why this ride

604 km from Namyangju to Hwado, about 10 h 41 of riding. It goes by way of Gapyeong lake road, Cheongpyeong, near Hwado and Scenic stretch near Gwangju. near Osan is the high point. It crosses from South Korea into North Korea - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. The coast is the point of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Namyangju river gate

    Namyangju lies where the Han river's two great branches come together, the North Han running down from the mountains of Gangwon and the South Han from the east, and the point where they meet, a few kilometres east of the city at Dumulmeori on the Yangpyeong bank, is one of the loveliest spots near Seoul. The name means two waters, and the broad, calm river, framed by hills, is a favourite subject for photographers at dawn, when mist rises from the surface. Paldang, where the river is dammed, has become the favourite gathering place for riders and cyclists from the capital.

  2. 2. Gapyeong lake road

    Gapyeong is a river town in the mountains east of Seoul, where the Bukhan river is dammed to form Cheongpyeong lake, and the roads along the shore are among the best loved in the region. The town, at the gate of the high country, has about fifty-five thousand people, and its streets are full of cafes, pensions and shops catering to weekenders from the capital. The air is cooler than in the city, and the hills around the lake are covered in pine and oak.

  3. 3. Cheongpyeong food

    Cheongpyeong lake is a long, narrow reservoir created in the early 1940s when a dam was built across the Bukhan river, and its waters now fill several arms of the valley, with the road following the shore. The dam was built to produce electricity for the growing city of Seoul, and it remains a source of power and drinking water. The shoreline, with its steep wooded slopes and small coves, is far more attractive than the story of its origin would suggest.

  4. 4. Back to Namyangju

    Namyangju lies east of Seoul, where two rivers, the Bukhan and the South Han, join to form the Han, and the meeting place is one of the most pleasant landscapes in the capital region. A quiet bend of water, reeds, boats and low hills with Mount Cheonma standing behind, it has been a place for poets and philosophers for centuries. The town, with ninety thousand people, is a base for riders heading up the Bukhan valley, and its lanes are lined with cafes with river views that are popular on weekends.

  5. 5. Fuel stop near Hwado

    Hwado is a town in Namyangju, east of Seoul, in a landscape of forested mountains and river valleys where the Bukhan river flows down from the north towards its meeting with the Namhan river at Yangsu-ri. It is one of the gateways to the classic weekend rides out of Seoul, along the river towards Gapyeong and Chuncheon, and on Saturday and Sunday mornings you will see groups of motorcyclists gathering at cafés and service stations along the route.

  6. 6. Scenic stretch near Gwangju

    Gwangju in Gyeonggi province is not to be confused with the much larger city of Gwangju in the south-west. This Gwangju lies south-east of Seoul, in a landscape of wooded hills and valleys, and it is home to one of Korea's most important historic sites: Namhansanseong, a mountain fortress on the ridges above the city, listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2014.

  9. 9. Food stop near Kaesŏng

    Kaesŏng is a name that carries weight in Korea. For nearly five centuries it was the capital of the Goryeo kingdom, the dynasty that gave Korea its name in Western languages, and it was famous for its merchants, its ginseng and its cuisine. Today it lies in North Korea, just across the Demilitarised Zone, and for riders in the South it is a city seen only from a distance, across the water and the wire, from the northern shores of Ganghwa Island and the Gimpo peninsula.

  10. 10. Near Kwangmyŏng

    Under a hill in Gwangmyeong, a disused gold mine has been turned into one of the stranger tourist attractions near Seoul. The Gwangmyeong Cave, worked for metal for much of the twentieth century and later used to store salted shrimp, now has light shows, an underground aquarium and a wine cellar stocked with Korean wines, all in tunnels kept at a cool, constant temperature even in the heat of August.

    Namyangju, to the south-east, is a large satellite city of Seoul, with a mix of apartments, farmland and woods. The town has a quiet, semi-rural feel, with vegetable fields and small restaurants strung along the roads.
