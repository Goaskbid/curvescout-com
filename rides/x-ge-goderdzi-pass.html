  1. 1. Batumi

    Batumi is Georgia's seaside city, and it feels a little like three cities layered together. The old Black Sea port still has its cobbled streets and nineteenth-century buildings, the boulevard runs for miles along a pebbly beach, and behind it all rise the glassy towers of a recent building boom, some of them rather outlandish. Behind the city the Lesser Caucasus climbs steeply into green hills, and that backdrop is what makes the town interesting for riders.

  2. 2. Goderdzi Pass

    Goderdzi is the quiet cousin of Georgia's famous passes. It crosses the Meskheti range, also called the Shavsheti, at about 2,025 metres, connecting the green, humid coast of Adjara to the dry, high plateau of Samtskhe-Javakheti inland. The change in a single morning is startling. Coming from the Black Sea side you ride through steep tea-country hills, dense beech and spruce forest, rhododendron thickets and fog, and then you pop out onto open alpine meadows where the air is thin and cold and the sky is wide.

  3. 3. Akhaltsikhe

    Akhaltsikhe means new fortress, and the fortress is the first thing you see: Rabati Castle, a large, restored walled complex on a rise above the Potskhovi river, with a golden-domed mosque, a church and a few towers, floodlit at night. The town of about seventeen thousand people lies in the south of Georgia, in the region of Samtskhe-Javakheti, where the green valleys of the Kura river meet the dry, high plateaus that run toward Turkey and Armenia. Vale, a smaller town, lies to the south.
