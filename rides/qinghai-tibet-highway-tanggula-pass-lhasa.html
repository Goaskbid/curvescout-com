Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Tanggula Pass

    Tanggula Pass is a place of thin air and big silence. At 5,231 metres on the Qinghai-Tibet Highway, it is one of the high points of the road that crosses the plateau from Golmud to Lhasa, and it marks the boundary between Qinghai and the Tibet Autonomous Region. There is little here but a stone marker, a wide view and a landscape of bare brown hills dusted with snow. You will feel the altitude as soon as you step off the bike: a pounding head, short breath and a strange slowness in your limbs.

  2. 2. Nagqu

    Nagqu is the biggest town you will meet on the northern half of the Tibetan plateau, and it feels like a frontier. It sits at roughly 4,500 metres on the open grassland between the Tanggula range and Lhasa, a high, cold country of yak herders, black tents and wide rolling pasture, and the Chinese name for it is Naqu. The Tibetan name means the black river, and the pastures around it have fed nomadic families for centuries.

  3. 3. Lhasa

    Lhasa lies at about 3,650 metres in a wide, dry valley beside the Kyichu River, and almost every rider who arrives there from the east is a little dizzy from the altitude, a little sunburnt and more than a little humbled. The G318, known as the Sichuan-Tibet Highway, runs over two thousand kilometres from Chengdu, crossing a series of passes above 4,000 metres, plunging through gorges and following the great bend of the Yarlung Tsangpo before it finally settles on this plateau. Reaching the city is the culmination of one of the world's classic overland roads.
