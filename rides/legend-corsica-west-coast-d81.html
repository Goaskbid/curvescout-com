Why this ride

curated scenic road. granite coast, sea cliffs, mountains. A one-way ride of 79 km, Ajaccio to Calvi - roughly 1 h 26 in the saddle. It goes by way of Cargèse and Corsica West Coast D81. The highest point is about 850 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Scenic road chapters

Corsica West Coast D81

Slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did. Water arrives beside the road and stays for 6 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ajaccio

    Napoleon Bonaparte was born in Ajaccio in 1769, a year after France bought Corsica from Genoa, and the town has never let anyone forget it. His family house is now a museum, a statue of him on horseback stands in the main square, and street names, cafés and shops all carry his name. The old town is a cluster of tall ochre houses along the harbour, with a modest cathedral where he was baptised, and the best way to feel the place is to sit at a harbourside table with a coffee and watch the ferries and fishing boats come and go.

  2. 2. Cargèse

    Cargèse has two churches facing each other across a small valley, one Roman Catholic and one Greek Orthodox, and that tells you almost everything about the town. In 1676, a group of Greek families from the Mani peninsula in the Peloponnese, fleeing Ottoman rule, were given land on this stretch of the Corsican coast by the Republic of Genoa. They built their village, held to their rite and language for generations, and for a long time the Greeks and the Corsicans lived side by side without mixing. The two churches, one Latin and one Greek, stand on either side of the village, and the Greek church, with its icons and painted interior, is a delight.

  4. 4. Corsica West Coast D81 point 4

    Porto sits at the head of a deep gulf, where the mountains come down to the sea in a great amphitheatre, and it is the base for exploring the whole of this part of the coast. It is a small place, a marina and a few hotels and restaurants around a shingle beach, with a Genoese tower of the sixteenth century on a rock at the end of the bay and a row of eucalyptus trees along the road. The inland village, Ota, climbs the hillside behind and is older and more authentic.

  5. 5. Corsica West Coast D81 point 5

    The climb out of Porto towards the north brings you to the Col de Palmarella, one of the finest viewpoints on the coast. The pass is not high, but it hangs above the sea, and from the roadside you look down on the Gulf of Girolata, a wide curve of blue water bounded by the dark promontory of Scandola.

  6. 6. Calvi

    Calvi announces itself from a long way off, with the Genoese citadel on its rock above the bay and the mountains of the Balagne rising behind, snow-capped in spring. It is the natural end, or start, for the west coast road, the D81, which runs south towards Galéria and Porto.
