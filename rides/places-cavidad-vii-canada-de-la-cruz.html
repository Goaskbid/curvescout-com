Cavidad VII and Cañada de la Cruz

Why this ride

From Nerpio it runs 184 km to Nerpio; allow about 3 h 10 of riding. On the way it passes Arroyo de la fuente de las Zorras, Letreros de los mártires, Cañada de la Cruz and back through Letreros de los mártires. The highest point is about 1,400 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cavidad VII

    Nerpio's numbered Cavidades are a series of rock cavities scattered over the hills east of the village, and Cavidad VII lies seven or eight kilometres away from it, on the borders of the Sierra del Segura. The paintings in the shelters of this district form one of the largest and most varied groups in Spain, with Levantine figures and later schematic marks, and they fall within the UNESCO listing of 1998. Cavidad VII is on a route with the Cueva de la Plata and the Cueva del Peliciego, both of which are better known.

  2. 2. Arroyo de la fuente de las Zorras

    The name means the stream of the foxes' spring, which is the kind of place name that only survives in country where people still walk. The ravines around Nerpio hold dozens of painted rock shelters, with Levantine and Schematic paintings of hunters, animals and symbols, and they form part of the UNESCO listing of rock art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula. Visits to many of them need to be arranged through the local interpretation centre.

  3. 3. Letreros de los mártires

    Out in the high north-east of Granada province, Huéscar feels a long way from the Alhambra and the beaches of the Costa Tropical. This is the altiplano, a broad upland of wheat fields, almond trees and pale badlands at around a thousand metres, ringed by mountains, with the great limestone hump of La Sagra rising to the north. The rock shelter called Letreros de los Mártires lies in this country, a dozen or so kilometres from the town.

  4. 4. Cañada de la Cruz

    The Cañada de la Cruz is a high valley or drovers' pasture in the Segura mountains; cañada is the word for the traditional livestock routes and grazing grounds used by shepherds moving their flocks between summer and winter pastures, and this part of Spain has a long history of transhumance.
