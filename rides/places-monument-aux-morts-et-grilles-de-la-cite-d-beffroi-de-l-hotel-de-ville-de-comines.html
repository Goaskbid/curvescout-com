Why this ride

138 km round trip from Mazingarbe, about 3 h 11 of riding. It runs out through Bâtiment de la Goutte de Lait, Beffroi de l’Hôtel de Ville de Bailleul and Beffroi de l’Hôtel de Ville de Comines and comes back by another road.

The stops

  1. 1. Monument aux morts et grilles de la cité d

    Start here.

    In the mining towns of northern France, the companies built not only pits and houses but whole communities, with churches, schools, dispensaries and, after the First World War, monuments to the dead. This war memorial and the iron gates of a mining cité in Mazingarbe are small parts of that story, included in the Nord-Pas de Calais Mining Basin World Heritage site. They stand among rows of brick houses, gardens and allotments in a town shaped by coal.

    Read the complete Monument aux morts et grilles de la cité d write-up.

  2. 2. Bâtiment de la Goutte de Lait

    22 km in.

    Mining companies in northern France did more than dig coal. They built housing, schools, churches, shops and clinics, so that workers' lives were shaped from birth to burial by the company, and the Goutte de Lait, the Drop of Milk, is a piece of that system. The name refers to the charitable clinics set up across France from the late nineteenth century to give mothers advice and clean milk for their babies, in an age when infant mortality was terrifying. In Auchel, the building belonged to the mining community, and it is listed as part of the Nord-Pas de Calais Mining Basin.

    Read the complete Bâtiment de la Goutte de Lait write-up.

  3. 3. Beffroi de l’Hôtel de Ville de Bailleul

    63 km in.

    Bailleul sits in French Flanders, so close to the Belgian border that you can hear Flemish place names on every signpost, and its town hall belfry, rising over the main square with a pointed spire and a carillon, looks every bit as Flemish as the towers of Ypres or Bruges. Like much of the town, it is a rebuilding: Bailleul was almost entirely destroyed in the spring of 1918, during the German offensive in Flanders, and was reconstructed in the 1920s in a neo-Flemish style of brick, stepped gables and decorative stonework. The belfry is one of the belfries of Belgium and France listed by UNESCO.

    Read the complete Beffroi de l’Hôtel de Ville de Bailleul write-up.

  4. 4. Beffroi de l’Hôtel de Ville de Comines

    91 km in.

    Comines is one of those towns the border runs straight through. The river Lys, the Leie to the Flemish, divides French Comines from Belgian Comines-Warneton, and you can cross from one country to the other on a bridge without noticing much more than a change in the shape of the street signs. On the French side the town hall and its belfry stand over the main square, a tall brick tower in the Flemish style with a pointed roof and a carillon, part of the group of belfries of Belgium and France listed by UNESCO.

    Read the complete Beffroi de l’Hôtel de Ville de Comines write-up.
