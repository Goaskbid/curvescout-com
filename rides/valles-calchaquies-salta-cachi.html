Why this ride

156 km from Salta to Cachí, about 3 h of riding. The highest point is about 3,348 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

The roads

Salta → Cuesta del Obispo

88 km from Salta to Cuesta del Obispo, mostly on RP33. Mostly uphill - about 1640 m, topping out near 2,781 m. Snow can close it outside summer, so check before you go.

The stops

  1. 1. Salta

    Start here.

    Salta calls itself La Linda, the beautiful, and the old centre makes a decent case for it. The city lies in a green valley in the far north-west of Argentina, about twelve hundred metres above the sea, with the Andes to the west and the humid forest edge to the east. The Spanish founded it in 1582, and the colonial core still shows it, with the cathedral's pink facade, the arcaded cabildo on the main plaza and the cafés underneath the verandahs. In the evening the plaza fills with families, buskers and street food, and the empanadas salteñas, small and juicy with beef, are an essential meal.

    Read the complete Salta write-up.

  2. 2. Cuesta del Obispo

    88 km in. Nothing on this route is higher.

    The Cuesta del Obispo is one of the great roads of northern Argentina, a gravel and tarmac climb that crosses the Sierra del Obispo between Salta and the Calchaquí valleys. The summit, called Piedra del Molino, is at 3,348 metres, high enough to feel the thin air, and the views are enormous. The road is named after a bishop of Salta who is said to have crossed here, and the name has lasted longer than his story.

    Read the complete Cuesta del Obispo write-up.

  3. 3. Cachi

    The end: 156 km.

    Cachí sits at around 2,300 metres in the Calchaquí valleys of Salta province, and everything about it seems to have been made quiet by altitude and dryness. White adobe houses line narrow streets, the plaza is shaded by trees, and the church has a roof and doors made from the wood of the cardón, a tall columnar cactus that grows on the slopes around. The backdrop is the Nevado de Cachi, a snow-capped peak in the range that stands above 6,000 metres.
