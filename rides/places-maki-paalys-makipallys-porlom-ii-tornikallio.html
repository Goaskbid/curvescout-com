Why this ride

It goes by way of Sunila Housing Area, PORLOM II Tornikallio, back through Sunila Housing Area and MÄKI-PÄÄLYS Mäkipällys. It crosses from Russia into Finland - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

  1. 1. MÄKI-PÄÄLYS Mäkipällys

    The name is Finnish for something like a hill with a crest, and the Finnish coast has a lot of those: smooth, ice-scoured rocks rounded by the last glaciation and still rising from the sea by several millimetres a year.

  2. 2. Sunila Housing Area

    Alvar Aalto designed this place before the Second World War, and you can still read the plan on the ground. Sunila is a pulp mill on a rocky island near Kotka, on the Gulf of Finland, and Aalto designed both the mill buildings and the housing for the workers, in the late 1930s. The houses step down the slope in terraces among the pines, with an eye for views and shared space, which was unusual for an industrial settlement then. The mill still runs, with smoke rising over the pines on cold mornings, so the place is a working one, not a museum, and the housing area is lived in by residents who deserve some quiet.

  3. 3. PORLOM II Tornikallio

    Tornikallio means tower rock in Finnish, and it is a common name for a rocky hill with a view. Artjärvi and Myrskylä lie in the rolling farmland where Uusimaa meets Kymenlaakso, a landscape of small lakes, red barns, rye and oat fields and patches of spruce forest, drained by the Porvoonjoki river on its way to the Gulf of Finland. It is gentler than the lake district farther north, and it has a well-worn look, with old wooden farmhouses and village churches painted white or ochre.
