  1. 1. Opatija

    Opatija became fashionable almost by accident. In 1844 a wealthy merchant from Rijeka built a villa among the laurels on this sheltered stretch of the Kvarner coast and called it Villa Angiolina, after his late wife. Within a few decades the Austrian Southern Railway had arrived, doctors were recommending the mild climate, and the resort had become the seaside playground of the Habsburg empire. Grand hotels in cream and ochre went up along the shore, with balconies, domes and palm gardens, and the Viennese aristocracy came to take the sea air and be seen.

  2. 2. Labin

    Labin crowns a hill in eastern Istria, three hundred-odd metres above the Adriatic, and you reach it by climbing a sequence of bends through scrub oak and cypress. The upper town is a knot of Venetian-era lanes, with pastel houses, stone doorways and a church tower, set around a small square, and from the terraces you look straight across the Kvarner gulf to the islands of Cres and Krk. Below, down a twisting road, lies Rabac, a former fishing village turned family beach resort.
