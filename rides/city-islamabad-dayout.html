Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Islamabad

    Seen as a base for day rides rather than a destination, Islamabad has two historic targets within reach, and they pull in opposite directions. West, about thirty-five kilometres away, lies Taxila, the ruins of a centre of Gandhara culture where Buddhism and Greek-influenced art met around two thousand years ago. The stupas at Dharmarajika and the monastery at Jaulian stand among dry hills, and the museum holds remarkable sculptures of the Buddha with a face that looks almost Mediterranean. South-east, along the Grand Trunk Road past Jhelum, stands Rohtas Fort, a vast sixteenth-century fortress built by the Afghan ruler Sher Shah Suri to guard against the Mughals, whose huge walls and gates follow the ridge above a river.

  2. 2. Rohtas Fort

    Rohtas is a fort built with a grudge. Sher Shah Suri, the Afghan ruler who drove the Mughal emperor Humayun out of India in the 1540s, ordered it to be raised in the Salt Range foothills, partly to command the road to Kabul and partly to keep the Gakhar tribe of the region in check. The Gakhars refused to cooperate with him, and the fort became a standing rebuke. Work began in 1541 and continued for years, though it was never fully completed, and Sher Shah died in 1545, before he could see his dream finished.
