Why this ride

A 68 km round trip from Albocàsser - roughly 1 h 10 in the saddle. The stops in between: Racó d'en Gil, Els Covarxos and back through Racó d'en Gil. The highest point is about 1,250 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. La Saltadora- Abric VI

    Peníscola is a rock that sticks out into the Mediterranean about twenty kilometres from here, topped by a Templar castle, and it is the one place on this stretch of coast that every visitor knows. The castle later gave refuge to Pedro de Luna, Pope Benedict XIII, who refused to give up the papacy after the Western Schism and lived out his years here, earning the nickname Papa Luna. His coat of arms and the story of his stubbornness are all over the town, which is now a busy summer resort. The shelter at La Saltadora, Abric VI, is a world away from that. It lies in the dry hills above Albocàsser, about six and a half kilometres from the village, and carries painting in the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain. The ride links it with another site, the Racó de Nando.

  2. 2. Racó d'en Gil

    There is a small triangle of country in the Alt Maestrat, between Benassal, Catí and Albocàsser, where the hills fold into narrow ravines and the stone walls of old terraces climb every slope. Racó d'en Gil, a name meaning something like Gil's corner, lies in this landscape, and it sits within easy reach of the Valltorta gorge, the place that put Levantine rock art on the map.

  3. 3. Els Covarxos

    Culla stands on a hilltop in the interior of Castellón, a medieval village of stone houses and narrow lanes wrapped around the remains of a castle. In the thirteenth century, after the Christian conquest, the area was granted to the Knights Templar, and later to the Order of Montesa, which explains the old walls and the sense of a place built for defence. Below the village, among the ravines and terraces, the rock shelter of Els Covarxos holds prehistoric paintings, part of the UNESCO site of Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula. Like many such shelters, it is protected and not easy to find on your own; enquire locally before setting off on foot.
