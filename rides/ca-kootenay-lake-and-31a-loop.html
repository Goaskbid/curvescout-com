  1. 1. Nelson

    Nelson is a heritage town in the Selkirk Mountains of southeastern British Columbia, built on a steep hillside above the West Arm of Kootenay Lake. Silver strikes brought miners and money here in the 1880s and 1890s, and the town's downtown, with more than three hundred restored heritage buildings, is one of the best-preserved Victorian cores in western Canada. A vintage streetcar, number 23, runs along the waterfront, restored by volunteers, and the main street, Baker, is lined with brick storefronts, cafes and bookshops.

  2. 2. Nakusp

    A long fjord-like lake fills the valley below Nakusp, steep forested mountains rising on either side. This is Upper Arrow Lake, part of the Columbia River, which was widened and raised into a reservoir in the late 1960s when the Hugh Keenleyside Dam was built downstream. Whole villages and orchards along the old shoreline were flooded, and their people moved. Nakusp, a village of about seventeen hundred, survived on its bench above the water, and today it is a quiet place with a lakeside promenade, a few shops and some of the best scenery in the Kootenays.
