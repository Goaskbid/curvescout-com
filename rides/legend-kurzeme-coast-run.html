Why this ride

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Jūrmala

    A dune forest and a long white beach is not the first thing most people expect near Riga, but that is what Jūrmala is. The town is a string of seaside villages strung along a narrow strip of land between the Gulf of Riga and the Lielupe river, about twenty-five kilometres west of the capital. Over fifty thousand people live here, and in the Soviet era it was the main resort of the Baltic region, a place where party officials and ordinary workers took their holidays. Today it keeps that holiday atmosphere, with spa hotels, sanatoriums and a very long beach of soft pale sand that stretches for some thirty kilometres.

  2. 2. Engure

    Engure is a quiet place on the Gulf of Riga, where the land is flat, the pine forests run right to the dunes and the water is shallow and cold. The village sits on a coast of pale sand and low dunes in western Latvia, with a long beach where you can walk for kilometres without meeting anyone, and behind it lies Lake Engure, a big, shallow, reed-fringed lake that is one of the most important bird habitats in the country. It is a protected nature park, with thousands of waterfowl, from ducks and grebes to cranes and swans, and in spring and autumn the migration brings clouds of birds.

  3. 3. Roja

    Roja is a fishing town at the mouth of a small river in north-western Latvia, on the coast of the Gulf of Riga, and it carries the sea in its character. The harbour is a working one, with fishing boats, a small marina and a smell of salt and smoke, and the town has a long maritime history. A fishing museum tells the story of how people along this coast lived and worked, from the sailing boats of the nineteenth century to the large collective fleets of the Soviet era, when Roja was a significant fishing port. The beach nearby is wide, clean and often empty, and a pine forest runs along the dunes.
