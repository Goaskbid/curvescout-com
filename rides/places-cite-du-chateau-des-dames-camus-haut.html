Why this ride

A 70 km round trip from Bruay-la-Buissière - roughly 1 h 24 in the saddle. On the way it passes Monument du soldat Marche, Camus Haut and back through Monument du soldat Marche. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Cité du château des Dames

    Start here.

    South of Bruay-la-Buissière the land stops being a miners' plain and starts to feel like Artois, with small rivers, wooded valleys and the first rolling hills. It is the sort of cité that you come across by accident, a cluster of brick houses with gardens, set a little apart from the village, built for the Compagnie des mines de Béthune or one of its neighbours. UNESCO's 2012 listing included many of these, and each has a slightly different plan.

    Read the complete Cité du château des Dames write-up.

  2. 2. Monument du soldat Marche

    19 km in.

    Bully-les-Mines lies just below the long ridge of Notre-Dame-de-Lorette, where the great French military cemetery overlooks the coalfield, and the juxtaposition says something about this part of France. The land around is a battlefield and a mining basin at the same time. In 1914 and 1915 the front line ran through here, and the villages were shattered. After the war they were rebuilt, and the new mining estates, the cités de la Victoire and des Arbres, took names that spoke of recovery.

    Read the complete Monument du soldat Marche write-up.

  3. 3. Camus Haut

    35 km in.

    Camus Haut lies on the eastern edge of the mining basin, near Annay, Pont-à-Vendin and Harnes, where the canalised Deûle carries barges north towards Lille. The label refers to one of the many mining sites and cités of the area, and while the exact spot is hard to identify from a distance, the landscape tells you what kind of place it is: brick housing estates, old pit heads, spoil heaps going green, and the wide, flat horizon of the Gohelle.

    Read the complete Camus Haut write-up.
