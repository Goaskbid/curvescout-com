  1. 1. Gjirokastër

    Gjirokastër is called the city of stone, and it earns the name. The old town climbs steeply up the flank of the Mali i Gjerë mountain, a mass of tall grey houses with slate roofs, cobbled lanes and fortified towers, and above it all stands a huge fortress that dominates the Drino valley. The whole place is on the UNESCO World Heritage list. It was the birthplace of the dictator Enver Hoxha and of the writer Ismail Kadare, and Kadare's novel Chronicle in Stone describes growing up here during the war, in a city of rooftops, cisterns and rumours.

  2. 2. Sarandë

    Sarandë sits at the bottom of Albania's long coast, in a curving bay facing the Greek island of Corfu, which on a clear evening looks close enough to swim to. It is a town of about fifteen thousand, built in tiers up a steep hillside, with a promenade running the length of the waterfront and a view that makes the end of the SH8 feel like a reward. The road you have just finished, the coastal highway from Vlorë over the Llogara Pass, is among the most dramatic in the Balkans.
