Why this ride

From Petrila it runs 182 km to Petrila; allow about 3 h 08 of riding. It goes by way of Banita, Costești - Grădiște, Cigmău - Germisara / Cetatea / Dealul Urie and back through Costești - Grădiște. The highest point is about 1,450 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Comărnicel III

    Not every ruin in the Orastie Mountains is a famous one. Comarnicel III sits on the same forested massif as the great Dacian capital of Sarmizegetusa Regia, a few kilometres east of it as the crow flies, and the number in its name tells you that archaeologists have found several sites by that name, each a patch of terrace, wall or platform on a ridge. What I can say is that the Dacians, before the Roman conquest in the early second century, shaped these mountains with terraces cut into the slopes, with houses, workshops, shrines and defensive walls, and that the remains are scattered across many hillsides.

  2. 2. Banita

    The Dacians, before the Romans conquered them, built a ring of fortresses in the mountains around their capital Sarmizegetusa Regia, and Banița is one of them, perched on a limestone peak, Piatra Cetii, above the village of the same name. It guarded the southern approach to the Dacian heartland, controlling the route from the Jiu valley, and it was taken by the Romans during Trajan's wars at the beginning of the second century. The Dacian Fortresses of the Orăștie Mountains, including Banița, were inscribed on the UNESCO list in 1999.

  3. 3. Costești - Grădiște

    The kings of Dacia put their holy places and their fortresses on the tops of mountains, and the best of them lie in the Orăștie Mountains of Hunedoara county, where six fortresses were built in the first century BC and first century AD to defend the heart of the kingdom. Costești is the entry point. The fortress of Costești-Cetățuie, on a steep hill above the valley of the Orăștioara, and its neighbour Blidaru, stood on limestone ridges with thick walls of carefully dressed stone, murus dacicus, a technique that combined stone facing with timber lacing. The Dacians fought Rome in two wars, in AD 101-102 and 105-106, under their king Decebalus, and the Romans eventually took and destroyed the fortresses, stripping the hills of their defences. UNESCO listed the group in 1999.

  4. 4. Cigmău - Germisara / Cetatea / Dealul Urie

    Warm water has drawn people to this corner of the Mureș valley for at least two thousand years. Below the hill of Dealul Urie, near the spa town of Geoagiu-Băi, the Romans built Germisara, a fort on the hill at Cigmău and a bath settlement around the hot springs a few kilometres away, and soldiers and settlers from across the empire came to soak their aches in the thermal pools. Excavations in one of the old spring basins brought up a remarkable hoard of votive offerings, including small gold plaques dedicated to the healing gods and the nymphs, thrown into the water by grateful bathers. The ruins of the fort on Dealul Urie are low and grassy, but the setting, above a broad river plain with mountains on both sides, makes the climb worthwhile.
