Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Rempart du bourg

    Most people who stop in Bellinzona look up at the castles. Spend a little time at street level instead and you will find the other half of the story: the walls of the old borgo, the medieval town that grew up beneath Castel Grande. Stretches of these ramparts still enclose the old centre, linking the castles to each other and to the great Murata, the wall that once ran right across the floor of the Ticino valley to the river, so that nothing could pass north or south without the Duke of Milan's permission.

  2. 2. Isolino Virginia-Camilla-Isola di San Biag

    Lago di Varese is the quiet one. The big lakes, Maggiore and Lugano, grab the postcards and the coach tours, while this shallow, reedy sheet of water just west of Varese gets on with being pretty in a modest way. Off its western shore, a few hundred metres out from Biandronno, floats a tiny wooded island called Isolino Virginia, and that speck of land is one of the most important prehistoric sites in the Alpine world.

  3. 3. Sacro Monte Calvario

    Domodossola's holy mountain rises immediately above the town, on the wooded Mattarella hill, and the walk or ride up gives you the whole Ossola valley at your feet. The Sacro Monte Calvario was begun in the seventeenth century as a devotional path of chapels depicting the Passion of Christ, each holding painted and sculpted scenes, leading up to a sanctuary church at the top. It is one of the nine Sacri Monti of Piedmont and Lombardy inscribed together on the UNESCO list, and it has a particular link with Antonio Rosmini, the philosopher-priest who founded his religious institute here in the nineteenth century.
