Why this ride

2,164 m between reservoirs, granite the whole way. From Meiringen it runs 20 km to Andermatt; allow about 22 min of riding. The highest point is about 2,350 m. Grimsel Pass is subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. The line favours views over speed.

Scenic road chapters

Furka Pass

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 12 km this is Furka Pass, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Near Meiringen

    Above Meiringen the Hasli valley opens out, and this spot lies on the slopes between the town and the Brünig, the low pass that carries the road over to Lungern and Obwalden. Below, the Aare runs flat and straight across the valley floor toward Lake Brienz, and above rise the steep walls of the Bernese Oberland, with the Wetterhorn and Engelhörner peering over the ridge to the south. It is a place where several of the great Swiss passes come together.

  2. 2. Grimsel Pass point 2

    Handeck is a small place where a great river falls over a cliff. The Aare plunges about forty-six metres down a rocky chasm, and a footbridge crosses the gorge close to the water, so that the spray drifts over you and the roar fills your ears. It is a dramatic sight, particularly in early summer, when the snowmelt is at its peak. A short path leads to the viewing platforms, and a few minutes' walk is enough to feel the power of the water.

  3. 3. Grimsel Pass point 3

    The summit of the Grimsel is at 2,164 metres, and it is a grey, stony place, with the character of a high mountain that has seen everything. The pass lies on the border between the cantons of Bern and Valais, and also on the great European watershed, with water on the north side flowing toward the Rhine and the North Sea and the south side toward the Rhône and the Mediterranean. Around the road, there are no trees, only bare granite, scoured by ice, with small lakes lying in hollows. The Totesee, the Lake of the Dead, sits right beside the road at the top, a cold, dark pool.

  4. 4. Grimsel Pass point 4

    The top of the Grimsel is a bare, grey bowl at 2,164 metres where the Bernese Oberland hands over to the Valais, and the first thing you notice is how little grows here. Granite polished by ice, a few tarns, and the small dark Totesee, the Dead Lake, lying beside the road as if someone had set down a slab of slate. Meiringen is the nearest proper town, a good twenty kilometres of road behind you, and Andermatt is about the same distance the other way.

  5. 5. Grimsel Pass point 5

    Below the summit on the Valais side the Grimsel changes character completely. The road leaves the polished granite and drops in a run of tight hairpins toward Gletsch, a tiny cluster of buildings at 1,757 metres where three roads meet: the Grimsel you have just come down, the Furka climbing east, and the long valley of the Rhone heading away south-west. Andermatt and Airolo are each roughly twenty kilometres from here as the crow flies, but the crow does not have to do the Furka.
