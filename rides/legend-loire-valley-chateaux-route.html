Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Orléans

    Orléans is the city of Joan of Arc, and it takes this seriously. In the spring of 1429, during the Hundred Years' War, the English had besieged the city for months, and a seventeen-year-old peasant girl who claimed to hear saints arrived with a relief force and, in the space of nine days, lifted the siege. The victory turned the war, and the city has celebrated it every year since, with a festival held between 29 April and 8 May. A statue of Joan on horseback stands in the main square, the Place du Martroi, and a reconstructed house of the period shows how she lived while she was here.

  3. 3. Blois

    Blois is a town built on a slope, and the château is the reason you see it from miles away. It rises above the Loire on a spur of rock, a jumble of different eras in a single courtyard: a medieval hall, a Gothic wing from the time of Louis XII in brick and stone, a Renaissance wing with a spectacular open spiral staircase, built for François I, and a classical wing by François Mansart. Four centuries of French taste, neatly stacked, and all of it was at one time a royal residence.

  4. 4. Amboise

    Leonardo da Vinci is buried in Amboise, in a small chapel in the grounds of the château, and he came here at the invitation of François I, who gave him the manor house of Clos Lucé, a few hundred metres away, and a pension. He died there in 1519, aged sixty-seven. The house is now a museum with models of his inventions, built from his drawings, and a garden where some are displayed outdoors. Whether the bones in the chapel are really his is debated, since the church where he was first buried was largely destroyed in the Revolution, but the place has its own quiet dignity.
