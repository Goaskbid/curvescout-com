Why this ride

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Thames fuel

    Thames is the gateway to the Coromandel, and it behaves like one, a handsome, slightly worn gold town that still has a main street of old verandahs and a stack of heritage stories. In the 1860s the strike of gold in the nearby hills brought thousands of diggers, and for a few years this was one of the largest towns in New Zealand. The mines have gone, but the School of Mines building and the mineral museum are reminders, and the long tidal flats on the Firth of Thames in front of the town are a world-class stopover for migrating shorebirds.

  2. 2. Coromandel town

    Coromandel Town is the real thing: a small, unhurried settlement of around fifteen hundred people on the west side of the peninsula, facing the Hauraki Gulf at the head of a quiet harbour. It is the last proper town before the road goes feral in the north, and it earns its place on the Auckland loop as a lunch stop and a point of arrival, since the road in from Thames is one of the great coastal rides in the country.

  3. 3. Whitianga beach

    Mercury Bay is the name on the map, and Whitianga is the town that wraps around it, on the eastern side of the Coromandel Peninsula where the Pacific comes in flat and blue and rather unhurried. Captain Cook anchored here in November 1769 to watch the transit of Mercury, which is how the bay got its name, and Māori ancestors had been using the harbour long before that. The beach in front of the town is a long, gentle curve of pale sand with shallow water, the sort of beach where you can leave your jacket and boots on a bag and actually swim without negotiating surf.

  4. 4. Kauaeranga valley

    The Kauaeranga Valley runs up from the edge of Thames into the steep, wooded core of the Coromandel Peninsula, and it tells the story of the region more honestly than the postcard beaches on the other coast. Thames, about six and a half kilometres away, was a gold-rush town in 1867, and when the gold gave out the loggers turned to the great kauri forests. The valley is full of reminders. Dams built of kauri logs were used to hold water and release it in a flood to float timber down to the mill, and the remains of one, the Dancing Camp dam, can still be walked to.

    Read the complete Kauaeranga valley write-up.

  6. 6. Fuel stop near Papakura

    Papakura grew up on the Great South Road, built by British troops in the early 1860s to move soldiers and supplies south for the invasion of the Waikato, a key episode of the New Zealand Wars. Redoubts and military settlements dotted the route, and Papakura was one of them. Today it is a busy town on the southern edge of Auckland, with a main street, a railway station and expanding suburbs.

  9. 9. Titirangi

    Titirangi feels like the last stop before the wild west of Auckland, and in a sense it is. The village sits on a ridge at the edge of the city, with cafes, a few shops and houses hidden among native bush, and beyond it the Waitākere Ranges roll west toward the Tasman Sea. The name is usually translated from Māori as fringe of heaven, and on a clear evening, looking out over the Manukau Harbour with the light fading behind the hills, it is hard to argue.

  11. 11. Scenic stretch near Waiuku

    Waiuku sits at the head of a long, muddy arm of the Manukau Harbour, south-west of Auckland, and for centuries it was a crossroads. Māori travellers hauled canoes across the short portage between the Manukau and the Waikato River here, linking the harbours of the north with the great river system of the interior, and early European traders used the same route. Today it is a quiet country town serving farms in the surrounding hills, with a main street of shops and cafés and an easy, unhurried rhythm.

  12. 12. High point near Pukekohe East

    The soil around Pukekohe is a deep chocolate red, the gift of old volcanoes, and it grows a startling share of New Zealand's vegetables. Market gardens, many started by Chinese and Indian immigrants in the twentieth century, cover the slopes with neat rows of onions, potatoes and greens, and the area remains one of the most productive horticultural districts in the country. Pukekohe East is a rural community on the low hills to the east of the town, with long views over the fields.

  13. 13. Back to Rotorua Lakes

    The point on the map falls in the rural country southeast of Auckland, between the sprawl of Papakura and the heights of the Hunua Ranges, an area of farmland, small vineyards and forested hills that most visitors never see. Auckland ends here rather suddenly, with the last houses giving way to paddocks, cattle, hedges of macrocarpa and the occasional lifestyle block. The Hunua Ranges, a large regional park, hold some of the city's water supply and the remnants of native forest, with the Hunua Falls and tracks to waterfalls.
