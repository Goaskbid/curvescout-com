  1. 1. Vilnius

    Vilnius is a city that takes a few hours to like and a few days to love. The old town, one of the largest surviving medieval centres in Eastern Europe, is a tangle of cobbled lanes and courtyards on the banks of the Neris and Vilnia rivers, and it is crowded with churches, more than forty of them by most counts, in Gothic, Renaissance and especially Baroque styles. From the top of Gediminas Hill you see red roofs, green domes and the dark line of forest beyond.

  2. 2. Trakai

    Lithuanians hold up Trakai the way the French hold up Mont-Saint-Michel. A red-brick castle with a tall keep stands on an island in Lake Galvė, joined to the shore by a wooden bridge, with reeds, water lilies and sailing boats all around it. It was built in the fourteenth century by the Grand Dukes of Lithuania, then a pagan power that spread across much of what is now Belarus and Ukraine, and the dukes Kęstutis and Vytautas made it a key seat of their power. Vytautas the Great, the most celebrated medieval ruler of Lithuania, is said to have died here in 1430. After centuries of decay the castle was largely rebuilt in the twentieth century.
