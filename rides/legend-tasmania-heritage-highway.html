Why this ride

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hobart

    Hobart tucks itself against the foot of Mount Wellington, with the wide Derwent estuary at its feet, and it has the compact, sandstone-and-timber feel of a port that never grew too large. It is the capital of Tasmania and Australia's second oldest city, founded in 1804 as a convict settlement, and the old warehouses along Salamanca Place and the cottages of Battery Point survive from the early years of the colony. On Saturdays, Salamanca Place fills with a crowded market of stalls selling food, crafts and plants.

  2. 2. Richmond

    Richmond is the Tasmanian village where the bridge is older than almost anything on the mainland. The sandstone arch across the Coal River was built by convict labour and completed in 1823, and it is the oldest bridge in Australia still in use. Cars cross it, cyclists wobble over it, and ducks paddle underneath, and standing on the grass looking at the weathered golden stone it feels as if the nineteenth century has simply been left in place.

  5. 5. Launceston

    Launceston sits where three rivers meet, the North Esk, the South Esk and the Tamar, and the town takes its character from the water and the hills. It is Tasmania's second city, with something over a hundred thousand people, and it feels more like a large, well-kept country town than a metropolis. The Victorian architecture is excellent, because the city grew rich on wool, timber and mining in the nineteenth century, and the brick and sandstone buildings of the centre are among the best preserved in Australia.
