Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Oudtshoorn

    Oudtshoorn owes its elegant sandstone buildings to the feathers of a very large and bad-tempered bird. In the years before the First World War, ostrich feathers were fashionable in Europe and America, and farmers in the Little Karoo made fortunes supplying them. Merchants built grand houses known as feather palaces, with fine stonework and ornate interiors, and some of them still stand. The boom collapsed around 1914, when fashion changed and the war disrupted trade, but ostrich farming continues, now for meat, leather and tourism.

  2. 2. Swartberg Pass

    Swartberg Pass is gravel, and that is exactly why people love it. It crosses the Swartberg range between Oudtshoorn and Prince Albert, rising to about 1,585 metres at the summit, and it was built between 1881 and 1888 by Thomas Bain with convict labour, who cut and packed the stone walls and zigzags by hand. Today it is a national heritage site, one of the finest examples of pass-building in the country, with dry-stone retaining walls that look more like medieval masonry than road engineering.

  3. 3. Prince Albert

    Prince Albert sits at the foot of the Swartberg mountains on the edge of the Great Karoo, a small, whitewashed town with Cape Dutch gables, irrigation furrows running down the main street, and an air of having been forgotten on purpose. Water from the mountains is the secret. It runs through the town in channels, feeds orchards and olive groves, and turns a patch of desert into a place that grows apricots, figs, grapes and olives on a surprising scale. Nobody comes here for speed, and the town is very good at that.
