Why this ride

417 km from Beelitz to Großbeeren, about 6 h 42 of riding. The stops in between: Bad Belzig castle hill, Wiesenburg park roads, Rabenstein castle and in Großbeeren. The climb of the day is near Michendorf. It keeps to quiet secondary roads.

The stops

  1. 1. Beelitz

    Start here.

    There are stations at the edge of town, supermarkets, and a handful of bakeries, and the A10 motorway is close enough that the town gets its share of passing trade. From here, the roads south and west leave the flat farmland and climb gently into a country of old castles and quiet villages.

    Read the complete Beelitz write-up.

  2. 2. Bad Belzig castle hill

    36 km in.

    Above the little spa town of Bad Belzig, on a low hill with a view across the Fläming, stands Burg Eisenhardt, a medieval castle that has survived better than most in Brandenburg. Its round keep, nicknamed the Butterturm, rises over the walls, and the castle's courtyards and bastions hold a museum and a youth hostel. The hill itself was a lookout for centuries, guarding a trade route across the heath. The town below has about twelve thousand people and a pretty, cobbled centre, with timber-framed houses and a market square, and the castle gives it a faintly storybook air.

    Read the complete Bad Belzig castle hill write-up.

  3. 3. Wiesenburg park roads

    47 km in.

    Wiesenburg lies on the northern edge of the Hoher Fläming, a ridge of low hills, forest and heath south-west of Berlin that counts as the highest ground for a long way around. The town has about five thousand people and a small palace with a landscape park, laid out in the English style, where old and unusual trees stand in wide lawns and a stream winds through. The park is open to visitors and is a pleasant place for a short walk.

    Read the complete Wiesenburg park roads write-up.

  4. 4. Rabenstein castle photo

    62 km in.

    Burg Rabenstein is one of the best-preserved medieval castles in Brandenburg, standing on a wooded hill in the Hoher Fläming, and it looks every bit like a child's picture of a castle: a round keep, stout walls and a pointed roof rising from the trees. It dates back to the twelfth or thirteenth century, and it has been a lookout, a family home, a ruin and, in modern times, a museum and a venue for events. Falconry displays have long been a feature, and the site has an owl and eagle collection that draws families in summer.

    Read the complete Rabenstein castle photo write-up.

  8. 8. Near Grunewald

    188 km in.

    Berlin has a racetrack hidden in plain sight. The AVUS, which opened in 1921 through the Grunewald forest, was both one of the first motorways in the world and a racing circuit, where drivers and riders competed at astonishing speeds on its long straights and steep, banked north curve. Racing ended long ago and the banking is gone, but the road is still there, now part of the A115 motorway, and the old grandstand and timing tower survive by the roadside. Passing them on the way into the city is a nod to history.

    Read the complete Near Grunewald write-up.

  10. 10. Photo stop near Oranienburg

    243 km in.

    Oranienburg is a town on the Havel river, a short way north of Berlin, and it carries two very different histories side by side. On one side there is the palace, built in the seventeenth century for Louise Henriette of Orange, the Dutch-born wife of the Elector of Brandenburg, who gave the town its name. It was the first baroque palace in Brandenburg, and its Dutch influence is seen in the proportions and brick. The town became known as Oranienburg, the castle of Orange, and the palace now houses a museum and gardens.

    Read the complete Photo stop near Oranienburg write-up.

  11. 11. Near Waldsieversdorf, on the way back

  12. 12. Food stop near Grünheide

    351 km in. A good place for a break.

    For most of its history, Grünheide was a sleepy village of lakes and pine forests on the south-eastern edge of Berlin. Then Tesla arrived. The enormous electric car factory that opened here in 2022 transformed the place into a symbol of industrial change, controversy over water and forests, and new traffic. Even so, the countryside around it still offers some of the nicest lake landscapes near the capital.

    Read the complete Fuel stop near Grünheide write-up.

  13. 13. Back to Beelitz

    The end: 417 km.

    It owes its modest fame to a battle. In August 1813, during the Napoleonic Wars, a Prussian and Swedish army under Bülow defeated a French force marching on Berlin, which saved the Prussian capital from occupation. A monument in the town marks the spot, and the battle is remembered each year with a local commemoration.
