Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Auckland

    Auckland is built on a volcanic field. More than fifty cones and craters, some now covered in grass and sheep, others in houses, stand across the narrow isthmus between two harbours, the Waitematā to the north and the Manukau to the south. The Māori name for the city, Tāmaki Makaurau, is often translated as Tāmaki desired by many, a nod to the fertile soil and the sea on either side that made it a prized place to live long before Europeans arrived. The largest cones, such as Maungawhau, Mount Eden, and Maungakiekie, One Tree Hill, still carry the terraces of old fortified villages.

    Read the complete Auckland write-up.

  2. 2. Raglan

    Surfers have been making the pilgrimage to Raglan since the 1960s, when the left-hand point break at Manu Bay appeared in the classic surf film The Endless Summer. The waves peel along a rocky shoreline for what feels like forever, and on a good swell you can sit on the bluff and watch riders carving one long wall after another. The town has kept a relaxed, slightly bohemian character that grew out of that culture, with cafés, galleries, surf shops and people walking barefoot down the main street.
