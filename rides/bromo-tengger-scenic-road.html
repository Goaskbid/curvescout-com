Why this ride

A 100 km one-way ride from near Malang, about 1 h 42 of riding. On the way it passes Tumpang, Jemplang and Mount Bromo. The highest point is about 2,400 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Bromo Tengger Scenic Road

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2400 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance. Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Malang

    Malang is a cool, hilly city in East Java, at about 450 metres, and the altitude is the thing everyone mentions. After the heat of Surabaya, a couple of hours to the north, the evening air is a relief, and for the Dutch it was a favourite hill station, laid out in the early twentieth century with broad avenues, big trees and neat colonial villas around the central square. Some of that has survived, and the old streets near the Tugu monument still have a faded charm, though the city is now large and busy, with a university population and a lot of motorbikes.

  2. 2. Tumpang

    Tumpang is a busy market town at the eastern edge of the Malang plain, in the heart of East Java, and for most visitors it is the place where the road to Mount Bromo begins. The town is where the highway from Malang squeezes through a bottleneck of shops, stalls and minibuses, and where the jeeps wait that take travellers up to the volcanoes. Behind it, the Tengger mountains rise in blue ridges, with the smoking cone of Semeru, the highest peak on Java, hovering above on a clear day.

  3. 3. Jemplang

    Jemplang is the place where the earth gives way. The road from Tumpang climbs through villages and pine forest to a rim at about 2,400 metres, and suddenly the whole Tengger caldera opens below: a flat, grey floor of volcanic sand, ten kilometres across, with the cones of Bromo and Batok rising from it like toys on a tray, and beyond them, the great symmetrical bulk of Semeru, the highest mountain on Java at 3,676 metres, puffing a plume of ash every twenty minutes or so. It is one of the most startling views in Indonesia, and it takes a moment to understand the scale.

  4. 4. Mount Bromo viewpoint

    Bromo is not the tallest peak in the Tengger caldera, but it is the one that smokes, and nobody who sees it forgets it. The crater, at 2,329 metres, sits in the middle of a plain of grey volcanic sand, with a steady plume of white sulphurous steam rising from its throat, and the smell of rotten eggs drifting down the slope. To the south, the neat cone of Batok, covered in green ridges, stands beside it, and behind them both, Semeru, bigger and often more active, sends up puffs of ash. The whole scene looks like an illustration from a children's book about volcanoes.

  5. 5. Cemoro Lawang

    Cemoro Lawang sits on the rim of the Tengger caldera in East Java, and the view from the village edge is one of the strangest in the tropics. Below you lies a vast, flat sea of grey volcanic sand, and out of it rise the cones of Bromo, with its smoking crater, and Semeru beyond, the highest peak on Java. The village is high, cold at night and wrapped in mist at dawn, and everything about it feels a long way from the sweaty lowlands you left. Pasuruan and Lawang are the nearest big towns, around thirty kilometres away and a long climb below.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Malang to Tumpang

Malang → Tumpang is 18 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Tumpang to Jemplang

Tumpang → Jemplang climbs about 2400 m in 24 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Jemplang to Mount Bromo viewpoint

Jemplang → Mount Bromo viewpoint drops about 2400 m over 20 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Mount Bromo viewpoint to Cemoro Lawang

Mount Bromo viewpoint → Cemoro Lawang is 3 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
