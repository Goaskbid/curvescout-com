Why this ride

Out of Manitou Springs, one way: 28 km, roughly 28 min in the saddle. On the way it passes Crystal Reservoir and Devils Playground. The highest point is about 4,300 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Pikes Peak Highway

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 3920 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance. Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cascade Colorado

    Cascade is a small settlement on the western edge of Colorado Springs, at the foot of Pikes Peak, and it is where the toll road begins. The village sits at the entrance to Ute Pass, an old Native American trail that climbs from the plains into the mountains, and the main road to the west still follows its route. At the gate, you pay the toll, check the weather, and begin a climb of nearly eight thousand feet in around nineteen miles.

  2. 2. Crystal Reservoir

    Not many reservoirs cost an entry fee for the road, but then not many are on the way up a fourteen-thousand-foot mountain. Crystal Reservoir sits in a granite bowl partway up the Pikes Peak Highway, a small, still lake edged with spruce, with the slopes of the peak rising behind. It is the first place on the climb where the world feels properly alpine, and a good moment to take a breath, check the weather, and look at what the sky is planning.

  3. 3. Devils Playground

    Devils Playground is the name of a stack of weathered pink granite boulders beside the road, on the shoulder of Pikes Peak at around 3,900 metres. The wind, frost and violent temperature swings of the high country have broken the rock into jumbled tors and slabs, and the effect is a landscape that looks half-built by a bored giant.

  4. 4. Pikes Peak summit

    At fourteen thousand one hundred and fifteen feet, or 4,302 metres, the summit of Pikes Peak is a cold, bright, windy platform at the eastern edge of the Rockies, and there is a feeling of having arrived somewhere that is not quite the planet you started on. The air holds about sixty percent of the oxygen you are used to, so walking from the bike to the summit house is enough to make you breathe hard, and a headache or dizziness is common. Take it slowly, drink water and do not rush.
