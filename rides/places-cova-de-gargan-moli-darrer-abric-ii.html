Why this ride

From Vistabella del Maestrazgo it runs 220 km to Vistabella del Maestrazgo; allow about 4 h 49 of riding. It goes by way of La Covassa, molí Darrer- Abric I, mas d'en Badenes and molí Darrer- Abric II. The stored terrain estimate reaches about 1,250 m; a measured road-elevation profile is not available.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cova de Gargán

    High in the interior of Castellón, where the coastal orange groves are long forgotten and the land climbs toward the Penyagolosa massif, the shelter known as Cova de Gargán sits in a country of limestone ledges, holm oak and dry-stone terraces. Vistabella del Maestrazgo is the nearest village, a handful of kilometres away on its high plateau, with Villahermosa del Río down in its valley to the other side and Lucena del Cid a little further south. It is quiet up here in a way that the coast never is.

  2. 2. La Covassa

    Culla sits on a narrow rocky crest in the Alt Maestrat, the inland highlands of Castellón, its houses wrapped around the remains of a castle that once controlled a large swathe of these mountains. The Knights Templar held it in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, and the village still has the feel of a lookout, with views in every direction over terraced slopes, stone walls and empty ridges. La Covassa, just below the village, is one of the many rock shelters in this part of Valencia that hold prehistoric paintings, part of the Mediterranean rock art group that UNESCO protects across eastern Spain.

  3. 3. molí Darrer- Abric I

    The Alt Maestrat is the high, rugged interior of Castellón province, a country of limestone plateaus, deep ravines and stone villages perched on ridges, and it is crossed by a web of dry-stone walls that farmers have been building and rebuilding for centuries. Near the villages of Benassal, Catí and Villafranca del Cid, under a rock overhang beside an old mill site, the shelter called Molí Darrer holds prehistoric paintings, part of the scattered Levantine rock art that UNESCO inscribed as one site across eastern Spain.

  5. 5. molí Darrer- Abric II

    A molí is a mill, and the one that lends its name to this shelter was presumably a water mill on one of the small streams that trickle through the hills around Benassal, in the high interior of Castellón province. Molí Darrer, the Last Mill, was probably the last one before the water ran out or the valley ended, and the shelter here, Abric II, lies about nine kilometres from the town. It carries paintings of the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain, and is linked to a neighbouring shelter at Mas d'en Badenes and to the Centelles site, which are on the same ride.
