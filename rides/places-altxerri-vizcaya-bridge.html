Why this ride

A 327 km round trip from Zarautz - roughly 4 h 23 in the saddle. On the way it passes Cathedrale de Vitoria-Gasteiz, Vizcaya Bridge and back through Cathedrale de Vitoria-Gasteiz. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Altxerri → Cathedrale de Vitoria-Gasteiz

Altxerri to Cathedrale de Vitoria-Gasteiz: 86 km on Kantauriko autobidea. A proper up-and-over: 800 m of climbing, 579 m at the top, 300 m down. Fill the tank before the climb.

The stops

  1. 1. Altxerri

    Start here.

    The Basque coast between Orio and Getaria is a place where steep green hills fall straight into the Bay of Biscay, and Altxerri is a cave in those hills, a few kilometres from Zarautz, one of the great surf and holiday towns of the province. Zarautz has a long beach, a seafront promenade and over twenty thousand people, which means fuel, lunch and everything else you might need.

    Read the complete Altxerri write-up.

  2. 2. Cathedrale de Vitoria-Gasteiz

    86 km in.

    Vitoria-Gasteiz is the capital of the Basque Country and possibly its most underrated city. It sits on a high plateau, the Llanada Alavesa, surrounded by low mountains and wide farmland, and its medieval old town climbs a small hill in the shape of an almond, with the old cathedral of Santa María at the top. This is the cathedral the label refers to, a Gothic church begun in the thirteenth century as both a temple and part of the town's defences.

    Read the complete Cathedrale de Vitoria-Gasteiz write-up.

  3. 3. Vizcaya Bridge

    161 km in.

    Few riders get the chance to cross a river hanging from a bridge, but at Portugalete you can. The Vizcaya Bridge, opened in 1893 across the mouth of the Nervión just downstream of Bilbao, is the oldest transporter bridge in the world: a tall iron structure from which a gondola, suspended on cables, glides from one bank to the other carrying cars, motorcycles, bikes and pedestrians in a couple of minutes. It was designed by the Basque architect Alberto de Palacio, often described as a disciple of Gustave Eiffel, to let tall ships pass beneath while still linking the two towns, and UNESCO listed it in 2006.

    Read the complete Vizcaya Bridge write-up.
