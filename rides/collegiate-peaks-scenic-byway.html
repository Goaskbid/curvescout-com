Why this ride

A one-way ride of 200 km, Salida to Leadville - roughly 2 h 42 in the saddle. The stops in between: Buena Vista and Twin Lakes. The highest point is about 3,450 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Top of the Rockies Scenic Byway

Slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did. Water arrives beside the road and stays for 45 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Salida

    Salida stands where the Arkansas River leaves its mountain valley and starts to spread out, a former railroad town of about five thousand in central Colorado, at about seven thousand feet. The old brick downtown is on the National Register of Historic Places, and has the kind of independent bookshops, galleries, bakeries and brewpubs that make it a pleasant place to spend an evening. The river runs right through town, and in summer it is full of kayakers, rafters and paddleboarders enjoying a whitewater park.

  2. 2. Buena Vista

    Buena Vista sits in the Arkansas River valley at the foot of the Collegiate Peaks, and the first thing to know is that nobody there says it the Spanish way. Locals call it Byoo-na Vista, and they will tell you so with a smile. The town is small, with a few thousand people, a main street of brick buildings and a courthouse square, and it stands on a broad, high valley floor with the wall of the Sawatch Range rising to the west, a line of fourteen-thousand-foot summits capped with snow for most of the year.

  3. 3. Twin Lakes

    Twin Lakes sits in a basin under the highest mountain in Colorado, and it is one of the places where the Rockies feel larger than life. The two lakes, glacial in origin, lie side by side at about 9,200 feet, fed by streams from the surrounding peaks, and the great pale bulk of Mount Elbert, at 14,433 feet the highest point in the whole Rocky Mountain range, rises behind them. The village is tiny, with a few dozen residents, and the road that runs past it is the approach to Independence Pass, one of the great mountain crossings in the American West. Leadville is the nearest town, twenty kilometres away, and Aspen is on the far side of the pass.

  4. 4. Leadville

    Leadville claims to be the highest incorporated city in the United States, sitting at 3,094 metres, or 10,152 feet, and when you step off the bike you will believe it. The air is thin, the nights are cold even in July, and the town itself, a grid of Victorian brick and false-front wooden buildings on a broad plateau, feels like a place that has survived on stubbornness. Around it stand the highest mountains in Colorado, with Mount Elbert, the state's top summit at 4,401 metres, and Mount Massive both visible on a clear day.
