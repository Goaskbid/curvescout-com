Why this ride

Jura curves and a lake return with big views. A 111 km loop that starts and ends near Gex, about 2 h 07 of riding. On the way it passes Jura balcony road, Nyon lakeside and La Dôle view area. The highest point is about 950 m. It crosses from France into Switzerland - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. The passes are the reason to go.

Roadbook

  1. Col de la Faucille start

    The Col de la Faucille is the Jura's front door to Lake Geneva. At about 1,320 metres, this pass on the French side of the border carries the main road from Gex up and over the ridge toward Les Rousses and the Haut-Jura, and the first view from the top is the thing people remember: a long drop through meadows and forest to the lake, a smudge of Geneva beyond it, and on clear days the white dome of Mont Blanc floating above everything. The name means the sickle pass, after the curved shape of the ridge, and it has been used as a route for centuries.

    The ride up from Gex is the kind of road that makes you glad to have left the city. It starts among the villages and fields of the Pays de Gex, a flat shelf between the Jura and the lake, then climbs through beech and spruce in a succession of well-banked bends. The surface is generally good, with moderate gradients and enough hairpins to keep things interesting. It is popular with cyclists and sports-car drivers, so on weekends you will want to ride with patience, and winter can bring snow and ice. Early morning, with the mist in the valley below and the sun hitting the ridge, is the best time.

    The Jura is limestone country, and the cheese reflects it. This is the area of Bleu de Gex, a mild, creamy blue cheese made in the high pastures, and of Comté and Morbier a little farther north. The pass has a small ski area, with lifts and a hotel or two, and a few places selling hot drinks, crêpes and local specialities. The pastures are grazed by cows with big bells, and in spring the slopes are covered with gentians and narcissi.

    Gex, about five kilometres below, has fuel, supermarkets, bakeries and cafes, so top up there before you climb, and stock up on water and snacks. Services at the pass itself are limited and seasonal. Weather can change quickly on the ridge, with fog rolling in from the Swiss side and a wind that can find every gap in a jacket. It is a good place to begin a ride, with a coffee and a map, and the ability to see exactly where you are going.

  2. Jura balcony road scenic

    The Haut-Jura is the part of France that tourists tend to overlook, and that suits the people who live here very well. The roads run along high plateaus and wooded ridges north-west of Geneva, with long views across the green rolling hills toward the distant white line of the Alps. On a clear day, the Mont Blanc massif appears above the haze like a mirage, and the road seems to be a balcony from which to watch it. It is a gentle landscape, with dairy farms, tall spruce forests, and a quiet that is easy to enjoy.

    The area is famous for its cheese. Comté, the hard, nutty cheese aged in mountain cellars, comes from the cows that graze these pastures, and Morbier, with its dark line through the middle, and Bleu de Gex are made nearby. The craft of the region is also worth noting: Morez, a nearby town, has long been the centre of French spectacle-making, and its workshops once supplied much of the country's eyewear. In winter the area turns to cross-country skiing, with hundreds of kilometres of trails, and the small town of Les Rousses is a hub for it.

    The roads are a delight for a rider who likes flow rather than drama. The bends are open, the gradients gentle, and the surface is usually good. Traffic is light outside the summer holidays, and the villages are quiet. The weather can change fast, with fog and low cloud on the plateau, and in winter snow and ice are common from late autumn to spring. Mornings in early summer, when the meadows are full of flowers and the air smells of cut grass, are the best time.

    Les Rousses, about six kilometres away, has fuel, cafes, and a lake, and Morez, nine kilometres off, is the bigger town with supermarkets and more services. Longchaumois is a smaller village with a good bakery or two. Pick up some Comté from a local dairy as a souvenir, and take time to taste it with a glass of Jura wine, which can be pale, nutty, and unexpected. It is a place that rewards a slow pace, and the best views come when you stop and look back.

  3. Nyon lakeside food

    Nyon is a small Swiss town on the north shore of Lake Geneva, and it carries a surprising amount of history in a very pleasant setting. The old town rises from the lakeside in a tumble of tiled roofs, with a castle of round towers at the top and a promenade along the water at the bottom. Behind it, the vineyards of La Côte slope up toward the Jura, and in front of it the lake stretches across to the French shore and the snowy Alps beyond.

    The Romans founded a colony here, Colonia Iulia Equestris, around 45 BC under Julius Caesar, and fragments of columns and a small museum on the site of the old basilica preserve the memory. Centuries later, in the late eighteenth century, the town became known for its fine porcelain, delicate white pieces painted with flowers. Today it is home to the Paléo Festival, one of Europe's largest open-air music events, which takes over the fields on the edge of town for a week each July.

    For a rider, Nyon is a convenient and pleasant place to rest. The town has fuel, shops, cafes, and restaurants, and the lakeside is perfect for a coffee or a long lunch. The lake road along the shore is busy and slow, with traffic lights, roundabouts, and speed cameras, and Swiss speed limits are enforced strictly, so keep your eyes open. The higher road through the vineyards is more rewarding, with gentle bends and wide views. Geneva is about twenty-five kilometres to the south-west, and the Jura is the obvious destination for the afternoon.

    Food and drink are part of the charm. The local white wine, made from the Chasselas grape, is light and crisp, and it goes well with perch fillets from the lake, a Swiss favourite. A walk along the promenade is a good way to loosen up after a long ride. The lake has a mild climate, and in spring and autumn the light is lovely. In winter the bise, a cold north-east wind, can blow strongly across the water, and the lake road can be icy in the mornings. Remember that motorway driving in Switzerland needs a vignette.

  4. La Dôle view area stop

    La Dôle is the high point of the Swiss Jura's southern end, a rounded summit of about 1,677 metres above Lake Geneva, topped with a radar dome and a communications mast that you can see from half of western Switzerland. From the ridge, the view is one of the great panoramas of the country: the whole of Lake Geneva, the city at its tip, the Savoy Alps and the long white line of the Mont Blanc massif, with the Bernese Alps on the horizon in clear weather. On a good day you can see for well over a hundred kilometres.

    The mountain is mostly reached by walking, from the Col de la Givrine or from the small resort of Saint-Cergue, a few kilometres away, so a motorcycle is usually left at the roadside while you climb a trail through pasture and forest. The summit is not far above, and the walk takes less than a couple of hours for most people. Along the way, you cross high meadows grazed by cattle, with limestone outcrops, gentians and in places the sound of cowbells. The ridge here marks the border with France, with the Jura running southwest into the French Haut-Jura.

    The road in is a fine one. From the lakeside town of Nyon, a twisting mountain road climbs through woodland and farmland to Saint-Cergue, with decent surface, medium-radius bends and gradual gradients, and views opening up as you rise. The air is cooler and fresher, and in autumn, the forests turn orange and gold. Weather changes rapidly: you may be riding in clear sun at the lake and find thick fog at the ridge, so choose your day carefully. Winter brings snow, and some of the minor roads are closed.

    For services, Saint-Cergue has cafes, a bakery and a few small hotels, and mountain restaurants in the area serve fondue, saucisson and tarte aux myrtilles. Fuel is available at larger towns, and on the French side, Les Rousses has stations and shops. Divonne-les-Bains, a spa town, is the nearest bigger place on the lower slopes. Bring a windproof layer and a bit of patience for hairpins behind slow cars, and make time to sit on the grass and watch the lake shimmer below you.

  5. Back to Col de la finish

    Coming back to the pass at the end of a ride is a different thing from setting out. The light has changed, the legs are looser, and you notice the small stuff you rushed past in the morning: the cowbells, the smell of cut grass and cold stone, the way beech trees lean over a bend as if they were reading the road. The Col de la Faucille sits at about 1,320 metres on the French side of the Jura, and its best trick is that it hides the lake until the last moment, then lays the whole of Lake Geneva out below you in one go.

    Heading down from the col toward Gex, the road drops in a run of broad bends with the Pays de Gex spread beneath like a tablecloth. In the late afternoon, the Alps are lit from the side, and on a clear evening Mont Blanc turns pink while the valley below goes blue. It is the best view of the day and a good reason to stop for a few minutes. Be careful of the gravel that collects on the outside of the hairpins, and of the cyclists who toil up the same road and then come back down faster than you would believe.

    The area has odd and interesting neighbours. A short way below, at the foot of the slope, is Ferney-Voltaire, where the writer Voltaire lived for the last two decades of his life, writing, farming and annoying kings from a safe distance across the border. Not much further, on the plain near Geneva, CERN's rings run under the fields, so the quiet pastures sit above one of the largest scientific machines on earth. Both facts feel absurd when a herd of cows is blocking the road in front of you.

    For the end of a loop, Gex is the sensible place to finish. It has fuel, bakeries, cafes and supermarkets, and a sense of ordinary life that is welcome after the mountains. If you still have energy, the climb back to the pass for a last look at sunset is worthwhile, though temperatures drop quickly after dark, and fog can roll in from the Swiss side in minutes. Take a layer, and give the cows right of way.
