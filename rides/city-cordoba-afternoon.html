Why this ride

A 320 km round trip from Córdoba - roughly 5 h 31 in the saddle. It goes by way of Estancia of Alta Gracia, Estancia of Caroya, Estancia of Jesús María and back through Estancia of Caroya. The highest point is about 1,050 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cordoba

    Córdoba is Argentina's second city by size and first by claims to learning, a university town where the baroque churches of the Jesuits stand beside student cafés and traffic that never seems to stop. The Jesuits arrived in the sixteenth century and built what is now called the Jesuit Block, a group of church, college and residences that UNESCO listed in 2000. Their university, founded in 1613, is one of the oldest in South America, and in 1918 students here launched a reform movement that spread through Latin American universities. The city is also where Argentines drink fernet with cola, an acquired taste that locals will defend with some force.

  2. 2. Estancia of Alta Gracia

    Alta Gracia sits where the Córdoba plains begin to buckle into the Sierras Chicas, and its Jesuit estancia is the reason the town exists. The Jesuits established the estate in the seventeenth century, and by the eighteenth it was a thriving enterprise: a church, a residence around a cloister, workshops, and an ingenious dam with a reservoir feeding irrigation canals. It was part of the great network of Jesuit properties around Córdoba, collectively a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2000, which funded the university and college in the city.

  3. 3. Estancia of Caroya

    The Jesuits ran their farms like small empires. In the seventeenth century the Society of Jesus built a chain of estancias around the city of Cordoba, in the centre of Argentina, which supported the university and the college in town with grain, cattle, wine and mules, and these, together with the Jesuit Block in Cordoba itself, were inscribed on the World Heritage list in 2000. The Estancia of Caroya, founded in 1616, was the first of them, and its low, thick-walled buildings, with their long arcaded gallery, courtyard and a chapel, look out over a plain bordered by the hills of the Sierras Chicas.

  4. 4. Estancia of Jesús María

    In the hills north of Cordoba, in Argentina, there is a row of old Jesuit estancias, the ranches that paid for the order's missions and schools in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. The one at Jesus Maria dates from 1618, and with the Jesuit Block in Cordoba city and four other estancias, it forms part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site inscribed in 2000. The Jesuits ran these properties as farms, workshops and vineyards, with indigenous and African labour, and they prospered until King Carlos III expelled the order from Spanish territory in 1767.
