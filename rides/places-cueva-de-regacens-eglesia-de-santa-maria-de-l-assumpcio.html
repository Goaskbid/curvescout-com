Why this ride

A 434 km round trip from Alquézar - roughly 7 h 22 in the saddle. On the way it passes Cueva de Revilla, Eglésia de Santa Maria de l'Assumpció and back through Cueva de Revilla. It tops out around 1,850 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Pyrenees N260 Route

Cueva de Revilla is 106 km from Cueva de Regacens, mostly on A-138. Up about 780 m to 1,087 m, then 320 m back down the far side. Top up fuel at the bottom - pumps thin out on the way up.

The stops

  1. 1. Cueva de Regacens

    Start here.

    Alquézar takes its name from the Arabic for the fortress, al-qasr, and the name fits: a hill of honey-coloured rock above the Vero, crowned by a castle and a church, with a tangle of arcaded lanes tumbling down below. The Cueva de Regacens lies about eight hundred metres from the village, a shelter in the limestone that carries painting in the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain. It is one of the closest sites to a village of any on these rides, which makes it an easy and pleasant walk, though the paintings are small and faded.

    Read the complete Cueva de Regacens write-up.

  2. 2. Cueva de Revilla

    106 km in.

    Revilla is one of the most spectacularly placed hamlets in the Spanish Pyrenees, perched on a ledge above the Garganta de Escuaín, a deep gorge cut into the limestone of the Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park. It looks down into the canyon from a terrace of meadows, with the high peaks behind. The Cueva de Revilla is a cave in the cliffs near the hamlet, and the area between Bielsa, Plan and Boltaña is some of the wildest country in Aragón.

    Read the complete Cueva de Revilla write-up.

  3. 3. Eglésia de Santa Maria de l'Assumpció

    219 km in.

    Valleys in the high Pyrenees of Catalonia and Aragon are full of small Romanesque churches, and the area around the Alta Ribagorça is among the richest. They were built in the eleventh and twelfth centuries by craftsmen working in what is called the Lombard style, with plain stone walls, round arches and tall, slender bell towers decorated with blind arcades, and they stand in villages that often have only a few houses.
