  1. 1. Photo stop near Sperenberg

    The Soviets called it the Forbidden City. At Wünsdorf, east of Sperenberg, the high command of the Soviet forces in Germany had its headquarters until 1994, a vast garrison town hidden in the forest, home to tens of thousands of soldiers and their families, with schools, shops and theatres, closed to ordinary Germans. Before that, the German army had its own headquarters here, with huge bunkers built in the 1930s. Guided tours of the bunkers and the old garrison offer a fascinating glimpse into the military history of the twentieth century.

  2. 2. Near Glienicke

    Until 1989 the Berlin Wall ran right past here. Glienicke/Nordbahn, just across the boundary in Brandenburg, was home to the Entenschnabel, the duck's bill, a narrow strip of East German territory that poked into West Berlin, with houses whose gardens backed onto the Wall. Today the Mauerweg, a walking and cycling path, follows the old border line, and a few traces remain.

  3. 3. Back to Lehnin Abbey

    Werneuchen is not on anyone's list of famous places, and that is part of its charm. It sits on the Barnim plateau northeast of Berlin, a town of a little under eight thousand people surrounded by wide, open farmland, big skies and the thin line of a tree-lined road that always seems to run straight to the horizon. The ride that ends here has spent the day around Lehnin Abbey on the other side of the capital, so this is the long loop home, a different Brandenburg from the pine country in the west.
