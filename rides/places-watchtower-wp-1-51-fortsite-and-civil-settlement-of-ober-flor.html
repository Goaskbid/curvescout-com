Why this ride

From Bendorf it runs 324 km to Bendorf; allow about 6 h 09 of riding. It goes by way of Watchtowers WP 2/14 to 2/16, Watchtower WP 3/1 to 3/14 including the ci, Fort site and civil settlement of Saalburg and Fortsite and civil settlement of Ober-Flor. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Watchtower WP 1/51

    Start here.

    The Upper Germanic Limes does not start with a bang. Near Bendorf, on the slopes where the Westerwald drops toward the Rhine at the Neuwied basin, it is a matter of reading the woods: a low bank here, a shallow ditch there, and the spot where a Roman watchtower once stood, numbered by archaeologists as WP 1/51. The towers were placed within sight of each other, timber at first and later stone, so that a signal could pass along the frontier faster than any rider. Standing here among the beech trees you are close to the northern end of a border that ran more than five hundred kilometres to the Danube.

    Read the complete Watchtower WP 1/51 write-up.

  2. 2. Watchtowers WP 2/14 to 2/16

    38 km in.

    Imagine standing in a small timber or stone tower on a cold winter night, looking out over the dark forest for signs of movement, with a fire ready to signal to the next tower along the line. That was the life of the soldiers who manned the watchtowers of the Roman frontier, and the remains of three of them, numbered 2/14 to 2/16 by archaeologists, lie in the woods between Singhofen and Miehlen, on the plateau above the Rhine and the Lahn. Their foundations survive as low mounds and traces in the ground, and the line of the frontier, the ditch and bank, can be followed between them. They are part of the Upper German-Raetian Limes, one of the components of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire World Heritage Site.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 2/14 to 2/16 write-up.

  3. 3. Watchtower WP 3/1 to 3/14 including the ci

    78 km in.

    The Taunus is the green upland that keeps Frankfurt's weekend riders sane, and running right across it is the line of the Roman frontier. The stretch of watchtowers numbered 3/1 to 3/14 lies near Taunusstein, north of Wiesbaden, where the Upper Germanic Limes crossed the ridge, and it includes the remains of forts and their civilian settlements. One of the best known nearby is the fort of Zugmantel, near the hamlet of Orlen, where the outline of the fort, the palisade line and reconstructed watchtowers give a real sense of the frontier. The whole line is part of the UNESCO Frontiers of the Roman Empire.

    Read the complete Watchtower WP 3/1 to 3/14 including the ci write-up.

  4. 4. Fort site and civil settlement of Saalburg

    126 km in.

    If you want to know what a Roman frontier fort actually looked like, this is the place. The Saalburg, on the ridge of the Taunus above Bad Homburg, is the only Limes fort in Germany rebuilt in full: walls, gates, towers, granary and the headquarters building, all standing again on their original foundations. The reconstruction was ordered by Kaiser Wilhelm II around 1900, who liked his Romans grand and imperial, and while archaeologists today debate some details, walking through the main gate is still a jolt.

    Read the complete Fort site and civil settlement of Saalburg write-up.

  5. 5. Fortsite and civil settlement of Ober-Flor

    162 km in.

    The Wetterau is a broad, fertile basin north of Frankfurt, a land of rich loess soils, wheat fields, orchards and old villages, and the Romans wanted it badly. Their frontier, the Upper Germanic-Raetian Limes, bulged out here to take in the whole basin, looping around it in a wide arc. At Ober-Florstadt stood a fort with a civilian settlement beside it, where soldiers' families, traders and craftsmen lived. Today little is visible, but the site is part of the World Heritage frontier, and information boards help to picture the scene.

    Read the complete Fortsite and civil settlement of Ober-Flor write-up.
