Why this ride

A 117 km round trip from Tangxiang - roughly 2 h 01 in the saddle. On the way it passes Shimenshan Cliffside Carvings, Shizhuanshan Cliffside Carvings and back through Shimenshan Cliffside Carvings. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Dazu Rock Carvings

    Dazu is the place where a medieval Buddhist vision was carved on a human scale, and the surprise is how alive it still feels. Across the hills around the town in Chongqing, more than seventy sites hold carvings from the ninth to the thirteenth centuries, made in the late Tang and Song dynasties, when a local community of monks, officials and lay believers turned the soft red sandstone into a theatre of faith. UNESCO recognised the group in 1999. Visitors usually see two main sites, Beishan and Baodingshan, and they make a pair.

  2. 2. Shimenshan Cliffside Carvings

    Shimenshan is one of the clusters of rock carvings around Dazu, in Chongqing, where sandstone cliffs were turned into sacred art between the ninth and thirteenth centuries. The Dazu Rock Carvings, a UNESCO listing since 1999, include several sites: Beishan with its small niches, Baodingshan with its sweeping Buddhist tableaux, and Shimenshan, Nanshan and Shizhuanshan, each with its own character. At Shimenshan the carvings, made in the Song dynasty, blend Buddhist, Daoist and Confucian figures, something rare at a single site.

  3. 3. Shizhuanshan Cliffside Carvings

    Dazu is a quiet, green corner of the Chongqing hills, a long way from the skyscraper image the municipality usually gets. The Shizhuanshan carvings sit in the countryside south-west of the old town, cut into a sandstone cliff beneath trees, and they belong to the same group of Song-dynasty sites that earned Dazu a place on the World Heritage list in 1999. They are smaller and more intimate than the better-known Baodingshan, and many visitors say they are the more peaceful of the two.
