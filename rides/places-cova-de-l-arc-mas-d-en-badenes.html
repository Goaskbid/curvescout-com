Why this ride

From Albocàsser it runs 69 km to Albocàsser; allow about 1 h 11 of riding. The stops in between: Barranc del Puig- Abric I, mas d'en Badenes and back through Barranc del Puig- Abric I. The highest point is about 1,250 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cova de l'Arc

    Albocàsser, Catí and Les Coves de Vinromà share a landscape of limestone ridges and wide, dry valleys, in the north of Castellón, where the first foothills of the Maestrat meet the plain. Cova de l'Arc, six kilometres from Albocàsser, is one of the shelters of this country, and its name, the cave of the arch, suggests the shape of the rock. The district is known to archaeologists for its painted shelters, which form part of the Mediterranean Basin rock art that UNESCO listed in 1998.

  2. 2. Barranc del Puig- Abric I

    The Maestrat is the high, rugged interior of the province of Castellón, a land of limestone plateaus, deep ravines and stone villages that seem to grow out of the rock. Near Benassal, the Barranc del Puig cuts into the hills, and its rock shelters contain prehistoric paintings, part of the UNESCO serial site of the rock art of the Mediterranean Basin. This area is one of the richest in Spain for Levantine rock art, and the nearby Valltorta gorge, between Albocàsser and Tírig, has some of the most famous paintings, including scenes of hunters chasing deer and wild goats. The Valltorta museum in Tírig explains the art and organises visits to the shelters.
