  1. 1. Cairns

    Cairns sits in the tropical far north of Queensland, with the Coral Sea on one side and a wall of rainforest mountains on the other, and the whole town is organised around the idea of being a launch pad. Boats leave daily for the Great Barrier Reef, small planes head for Cape York, and the road north runs up to the Daintree, where the oldest rainforest on earth meets the beach. The town was founded in the 1870s as a port for the goldfields at Hodgkinson River and later became a sugar and tourism centre. It is not especially pretty in the old-town sense, but the Esplanade, with its saltwater lagoon, mudflats full of waders and cheerful backpackers, has a relaxed tropical air.

  2. 2. Ravenshoe

    Ravenshoe is the highest town in Queensland, at about nine hundred and thirty metres, and you feel it the moment you climb out of the tropical coast: the air thins and cools, the cane fields give way to cattle pasture and tall eucalypt forest, and in winter there is a morning chill that surprises people who have come from Cairns in shorts. The town sits on the volcanic soils of the Atherton Tableland, red and rich, with remnants of rainforest on the edges. It began as a timber settlement, and was named after the novel Ravenshoe by Henry Kingsley, which a local timber getter was fond of, according to the story.

  3. 3. Atherton

    Atherton sits at about 760 metres on the Tableland behind Cairns, and the first thing you notice after the climb from the coast is the air. It is cooler, drier and smells of cut grass and woodsmoke instead of mangrove mud. The Tableland is a high plateau of old volcanic soil, rich enough that the area grows maize, peanuts, potatoes, bananas, coffee and tea, and the countryside looks more like a patch of rolling green dairy land than the tropical north. Cattle graze under big, spreading trees, and crater lakes fill the hollows left by ancient eruptions.
