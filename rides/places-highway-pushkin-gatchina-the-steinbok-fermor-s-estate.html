Why this ride

A 213 km round trip from Kommunar - roughly 3 h 36 in the saddle. On the way it passes Tallinskoe Highway, The English Park, The Steinbok-Fermor's Estate and back through The English Park. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

Tallinskoe Highway to The English Park: 22 km on Санкт-Петербургское шоссе. An easy profile, never above 27 m. Best in the early light, before the traffic.

The stops

  1. 1. Highway Pushkin - Gatchina

    Start here.

    Two imperial palaces lie at either end of this road, and between them the flat country south of Saint Petersburg still carries the scars of the twentieth century's worst siege. At the northern end is Pushkin, the former Tsarskoye Selo, where the Catherine Palace stretches its blue and white façade for hundreds of metres and the reconstructed Amber Room glows inside. At the southern end is Gatchina, Paul I's palace, grey and fortress-like, in a park of lakes and islands. The historic road between them is one of the many components of the World Heritage Site of the historic centre of Saint Petersburg and its related monuments.

    Read the complete Highway Pushkin - Gatchina write-up.

  2. 2. Tallinskoe Highway

    29 km in.

    Not every historic road is a pretty one, and the Tallinn highway on the south-western edge of St Petersburg is not trying to win prizes for charm. It is, however, one of the old imperial routes out of the capital, the road toward Narva and Reval, today's Tallinn, and as such it forms part of the UNESCO listing of the historic centre of St Petersburg and its related monuments, which includes the network of roads tying the city to its palaces and estates. Along it, the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries planted suburban residences, military camps and post stations.

    Read the complete Tallinskoe Highway write-up.

  3. 3. The English Park

    50 km in.

    Everybody who comes to Peterhof heads for the fountains, the Grand Cascade and the gilded Samson tearing open the lion's jaws, and almost nobody walks the few hundred metres west into the English Park. That is their loss. Catherine the Great, who found Peter the Great's straight avenues and clipped hedges old-fashioned, had this park laid out from 1779 by the English gardener James Meader, in the natural landscape style then sweeping Europe: a chain of ponds, meadows, clumps of oak and lime, and paths that wander rather than march. It belongs to the World Heritage Site covering the historic centre of Saint Petersburg and its ring of palaces and estates.

    Read the complete The English Park write-up.

  4. 4. The Steinbok-Fermor's Estate

    105 km in.

    On the north shore of the Neva Bay, at the edge of the modern city, lies Lakhta, a district that was a village of Finnish and Ingrian farmers when Peter the Great first built his capital. By the nineteenth century the waterfront was dotted with country estates for the St Petersburg aristocracy, and the Steinbock-Fermor family held one of them, a park of old trees and lawns with a manor house by the Gulf of Finland. The estate park, shaded and quiet, is the kind of place where the city's dacha culture began.

    Read the complete The Steinbok-Fermor's Estate write-up.
