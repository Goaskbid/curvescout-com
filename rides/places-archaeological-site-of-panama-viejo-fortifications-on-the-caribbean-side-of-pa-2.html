Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Archaeological Site of Panamá Viejo

    A pirate raid ended one city and started another here. Panamá Viejo, the ruins on the shore east of the modern centre, was founded in 1519 by Pedrarias Dávila as the first Spanish settlement on the Pacific coast of the Americas, and for over 150 years it was the staging post where Peruvian silver was loaded for the crossing to the Atlantic. In 1671 the privateer Henry Morgan crossed the isthmus with a force of buccaneers, took the town and left it burning. The Spanish never rebuilt it on the same site.

  2. 2. Near Colón

    West of the Panama Canal's Caribbean end, the land goes quiet surprisingly fast. You cross from the Colón side on the Atlantic Bridge, the long cable-stayed span that finally replaced the old swing bridge and the ferry over the canal entrance, and within minutes the container cranes give way to rainforest, old American base buildings and a narrow road heading north toward the mouth of the Chagres River.

  3. 3. Fortifications on the Caribbean Side of Pa

    Columbus named it Puerto Bello, the beautiful harbour, in 1502, and for once a sailor's first impression was right. Portobelo sits on a deep, sheltered bay on Panama's Caribbean coast, backed by steep green hills, and for two centuries it was the point where the wealth of Peru left the New World. Gold and silver came by sea up the Pacific coast to Panama City, crossed the isthmus on mule trails like the Camino de Cruces, and were loaded here onto the Spanish treasure fleets. Every year, a great fair was held, with merchants and soldiers crowding the little port, and prices and diseases both soared.
