Why this ride

Norwegian Scenic Route. highest mountain road, glaciers, plateau. 85 km from Fossbergom to Gaupne, about 1 h 33 of riding. The highest point is about 1,450 m. Sognefjellshytta is open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

  2. 2. Norwegian Scenic Route Sognefjellet point 2

    The top of the Sognefjellet has the air of another planet, and I mean the cold, bare, magnificent kind. At about 1,430 metres it is the highest mountain road in northern Europe, and here the vegetation has given up entirely. What is left is rock, lichen, patches of old snow even in summer, and small black lakes that reflect the sky like pieces of slate. The mountains of Jotunheimen ring the horizon, and some of them wear glaciers that glint in the sun.

  3. 3. Norwegian Scenic Route Sognefjellet point 3

    The descent from the high plateau into the western valleys is the part of the Sognefjellet that riders remember longest, because the landscape changes so fast. One moment you are looking across bare rock and snowfields, and a few kilometres later you are among birches, alder and the first farms, with a roaring river beside the tarmac and waterfalls leaping from the cliffs. This is the upper end of the Fortundalen side, in the mountains above Luster.

  4. 4. Gaupne

    Gaupne sits at the head of the Lustrafjord, which is the innermost arm of the Sognefjord, and it is a small village in a very large landscape. The fjord here is narrow and deep, with steep mountainsides rising straight from the water and farms perched on the shelves above, and from the village you can see glimpses of the Jostedalsbreen glacier plateau to the north. Gaupne has just over a thousand people and the municipal centre of Luster, with shops, a petrol station and a few places to eat, and Sogndal, about twenty kilometres away, is the larger town if you need more.
