Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kamanjab

    Kamanjab is a farm-service town on the edge of Damaraland, a place of dust, cattle trucks and big skies that serves as the practical heart of north-western Namibia. It sits at a junction where the tarred road from Outjo and Etosha meets the gravel routes heading west into the empty country of Kunene, and for travellers it is the last place to buy proper supplies for a long way. The surroundings are a mix of commercial cattle farms and scrub-covered hills, dry for most of the year and briefly green after rain.

  2. 2. Okaukuejo

    Okaukuejo is the oldest of the rest camps in Etosha National Park, and its waterhole is the best reason to go there. The camp lies on the southern edge of the great Etosha Pan, a vast salt flat that glitters white in the dry season and holds shallow water in the wet, and the name of the park means great white place. A fort, built in 1901 by German troops, now serves as a museum and tower, and from the top, you can look across the plains and the pan, a view that is simply huge.
