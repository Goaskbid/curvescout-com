Why this ride

140 km from Shimla to Shimla, about 2 h 25 of riding. On the way it passes Kalka Shimla Railway, Complexe du Capitole and back through Kalka Shimla Railway. The highest point is about 1,900 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Shimla

    Shimla is where the British came to escape the heat of the plains, and it still has the look of a Surrey town that took a wrong turn into the Himalaya. Built along a ridge at roughly twenty-two hundred metres, with pine and cedar trees and rows of red-roofed houses, it served as the summer capital of British India, and decisions affecting hundreds of millions of people were made here between May and October. The Ridge, an open promenade in the centre, is lined with Christ Church, a pale yellow neo-Gothic building of the 1850s, and the Mall, a long shopping street, runs alongside. Vehicles are restricted on the Mall and the Ridge, so you will park below and walk up.

  2. 2. Kalka Shimla Railway

    The Kalka–Shimla Railway is a two-foot-six-inch narrow gauge line that climbs about ninety-six kilometres from the plains at Kalka to Shimla, the old summer capital of British India. It opened in 1903, was built to carry officials and their luggage up to the hills away from the heat, and it does the job by means of an extraordinary number of tunnels, around a hundred, plus hundreds of bridges and arched viaducts. The longest tunnel, at Barog, is more than a kilometre long, and the story of the engineer who miscalculated the alignment and was fined a rupee is a local legend. UNESCO added the line to the World Heritage List in 2008.
