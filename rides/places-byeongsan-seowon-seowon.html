Why this ride

From Andong it runs 187 km to Andong; allow about 3 h 13 of riding. It goes by way of Dosan-seowon, Bongjeongsa Temple, Seowon and back through Bongjeongsa Temple.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Byeongsan-seowon

    Byeongsan Seowon sits above a bend of the Nakdong river, a few kilometres from the village of Hahoe and about fifteen kilometres from Andong, in the rolling, rural interior of North Gyeongsang province. The river curves around a cliff face called Buyongdae, and the academy looks across the water to the opposite cliff in a way that is almost a painting. Andong, a city of about a hundred and thirty thousand people, is the nearest place of any size.

  2. 2. Dosan-seowon

    If you have handled a Korean thousand-won note, you have already seen Dosan Seowon. The building on the back of the note is this Confucian academy, and the scholar on the front is Yi Hwang, known by his pen name Toegye, one of the most influential Neo-Confucian thinkers of Korea. He set up a small study hall here in the sixteenth century, on a quiet slope above the Nakdong river, and after his death in 1570 his disciples expanded it into a full academy, a seowon, where students came to study the classics and honour his memory. It is one of the nine Korean Confucian academies listed by UNESCO in 2019.

  3. 3. Bongjeongsa Temple

    Tucked into the wooded slopes of Cheondeungsan, a modest mountain north-west of Andong in North Gyeongsang province, Bongjeongsa is a temple that wears its age quietly. Its main claim to fame is a small, plain hall called Geungnakjeon, believed to be the oldest surviving wooden building in Korea, dating back to the Goryeo dynasty. Repairs in the twentieth century turned up records suggesting the hall had been rebuilt in the thirteenth century, which makes it older than almost anything else made of timber in the country. Bongjeongsa is one of seven mountain monasteries inscribed by UNESCO in 2018 as Sansa, Buddhist Mountain Monasteries in Korea.

  4. 4. Seowon

    Dosan Seowon is a school, and it is the school of a man who is still on the thousand-won note. Yi Hwang, better known as Toegye, was the greatest Neo-Confucian philosopher of Korea's Joseon dynasty, and after he died in 1570 his students built this academy beside the Nakdong river where he used to teach. It opened a few years afterwards and became the model for hundreds of others. The site was listed as one of nine Neo-Confucian academies of Korea on the World Heritage list in 2019.
