Why this ride

curated scenic road. Lake Kivu, volcanoes, tea hills, forest. A one-way ride of 239 km, Kigali to Cyangugu - roughly 4 h 21 in the saddle. On the way it passes Musanze, Gisenyi, Kibuye and Rwanda Congo Nile. The highest point is about 2,300 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kigali

    Rwanda calls itself the land of a thousand hills, and Kigali is draped over a good number of them, at roughly fifteen hundred metres above the sea. The city climbs and drops in short steep pitches, with red-earth lanes running off the tarmac and views across to the next ridge every time you crest a rise. It is clean, notably so, with a monthly community clean-up day and a ban on plastic bags that visitors notice at the airport. The roads are well surfaced, the lane discipline is better than in most capitals in the region, and the air at this altitude is cooler than the equator suggests.

  2. 2. Musanze

    Musanze is the town you go through to reach the gorillas. It lies at the foot of the Virunga volcanoes in the north-west of Rwanda, a high, cool town of tidy streets and bustling markets, with the cones of Bisoke, Karisimbi and Sabyinyo rising behind it like props from a film. The mountains are shared with Uganda and the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and the border lies not far off. The population is large, more than eighty thousand, so Musanze has fuel stations, banks, supermarkets and a whole industry of guides and drivers.

  3. 3. Gisenyi

    Two countries share one shoreline here, and the border runs between the beach bars. Gisenyi, now officially Rubavu, is Rwanda's lakeside resort on the northern shore of Lake Kivu, and the Congolese city of Goma starts a few kilometres across the line, under the dark cone of Nyiragongo. The volcano is the dominant fact of the landscape: its 2002 eruption sent lava through Goma, and its crater holds one of the world's largest lava lakes, glowing red at night when the sky is clear. Cross-border crowds, market women with baskets on their heads and boda riders weaving between them give the town a restless energy.

  4. 4. Kibuye

    Kibuye sits on the shore of Lake Kivu in western Rwanda, and the setting is the best argument for coming. The lake is long, deep and hemmed in by steep green hills, with a coast of small bays and peninsulas and islands covered in forest. On a calm morning the water is a sheet of pewter and fishermen paddle out in wooden boats lashed together in threes, their nets draped across long poles. The town, called Karongi in official use, is a quiet place with a small harbour, a few hotels and a handful of lakeside cafes.
