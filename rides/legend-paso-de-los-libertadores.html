Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mendoza

    Mendoza is a city of trees. Walk or ride any of its wide avenues and you will pass under rows of old plane trees, fed by narrow irrigation channels called acequias, a system that the indigenous Huarpe people started long before the Spanish arrived. The reason is the desert: Mendoza gets very little rain, and every green leaf here exists because snowmelt from the Andes is carried down in channels and spread across the plain. It is a city of nearly nine hundred thousand people in the greater area, set against a wall of mountains that, on a clear winter morning, includes the white shoulder of Aconcagua far to the west.

  2. 2. Paso de los Libertadores point 2

    West of Mendoza the plain is left behind and the road starts to climb into dry foothills along the Mendoza River, passing reservoirs, dams and the old spa settlement of Cacheuta, famous for its thermal springs. Here the landscape turns to rock: striped red, ochre and grey hills, with cactus and thorn scrub. Potrerillos is a popular weekend lake for the Mendocinos, with a reservoir used for sailing and rafting, and the road follows its shore with easy, fast curves.

  3. 3. Paso de los Libertadores point 3

    Uspallata is a dusty oasis in a high valley, about 1,900 metres up, and it feels like a place on the way to somewhere else, which it is. A line of poplars marks the irrigated strip, the mountains stand bare and coloured on every side, and a handful of small shops, cafes and fuel stations serve the trucks and travellers heading to or from Chile. If you have not filled the tank since Mendoza, here is the place, because the road ahead climbs high and there is nothing to speak of until the border area.

  4. 4. Paso de los Libertadores point 4

    Puente del Inca is a natural stone bridge across the Las Cuevas River, and it is one of the strangest things on the road. Mineral springs have stained the arch a rusty orange and yellow, and the ruins of an old spa hotel, swept away partly by an avalanche decades ago, cling to the bank below. Hot sulphurous water still seeps out of the rock, and the whole scene looks like a painting that has been left out in the rain. Local stalls sell crystals, souvenirs and snacks beside the bridge.

  5. 5. Paso de los Libertadores point 5

    At the head of the valley the road gives up on the open air and goes into the mountain. The Cristo Redentor tunnel runs under the pass, about three thousand two hundred metres up, and marks the boundary between Argentina and Chile. Just beside it, the old road goes over the top, a narrow, steep gravel route that was the original crossing, famous for the statue of Christ the Redeemer placed on the frontier in 1904 as a sign of peace between the two countries after a border dispute.

  6. 6. Los Andes

    Los Andes is the last Chilean town before the mountains, a lively market town of nearly sixty thousand people at 820 metres in the green valley of the Aconcagua River, and from here the road climbs steeply towards the Paso de los Libertadores and Argentina. The town is surrounded by orchards and vineyards, with peaches, grapes and avocados growing in the warm, sunny valley, and the snowy peaks of the Andes rise sharply to the east.
