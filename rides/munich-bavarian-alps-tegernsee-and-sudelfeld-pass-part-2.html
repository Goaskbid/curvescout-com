  1. 1. Photo stop in Peißenberg

    Peißenberg owes its existence to coal. For more than a century a deep mine worked the seams of pitch coal under the town, and when it closed in 1971 the place had to reinvent itself. You can still see traces of the mining past in the old shaft buildings and a small museum, but the modern town is a pleasant, unpretentious market centre in the rolling foothills between the Ammersee and the Alps.

  3. 3. High point near Miesbach

    In 1882, a wire strung on poles between Miesbach and Munich carried electricity over a distance of fifty-seven kilometres, one of the first long-distance power transmissions in history, a demonstration organised by the engineer Oskar von Miller and the French physicist Marcel Deprez. It powered a small pump driving an artificial waterfall at an exhibition in Munich. The achievement helped launch the age of electricity, and Oskar von Miller went on to found the Deutsches Museum.

  4. 4. Scenic stretch in Bruckmühl

    The Mangfall flows out of the Tegernsee and winds north through a deep, wooded valley before turning east towards Rosenheim and the Inn, and Bruckmühl sits on its banks, a modest industrial town with a church, a few factories and modern homes. The valley's springs supply a large part of Munich's drinking water, which is famously pure, and the forests along the river are protected to keep it that way.

  5. 5. Back to Tegernsee and

    East of Munich the Alps vanish from view, and the landscape becomes one of gravel terraces, fields of wheat and rapeseed, and little towns clustered around onion-domed churches. The dot sits in the Erding district, near Buch am Buchrain and Pastetten, where the main business is farming and where the roads are straight and the villages have a tidy, prosperous look. It is the tail end of a long day that took in the Chiemgau and the Tegernsee valley.
