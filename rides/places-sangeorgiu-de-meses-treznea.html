Why this ride

From Cizer it runs 104 km to Cizer; allow about 1 h 48 of riding. On the way it passes Fildu de Sus - Grebăn, Treznea and back through Fildu de Sus - Grebăn. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sângeorgiu de Meseș

    Saint George is the patron of this village, and on 23 April, his feast day, Sângeorgiu de Meseș comes to life. Romanians call the day Sângeorz or Sfântul Gheorghe, and in rural Transylvania it has long marked the start of the pastoral year, the day when sheep are taken to the hills and the shepherds make their contracts for the season. Green branches are hung on gates, fires are lit, and people make sure to eat lamb. The village sits in the western Meseș, about six kilometres from Cizer, a village of some two and a half thousand.

  2. 2. Fildu de Sus - Grebăn

    Fildu de Sus is strung along a valley in the hills between Ciucea and the Crasna country, where the Apuseni mountains soften into rounded, grassy ridges, and Greban, the hill in the label, is one of those ridges. The valley is small and green, with a stream, a line of houses, and orchards climbing the slopes, and it feels a long way from the main road that carries the traffic between Cluj and Oradea. Poieni and Ciucea are the nearest villages, and Negreni lies a short distance to the east.

  3. 3. Treznea

    The northern frontier of Roman Dacia ran through the hills of Sălaj county, near Zalău, and Treznea is one of the places where its watchtowers, ramparts and forts left traces. The Frontiers of the Roman Empire, Dacia, were listed as a World Heritage Site in 2024, covering hundreds of sites across Romania. Nearby, on a ridge above the valley, Porolissum was the largest Roman town and military complex on this frontier, with a reconstructed gate, an amphitheatre and walls, and it is one of the most impressive Roman sites in Romania.
