  1. 1. Almaty

    Almaty means place of apples, or near enough, and the apples are not just a name. Wild apple forests, the ancestors of many of the world's cultivated varieties, still grow on the slopes of the Tian Shan mountains above the city, and botanists believe the fruit began its long journey to the world's orchards somewhere in this region. For most of the twentieth century Almaty was the capital of Kazakhstan, until the government moved to Astana in 1997, and it remains the country's biggest and most relaxed city, with wide tree-lined avenues, Soviet blocks and a skyline of snow peaks to the south.

  2. 2. Taldykorgan

    Taldykorgan is the capital of the Zhetysu region, a calm, green city on the plain at the foot of the Dzungarian Alatau. Zhetysu means seven rivers, a name that comes from the streams running down from the mountains into the basin around Lake Balkhash, and it has long been a corridor for nomads and traders. The city has around a hundred and sixteen thousand people and an easy, orderly feel, with wide avenues, parks and clean public spaces. Behind it, the snow-capped range gleams on clear days, and the nearby foothills are a popular weekend escape.

  3. 3. Altyn Emel

    Altyn Emel is a national park of enormous scale in the Ili river basin, east of Almaty, and its name is often translated as golden saddle. The land is flat steppe and desert plain, rimmed by two ranges of hills: the Aktau, white mountains, and the Katutau, a banded landscape of reds, oranges, yellows and greens, laid down in layers and baked by the sun. At sunset these hills glow, and photographers arrive from across the country to catch them.
