Why this ride

The stops in between: Sangha Trinational, Parc National de Lobeké - Cameroon and back through Sangha Trinational. It crosses from Central African Republic into Cameroon - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Parc National de Dzanga-Ndoki - CAR

    In the extreme south-west corner of the Central African Republic, where the forest runs on into Cameroon and Congo, the Sangha river sweeps past a national park that protects one of the last great blocks of Congo Basin rainforest. Dzanga-Ndoki is part of the Sangha Trinational, a World Heritage area shared by three countries, and it is one of the few places on Earth where you can sit in the forest and watch elephants and gorillas in the open.

  2. 2. Sangha Trinational

    The Sangha is the river that gives this place its name, and the forest around it is divided among three countries: the Central African Republic, Cameroon and the Republic of Congo. In 2012 UNESCO recognised the three adjoining parks, Dzanga-Ndoki, Lobéké and Nouabalé-Ndoki, as a single World Heritage site, a block of lowland rainforest that is one of the best preserved in the Congo basin.
