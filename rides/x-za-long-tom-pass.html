Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Lydenburg

    The name Lydenburg means town of suffering, and there is real history behind it. In the 1840s, Voortrekker families settled at Ohrigstad, in the lowveld, but malaria drove them out, and in 1849 they moved up to higher, healthier ground and founded a new town, which they named in memory of what they had endured. Today Lydenburg sits at around 1,400 metres in the Mpumalanga highveld, a quiet market town of about twenty thousand people, with a wide main street, tree-lined avenues and a feel of farming country, where cattle and timber matter more than tourists.

  2. 2. Long Tom Pass

    Anyone who loves old guns has heard of Long Tom. In the Anglo-Boer War of 1900, the Boers dragged a few of these huge French-made siege cannon, with barrels long enough to throw shells across valleys, up and down the escarpment to slow the British advance, and the pass takes its name from them. A replica gun stands beside the road near the summit, pointing out over the hills, and it is worth a pause and a photograph before you carry on.

  3. 3. Sabie

    Sabie is a forestry town in the hills of Mpumalanga, where the Lowveld climbs into the Drakensberg escarpment and mist rolls through the pine plantations almost every morning. The surrounding area holds one of the largest man-made forests in the world, planted over the last century with pine and eucalyptus, and the sweet resin smell lingers in the air. Water pours off the escarpment everywhere, and the town is the base for a string of waterfalls: Mac Mac, Lone Creek, Bridal Veil and Horseshoe all lie within a short ride.
