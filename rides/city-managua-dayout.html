  1. 1. Managua

    Managua is not an obvious beauty, and Nicaraguans will tell you so before you do. It lies on the southern shore of Lake Xolotlán, with the cone of the Momotombo volcano rising across the water, and its centre was destroyed by an earthquake in December 1972 that killed thousands. The old downtown was never rebuilt, and today the city sprawls in low neighbourhoods, with open lots, new roundabouts and the old cathedral standing cracked and empty as a reminder.

  2. 2. Ruins of León Viejo

    Hernandez de Cordoba founded the first city of Leon in 1524 on the shore of Lake Managua, and what remains of it are the foundations of a Spanish colonial town sunk in a dry, hot plain under the cone of Momotombo. The volcano is the great feature of the landscape, a near-perfect cone that rises across the water and sometimes sends up a plume of steam. Its eruptions and a series of earthquakes made the site dangerous, and in 1610 the inhabitants abandoned it and rebuilt the city further west, where Leon stands now. Archaeologists excavated the old town from the 1960s, and the ruins of the plaza, the cathedral, the convents and the governor's house now lie in neat rows of low stone walls.

  4. 4. León Cathedral

    León is a hot, lively university town in the north-west of Nicaragua, and its cathedral is the most sensible reason to stop. The Basilica Cathedral of the Assumption, as it is formally known, is the largest cathedral in Central America, a vast white block that dominates the central square. Construction ran from the middle of the eighteenth century into the early nineteenth, a long project in a region where earthquakes and wars kept interfering, and the result mixes Baroque and neoclassical touches under a roof that glares in the sun.
