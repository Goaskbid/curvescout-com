Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Castro Chile

    Castro is the capital of Chiloe, a green, rainy island off the coast of southern Chile that feels like a world apart, with its own food, mythology and architecture. About thirty thousand people live here, in a town spread along a bay and across hills, with the colourful stilt houses called palafitos lining the water. Dalcahue, a smaller port on the island's east coast with a lively craft market, lies a short ride away.

  2. 2. Cucao

    Cucao is the place where the road across Chiloé runs out of island. The small village sits on the wild Pacific coast of the Isla Grande, about thirty kilometres from the towns of Chonchi and Castro, beside a long lake and at the gate of Chiloé National Park. Beyond the last houses, the land becomes a wilderness of dunes, wide grey beaches, temperate rainforest and boggy meadows, and the sea is powerful and cold. The weather arrives from the west unfiltered, so expect rain, wind and sudden gaps of brilliant light.

  3. 3. Dalcahue

    Dalcahue is a small port on the east coast of Chiloé, the big island off the coast of southern Chile, and its name means, in the Huilliche language, a place of dalcas, the plank boats that islanders once used. It sits on a channel facing the smaller island of Quinchao, and a short ferry crosses the water every few minutes, which gives the place its restless harbour life. On a grey morning the sea and sky blend in pewter, with gulls, fishing boats and the smell of salt and woodsmoke.
