Why this ride

A 68 km round trip from Annan - roughly 1 h 10 in the saddle. The stops in between: Beckfoot Roman fort, Maryport and back through Beckfoot Roman fort. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cardurnock

    From Cardurnock the whole Solway coast opens out, and on a clear day you can see Criffel and the Scottish shore, the Lake District fells to the south and the long line of the tide marking out the sand. The peninsula is a lonely place, flat and breezy, with a few farms, a lot of birds and the remains of an old railway viaduct at Bowness a few kilometres away. That structure, a long iron bridge across the firth, linked Cumberland with Dumfriesshire from 1869 until it closed in the 1920s and was taken down in the 1930s. It carried iron ore from Cumbria to the Scottish furnaces and, according to the stories, thirsty Scots across to Bowness on the days when their pubs were closed.

  2. 2. Beckfoot Roman fort

    Most people think Hadrian's Wall ended at Bowness-on-Solway, and in stone it did. But the Romans did not trust the shallow Solway Firth to keep raiders out, so they carried their defences on down the Cumbrian coast for many miles: a chain of small milefortlets and watchtowers, with proper forts at intervals, all the way to Maryport and beyond. Beckfoot is one of those forts, on a low bluff a few kilometres south of Silloth, looking straight across the water to Scotland. Above ground there is almost nothing, just a field with faint outlines, but aerial photos and excavations have shown the fort, its civilian settlement and its cemetery, and coastal erosion keeps nibbling at the remains.

  3. 3. Maryport

    Maryport is a harbour town on the Cumbrian coast, a place of Georgian terraces, fishing boats and a long quay, with the Solway Firth and the hills of Galloway across the water. The town was laid out in the 1750s by Humphrey Senhouse, who named it for his wife Mary, and it grew on coal, shipbuilding and trade. Thomas Ismay, the founder of the White Star Line that later built the Titanic, was born here in 1837, and Fletcher Christian of the Bounty came from nearby Cockermouth. The harbour has been restored, with a maritime museum, a row of old boats, and a pleasant waterfront to walk.
