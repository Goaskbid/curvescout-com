Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cairns

    Cairns sits in the tropical far north of Queensland, with the Coral Sea on one side and a wall of rainforest mountains on the other, and the whole town is organised around the idea of being a launch pad. Boats leave daily for the Great Barrier Reef, small planes head for Cape York, and the road north runs up to the Daintree, where the oldest rainforest on earth meets the beach. The town was founded in the 1870s as a port for the goldfields at Hodgkinson River and later became a sugar and tourism centre. It is not especially pretty in the old-town sense, but the Esplanade, with its saltwater lagoon, mudflats full of waders and cheerful backpackers, has a relaxed tropical air.

  2. 2. Palm Cove

    Palm Cove is the quiet end of the northern beaches north of Cairns, a village of under four thousand people on a curve of sand with a long jetty and an avenue of old paperbark trees along the beachfront, their bark peeling in soft white layers. The paperbarks, or melaleucas, lean over the footpath and give shade to the cafes and restaurants beneath. It is a resort village, calm and a bit upmarket, but small enough that a rider on a road trip can wander in without feeling out of place.

  3. 3. Rex Lookout

    Rex Lookout is a pull-in on the Captain Cook Highway, high on the headland between Cairns and Port Douglas, where the road leaves the cane fields and clings to a steep, green coastline. From the platform you look straight down onto a succession of bays, long white beaches, scattered islands and a sea that goes from pale green to deep blue. On a clear morning you can see all the way along the coast to the north, with the rainforest hills of the Daintree fading into haze.

  4. 4. Port Douglas

    Port Douglas is the northern end of one of Australia's loveliest coast roads, and a town that has gone through at least three lives. It began in the 1870s as a port for the goldfields inland, was nearly abandoned when the railway went to Mossman instead, and then reinvented itself in the 1980s as a laid-back resort town. The population is a little over eight thousand, with a long main street of cafés and bars, a sheltered marina and Four Mile Beach, which is wide, pale and well patrolled by lifesavers. There is fuel, food and every kind of bed, and it is a very easy place to finish a ride.
