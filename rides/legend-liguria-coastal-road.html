Why this ride

curated scenic road. Mediterranean coast, villages, cliffs. A one-way ride of 87 km, La Spezia to Genoa - roughly 1 h 35 in the saddle. The stops in between: Portovenere, Levanto and Santa Margherita Ligure. The line favours views over speed.

Scenic road chapters

Liguria Coastal Road

Slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did. Water arrives beside the road and stays for 6 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. La Spezia

    La Spezia is a naval city at the head of a deep bay, and it is not trying to charm you. About eighty-eight thousand people live here, the Italian navy has a major base on the waterfront, and the port handles container ships and ferries. That makes it a practical place: big enough for fuel, garages, banks and a proper supermarket, and the starting point for the Cinque Terre, which lie just along the coast to the west. Many travellers pass through here without stopping, but the town has a pleasant seafront promenade and a market with excellent produce.

  2. 2. Portovenere

    Portovenere stands at the tip of a rocky peninsula at the southern end of the Ligurian coast, where the cliffs finish in a narrow point facing the island of Palmaria. It is a village of tall, narrow houses painted in faded yellow, pink and ochre, with a fortified waterfront that looks like a wall of colour from the sea. At the end of the point, on a rock above the waves, stands the black-and-white church of San Pietro, a Gothic building from the late twelfth century, built on the site of an earlier temple. The name itself means Port of Venus, and the old goddess seems to have been on good terms with the sailors.

  3. 3. Levanto

    Levanto is the quiet neighbour of the Cinque Terre, close enough to use as a base and far enough to avoid the worst of the crowds. It sits at the head of a small bay on the Ligurian coast, with a long, sandy beach, which is rare in this part of Italy, and a town of arcaded streets, a medieval loggia and a Gothic church striped in black and white stone. The hills behind are steep and green with olive groves, vines and chestnut woods, and the coast on either side is a series of rocky headlands. The town is relaxed, with a mix of residents and visitors, and in summer it has the easy atmosphere of a seaside resort without the glitz.

  5. 5. Genoa

    Genoa is a city built on a slope, with a port at the bottom and neighbourhoods stacked above it like seats in a theatre. It has been a great maritime power since the Middle Ages, and the Republic of Genoa ruled trade routes across the Mediterranean, financed kings and sent Christopher Columbus, who was born here, to his voyages. The old town is one of the largest medieval centres in Europe, a maze of narrow lanes called caruggi, where sunlight rarely reaches the street and every doorway seems to hold a shrine, a shop or a bakery. With over 580,000 people, the city is a proper metropolis, with everything from fuel to repair shops.
