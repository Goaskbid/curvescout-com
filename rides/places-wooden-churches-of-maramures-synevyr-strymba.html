Why this ride

From Bârsana it runs 525 km to Bârsana; allow about 11 h 52 of riding. It goes by way of Yasynia-Tserkva of Our Lord’s Ascension, Synevyr - Strymba and back through Yasynia-Tserkva of Our Lord’s Ascension. It tops out around 1,150 m. It crosses from Romania into Ukraine - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

The stops

  1. 1. Wooden Churches of Maramureş

    Start here.

    Maramureș is a region in the far north of Romania, along the Ukrainian border, where traditions have survived more strongly than almost anywhere else in Europe. Its villages are known for their carved wooden gates, their haystacks and their wooden churches, eight of which form a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Bârsana, in the valley of the Iza river, has one of them, a church built in the eighteenth century, with a tall slender spire and a steep shingled roof.

    Read the complete Wooden Churches of Maramureş write-up.

  2. 2. Yasynia-Tserkva of Our Lord’s Ascension

    92 km in.

    High in the Ukrainian Carpathians, in the valley of the Black Tisa, the village of Yasinia stretches along the river beneath steep spruce-clad slopes. On a rise near its centre stands a small wooden church, the Church of the Ascension, sometimes called the Strukivska church, built in the early nineteenth century by Hutsul craftsmen without nails. Its stepped roofs, covered in wooden shingles, and its small domes give it a cosy, fairytale shape, and it is part of the UNESCO listing of wooden tserkvas of the Carpathian region in Poland and Ukraine.

    Read the complete Yasynia-Tserkva of Our Lord’s Ascension write-up.

  3. 3. Synevyr - Strymba

    262 km in.

    In the Carpathian mountains of Transcarpathia, in western Ukraine, the forests around Synevyr include some of the last remaining primeval beech forests in Europe, part of the transnational World Heritage Site of the Ancient and Primeval Beech Forests of the Carpathians and Other Regions of Europe. Strymba is one of the protected forest areas, in the hills near the village of Kolochava, where beech trees grow old and die without human interference, creating a rich habitat for fungi, insects, birds and animals.
