Why this ride

A 408 km round trip from Culfa - roughly 7 h 02 in the saddle. The stops in between: The Monastery of Saint Thaddeus, Khoy and back through The Monastery of Saint Thaddeus. The highest point is about 2,350 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. The main church

    The Monastery of Saint Thaddeus is known locally as Qara Kelisa, the Black Church, and the name comes from the dark stone used in its lower walls, set against lighter stone above. It stands in the hills of north-western Iran, in West Azerbaijan province, in a green valley where the road runs between orchards and low ridges. Tradition says that the apostle Thaddeus founded a church here in the first century and was buried on the spot, and while no one can prove that, the site has been a place of Armenian pilgrimage for many centuries.

  2. 2. The Monastery of Saint Thaddeus

    On a bare hillside near the border between Iran and Turkey, close to the foot of Mount Ararat, stands a black-and-white church that has survived earthquakes, wars and neglect for most of a thousand years. The Monastery of Saint Thaddeus, known in Iran as Qara Kelisa, the Black Church, is one of the oldest and most revered Armenian churches in the world. It is built of alternating bands of black and white stone, in a way that gives it a striking, almost chequered appearance, and it stands in a high, windy valley of the north-west.

  3. 3. Khoy

    Khoy is the big, practical city of Iran's far north-west, a place of about a hundred and seventy-five thousand people on a wide fertile plain between the Turkish border and Lake Urmia. It is not a showpiece. It is a working town of bazaars, apricot and grape growers, trucking depots and solid apartment blocks, and that is exactly what makes it useful. This is where you buy supplies, change money, find a proper meal and get fuel before the road climbs toward the mountains.
