Why this ride

A 657 km round trip from Tashkent - roughly 14 h 46 in the saddle. It goes by way of The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve, Besh-Aral State Nature Reserve - main part and back through The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve. The stored terrain estimate reaches about 2,700 m; a measured road-elevation profile is not available. It crosses from Uzbekistan into Kyrgyzstan - carry ID and check the toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. State Museum of History

    Long before Tashkent became a Soviet showpiece, it was a stop on the caravan road, and the State Museum of History tries to tell that long story. Under the name Chach, the oasis was known to the ancient Persians and Greeks, and later to Chinese travellers, who called it Shi. It lay on the Silk Road, on the route that led from Samarkand across the Syr Darya into the Fergana valley and the Chinese lands beyond. Sogdian merchants carried silk, glass, metalwork and religious ideas along it, and the museum's collections include coins, ceramics, carved stone and remains from sites across Uzbekistan, from the Bronze Age to the Timurid empire.

  2. 2. The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve

    East of Tashkent, the land rises steeply into the western ranges of the Tian Shan, and the Chatkal range forms the border with Kyrgyzstan. The Chatkal State Biosphere Reserve, established in the Soviet era, protects a stretch of these mountains, with juniper forests, walnut and wild fruit trees, alpine meadows and rocky peaks.

  3. 3. Besh-Aral State Nature Reserve - main part

    Far up the Chatkal valley in western Kyrgyzstan, where the river runs between the Chatkal and Sandalash ranges of the Western Tien Shan, lies the Besh-Aral State Nature Reserve. It protects mountain steppe, juniper woodland, walnut and apple groves and rocky high country, and it is part of the transnational World Heritage Site of the Western Tien Shan, inscribed in 2016 and shared with Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan. The reserve was created in particular to protect Menzbier's marmot, a small marmot found only in this part of Central Asia.
