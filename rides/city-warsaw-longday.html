Why this ride

A 416 km round trip from Warsaw - roughly 7 h 10 in the saddle. On the way it passes Krzemionki Prehistoric Striped Flint Minin, Krzemionki Opatowskie Mining Field, Borownia Mining Field and Korycizna Mining Field. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Warsaw

    Warsaw is a city that was erased and then rebuilt, and the second part is what makes it extraordinary. In August 1944 the Polish Home Army rose against the German occupiers, and after two months of fighting the Germans, on Hitler's orders, systematically destroyed most of the city, leaving it a field of rubble. The Old Town was reconstructed after the war, brick by brick, using old paintings, notably those of the eighteenth-century artist Bernardo Bellotto, and documents saved by the city's architects. UNESCO listed the result in 1980 as a remarkable act of cultural will.

  2. 2. Krzemionki Prehistoric Striped Flint Minin

    Five thousand years ago, people in these low wooded hills in the Świętokrzyskie region were digging down into the ground for a stone that looked like nothing else. Striped flint, with its bands of grey, brown and cream, is a rarity, and it was prized for making polished axes that were traded across a large part of central Europe. The mines at Krzemionki, along with sites at Borownia, Korycizna and Wierzbica, make up one of the most extensive prehistoric mining landscapes known, and UNESCO listed the group in 2019.

  3. 3. Krzemionki Opatowskie Mining Field

    This is the place where you can actually go down. The Krzemionki Opatowskie mining field has an underground tourist route of about half a kilometre, through a network of old galleries, chambers and narrow passages cut by Neolithic miners and then protected for visitors. The temperature below the ground stays cool all year, so bring a sweater even in August, and the air has the damp, mineral smell of old stone. A guide leads you through, with a light that picks out the striped flint in the walls, and the pits where miners once worked by the light of fires.

  4. 4. Borownia Mining Field

    Borownia is the quiet cousin among the flint mining fields, and that is part of its charm. Where Krzemionki has a visitor centre and a guided underground route, Borownia is a forested landscape of low hills, in which the traces of Neolithic mining appear as a scatter of shallow hollows on the forest floor. It is one of the sites in the UNESCO listing of 2019, and its value lies in showing how widely the mining spread across the district, rather than in a single dramatic display.
