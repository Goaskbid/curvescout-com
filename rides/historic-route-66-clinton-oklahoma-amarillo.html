Historic Route 66: Clinton Oklahoma to Amarillo

Why this ride

From Clinton it runs 671 km to Amarillo; allow about 9 h 12 of riding. The highest point is about 1,100 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Clinton Oklahoma

    Clinton sits in western Oklahoma on the old road that Americans called the Mother Road, and the town has leaned into that fact. The Oklahoma Route 66 Museum is here, laid out decade by decade from the road's birth in 1926 through the Dust Bowl, the migration west that John Steinbeck wrote about, and the postwar boom of motels and neon. For someone about to ride the same alignment, it works as a good briefing before the miles start. The Washita River runs just to the south, and the land around is red soil, wheat, cattle and wind.

  2. 2. Shamrock Texas

    Shamrock, Texas, is a town named by an Irishman, and it has never been shy about the fact. A homesteader called George Nickel, so the story goes, gave it its name in 1890, and the town now holds an annual St Patrick's Day festival with a parade, a Blarney Stone and a great deal of green. It sits on the old Route 66, in the Texas Panhandle, a country of flat horizons, grain elevators, cattle and wind. The population is under two thousand, and the nearest other town of any size, Wheeler, is a good half-hour away.

  3. 3. Amarillo

    Amarillo is the city where the Texas Panhandle shows its hand: a flat, windy plain stretching to every horizon, with a skyline of grain elevators, water towers and big sky. At around 1,100 metres above sea level it is higher than you think, and the wind is a constant companion, often strong enough to lean a bike over on the long straights. For anyone riding Route 66, it is the natural place to start or end a day, with everything a traveller needs and a handful of landmarks that have become part of the road's folklore.
