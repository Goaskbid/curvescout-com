Why this ride

A 415 km round trip from Ban Na - roughly 7 h 09 in the saddle. It goes by way of Si Satchanalai Historical Park, Kamphaeng Phet Historical Park and back through Si Satchanalai Historical Park. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sukhotai Historical Park

    In 1238, two Thai chiefs drove the Khmer governor out of a small city in the north of the central plain and founded a kingdom, and Sukhothai, the Dawn of Happiness, became the first capital of what we now call Thailand. The kingdom reached its height under King Ramkhamhaeng, who is credited with creating the Thai alphabet. A stone inscription dated to 1292, which is attributed to him, describes a country where there was fish in the water and rice in the fields, and where anyone with a grievance could ring a bell at the palace gate to be heard by the king. Whether the picture is wholly true is debated, but the image of a benevolent kingdom has clung to Sukhothai ever since.

  2. 2. Si Satchanalai Historical Park

    Si Satchanalai is the quieter sibling of Sukhothai, and many riders prefer it for exactly that reason. It sits on the Yom River in northern Thailand's lower north, in a bowl of low hills, and what remains of the old city is spread across a park of trimmed lawns, laterite walls and big shade trees. The kingdom of Sukhothai used this as a sister city in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, and the monuments here, in a style that mixes Khmer, Sinhalese and Thai ideas, are some of the earliest Thai Buddhist architecture you can still walk among.

  3. 3. Kamphaeng Phet Historical Park

    Kamphaeng Phet means diamond wall, though the name probably refers to the strength of the fortifications rather than to any gems. The historical park lies on the Ping river in the lower north of Thailand, a short ride from a provincial town of nearly sixty thousand, and it protects the remains of a city that served as a fortress of the Sukhothai kingdom in the fourteenth century. Together with Sukhothai itself and Si Satchanalai, it makes up a UNESCO World Heritage group that records the first great Thai kingdom.
