Why this ride

From Røyken it runs 819 km to Åneby; allow about 15 h 48 of riding. The stops in between: Sandvika, Scenic stretch near Grua and near Maura. The climbs: near Drammen and near Flateby.

The stops

  1. 1. Fuel stop near Røyken

    Start here.

    Røyken sits on the hilly neck of land between the Drammensfjord and the Oslofjord, south-west of Oslo, a mix of villages, farms, forests and commuter housing. It is a practical place to fill the tank before heading south through the Hurum peninsula or west towards Drammen and Telemark.

    Read the complete Fuel stop near Røyken write-up.

  2. 2. High point near Drammen

    25 km in. You top out here; the road stops climbing.

    Drammen is a city on the river Drammenselva, where it flows into the Drammensfjord, south-west of Oslo. Once an industrial town with a polluted river, it has transformed itself over the past few decades, cleaning up the river, building parks and promenades along its banks, and restoring its old buildings. Today it is a pleasant, lively city, with a fine square at Bragernes, a theatre and riverside cafés.

    Read the complete High point near Drammen write-up.

  3. 3. Near Sandvika

    62 km in.

    You end the day in Bærum, the big, comfortable municipality that runs west from Oslo along the fjord, and Sandvika is its centre: a cluster of offices, shopping and apartment blocks where two rivers come out into a sheltered inlet. It is suburban, yes, but suburban with sea water, small islands and wooded hills behind, and after a long ride through mountain country that is not a bad place to roll to a halt.

    Read the complete Near Sandvika write-up.

  4. 4. Scenic stretch near Grua

    126 km in.

  5. 5. Photo stop near Maura

    162 km in.

    North of Oslo, beyond the airport at Gardermoen, the land opens into a quiet country of farms, forests and lakes. Maura is a small village here, in the municipality of Nannestad, and it gives a sense of rural Norway within an hour of the capital, with red barns, white farmhouses and spruce forests stretching to the horizon.

    Read the complete Photo stop near Maura write-up.

  6. 6. Near Rømskog, on the way back

    272 km in.

    Out where Norway runs into Sweden along a line of forest and water, Rømskog sits on the shore of Rømsjøen, a long lake surrounded by spruce and pine. Until a recent merger it was one of the smallest municipalities in the country, and it still feels like the end of the road: a few farms, a church, a school and endless trees.

    Read the complete Near Rømskog, on the way back write-up.

  7. 7. Food stop near Mysen

    315 km in.

    Mysen is the main town of Eidsberg, in the inland part of Østfold, south-east of Oslo, and it is the sort of Norwegian town that rarely appears in travel brochures: a railway station, a shopping street, a few cafés and a calm, practical air. Around it lies some of the most productive farmland in Norway, rolling fields of grain broken by patches of spruce and birch forest, red barns and white farmhouses.

    Read the complete Food stop near Mysen write-up.

  8. 8. Back to Røyken

    396 km in.

    It is the edge of the Oslo region, a wooded landscape of low hills, farms, inlets and small boats, with the Hurum peninsula to the south and the Drammensfjord to the west. After the Telemark canal roads, this is the way home.

    Read the complete Røyken write-up.

  9. 9. Fuel stop in Åneby

    457 km in.

  10. 10. Scenic stretch near Kløfta

    496 km in.

    Kløfta is a busy junction north-east of Oslo, where the E6 motorway north towards Trondheim meets Rv2 east towards Kongsvinger and the Swedish border. Oslo Airport at Gardermoen is just to the north, and the area is a hub of roads, railways, service stations and shopping centres. It is not a place to linger, but it is a practical start for a long day.

    Read the complete Scenic stretch near Kløfta write-up.

  11. 11. High point near Flateby

    529 km in. High point near Flateby is the summit of this stretch.

  12. 12. Photo stop near Ås

    592 km in.

  13. 13. Near Kolbotn

    622 km in.

    Kolbotn is a quiet suburb in the woods south of Oslo, built around a lake, Kolbotnvannet, and close to the much larger Gjersjøen, with forest all around and the inner Oslofjord glinting just to the west. It is a place of family homes, sports fields and ski trails, and it is hard to believe that it gave the world one of the most influential black metal bands, Darkthrone, which formed here in the late 1980s and recorded albums that shaped a whole genre.

    Read the complete Near Kolbotn write-up.

  14. 14. Food stop in Drammen

    677 km in. Food stop in Drammen is where this day stops to eat.

  15. 15. Near Hønefoss, on the way back

    743 km in.

    North-west of Oslo, beyond the forested ridge of Krokskogen, the land opens out into the broad farmland of Ringerike, around the long lake of Tyrifjorden and the smaller Steinsfjorden. Hønefoss is the main town, built around a waterfall where the Begna and Randselva rivers join, and from here roads radiate north into the valleys and mountains of central Norway.

    Read the complete Near Hønefoss, on the way back write-up.

  16. 16. Back to Røyken

    The end: 819 km.

    It is gentle country of clay fields, small woods and low ridges, where Norwegians who work in the capital have settled in neat suburbs and villages.

    Read the complete Røyken write-up.
