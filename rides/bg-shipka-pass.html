Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Gabrovo

    Gabrovo is the town where the Balkan Mountains begin to rise in earnest, strung along the narrow valley of the Yantra River in central Bulgaria, and it is best known for a joke. The town has a reputation, among Bulgarians, for thrift, and the legends of its stinginess have produced a whole genre of humour. The House of Humour and Satire, a museum devoted to it, is one of the stranger attractions in the country, and the town holds a biennial carnival of humour. Locals take the whole thing with good grace, and a visitor who tells a thrift joke will probably be rewarded with a better one.

  2. 2. Shipka

    The village of Shipka sits at the foot of the Balkan range on the edge of the Rose Valley, and above it, on the slopes leading to the pass, the golden onion domes of a Russian church shine out of the forest like something from a fairy tale. The Shipka Memorial Church was built in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries to honour the Russian and Bulgarian soldiers who died in the battles for the pass during the Russo-Turkish War of 1877 to 1878, and its bells were cast from the spent cartridges collected on the battlefield. It is one of the most striking buildings in Bulgaria.

  3. 3. Kazanlak

    In early June the air around Kazanlak smells faintly of roses, and it is no marketing trick. The town of about fifty-five thousand sits in a broad valley between the Balkan range to the north and the lower Sredna Gora to the south, and the fields here have for centuries been planted with the damask rose, whose petals are picked at dawn and distilled into oil that perfumers buy by the gram. The Rose Festival, held in the first weekend of June, fills the streets with parades and folk dancing and a general air of cheerful chaos.
