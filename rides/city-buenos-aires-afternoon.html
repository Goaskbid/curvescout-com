Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Buenos Aires

    Buenos Aires is the capital that looks across a river wide enough to be mistaken for the sea. The Río de la Plata is brown, shallow and enormous, and the city that sits on its western shore has always seemed to face outward, toward Europe. Pedro de Mendoza first tried to found a settlement in 1536 and failed, and Juan de Garay refounded it in 1580. Since then the port has grown into one of the great cities of the southern hemisphere, with about thirteen million people in its wider area, wide avenues designed on a Parisian scale, and cafés that keep a table for you all afternoon.

  2. 2. Historic Quarter of the City of Colonia de

    The ferry from Buenos Aires crosses the muddy, brown Rio de la Plata in about an hour, and when you arrive in Colonia del Sacramento, the city seems to have been kept in a jar. The Portuguese founded it in 1680 as a smuggling base to rival Spanish Buenos Aires on the opposite shore, and for the next century it was fought over by Portugal and Spain, passing from hand to hand. The old quarter on the peninsula is the result, with cobbled streets, low whitewashed houses with tiled roofs, and a mix of Portuguese and Spanish colonial styles. It was listed by UNESCO in 1995.

  4. 4. Maison du docteur Curutchet

    La Plata is a city with an unusual origin: it was planned from scratch in the 1880s as the new capital of Buenos Aires province, after the old city of Buenos Aires was made a federal district. Its founder, Dardo Rocha, commissioned a grid of streets with diagonals cut across it, and plazas every six blocks, a design inspired by Washington and Paris. The result is a calm, tree-lined place with a vast neo-Gothic cathedral and a university tradition.
