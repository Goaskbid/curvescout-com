Why this ride

North-east villages, gentle sweepers and a good low-risk ride. 170 km round from Isernhagen Farster Bauerschaft - plan on about 3 h 32 of riding. It goes by way of Burgdorf, Celle old-town sight, Wienhausen abbey road and Gifhorn water stop. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hannover north-east edge

    Hannover is not a city that people brag about, and that suits a motorcyclist, because it means the escape routes are uncrowded. The north-eastern edge, where the suburbs of Isernhagen and Burgdorf give way to farmland and pine woods, is where a ride in this part of Lower Saxony begins. The land is flat and sandy, shaped by ice-age meltwater, and it seems to hold its breath before the moors and heaths of the Lüneburg country begin. On the way out you pass the city's famous green space, the Eilenriede, one of the biggest city forests in Europe.

  2. 2. Burgdorf fuel gate

    Burgdorf is a town of around thirty thousand people in the flat country northeast of Hanover, on the sandy plain between Hanover and the Aller valley, and it serves as a useful gate for riders heading north into the Lüneburg and Südheide country. The landscape is open and agricultural, with fields of wheat, rape and sugar beet, and long, straight roads lined with trees. This is not mountain land, and the pleasures of the ride lie in rhythm, light and the quiet, unpressured miles.

  3. 3. Celle old-town sight

    Celle is a town that was almost forgotten and so was saved. It sits on the Aller river at the southern edge of the Lüneburg Heath, a handsome ducal residence of seventy-one thousand people, and its old town escaped the bombing of the Second World War, leaving hundreds of half-timbered houses in perfect shape, with carved beams, painted inscriptions and gables leaning over the cobbles.

  4. 4. Wienhausen abbey road

    Wienhausen is a village on the River Aller, a few kilometres from Celle, and it has something remarkable behind its walls: a medieval convent that never really stopped being a convent. Kloster Wienhausen was founded in the thirteenth century as a Cistercian nunnery, and when the Reformation arrived in the sixteenth, it turned Lutheran but carried on as a community of women. A group of Protestant sisters still lives there today. The brick Gothic buildings, with their stepped gables and cloister, are among the best preserved of their kind in northern Germany.

  5. 5. Gifhorn water stop

    Gifhorn is a town of mills, and it has an unexpected claim to fame: the International Mill Museum, which has one of the largest collections of windmills and watermills in the world, with full-sized examples from Germany, the Netherlands, Greece, Russia, and as far away as China. They stand in a park on the edge of town, some turning in the breeze, and a small Russian Orthodox chapel and a Japanese pavilion add to the mix. It is an odd, delightful place, and children and adults alike seem to enjoy it.

  6. 6. Peine food return

    Peine is an industrial town between Hanover and Braunschweig, and it does not pretend to be anything else, which is its charm. For more than a century, steel was its business: the Ilseder Hütte steelworks, founded in 1858, shaped the town and the surrounding villages, and the Peiner Träger, steel beams made here, were exported across Europe. The heavy industry has declined, but it left behind a working-class town with a strong sense of identity, brick housing, and honest, unfussy places to eat.

  7. 7. Back to Hannover

    On the eastern side of Hannover, the city stretches into leafy suburbs, and the dot lies near the Mittelland Canal, among tidy housing estates and patches of woodland. Hannover is a big, sensible city, rebuilt after heavy bombing in the Second World War, and it has an unexpectedly green heart in the Eilenriede, a city forest of old oaks and beeches, one of the largest urban forests in Europe, where people ride, jog and walk dogs between the lakes and meadows.

Hannover north-east edge to Burgdorf fuel gate

Use Hannover north-east edge → Burgdorf fuel gate as clean positioning. Keep it tidy, ignore impatient traffic and save the enthusiasm for the good road ahead. The value here is not drama; it is arriving at the riding section with space, fuel and concentration intact.

This Burgdorf fuel gate → Celle old-town sight stretch is for rhythm and scenery. Keep your head up, let the view breathe, and resist turning every open bend into a target. Stop once properly rather than half-stopping everywhere.
