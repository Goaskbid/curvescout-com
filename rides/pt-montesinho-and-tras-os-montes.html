  1. 1. Bragança

    Bragança is far from everywhere, and its people will tell you that is how they like it. The city sits in the northeast corner of Portugal, in the region called Trás-os-Montes, Beyond the Mountains, with Spain only a few kilometres away. The old walled citadel on its hill is the star: a medieval castle with a tall keep and, inside the walls, the Domus Municipalis, a five-sided Romanesque building that has no real parallel elsewhere in the country. Its original purpose is still debated, though it seems to have been a council chamber and cistern.

  2. 2. Vinhais

    Vinhais is a hill town in the green, forested uplands of Trás-os-Montes, and it has the air of a place that eats very well. The town is known above all for its smoked meats. Every winter it holds a fair devoted to the fumeiro, the traditional smoked sausages, hams and other cured products of the region, and the pigs that supply them are a local breed, the Bísaro, which is raised in the hills and fed on chestnuts and acorns. If you arrive hungry, the cooking here is a pleasure, and you will find it hearty and generous.

  3. 3. Chaves

    Chaves has been a border town for two thousand years, and it still has the look of one. It lies in the broad Tâmega valley in northern Portugal, a few kilometres from Spain, with a Roman bridge over the river, Trajan's Bridge, built in the first century and still carrying foot traffic. The Romans came for the hot springs, which they called Aquae Flaviae, after the emperor Flavius Vespasian, and the water that rises in the town at over seventy degrees is still used in the spa today.
