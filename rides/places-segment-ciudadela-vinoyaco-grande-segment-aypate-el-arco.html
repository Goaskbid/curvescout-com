Why this ride

The stops in between: Subsection Quebrada Huatuchi - Plaza del I, Segment San José - Llamacanchi - Las Limas, Segment Aypate - El Arco and back through Segment San José - Llamacanchi - Las Limas. The highest point is about 2,800 m. It crosses from Ecuador into Peru - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Segment Ciudadela - Vinoyaco Grande

    The Inca built a road through the Andes of Ecuador as they did elsewhere, and in the south of the country a stretch of it runs through the hills near Saraguro, north of the city of Loja. This is a section of the Qhapaq Ñan, the great Andean road system recognised by UNESCO in 2014, and the segment from Ciudadela to Vinoyaco Grande follows a paved or cobbled path across high grassland and over low ridges, with stone steps in the steep places and drains to keep the water off. Saraguro is known for its indigenous people, the Saraguros, who wear black clothing, the men in knee-length trousers and flat hats and the women with shawls and long pleated skirts, and who keep a strong tradition of farming and festivals.

  2. 2. Subsection Quebrada Huatuchi - Plaza del I

    Far down in the southwest of Ecuador, in the province of Loja near the border with Peru, the old Inca highway crossed a dry and broken country of ridges and ravines on its way between the Andes of Ecuador and northern Peru. The subsection near the Quebrada Huatuchi is one of the stretches of the Qhapaq Ñan identified and protected here, part of a route that linked Inca settlements on both sides of the present border, including the ruins of Aypate on the Peruvian side. Out here the stone paving, retaining walls and drainage of the road can still be traced across the hillsides.

  3. 3. Segment San José - Llamacanchi - Las Limas

    The Inca road did not end at the Ecuadorian border; it ran on through the Andes of southern Ecuador towards Cuenca and Quito, and segments of it survive in the province of Loja, among them this one between San José, Llamacanchi and Las Limas. It is a remote stretch in hill country near the Peruvian border, where the Andes are lower and drier than further north, and the road crosses ridges and valleys covered with scrub, pasture and patches of seasonally dry forest. It belongs to the Qhapaq Ñan World Heritage site, which spans six countries.

  4. 4. Segment Aypate - El Arco

    High above the dry coast of northern Peru, the Andes rise into a cold, wet world of páramo and cloud forest, and in the highlands near Ayabaca sits Aypate, an Inca administrative centre that few visitors ever see. It was one of the major stops on the Inca road network in this part of the country, linked to the great highway that ran north toward Quito, and the Qhapaq Ñan road system was listed by UNESCO in 2014. The stone terraces, plazas and buildings lie scattered on a windy hilltop, with the mist drifting through, and the sense of remoteness is extraordinary.
