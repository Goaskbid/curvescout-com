Why this ride

65 km from Catí to Catí, about 1 h 07 of riding. The stops in between: molí Darrer- Abric I, mas d'en Badenes and back through molí Darrer- Abric I. The highest point is about 1,250 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cenrelles- Abric IV

    From the sea to the plateau is a climb of several hundred metres in less than fifty kilometres, and a rider who starts at Alcalà de Xivert, on the Castellón coast, can feel the world change on the way. Alcalà itself is a market town with a ruined Templar castle nearby at Xivert, orange groves and a long beach down the road at Alcossebre, and behind it the wild Serra d'Irta, a natural park of unspoilt cliffs and scrub. Inland, the land rises through olive and almond terraces to the Maestrat plateau, and by the time you reach Catí and Albocàsser, twenty-three kilometres from the coast as the crow flies, the air has changed.

  2. 2. molí Darrer- Abric I

    The Alt Maestrat is the high, rugged interior of Castellón province, a country of limestone plateaus, deep ravines and stone villages perched on ridges, and it is crossed by a web of dry-stone walls that farmers have been building and rebuilding for centuries. Near the villages of Benassal, Catí and Villafranca del Cid, under a rock overhang beside an old mill site, the shelter called Molí Darrer holds prehistoric paintings, part of the scattered Levantine rock art that UNESCO inscribed as one site across eastern Spain.
