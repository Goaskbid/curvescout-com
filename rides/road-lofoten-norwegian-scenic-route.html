Why this ride

From Melbu it runs 282 km to Melbu; allow about 4 h 42 of riding. The stops in between: Svolvaer, Leknes, Reine and A i Lofoten. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Road summary

A island coastal route from Raftsundet, Norway to A i Lofoten, Norway, via Svolvaer. Key character: jagged peaks; beaches; fishing villages. Surface: paved.

Scenic road chapters

Norwegian Scenic Route Lofoten

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 41 km this is Norwegian Scenic Route Lofoten, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

  1. 1. Raftsundet

    The cliffs are black and wet, the peaks are jagged, and in winter they hold snow to the shore. The strait is part of the route the Hurtigruten coastal steamer takes, and on a good day a ship can be seen inching between the walls.

  2. 2. Svolvaer

    The Svolværgeita, the Svolvær Goat, is a pair of rock horns on a peak above the town, and climbers jump the gap between them for the thrill of it. That is the sort of place Svolvær is. It is the largest town in the Lofoten islands, with some four thousand people on the island of Austvågøya, and it sits on the edge of a harbour surrounded by jagged mountains that rise straight out of the sea.

  3. 3. Leknes

    Leknes is the practical heart of Lofoten, a modest town with a lot to be grateful for. It sits on Vestvågøy, the flattest of the larger islands, and has the airport, the biggest supermarkets and the hospital, so everything you forgot to bring can probably be bought here. If you have ridden the long, scenic road from Svolvær, the sight of a proper grocery store is welcome.

  4. 4. Reine

    Reine is one of those places that looks unreal in photographs and looks better in life. A cluster of red and ochre fishing cabins, the rorbuer, stand on stilts at the edge of a narrow fjord, with the sheer spire of Reinebringen rising straight behind them, and the whole scene sits at the southern end of the Lofoten archipelago, a long wall of peaks that rises out of the Norwegian Sea well inside the Arctic Circle. Only a few hundred people live in the village, and in summer the number of visitors dwarfs them.

  5. 5. A i Lofoten

    Å is the last village on the Lofoten road, at the very end of the E10, and it has the shortest name in Norway. The village is a collection of red and ochre fishing huts, called rorbuer, on rocky shores, with drying racks for cod and a small harbour. About a hundred people live here, and Reine, a few kilometres back, is a little larger. The mountains behind are steep and jagged, and the sea is deep blue, with the outer islands of Værøy and Røst visible on clear days. Beyond Å, the road simply stops, and the sea goes on.
