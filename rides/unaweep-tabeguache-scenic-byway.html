Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Grand Junction

    Grand Junction sits where the Gunnison River flows into the Colorado, in the high desert of western Colorado, and it takes its name from that meeting of waters. Red sandstone mesas close it in on one side, the Book Cliffs on the other, and in between is a fertile valley full of orchards and vineyards. Peaches from nearby Palisade are famous in Colorado, and the town of Palisade has become a small wine region, with tasting rooms among the fruit farms. Orchard Mesa, a short way south, is part of the same agricultural belt.

  2. 2. Unaweep Canyon

    Unaweep Canyon is a geological oddity. Most canyons have a river running through them, in one direction, from a high end to a low one. This one has two creeks, East Creek and West Creek, flowing in opposite directions from a low divide in the middle, so that the valley appears to drain both ways at once. The canyon was carved by an ancient river, probably the Colorado or Gunnison, that flowed through it millions of years ago before something diverted it, and geologists are still arguing about what. The name is generally given as a Ute word, sometimes translated as canyon with two mouths.

  3. 3. Gateway

    Gateway, Colorado, sits where the Dolores River squeezes out of a red-rock canyon and the land opens into a wide, flat valley, and the name describes the geography exactly. Cliffs of rust-coloured sandstone rise on every side, stained with desert varnish and carved into fins, towers and shelves, and the town fits into the gap like a pebble in a doorway. There are a few dozen residents, a post office, and a very comfortable-looking resort, and not much else for miles.

  4. 4. Placerville

    Placerville is a handful of buildings beside the San Miguel River in the southwest Colorado mountains, and it is barely more than a name on the map, but it has a good one. The word placer refers to a deposit of gold in a streambed that can be washed out by hand, and the hunt for it brought prospectors here in the 1870s. Later, the Rio Grande Southern Railroad ran through the village, carrying ore and passengers down the canyon, and some of the old railroad buildings and grades can still be seen.
