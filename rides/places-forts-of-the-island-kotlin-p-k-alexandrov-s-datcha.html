Why this ride

A 73 km round trip from Kronstadt - roughly 1 h 25 in the saddle. On the way it passes The Mordvinov's Estate, P.K.Alexandrov's Datcha and back through The Mordvinov's Estate.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

24 km from Forts of the Island Kotlin to The Mordvinov's Estate, mostly on КАД. Fairly level - 0 to 30 m end to end. Water alongside for much of it - pick one good pull-out and stop.

The stops

  1. 1. Forts of the Island Kotlin

    Start here.

    Kotlin island carries so many forts that the usual way to describe it is as a single stone ship moored in the middle of the gulf. The whole island is fortified, and you can see the evidence from the water, from the road and from the air: low batteries, round towers, harbour walls and the great forts built on artificial islands in the shallows. Many carry the names of tsars and grand dukes, and some, like the fort known as Alexander I, have a more sinister reputation, since it was used as a research station for plague bacteria in the early twentieth century and is still called the Plague Fort.

    Read the complete Forts of the Island Kotlin write-up.

  2. 2. The Mordvinov's Estate

    24 km in.

    Peterhof's fountains get the attention, but the coast road west of it, running along the Gulf of Finland toward Oranienbaum, is the better introduction to the landscape. The Mordvinov estate, named for a family of Russian statesmen and naval officers, belongs to the string of country houses that nobles built along this shore in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, when Peter the Great's new capital pulled the whole aristocracy to the Baltic.

    Read the complete The Mordvinov's Estate write-up.

  3. 3. P.K.Alexandrov's Datcha

    38 km in.

    Strelna lies at the beginning of the road to Peterhof, on the southern shore of the Gulf of Finland, and its main feature is the Konstantinovsky Palace, begun in the reign of Peter the Great and named for a later grand duke, Konstantin. Like Peterhof, it was heavily damaged in the Second World War and later rebuilt, and in the early 2000s it was restored for the three-hundredth anniversary of Saint Petersburg. The park, with its canals and ponds, runs down toward the water, and the view across the gulf is wide and grey.

    Read the complete P.K.Alexandrov's Datcha write-up.
