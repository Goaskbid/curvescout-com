Why this ride

932 km from Al Muharraq to Al Muharraq, about 17 h of riding. The stops in between: Al Zubarah Archaeological Site and back through Al Zubarah Archaeological Site. It crosses from Bahrain into Qatar - carry ID and check the toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Nūkhidhah House

    Start here.

    In pearling, the captain was the nukhidhah, and he was everything: navigator, banker, judge and, for months at a time, the only authority a hundred men would see. The word comes from Persian and travelled across the Gulf with the trade. A house that carries the name belonged to one of those men, or to his family, in the old town of Muharraq, and it stands as a reminder of how the pearling hierarchy worked, from the captain at the top to the divers and rope-pullers underneath.

    Read the complete Nūkhidhah House write-up.

  2. 2. Al Zubarah Archaeological Site

    About 466 km in.

    Out on the north-west coast of Qatar, where the desert slides into a shallow turquoise sea, stand the low ruins of Al Zubarah, once one of the busiest pearling and trading towns in the Gulf. In the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the town thrived on pearls dived from the oyster beds offshore, traded on to India, Persia and beyond. Then it declined, was abandoned in the early twentieth century, and the sand covered it. Archaeologists have since uncovered streets, houses, a souk, mosques and a long town wall running in an arc around the old harbour. UNESCO inscribed the site in 2013.
