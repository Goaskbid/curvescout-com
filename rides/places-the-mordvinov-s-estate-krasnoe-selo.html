Why this ride

A 66 km round trip from Petrodvorets - roughly 1 h 21 in the saddle. The stops in between: P.K.Alexandrov's Datcha, Krasnoe Selo and back through P.K.Alexandrov's Datcha. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The Mordvinov's Estate to P.K.Alexandrov's Datcha: 14 km on Санкт-Петербургское шоссе. An easy profile, never above 28 m. Watch for sand and spray near the water.

The stops

  1. 1. The Mordvinov's Estate

    Start here.

    Peterhof's fountains get the attention, but the coast road west of it, running along the Gulf of Finland toward Oranienbaum, is the better introduction to the landscape. The Mordvinov estate, named for a family of Russian statesmen and naval officers, belongs to the string of country houses that nobles built along this shore in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, when Peter the Great's new capital pulled the whole aristocracy to the Baltic.

    Read the complete The Mordvinov's Estate write-up.

  2. 2. P.K.Alexandrov's Datcha

    13 km in.

    Strelna lies at the beginning of the road to Peterhof, on the southern shore of the Gulf of Finland, and its main feature is the Konstantinovsky Palace, begun in the reign of Peter the Great and named for a later grand duke, Konstantin. Like Peterhof, it was heavily damaged in the Second World War and later rebuilt, and in the early 2000s it was restored for the three-hundredth anniversary of Saint Petersburg. The park, with its canals and ponds, runs down toward the water, and the view across the gulf is wide and grey.

    Read the complete P.K.Alexandrov's Datcha write-up.

  3. 3. Krasnoe Selo

    33 km in.

    Readers of Anna Karenina will know Krasnoye Selo, even if they have never been within a thousand kilometres of it. It was here, at the summer camp of the imperial guards, that Tolstoy set the officers' steeplechase in which Vronsky rides his mare Frou-Frou to disaster in front of the court. For more than a century, from the early nineteenth century until the revolution, the guards regiments of the Russian army gathered each summer in this area south-west of Saint Petersburg for manoeuvres, parades and horse races, and the tsar and the court came to watch. It is listed as one of the monuments associated with the historic centre of Saint Petersburg.
