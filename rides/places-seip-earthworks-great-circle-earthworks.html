Why this ride

A 306 km round trip from Frankfort - roughly 5 h 17 in the saddle. It goes by way of Hopeton Earthworks, Great Circle Earthworks and back through Hopeton Earthworks. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Seip Earthworks

    The Hopewell people built in earth the way others build in stone. About two thousand years ago, in the valleys of southern Ohio, they raised enormous banks, circles, squares and octagons, some enclosing many hectares, and aligned them to the sun and moon. Seip Earthworks, in the valley of Paint Creek in Ross County, is part of the Hopewell Ceremonial Earthworks, which were inscribed as a World Heritage site in 2023, and the group also includes the Octagon Earthworks at Newark and the High Bank Works near Chillicothe. They were not forts or farms, but meeting places for gatherings, feasts and funerals.

  2. 2. Hopeton Earthworks

    Ohio is not the place you expect to find ancient geometry, but the Hopewell people built it here. Hopeton Earthworks lies on a terrace above the Scioto River just north of Chillicothe, and it is one of the sites listed by UNESCO in 2023 as the Hopewell Ceremonial Earthworks. These mounds and enclosures were built roughly two thousand years ago, and the scale is hard to grasp from the ground because many of the walls are low and softened by farm fields and time. The shapes were circles, squares and octagons, set out with a precision that suggests careful planning.

  3. 3. Great Circle Earthworks

    Where the Hopewell builders wanted to impress, they built big, and the Great Circle at Heath is the proof. The enclosure is a ring of earth about three hundred and sixty metres across, with walls that still rise to around four metres, inside a ditch that makes them look even taller. It is the largest circular earthwork in the Americas that survives, and standing at the entrance, you see the bank curve away into the trees on both sides. Nobody knows how many baskets of soil it took, but it must have been millions.
