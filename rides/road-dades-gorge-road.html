Why this ride

From Kelaat Mgouna it runs 101 km to Kelaat Mgouna; allow about 1 h 41 of riding. The stops in between: Ait Oudinar, Dades hairpins, Msemrir and back through Dades hairpins. The highest point is about 2,550 m.

Road summary

A canyon road from Boumalne Dades, Morocco to Msemrir, Morocco, via Ait Oudinar. Key character: red canyon; hairpins; kasbah villages. Surface: paved.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Boumalne Dades

    The road to Boumalne Dadès runs through country that looks like another planet. The town sits at about fifteen hundred metres on the edge of the High Atlas, on the old route known as the Road of a Thousand Kasbahs, and from here the Dadès river cuts a gorge into red rock that is among the finest in Morocco. The settlement itself is small and dusty, a stop on the highway with a market, cafes and a handful of hotels, but it is the gateway to some serious riding.

  2. 2. Ait Oudinar

    Ait Oudinar is the village where the Dades valley stops being a pleasant river road and starts to behave like a gorge. The Dades river, running down from the High Atlas, has cut through red and ochre rock here, leaving walls, towers and odd, weathered shapes, and the village sits among them with palms, fields of barley and a scatter of mud-brick houses in the bottom of the valley. The nearest towns are Tinghir to the east and Kelaat Mgouna, a market town of some fourteen thousand people, to the west, and fuel is available in both.

  3. 3. Dades hairpins

    The Dades hairpins are Morocco's answer to a stack of pancakes: a series of tight switchbacks that climb the side of the gorge, with the road folding back on itself again and again as it gains height above the river. From the viewpoint at the top, the whole route lies below you like a ribbon dropped on rock, and the red-orange walls of the Dades Gorge fall away into the valley. Tinghir is the nearest large town, a short ride to the east, and it is the place to find fuel and a proper meal before heading up.
