Why this ride

curated scenic road. rolling hills, farmhouses, covered bridges. From Bern it runs 58 km to Entlebuch; allow about 1 h 03 of riding. On the way it passes Burgdorf, Lützelflüh and Langnau. The highest point is about 1,500 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bern

    Bern is the odd capital, one that never calls itself a capital: the Swiss federal government sits here but the official term is federal city. It is also one of the best places in the country to park a bike and walk, because the old town fills a tight loop of the Aare river, with the water running green and fast on three sides far below the bridges. UNESCO put the medieval centre on its list in 1983, and the sandstone arcades along the main streets, about six kilometres of them in total, mean you can shop in the rain without an umbrella.

  2. 2. Burgdorf

    Burgdorf looks like what a Swiss town ought to look like, and has the good sense not to make a fuss about it. A hilltop castle of pale stone stands above the old town, rooflines step down the slope, and the river Emme runs below in a green gorge. It is a town of nearly fifteen thousand people, and the gateway to the Emmental, the hill country of canton Bern that gave its name to the most famous Swiss cheese.

  3. 3. Lützelflüh

    Lützelflüh is the sort of Emmental village that would be forgotten by everyone except that a pastor once lived here and wrote about it. Jeremias Gotthelf served as the parish priest from the 1830s until his death in the 1850s, and his novels about farmers, cheesemakers and stubborn families gave the valley its literary identity. You will see his name on the church, the museum and half the brochures, and for once the fuss is deserved: read one of his books and the green, rolling hills around you start to look like a stage set for a very old argument over inheritance.

  5. 5. Entlebuch

    Entlebuch gave the world a dog. The Entlebucher Sennenhund, a small, sturdy, tricolour herding breed, was developed in these valleys to drive cattle on the steep pastures, and the village is proud of it. The surrounding region is a UNESCO biosphere reserve, recognised in 2001, a landscape of moors, wooded ridges and green pasture, where the Kleine Emme river runs down from the Alps toward Lucerne.
