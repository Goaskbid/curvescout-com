Why this ride

812 km from Lagny-sur-Marne to Mitry-Mory, about 15 h 27 of riding. The stops in between: near Crépy-en-Valois, Scenic stretch near Meulan-en-Yvelines and Ézy-sur-Eure. The climbs: near Creil and near Persan.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fuel stop near Lagny-sur-Marne

    Lagny-sur-Marne sits on the river Marne about thirty kilometres east of Paris, a busy town with an old centre, a riverside quay and a large, unfinished Gothic church, Notre-Dame-des-Ardents, all that was completed of an ambitious abbey church begun in the thirteenth century. The town was an important market in the Middle Ages, part of the network of Champagne fairs, and Joan of Arc fought near here in 1430, shortly before her capture at Compiègne.

  2. 2. Food stop near Crépy-en-Valois

    Crépy-en-Valois was once the capital of the Valois, the old county whose name passed to a whole dynasty of French kings. Today it is a busy market town of some fifteen thousand people on the plateau north-east of Paris, a natural place to refuel before heading on to Laon and the Aisne valley.

  3. 3. High point near Creil

    Creil is an industrial town on the Oise, about fifty kilometres north of Paris, and it would be easy to pass it by. Yet within a few kilometres lie some of the finest forests and châteaux in northern France, and the high ground around the town gives you a good sense of how the river has carved its way through the chalk and limestone plateaux of the Île-de-France.

  4. 4. Scenic stretch near Meulan-en-Yvelines

    Meulan-en-Yvelines sits on a bend of the Seine, about forty kilometres north-west of Paris, where the river divides around an island. The old town climbs steeply up the hillside from the water, and from the top you get a fine view over the river, the bridges and the valley. Across the water, Les Mureaux has a long aerospace history, and parts of the Ariane rockets are made there.

  5. 5. Ézy-sur-Eure, on the way back

    Ézy-sur-Eure is a modest town of three thousand on the river Eure in Normandy, close to the border of the Eure and Eure-et-Loir, and its neighbour, Saussay, is a hamlet. The place is a few kilometres from Anet, a small town with one of the most celebrated châteaux of the French Renaissance, built in the 1540s by the architect Philibert de l'Orme for Diane de Poitiers, the favourite of King Henri II. The château, with its elegant courtyard and its famous carved entrance gate, is open to the public, and the story of Diane, who was about twenty years older than the king and ruled his heart for decades, is told in the rooms.

  7. 7. Pierrefitte-sur-Seine

    Pierrefitte-sur-Seine is not where anyone dreams of ending a ride, and that is fine. It is a dense northern suburb of Paris in the Seine-Saint-Denis, just above Saint-Denis, with tower blocks, small houses, busy junctions and the steady rumble of the routes nationales that carry traffic between the capital and the plain to the north. You arrive here because it is the doorway back into the city after a day in the forests of Chantilly and Ermenonville.

  9. 9. Fuel stop near Mitry-Mory

    The aircraft give it away. Mitry-Mory lies just south of Charles de Gaulle Airport, and the planes descending over the fields are a constant presence. This is the Plaine de France, a flat, open country north-east of Paris where large-scale farming mixes with logistics parks, motorways and suburban towns. It is not a place you come to for scenery, but it is a practical gateway for riders heading out of Paris towards the north or the west.

  10. 10. Food stop near Mouy

    Mouy is a small town in the valley of the Thérain, in the Oise department, about sixty kilometres north of Paris, halfway between Creil and Beauvais. The valley is green and pleasant, with water meadows, poplars and old mills along the river, and the town has a modest centre with a church and a market square.

  11. 11. High point near Persan

    Persan sits on the river Oise, about thirty-five kilometres north of Paris, facing Beaumont-sur-Oise across the water. The two towns have grown together along the river, and the old part of Beaumont, with its church and remains of a castle, stands on a hill above the valley.

  12. 12. Scenic stretch in Élancourt

    Élancourt is part of Saint-Quentin-en-Yvelines, one of the new towns built around Paris from the 1970s to absorb the capital's growth. Its landscape of housing estates, business parks and roundabouts is not the stuff of dreams, but the town has two surprises. One is France Miniature, a theme park where the country's monuments are reproduced at small scale. The other is the Colline d'Élancourt, a man-made hill built from construction spoil and landscaped into a park, offering one of the widest views in the west of the Paris region.

  13. 13. Near Épernon, on the way back

    West of Rambouillet forest the land loosens up into a patchwork of wheat, copses and small river valleys, and this stop sits in that gentle seam between the Yvelines and the Eure-et-Loir. Épernon is the nearest proper town, a few kilometres off, built where the Guesle and the Drouette meet before they wander on towards the Eure. It is quiet country, and after a day on the Seine loops it feels like the ride is exhaling.

  14. 14. Photo stop in Boissy-le-Cutté

    Boissy-le-Cutté is a small village in the Essonne, south of Paris, sitting on the edge of the Juine valley in the Gâtinais français regional park. It is a place of quiet streets, stone houses and fields, far from the bustle of the capital even though it lies only an hour away. The surrounding landscape is a mix of farmland, woods and river valleys, with sandstone outcrops scattered across the hills.

  15. 15. Enghien-les-Bains

    Enghien-les-Bains is the only town in the Paris region that feels like a spa resort, and that is exactly what it was built to be. The town grew up in the nineteenth century around sulphurous springs and a lake, and the Parisians who came to take the waters left behind villas, promenades and a casino. This finish puts you right on the lake shore, with Montmorency forest on the hills behind.

  16. 16. Back to Seine Loops

    East of Paris the land flattens into the Brie plateau's margin, and the towns here run together in a belt of low houses, retail parks and market gardens. Mitry-Mory is the nearest place of any size, with about 18,500 people, and Claye-Souilly and Villeparisis lie a little further along. It is not scenic in a postcard sense, yet it has a quiet logic: this is where Paris has always fed itself, and farmland still survives between the suburbs.
