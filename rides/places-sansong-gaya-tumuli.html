Why this ride

306 km from Gyeongju to Gyeongju, about 4 h 45 of riding. It goes by way of Oksan-seowon, Petroglyphs along the Bangucheon Stream, Gaya Tumuli and back through Petroglyphs along the Bangucheon Stream. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Sansong

    Start here.

    The nearest town is Gyeongju itself, a city of about a hundred and fifty thousand with fuel stations, noodle shops and a reputation as a museum without walls.

    Read the complete Sansong write-up.

  2. 2. Oksan-seowon

    30 km in.

    Oksan-seowon sits in a quiet valley northwest of Gyeongju, beside a clear stream that tumbles over smooth granite slabs. It was founded in 1572 to honour Yi Eon-jeok, one of the great Neo-Confucian scholars of the Joseon dynasty, who lived in retreat nearby, and it served as a private academy where young men studied the Confucian classics, prepared for the state examinations, and performed rites in honour of the master. The buildings are arranged according to the strict logic of Confucian architecture: a lecture hall, dormitories for students, a library and, at the back, a shrine for the rites. The architecture is restrained, with wooden halls, tiled roofs and open courtyards, designed to harmonise with the landscape. Oksan is part of the UNESCO listing of the Seowon, Korean Neo-Confucian Academies, inscribed in 2019.

    Read the complete Oksan-seowon write-up.

  3. 3. Petroglyphs along the Bangucheon Stream

    90 km in.

    On a cliff face above a quiet stream in the hills west of Ulsan, prehistoric people carved hundreds of images into the rock: whales, turtles, deer, tigers, boats full of men, hunters with spears and nets. The whales are the most striking, with species that can still be identified, such as right whales and humpbacks, and scenes that seem to show whaling from boats, making the Bangudae petroglyphs one of the oldest records of whaling anywhere. A little upstream, the Cheonjeon-ri petroglyphs, with geometric patterns, animals and later inscriptions from the Silla period, add another layer. In 2025, the Petroglyphs along the Bangucheon Stream were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

    Read the complete Petroglyphs along the Bangucheon Stream write-up.

  4. 4. Gaya Tumuli

    153 km in.

    Right in the middle of Gimhae, a busy city just west of Busan, a low ridge of grassy mounds rises between apartment blocks and roads. These are the Daeseong-dong tumuli, the burial ground of the rulers of Geumgwan Gaya, one of the small kingdoms of the Gaya confederacy that flourished in the south of the Korean peninsula from the first centuries AD until it was absorbed by Silla in the sixth century. In 2023 a group of Gaya burial sites, including these, was added to the UNESCO World Heritage list.

    Read the complete Gaya Tumuli write-up.
