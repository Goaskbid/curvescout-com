Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mirșid - Dealul Mare 2

    Mirsid lies on the edge of the Meses Mountains, and what makes it special is a Roman frontier post just up the road. At Porolissum, in the village of Moigrad, the Romans built a fort, a town and a customs station on the high ground above the pass, and from here they controlled the northern gate of their province of Dacia. Trade and troops passed through the Meses Gate, a narrow notch in the mountains, and the ruins, including the walls of the fort, the remains of an amphitheatre and a customs post, can still be seen on the hill. Porolissum is one of the best-preserved Roman fort complexes in Romania.

  2. 2. Agrij - Coasta Lată

    The Meseș mountains are a modest ridge by Carpathian standards, but for the Romans they were a natural wall, and they built their frontier along it. The north-western edge of Dacia ran along these hills, with forts, watchtowers and walls guarding the passes from the plains of the Barbaricum beyond. Agrij - Coasta Lată, near the villages of Stârciu, Buciumi and Meseșenii de Jos, is one of the watchtower sites named in the World Heritage inscription of 2024. Nearby, at Buciumi, are the remains of a fort, and a little further north, at Moigrad, lies Porolissum, the largest Roman site in the region, with a reconstructed gate.

  3. 3. Poieni - Cornu Sonului

    Poieni, which means clearings or glades in Romanian, is a small village in the hills of Cluj county, on the edge of the Apuseni mountains, and the point called Cornu Sonului lies a few kilometres from it. The landscape is that of western Transylvania as it begins to climb: rolling green hills, patches of beech and oak forest, meadows and farms with steep tiled roofs. To the east and south the land rises toward the great limestone country of the Apuseni, with its caves, gorges and high pastures, while to the west the road drops toward Oradea and the Hungarian border.

  4. 4. Gilău - Dealul Cetății

    Gilau sits where the flat plain of the Somesul Mic river meets the first hills of the Apuseni mountains, about fifteen kilometres west of Cluj-Napoca, in Transylvania, and the hill called Dealul Cetatii, which simply means Fortress Hill, rises almost on the edge of the town, about a kilometre away. The town has nearly eight thousand people, and from the road you can see the high, forested ridges of the Apuseni to the west.
