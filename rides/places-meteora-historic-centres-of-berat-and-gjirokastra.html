Why this ride

From Kalampáka it runs 474 km to Kalampáka; allow about 9 h 10 of riding. The stops in between: Zagori Cultural Landscape, Historic Centres of Berat and Gjirokastra and back through Zagori Cultural Landscape. It tops out around 1,150 m. It crosses from Greece into Albania - carry ID and check the toll rules. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

The roads

Greece Meteora and Pindus Route

Meteora to Zagori Cultural Landscape: 130 km on Τρικάλων - Παναγίας. Up about 1540 m to 1,265 m, then 950 m back down the far side. Take a break before the climb, not halfway up.

The stops

  1. 1. Meteora

    Start here.

    Meteora is the kind of place you assume is a trick of photography until you round the bend. Enormous grey pillars of rock rise out of the Thessaly plain, some more than 300 metres high, and on top of several of them sit monasteries, walls and windows flush with the cliff edge. The name means suspended in the air, and the sandstone and conglomerate towers were shaped over millions of years by water, wind and earthquakes, long before anyone thought to build on them.

    Read the complete Meteora write-up.

  2. 2. Zagori Cultural Landscape

    130 km in.

    In the mountains north of Ioannina, forty-odd stone villages cling to the slopes of the Pindus, linked by cobbled mule paths and arched bridges that seem to grow out of the rock. This is Zagori, a region of grey slate roofs, plane trees, rushing streams and one of the deepest gorges in the world, Vikos. In 2023 it was added to the World Heritage list as a cultural landscape, recognising how people and mountains shaped each other here over centuries.

    Read the complete Zagori Cultural Landscape write-up.

  3. 3. Historic Centres of Berat and Gjirokastra

    223 km in.

    Gjirokastër is built of stone, and it means that literally. The old houses climbing the slopes below the castle are made of grey limestone, roofed with heavy slabs of the same stone, and tall and narrow like small fortresses, with the lower floors used for storage and cisterns and the living quarters above. The town grew under Ottoman rule, and its architecture, preserved as a museum town in the communist era, earned it a place on the UNESCO list in 2005, with Berat added in 2008. The castle, one of the largest in the Balkans, holds a collection of artillery, a captured American aircraft from the Cold War, and a prison with a grim history.
