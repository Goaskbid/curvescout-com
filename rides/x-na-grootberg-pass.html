  1. 1. Palmwag

    Palmwag is a small oasis in the dry, stony north-west of Namibia, named for the palm trees that cluster around a spring on the Uniab River, and it is one of the few places in Kunene where you can find shade, cold water and a bed. The surroundings are a huge concession of desert and rocky hills, a wilderness of red earth and pale grass where animals roam freely, and the lodge sits at the edge of it like a small green island.

  2. 2. Grootberg Pass

    Grootberg means big mountain in Afrikaans, and the name fits a flat-topped plateau that rises out of the stony plains of Kunene in north-western Namibia. It is part of the Etendeka formation, a thick stack of ancient lava flows that now forms a long escarpment, and the pass is a steep gravel descent off the edge, with sharp bends and loose stones. At the top, a lodge perches on the rim with a view that runs across the valley of the Klip river to a horizon of red, brown and purple hills.

  3. 3. Kamanjab

    Kamanjab is a farm-service town on the edge of Damaraland, a place of dust, cattle trucks and big skies that serves as the practical heart of north-western Namibia. It sits at a junction where the tarred road from Outjo and Etosha meets the gravel routes heading west into the empty country of Kunene, and for travellers it is the last place to buy proper supplies for a long way. The surroundings are a mix of commercial cattle farms and scrub-covered hills, dry for most of the year and briefly green after rain.
