    Everything about the approach teaches you scale. The plains run out toward a horizon that looks too far away, mountains stand like cut-outs on the east side, and the road from the main gravel arteries delivers you here with a steady rumble of corrugation.

  2. 2. Sossusvlei Desert Route point 2

    Most people know this stretch by one number. Dune 45 is named for its distance from the park gate at Sesriem, forty-five kilometres, and it is about as far as many visitors go on a first morning, which means it is climbed, photographed and trampled long before the heat arrives.

  3. 3. Sossusvlei Desert Route point 3

    Past the main dune run, the tarmac ends and the desert takes over, which is the point where this route becomes more of a pilgrimage than a ride. The surface under your wheels shifts from asphalt to sand within a few hundred metres, and the standard advice is that anything without four-wheel drive stops at the car park and walks.

  4. 4. Sossusvlei Desert Route point 4

    Of all the places in this part of the Namib, Deadvlei is the one that turns up on postcards, and the reason is simple: it looks wrong in the best way. A white clay pan, baked hard, sits between walls of orange dune, and from its surface stand black, skeletal camelthorn trees, long dead, still upright.
