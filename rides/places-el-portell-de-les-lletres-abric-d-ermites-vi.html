El Portell de les Lletres and Abric d'Ermites VI

Why this ride

From Montblanc it runs 362 km to Montblanc; allow about 6 h 14 of riding. It goes by way of Aqueduct, Balma d'En Roc, Prop de la Cova Pintada and Abric d'Ermites VI. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. El Portell de les Lletres

    Montblanc has been a town of walls since the Middle Ages, and the walls are still most of what you see: a circuit of about a kilometre and a half of ramparts, with thirty-odd towers and gates that make it one of the best-preserved medieval enclosures in Catalonia. El Portell de les Lletres lies a short way beyond them, in the folds of the hills south-east of the town, and its name, which suggests a little gap or notch, fits the way a path slips between two rock faces. A rider can combine the shelter with a day of monastic and medieval sightseeing.

  2. 2. Aqueduct

    The Pont del Diable, the Devil's Bridge, is not a bridge for people. It is a Roman aqueduct, a tall, two-tiered stone arcade that carried water to Tarraco, modern Tarragona, from the Francolí river, about fifteen kilometres away. It spans a small valley near the road to Constantí, two hundred metres long and about twenty-seven metres high at its tallest point, built of big dressed stones without mortar, and it has stood for roughly two thousand years. UNESCO included it in the Archaeological Ensemble of Tarraco in 2000.

  3. 3. Balma d'En Roc

    Inland from the Costa Daurada, the Serra de Llaberia rises abruptly above the coastal plain, a jagged wall of limestone separating the sea from the valley of the Ebro. The rock shelter of Balma d'En Roc lies in this rough country, part of the Levantine rock art inscribed by UNESCO, and like most of its kind it is small, fragile and best visited with local guidance. The nearest villages are scattered: Ginestar on the Ebro, El Perelló on the coastal side, and Mont-roig del Camp to the north-east.

  4. 4. Prop de la Cova Pintada

    The Ports massif rises abruptly at the meeting point of Catalonia, Aragón and Valencia, a tangle of limestone peaks, deep gorges and pine forest that feels surprisingly wild given how close it is to the Ebro delta and the coast. Prop de la Cova Pintada, meaning near the painted cave, sits on its southern side, between Mas de Barberans, Valderrobres and the old city of Tortosa.

  5. 5. Abric d'Ermites VI

    Ulldecona sits at the foot of the Serra de Godall, in the southern tip of Catalonia, close to the border with Valencia and a short distance from the Ebro delta. The town is an old one, set among olive groves that produce a good oil, and above it stands the hermitage of La Pietat, with long views to the sea. The Abric d'Ermites VI is one of the rock shelters of the Ermites group, painted in the Levantine style with small red figures of hunters, and part of the Mediterranean Basin rock art inscribed by UNESCO in 1998. The shelters lie in the cliffs of the Godall range, and access is by foot with regulations to protect the paintings.
