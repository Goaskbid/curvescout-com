Why this ride

From Mérida it runs 126 km to Mérida; allow about 2 h 31 of riding. The stops in between: Cornalvo Dam, Thermal Baths at Alange and back through Cornalvo Dam. It follows river valleys.

The roads

Ruta de la Plata

From Roman Bridge over Albarregas River to Cornalvo Dam is 18 km. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 209 and 317 m. Take it at your own pace.

The stops

  1. 1. Roman Bridge over Albarregas River

    Start here.

    Mérida is built on two rivers. The big one is the Guadiana, crossed by the famous long bridge, and the small one is the Albarregas, a stream that curves around the northern and western edge of the old town and has been bridged since Roman times. The Roman bridge over the Albarregas is a modest structure of granite arches, nothing like the length of its big cousin, but it did a job: it carried the road out of the colony toward the west and the north, and it still stands. A few steps from it the old streets give way to a park with trees, a place where locals walk dogs and children ride bikes.

    Read the complete Roman Bridge over Albarregas River write-up.

  2. 2. Cornalvo Dam

    18 km in.

    Nearly two thousand years after Roman engineers built it, the dam at Cornalvo still holds water. It lies in the rolling countryside north-east of Mérida, in Extremadura, and it supplied the Roman city of Augusta Emerita, capital of the province of Lusitania. The dam is an earthen embankment faced with stone, with a tower in the reservoir that controlled the outflow, and it is part of the Archaeological Ensemble of Mérida, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

    Read the complete Cornalvo Dam write-up.

  3. 3. Thermal Baths at Alange

    63 km in.

    The Romans, who were serious about hot water, found a warm spring at Alange in what is now Extremadura and built a bathing complex around it, and the place is still used today. The Roman baths, about twenty kilometres from Mérida, include a domed chamber with stone benches and a pool fed by the spring, and the water comes out pleasantly warm, a gift in a dry country. The baths date from the time when Mérida, then called Emerita Augusta, was one of the great cities of Roman Spain, and wealthy citizens would travel here to relax and be cured.
