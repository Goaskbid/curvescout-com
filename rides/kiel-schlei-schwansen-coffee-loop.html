Why this ride

Small harbours, farm-road bends and one proper coffee reset. The best part sits around Gettorf road, Eckernförde bay, Schwansen villages, while the most useful town stop keeps the day practical instead of turning it into a fuel or food hunt. Check the OSM map, Google preview and GPX checkpoint order before departure; they use the same waypoint sequence. Ride it with legal pull-offs, patience through villages and enough daylight to stop without rushing.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Gettorf road

    Gettorf is a small town on the Schwansen peninsula, a few kilometres inland from the Baltic, with about six thousand inhabitants and all the basics a rider needs: fuel, a bakery, a supermarket and a handful of cafes. Everything here is gentle, rolling and green, and the sea is never far away.

  2. 2. Eckernförde bay

    Eckernförde Bay is a long, narrow arm of the Baltic that cuts into the coast of Schleswig-Holstein between the Danish Wohld and the Schwansen peninsula, and the little port of the same name sits at its head. The water is shallow, the beaches are wide and sandy, and the whole place has the relaxed air of a north German seaside town, with a promenade, a harbour full of boats and a smell of smoked fish. The altitude here is a modest eight metres, and the horizon is all sea and sky.

  3. 3. Schwansen villages

    Schwansen is the little peninsula that sticks out into the Baltic between the Schlei inlet and Eckernförde Bay, and it is one of the quieter corners of Schleswig-Holstein. The land is low, rolling and green, at only a few tens of metres above the sea, and the villages of Damp, Waabs and Rieseby are separated by fields, small woods and hedgerows. It was once part of the old Danish duchy of Schleswig, and the mix of Danish and German history shows in the names and the architecture.

  4. 4. Kappeln Schlei stop

    Kappeln is a small Baltic port on the Schlei, the long narrow inlet that cuts forty kilometres into Schleswig-Holstein, and its harbour has the busy, salty feel of a working fishing town. Wooden boats line the quays, nets hang to dry, and gulls wheel above the fish stalls. The town sits at the point where the Schlei narrows, so a bridge crosses it, and from the quay you can watch it lift to let tall-masted sailing ships through.
