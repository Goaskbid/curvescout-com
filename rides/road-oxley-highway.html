Why this ride

A 320 km round trip from Wauchope - roughly 5 h 20 in the saddle. On the way it passes Gingers Creek, Apsley Falls, Walcha and back through Apsley Falls. The highest point is about 1,200 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Road summary

A mountain highway from Wauchope, New South Wales to Walcha, New South Wales, via Gingers Creek. Key character: rainforest; escarpment; long bend sequences. Surface: paved.

Scenic road chapters

Oxley Highway

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 22 km this is Oxley Highway, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Wauchope

    Wauchope sits on the Hastings River in the green country behind Port Macquarie on the New South Wales mid-north coast, a country town of six thousand with a main street of pubs and bakeries, and with a fuel station and a decent meal, so this works as a warm start or a quiet finish. The river flows past the edge of town, and the paddocks are rich dairy and timber land between the coast and the escarpment.

  3. 3. Apsley Falls

    Apsley Falls is the place where the gentle New England tableland suddenly falls apart. The Apsley River drifts across open grazing country east of Walcha, and then, without much warning, it plunges over a lip of basalt into a deep gorge that belongs to Oxley Wild Rivers National Park. Standing at the railing, you can feel the spray and hear the water long before you see the base. The gorge walls are layered rock, and the whole scene has the look of a country that was cut open by water over millions of years.

  4. 4. Walcha

    Walcha sits on the Northern Tablelands of New South Wales at about a thousand metres, high enough to have real winters, with frosts, a few snowfalls and a cool, clear air that smells of eucalyptus and woodsmoke. The town is small, with about two and a half thousand people, and it wears its sheep country history openly. Fine merino wool built the district, and for a century the big properties around Walcha shaped both the landscape and the social life. English elms and oaks planted in the streets turn gold and red in April, which is not a sight many people expect in Australia.
