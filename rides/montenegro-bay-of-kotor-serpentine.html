Why this ride

30 km one way from Kotor - plan on about 32 min of riding. On the way it passes Kotor Serpentine and Krstac Pass. The highest point is about 950 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Adriatic Highway, Jadranska Magistrala

The valley floor is behind you and the road starts working for its height at about 35.9%. Corners stack above one another where the ground allows nothing straighter — first and second gear more often than not. Look through each corner to the next one and let the engine do the holding.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kotor

    The Bay of Kotor looks like a fjord but is really a drowned river canyon, a series of linked basins surrounded by limestone mountains that rise nearly straight from the water. Kotor itself sits at the innermost end, a walled town tucked under a steep cliff, with a fortress that climbs the slope behind it in a zigzag line of walls. It was a Venetian possession for centuries, and its narrow lanes, stone churches and small squares have kept their medieval layout. The Cathedral of St Tryphon, from the twelfth century, is the main landmark, and cats are everywhere, as the town's unofficial mascots.

  2. 2. Kotor Serpentine

    The old road out of Kotor does not waste time. It begins almost at the town walls and starts climbing at once, and the famous count is twenty-five hairpins, numbered on small signs, with the bay shrinking below you at each turn until the cruise ships in the harbour look like toys in a bath. The Austrians built it in the nineteenth century, when this coast belonged to the Habsburgs and they wanted a way up the mountain wall to the Montenegrin capital at Cetinje. The gradient is steady, the corners are tight and well engineered, and the retaining walls are old dry stone that still do their job.

  3. 3. Krstac Pass

    Krstac sits at 926 metres, and it is where the serpentine finally lets go of you. The last of the hairpins from Kotor swing out onto a shoulder of limestone, the bay disappears behind you, and what lies ahead is a different Montenegro: a high plateau of stone, thin pasture and beech, with Mount Lovćen rising on your right. The name Montenegro, black mountain, makes sense up here. The slopes look dark under the forest, and in cloud they look black indeed.

  4. 4. Cetinje

    Cetinje is a small town with a big past, the old royal capital of Montenegro. It lies in a high karst basin at about 650 metres, behind the mountain wall of Lovćen, and for centuries it was the seat of the prince-bishops who ruled this stubbornly independent land. The Cetinje Monastery, founded in 1484, still holds the right hand of St John the Baptist and a fragment of the True Cross, according to tradition. The streets are lined with former embassies from the days when European powers kept legations here, and the whole town has the air of a capital that has shrunk but kept its dignity. About fifteen thousand people live here.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Kotor to Kotor Serpentine

Kotor → Kotor Serpentine is 4 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Kotor Serpentine to Krstac Pass

Kotor Serpentine → Krstac Pass climbs about 926 m in 3 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Krstac Pass to Cetinje

Krstac Pass → Cetinje drops about 926 m over 10 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
