  1. 1. Bogota

    Bogotá sits on a high plain in the Andes at about 2,640 metres, which means thin air, cool temperatures and a sky that can go from blue to grey in an hour. Visitors from sea level often feel breathless on the first day and should take it slowly, drinking water and avoiding heavy meals.

  2. 2. Chingaza gate

    Most of Bogotá drinks from this mountain. Chingaza National Park is a block of high páramo, cloud forest and glacial lakes on the eastern slopes of the Cordillera Oriental, where the Andes begin to fall toward the Amazon and Orinoco basins, and it supplies a large share of the capital's water through a series of reservoirs. From the gate the land rises past 3,000 metres into a landscape of tussock grass, peat bogs and frailejones, the silvery, furry-leaved plants that grow nowhere else but the northern Andes and which give the highlands their ghostly look.
