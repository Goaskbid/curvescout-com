Why this ride

169 km from Alpera to Alpera, about 5 h 12 of riding. It goes by way of Barranco del Cabezo del moro, Olula and back through Barranco del Cabezo del moro. It tops out around 850 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Abrigo II

    Start here.

    Alpera is a small town on the plateau between Albacete and the Valencian coast, in the flat, windy country where La Mancha begins to tilt towards the sea. It is best known among archaeologists for Cueva de la Vieja, a shelter with one of the largest collections of Levantine paintings in the interior, and Abrigo II is part of the same group. The cluster of figures includes human forms, animals and symbols, painted in red and black, and the whole group is part of the UNESCO listing for the Mediterranean basin.

    Read the complete Abrigo II write-up.

  2. 2. Barranco del Cabezo del moro

    45 km in.

    Almansa is one of those towns you see from the motorway and do not stop at, and that is a mistake. Its castle is spectacular, a Gothic fortress built on a sharp limestone outcrop right in the middle of town, looking exactly like a child's drawing of a castle. Around it spreads a wide plain at about seven hundred metres, the meeting point of the Valencian, Murcian and Castilian lands, and a few kilometres out in the surrounding hills lies the ravine known as the Barranco del Cabezo del Moro.

    Read the complete Barranco del Cabezo del moro write-up.

  3. 3. Olula

    89 km in.

    Caudete and Almansa sit in a corridor of land where Castile-La Mancha, Valencia and Murcia meet, and for centuries this was frontier country, where kingdoms and armies passed and fought. Olula is a point in the hills near Caudete, linked on the rides with rock-art shelters in the surrounding sierras, part of the extensive UNESCO listing of prehistoric art in Mediterranean Spain.

    Read the complete Olula write-up.
