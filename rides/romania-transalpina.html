Why this ride

A one-way ride of 200 km, Novaci to Sebeş - roughly 2 h 41 in the saddle. On the way it passes Ranca, Urdele Pass and Obarsia Lotrului. The highest point is about 2,150 m. Urdele Pass is open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Romania Transalpina

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2145 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance. Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Novaci

    Novaci is the southern gate of the Transalpina, and for a town of fewer than three thousand people it has a good deal of mountain on its doorstep. It sits in Gorj county, on the southern edge of the Parâng range, where the Oltenian foothills fold up into the high Carpathians, and the air smells of sheep, hay and woodsmoke. Sheep farming has been the livelihood of the district for centuries, and the shepherds still drive their flocks up to the high pastures in summer, living in simple huts and making cheese.

  2. 2. Ranca

    Rânca is a ski village at the foot of the Parâng mountains, built on an alpine meadow that was grazing country for shepherds long before anyone put in a chairlift. It sits at around sixteen hundred metres on the southern side of the Transalpina, the high road that crosses the Southern Carpathians, and from here the road to the pass above takes off in earnest. The village is a scatter of chalets, guesthouses and cafés in the middle of grassy slopes and spruce woods, and in summer the main sounds are cowbells and the odd chainsaw.

  3. 3. Urdele Pass

    At 2,145 metres, Urdele is the highest paved road pass in Romania, and the Transalpina makes sure you earn it. The final stretch from Rânca runs along the bare crest of the Parâng range, above the tree line, where the landscape is grass, rock and sky. Wind is the main sound, and the clouds seem close enough to touch. There are no trees at all, only mountain pasture dotted with sheepfolds, and on the right day you can see almost the full length of the Carpathian chain.

  4. 4. Obarsia Lotrului

    Obârșia Lotrului means the source of the Lotru, and the name explains the place. The Lotru River rises in these mountains and flows down through a long wooded valley to the Olt, and here at its head the landscape opens into forest, meadow and the glint of lakes. It is a quiet mountain hamlet on the northern side of the Transalpina, with a handful of cabins, guesthouses and a ski area that is lively in winter and gentle in summer, and it makes a good pause after the bare heights of the pass.

  5. 5. Sebes

    Sebeş is where the Transalpina comes down to earth, a town of about twenty-seven thousand people on the Sebeş River in Transylvania, and the northern end of the great mountain road. It belongs to the world of the Transylvanian Saxons, German settlers who were invited here in the twelfth century by the Hungarian kings to defend the frontier and develop the land. They built fortified churches and walled towns, and Sebeş, which they called Mühlbach, grew into one of the seven fortified cities that gave Transylvania its German name, Siebenbürgen.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Novaci to Ranca

Novaci → Ranca is 17 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Ranca to Urdele Pass

Ranca → Urdele Pass climbs about 2145 m in 11 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Urdele Pass to Obarsia Lotrului

Urdele Pass → Obarsia Lotrului drops about 2145 m over 9 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Obarsia Lotrului to Sebes

Obarsia Lotrului → Sebes is 73 km of getting across. Ride it tidily and keep the enthusiasm for what comes after.
