Why this ride

Out of Bédoin and back again: 43 km, roughly 42 min in the saddle. The stops in between: Mont Ventoux summit, Sault and Gorges de la Nesque. The highest point is about 1,900 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Mont Ventoux Loop

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 1909 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance — cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bedoin

    Bédoin is the village at the foot of Mont Ventoux, the white, bald summit that dominates Provence, and it is where the hardest and most famous approach starts. The mountain rises to 1,912 metres, and from far away it looks like it is covered in snow, though the whiteness is bare limestone scree. The ascent from Bédoin is about twenty-one kilometres, climbing through vineyards and cherry orchards and then through a dense forest of cedar and pine, before bursting out above the tree line at Chalet Reynard into a lunar landscape of pale stones, with a weather station and a radio mast on top. The Tour de France has crossed it many times, and the tiny memorial to the British cyclist Tom Simpson, who died near the summit in 1967, is a place of pilgrimage for cyclists.

  2. 2. Mont Ventoux summit

    Mont Ventoux rises alone from the plains of Provence, and the summit, at 1,909 metres, is a bald white dome of shattered limestone that looks from a distance like a snowfield even in July. Nothing else stands near it. This isolation is the key to its character, because the mistral, the cold northerly wind of the Rhône valley, hits it with full force, and the weather on top is often wild when the lowlands are warm and sunny. Gusts above a hundred kilometres per hour are not unusual, and the summit has a long record of cold, wind and fog.

  3. 3. Sault

    On a hot July afternoon, Sault is the best place in Provence to understand why people write poems about lavender. The village sits on a rocky spur at about 750 metres, on the edge of the great plateau that runs between Mont Ventoux and the Luberon hills, and in the weeks of the bloom, from late June to late July, the fields around it turn violet in long, curved lines up to the horizon. The smell is overwhelming on warm days, sweet, resinous and slightly medicinal, and bees fill the air. The villagers hold a lavender festival every year on 15 August, with processions, markets and music.

  4. 4. Gorges de la Nesque

    The Gorges de la Nesque is a road that cuts across the limestone rim of a canyon, with sheer cliffs on one side and a drop of several hundred metres on the other. The Nesque river, which flows from the Monts de Vaucluse, has carved a gorge about twenty kilometres long and as much as four hundred metres deep through the plateau, and the road, the D942, follows the cliffs high above the river, with a series of belvederes, short tunnels and long, clean bends. It runs between Villes-sur-Auzon, on the western side, and Sault, on the lavender plateau, and it is among the most beautiful drives in Provence.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Bedoin to Mont Ventoux summit

Bedoin → Mont Ventoux summit climbs about 1909 m in 12 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Mont Ventoux summit to Sault

Mont Ventoux summit → Sault drops about 1909 m over 17 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Sault to Gorges de la Nesque

Sault → Gorges de la Nesque is 9 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Gorges de la Nesque to Bedoin

Gorges de la Nesque → Bedoin is 15 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
