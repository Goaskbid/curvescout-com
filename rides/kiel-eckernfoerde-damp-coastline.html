Why this ride

Beach air, holiday traffic discipline and a better return line. The best part sits around Eckernförde bay, Damp coast road, Schwansen fields, while the most useful town stop keeps the day practical instead of turning it into a fuel or food hunt. Check the OSM map, Google preview and GPX checkpoint order before departure; they use the same waypoint sequence. Ride it with legal pull-offs, patience through villages and enough daylight to stop without rushing.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Eckernförde bay

    Eckernförde Bay is a long, narrow arm of the Baltic that cuts into the coast of Schleswig-Holstein between the Danish Wohld and the Schwansen peninsula, and the little port of the same name sits at its head. The water is shallow, the beaches are wide and sandy, and the whole place has the relaxed air of a north German seaside town, with a promenade, a harbour full of boats and a smell of smoked fish. The altitude here is a modest eight metres, and the horizon is all sea and sky.

  2. 2. Damp coast road

    Damp sits on the northern shore of Eckernförde Bay, on the Schwansen peninsula, where Schleswig-Holstein stops pretending to be anything other than flat, open and wet around the edges. The Baltic here is not the dramatic kind. It is shallow, low in salt, and in July it warms up faster than almost any sea a Central European rider is likely to meet, which is exactly why people come and why this stop is listed for a swim.

  3. 3. Schwansen fields

    Schwansen is a low, rolling peninsula in the north of Schleswig-Holstein, tucked between the Baltic inlet of Eckernförde Bay to the south and the long, narrow fjord of the Schlei to the north. The land was shaped by the ice sheets, which left behind a patchwork of gentle hills, hollows and fertile soil, and it is farmed with a care that shows. In May, vast fields of rapeseed turn the whole landscape a startling yellow under a pale northern sky, with the sea glinting on one side. In summer, wheat, barley and maize take over.
