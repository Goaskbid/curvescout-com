Why this ride

A 237 km round trip from Hagi - roughly 3 h 25 in the saddle. It goes by way of The Imperial Steel Works and back through The Imperial Steel Works. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hagi Proto-industrial Heritage/ Hagi Castl

    Hagi is a castle town on the Sea of Japan coast, in the far west of Honshu, and a place where a small, quiet town changed the country. It was the capital of the Choshu domain, and in the nineteenth century young samurai from here played a leading part in bringing down the Tokugawa shogunate and starting the Meiji era. Several of the figures who later led modern Japan, including Ito Hirobumi, the first prime minister, came out of this town's circle.

  2. 2. The Imperial Steel Works

    Japan's modern industry has a birthday, and in Kitakyushu they celebrate it with a rusting blast furnace. The Imperial Steel Works at Yawata began producing iron in 1901, a state-run enterprise financed with money from the war indemnity that China paid after the First Sino-Japanese War, and it was built to give a country that had just opened to the world its own supply of steel. German engineers advised on the early design. The works grew into the heart of the northern Kyushu industrial belt, and Yawata became a city that smelled of coke and smoke for most of a century.
