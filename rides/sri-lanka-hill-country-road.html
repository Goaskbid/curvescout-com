Why this ride

A one-way ride of 206 km, Kandy to Wellawaya - roughly 2 h 50 in the saddle. On the way it passes Nuwara Eliya, Horton Plains and Ella. The highest point is about 1,350 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kandy

    Kandy is the heart of Sri Lanka's hill country, a city built around an artificial lake in a bowl of green hills at about five hundred metres. For over two hundred years it was the capital of the last Sinhalese kingdom, which held out against the Portuguese, Dutch and finally the British, who took it in 1815. Its great treasure is the Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic, the Sri Dalada Maligawa, which houses what is believed to be a tooth of the Buddha. The temple draws pilgrims every day, with drumming and offerings at set times, and the whole city is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Around 110,000 people live here, so fuel, supplies and hotels are easy.

  2. 2. Nuwara Eliya

    Nuwara Eliya is the town where Sri Lanka's tea planters and colonial officials tried to recreate an English country town, and the result is delightfully peculiar. It sits at nearly nineteen hundred metres in the hill country, in a bowl of green slopes below the island's highest peak, Pidurutalagala, and the air is cool and often damp. The architecture is a mixture of Tudor-style cottages with roses in the gardens, a red-brick post office, a golf course, a racecourse and a gentlemen's club with trophy heads on the walls. It earned the nickname Little England, and the locals have never quite let it go. The town was established in the 1840s, when the explorer and administrator Samuel Baker set up a settlement here, and became the favourite summer retreat of the British who ran the island.

  3. 3. Horton Plains

    Horton Plains is a high, windswept plateau in the middle of Sri Lanka, and nothing in the tropical lowlands prepares you for it. At more than two thousand metres, it is a land of rolling grassland, scattered patches of twisted cloud forest, tumbling streams and cool mist, and the air feels more like Scotland than the tropics. The plains are a national park and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, part of the Central Highlands, and are home to species found nowhere else, including a number of endemic birds, orchids and the rhododendron that flowers in red along the paths. Sambar deer, the largest deer on the island, graze on the open grass, and leopards live in the forest, though they are rarely seen.

  4. 4. Ella

    Ella is a small town on the edge of a huge drop, and the whole place has arranged itself around the view. It sits at about a thousand metres in the hills of Sri Lanka's Uva province, where the gap between two mountain ridges opens onto the plains to the south, and on a clear morning you can look straight through it and see the land falling away in layers toward the coast. Cafes and guesthouses line the main street, and the town has a relaxed, international travellers' feel, with banana pancakes on the menu and a stream of backpackers coming off the train from Kandy.

  5. 5. Wellawaya

    Wellawaya is a junction town in the dry south-east of Sri Lanka, and its main claim to fame is that the road to the coast and the road to the hills part company here. Around ten thousand people live in the town, which sits on flat, scrubby land at the foot of the escarpment, with the Uva highlands rising behind. Ella and Haputale, both in the hills, are a short climb away, and the contrast is sharp: from cool tea terraces you drop through bends and waterfalls to a hot, flat plain dotted with palms, paddy and thorn scrub.
