Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kenmare

    Kenmare sits at the head of a long sea inlet, with mountains on both sides and the Kerry hills rolling away to the north, and it is one of the best-placed small towns in Ireland. It was laid out as a planned town in the late eighteenth century by the Lansdowne estate, and the streets still form a tidy shape around a central market place, with colourful shopfronts, craft shops and some of the best restaurants in the south-west. Because it stands where the Ring of Kerry meets the Beara Peninsula, it is a natural starting point for either.

  2. 2. Beara Peninsula Ring point 2

    The Healy Pass is one of those roads that looks impossible on a map and merely improbable once you are on it. It crosses the spine of the Beara peninsula at about three hundred and thirty metres, cutting through the Caha mountains from Cork into Kerry, and the road zigzags up the southern side in tight hairpins before sweeping across the pass and descending the other side with views of Bantry Bay on one side and the Kenmare River on the other. The county border runs along the top.

  3. 3. Beara Peninsula Ring point 3

    Castletownbere is the working heart of the Beara peninsula, a fishing town on a deep sheltered harbour, with a long main street of brightly painted shops and pubs and the fishing boats lined up along the quay. It is among the largest whitefish ports in Ireland, and the harbour is busy with trawlers, with the smell of diesel, fish and salt in the air. Across the water lies Bere Island, a low hump of land that shelters the harbour and has been a fortress for centuries.

  4. 4. Beara Peninsula Ring point 4

    Allihies is a village of painted houses at the western tip of the Beara peninsula, and it has an unlikely history. In the nineteenth century this remote corner was the centre of a copper mining boom, and Cornish miners came to work the mines under the Puxley family, bringing their skills, their Methodism and their names. The chimneys and engine houses of the old mines still stand on the hillside above the village, empty and photogenic in a bleak way, and the houses of the miners are painted in yellow, blue, pink and green.

  5. 5. Near Bantry

    Glengarriff is a small village at the head of a sheltered inlet of Bantry Bay, on the edge of the Beara Peninsula in the southwest of Ireland, surrounded by wooded hills, rocky slopes and islands. The climate here is unusually mild, warmed by the Gulf Stream and protected from the wind, and plants grow in Glengarriff that you would not expect so far north: palms, tree ferns, rhododendrons and other subtropical species, which thrive in the damp air.
