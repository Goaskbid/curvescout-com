Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Koyna Wildlife Sanctuary

    Koyna Wildlife Sanctuary covers a long, rugged stretch of the Western Ghats in the Satara district of Maharashtra, around the Koyna reservoir, which the locals call Shivsagar. The dam across the Koyna river was built in the 1950s and 60s to produce hydroelectric power, and the lake behind it now stretches for more than sixty kilometres through the hills, one of the largest in the state. In December 1967 an earthquake of magnitude 6.3 struck near the dam, killing nearly two hundred people, and it is one of the best-known examples of earthquakes thought to be triggered by a reservoir.

  2. 2. Panhala Fort

    Panhala is where one of the most famous escapes in Maratha history began. In 1660 Shivaji was besieged here by the forces of the Bijapur sultanate and slipped away on a rainy July night, heading for the fort of Vishalgad, while a loyal commander, Baji Prabhu Deshpande, held back the pursuers in a narrow pass and gave his life doing so. The story is told in every Maharashtrian school and the pass, Pavan Khind, is a place of pilgrimage. The fort that Shivaji escaped from is among the largest in the Deccan, a long ridge top surrounded by walls and bastions, with three great gateways and a wealth of ruined buildings.

  3. 3. Vijaydurg

    Vijaydurg is a sea fort on the Konkan coast of Maharashtra, built on a rocky headland where the Vaghotan river runs into the Arabian Sea, and the setting alone is worth the ride. The fort was an old hill-fort of the Shilahara king Bhoja before Shivaji took it in the seventeenth century, rebuilt it, and made it one of the main bases of the Maratha navy. Later Kanhoji Angre, the admiral who held the coast against Portuguese, British and Dutch ships, used it as a harbour for his fleet, and the walls, bastions and triple line of defences look out over water that his ships once patrolled. They say there was an underwater wall to wreck enemy vessels that came too close, a story told at the gate with some pride.
