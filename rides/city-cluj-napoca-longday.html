Why this ride

A 336 km round trip from Cluj-Napoca - roughly 5 h 48 in the saddle. It goes by way of Muncel - Muchia Poienii Lupului, Tihău - Grădiște, Tihău - Pe Grădiște and Guga - Vârful Țâglii. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cluj-Napoca

    Cluj-Napoca is the unofficial capital of Transylvania, a university city of more than three hundred thousand on the Someș river, and it wears its mixed history lightly. The Romans founded Napoca here, the Hungarian kings made it a rich town, and for centuries Saxon, Hungarian and Romanian communities lived together within its walls. The great Gothic church of Saint Michael dominates the main square, with a spire visible across the roofs, and a statue of King Matthias Corvinus, who was born in the city in 1443, stands in front of it. On the hill above stands the Cetățuia fortress, which gives a view over the old town and the hills beyond.

  2. 2. Muncel - Muchia Poienii Lupului

    The name is a mouthful, and a good one: the Ridge of the Wolf's Clearing, on the hill of Muncel, above the Someș valley north-east of Dej. Somewhere along this wooded crest the Romans kept a lookout on the northern border of Dacia, watching the valleys for movement from the free tribes beyond. Watchtowers like this one were the eyes of the frontier, signalling to the forts below, and the site is part of the Dacian frontier inscribed by UNESCO in 2024. On the ground, expect very little: perhaps a low mound, a ditch or scattered stones under the trees. It is a place for imagination and a walk rather than a monument.

  3. 3. Tihău - Grădiște

    The Someș river makes its way through the hills of north-western Romania between Cluj and Baia Mare, and near the small town of Jibou, in Sălaj county, it passes through a narrow valley guarded on its slopes by the remains of a Roman fort. The site, known locally as Grădiște, which simply means old fort, lies above the village of Tihău, close to Surduc and Gâlgău Almașului.

  4. 4. Tihău - Pe Grădiște

    Jibou is a small town on the Somes river, and it holds a surprise that has nothing to do with Romans. The Wesselenyi family, Hungarian nobles who owned much of this part of Transylvania, built a castle here in the eighteenth century, and its grounds became the town's botanical garden, which is still a garden today, with glasshouses, old trees and flower beds that bring visitors from far afield. It is a quiet, slightly faded estate, and it makes a pleasant hour. Tihau lies six kilometres away, on the same river plain, among low hills that rise toward the Meses range.

  5. 5. Guga - Vârful Țâglii

    Rugășești, Vad and Poiana Blenchii are the kind of villages most people drive past on the way to somewhere else, and that is precisely the charm of the hills that rise between them. The point marked as Guga, Vârful Țâglii, lies on this rolling country north of the Someș, roughly a dozen kilometres from Dej, in the band of land where Rome drew the northern edge of Dacia. A summit called Țâglii is the sort of place where a watchtower would have looked out across the Someș corridor toward the free tribes beyond, and the archaeological lists record a frontier site here.
