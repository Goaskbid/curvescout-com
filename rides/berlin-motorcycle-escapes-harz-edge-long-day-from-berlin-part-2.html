Why this ride

Out of Wittstock, one way: 402 km, roughly 7 h 18 in the saddle. On the way it passes near Myślibórz and Grunewald. It crosses from Germany into Poland - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Photo stop near Wittstock

    In October 1636, on the hills south of Wittstock, a Swedish army crushed the combined forces of the Holy Roman Emperor and Saxony in one of the bloodiest battles of the Thirty Years' War. Archaeologists later found a mass grave of soldiers on the battlefield, and a museum in the tower of the old bishop's castle in Wittstock tells the story of the war and its brutal impact on ordinary people. The town itself is enclosed by one of the most complete medieval brick walls in Germany, with towers and a gate.

  3. 3. Near Grunewald

    Berlin has a racetrack hidden in plain sight. The AVUS, which opened in 1921 through the Grunewald forest, was both one of the first motorways in the world and a racing circuit, where drivers and riders competed at astonishing speeds on its long straights and steep, banked north curve. Racing ended long ago and the banking is gone, but the road is still there, now part of the A115 motorway, and the old grandstand and timing tower survive by the roadside. Passing them on the way into the city is a nod to history.

  4. 4. Back to Harz Edge

    Baruth/Mark lies in a landscape that is flatter than most people picture when they think of Germany, a sandy basin south of Berlin in which glaciers and meltwater carved wide shallow valleys at the end of the last ice age. The valley is called the Baruther Urstromtal, and it is filled with pine woods, heath and slow ponds, with the low ridge of the Fläming rising to the south-west.
