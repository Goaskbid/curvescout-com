  1. 1. Rock Springs

    Rock Springs is a coal town that ended up on the world's busiest east-west railroad, and it has the odd mix of origins you would expect. The Union Pacific arrived in the late 1860s, the mines dug into the surrounding desert, and workers came from dozens of countries, which is why the town's old tales mention such a long list of nationalities. It also has a darker chapter: in 1885 white miners attacked their Chinese coworkers in a massacre that is remembered in local history, and the place does not hide from it.

  2. 2. Green River

    The river that gives this town its name is also the reason the town matters. John Wesley Powell, the one-armed Civil War veteran, launched his two famous expeditions down the Green and Colorado Rivers from here in 1869 and 1871, and a small island in the middle of town, Expedition Island, marks the spot where his boats slid into the current. It is worth a short walk across a footbridge, because the sight of this wide, green water in the middle of the high desert is a small shock.

  3. 3. Manila

    Manila is a very small town at a very big view. It sits on the Utah side of the state line, only a few hundred metres south of the Wyoming border, on the western rim of Flaming Gorge Reservoir, and if you blink on the highway you might miss it. A few hundred people live here, a handful of motels and cafés serve the visitors, and the real attraction lies just beyond the last building, where the land drops into a long blue arm of water between red cliffs.
