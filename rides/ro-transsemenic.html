  1. 1. Reşiţa

    Reșița is a steel town in a deep green valley, and it wears its industrial past with a kind of stubborn pride. The ironworks here were founded in the eighteenth century under the Habsburgs, when the Banat was part of their empire, and for two centuries the town made iron, steel, machinery and locomotives. The first steam locomotive built on what is now Romanian territory came out of the Reșița works in the 1870s, and an open-air museum in the town displays a line of old steam engines, a stop that even riders who never think about trains tend to enjoy.

  2. 2. Văliug

    Văliug is a small mountain village at the head of a chain of lakes on the upper Bârzava, the place where the valley above Reșița stops being industrial and becomes simply green. It lies in the Semenic Mountains of the Banat, with fewer than a thousand people, and it is surrounded by beech and spruce forest that covers the hills as far as the eye can see. The lakes, Văliug and the larger Gozna just upstream, were made by dams built to supply water and power to the steelworks below, and today they are popular with families from Reșița for swimming, fishing and picnics.

  3. 3. Slatina-Timiş

    Slatina-Timiș lies in the long corridor of the Timiș river, the natural route that runs south from Caransebeș toward the Danube at Orșova, with the Semenic Mountains to the west and the Țarcu and Godeanu ranges rising to the east. It is a working village of around three thousand people, spread along the main road and the river, with fields on the valley floor and forest on the slopes. Most people pass straight through on the E70, but for anyone riding the Transsemenic, this is where the mountain road comes down from the Semenic plateau to meet the valley.
