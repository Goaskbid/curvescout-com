    Preetz is a lake town in the green middle of Schleswig-Holstein, and its old town squeezes between the Schwentine river and a pair of small lakes. Kiel is just fifteen minutes away, but Preetz has its own pace.

  2. 2. Plön lake view

    The Great Lake of Plön is the biggest lake in Schleswig-Holstein, and from this spot at the edge of the town you can see how much water the last Ice Age left behind. The glaciers that retreated from here some twelve thousand years ago scraped out hollows, dumped hills of gravel and clay, and left behind a landscape of lakes and rolling ridges that locals call Holstein Switzerland. It is a bit of a joke to anyone who has seen the Alps, since the highest hills are barely above a hundred metres, but the name stuck, and the landscape has a gentle charm of its own.

  3. 3. Bosau road

    The Bosau road runs out onto a narrow peninsula in the Great Plön Lake, the largest lake in Schleswig-Holstein, and it is the sort of road where the surface of the water appears on both sides at once. This is the Holstein Switzerland, a name that exaggerates the hills but not the beauty: a landscape left behind by the glaciers of the last ice age, with rounded hills, hollows filled by lakes, and woods of beech that come down to the shore. The lake has islands, reedy bays and swans.

  5. 5. Rendsburg canal

    Rendsburg is a town made by a ditch, and what a ditch. The Kiel Canal, known in German as the Nord-Ostsee-Kanal, was opened in 1895 as the Kaiser-Wilhelm-Kanal, cutting across the base of the Danish peninsula so that ships need not sail all the way around Skagen. It is still the busiest artificial seaway in the world by number of ships, and standing on the bank at Rendsburg you can watch container vessels, tankers and ferries glide past at what looks like walking pace, a few metres from the road. A quiet fact makes it more striking: the surrounding land is so flat and low, only a few metres above sea level, that the ships seem to be sailing through meadows.

  6. 6. Friedrichstadt stop

    Friedrichstadt feels as though a piece of Holland has been dropped on the flat marshes of North Frisia. The town was founded in 1621 by Duke Friedrich III of Holstein-Gottorf, who invited Dutch Remonstrants, a persecuted Protestant group, to settle on the banks of the Treene, and they brought their architecture with them: canals lined with stepped-gable brick houses, bridges, trees and little quays. The result is a miniature Amsterdam, complete with canal boats, and it is a delight to walk.

  7. 7. Husum harbour

    Husum calls itself the grey town by the sea, a phrase taken from the poem by the writer Theodor Storm, who was born here in 1817 and who made the town and its misty coast famous in his novellas. The grey is more affectionate than gloomy: the sky over the North Sea can be a pale silver, and the old harbour in the centre, with its brick warehouses and shrimp cutters bobbing at the quay, looks its best under a soft, cloudy light.
