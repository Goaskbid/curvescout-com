Why this ride

A 182 km one-way ride from near Sonora, about 3 h of riding. On the way it passes Dardanelle and Sonora Pass. The highest point is about 2,950 m. Sonora Pass is open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Sonora Pass Road

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2933 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance — cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Rider note

Check the pass is open before you commit to it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sonora California

    Sonora is a gold-rush town, and it was founded by people from the Mexican state it is named for. In 1848, miners from Sonora arrived in the foothills of the Sierra Nevada and found gold in the creek beds, and the camp they made grew into one of the richest in the Mother Lode. The mix of cultures was rough, with Mexican, Chilean, Chinese, European and American miners in close quarters, and conflicts over claims led to rules that pushed foreign miners out. Today the town has about five thousand people, and a main street of brick buildings and Victorian houses, cafes and antique stores.

  2. 2. Dardanelle

    Dardanelle is a bend in the Stanislaus River and a name on the map, and it is the last place of any size before Highway 108 starts to climb in earnest. The name comes from the Dardanelles, a long line of dark volcanic cliffs on the ridge above, which early travellers thought resembled the straits of the same name in Turkey. The cliffs are the remains of mudflows from ancient volcanoes, and they give the upper canyon a rugged, castellated look that you notice as soon as the trees open up.

  3. 3. Sonora Pass

    Say Sonora Pass to a Californian rider and watch for the pause. At 2,933 metres it is the second highest road over the Sierra Nevada, after Tioga, and it has a reputation for its gradients: the stretches on the eastern side are rated at around twenty-six percent, which is the sort of number that makes you check your clutch cable. Lanes are narrow, guard rails are rare and the edges are dramatic. It is not the place to try your first mountain pass, but it is a wonderful one to graduate to.

  4. 4. Bridgeport

    Bridgeport is a small, sun-baked county seat in the eastern Sierra, at the edge of a high valley of sagebrush and meadow, ringed by snow-capped peaks. Fewer than six hundred people live here, at an elevation of about sixty-five hundred feet, and the main street along Highway 395 is a short row of cafes, motels, a store and a stately white courthouse. That courthouse, built in 1880 in a Victorian style with a cupola, is one of the oldest in California still in use, and gives the town a quiet dignity.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

Sonora California to Dardanelle

Sonora California → Dardanelle is 78 km of getting across. Ride it tidily and keep the enthusiasm for what comes after.

Dardanelle to Sonora Pass

Dardanelle → Sonora Pass climbs about 2933 m in 21 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Sonora Pass to Bridgeport

Sonora Pass → Bridgeport drops about 2933 m over 45 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
