Why this ride

From San Luis Obispo it runs 869 km to Dana Point; allow about 12 h 09 of riding. It goes by way of Santa Barbara and Malibu. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. San Luis Obispo

    San Luis Obispo is a small city of about forty-seven thousand people, halfway between Los Angeles and San Francisco, and for riders on the coast it is a natural place to rest. It lies in a valley of the Santa Lucia foothills, a short way inland from the Pacific, with hills of oak and grass rising on all sides. Franciscan friar Junipero Serra founded Mission San Luis Obispo de Tolosa here in 1772, and the mission church still stands on the central plaza, next to a creek that runs through the downtown.

  2. 2. Santa Barbara

    The Pacific Coast Highway has a way of ending in places that look like film sets, and Santa Barbara is the best of them. The city sits on a narrow shelf of land between the Santa Ynez Mountains and the ocean, facing south, which is unusual on the Californian coast, so the sun seems to hang over the water in the afternoon. The red tile roofs, white stucco walls and arched arcades are not an accident. After an earthquake in 1925 destroyed much of downtown, the city rebuilt in a unified Spanish Colonial Revival style, and the result is a place that feels more like Andalusia than Los Angeles.

  3. 3. Malibu

    Twenty-one miles of the Pacific Coast Highway pass through Malibu, and most of them are within sight of the sea. The road squeezes between the steep brown slopes of the Santa Monica Mountains and a narrow ribbon of sand, a mix of beach houses, small plazas, surf breaks and sudden gaps with a view over the water. The town itself has about thirteen thousand people, a city of film stars and surfers, and a strong feeling of being at the edge of something. It was once a ranch owned by the Rindge family, who held off development for decades, and the land was divided only in the 1920s, after the state forced a public road through it.

  4. 4. Dana Point

    Dana Point has a better claim to literary fame than most harbour towns in Orange County. In 1835 a young Harvard dropout named Richard Henry Dana Jr. spent months hauling cow hides on this stretch of the California coast as a common sailor, and he later wrote it up as Two Years Before the Mast. He described the bluff here as the only romantic spot on the coast, and the town took his name. Today the bluff holds a headland park and a harbour full of yachts, and a replica tall ship sits at the dock as a reminder of the age of hide and tallow.
