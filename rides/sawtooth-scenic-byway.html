Why this ride

A one-way ride of 353 km, Shoshone to the finish - roughly 4 h 47 in the saddle. The stops in between: Ketchum and Galena Summit. The highest point is about 2,650 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Ketchum → Galena Summit

The ground tilts up and stays tilted for 78 km. 2652 m of it, taken steadily rather than in one wall — a steady rhythm suits this better than bursts. The height arrives whether you hurry or not.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Shoshone Idaho

    Shoshone sits on the Snake River Plain in south-central Idaho, a flat, dry, lava-floored country of sagebrush, irrigated fields and enormous skies, and it is the county seat of Lincoln County, with about fifteen hundred people. Its stone buildings date from the days when the railroad arrived, in the 1880s, and the town was a junction where cattle and sheep were shipped. The courthouse and the old storefronts have a worn, dignified look, and the whole place has the unhurried air of a town that has seen a lot and seen it go by.

  2. 2. Ketchum

    Ketchum is the small town below the big ski mountain, at about 1,800 metres in the Wood River Valley of central Idaho, and it is best known for two things: skiing, and Ernest Hemingway. The writer first came in the late 1930s as a guest of the new Sun Valley resort, returned often, and settled here in his last years. He died in Ketchum in 1961 and is buried in the town cemetery, under a simple slab that visitors still leave pennies and bottles on. A memorial to him stands nearby in the woods beside Trail Creek.

  3. 3. Galena Summit

    Galena Summit is a broad, quiet notch in the Smoky Mountains at 2,652 metres, and it is the high point of the Sawtooth Scenic Byway, but the reason people stop is the view from the overlook just to the north. From a wide pull-out on the rim you look north across the Sawtooth Valley, a vast, flat-bottomed basin of sagebrush and meadow with the Salmon River winding through it, and beyond it the long, jagged wall of the Sawtooth Mountains, grey and white against the sky. It is one of those views that feels like a map suddenly turned into land.

  4. 4. Stanley Idaho

    Stanley is a tiny town of a hundred or so people at about sixty-two hundred feet in the Sawtooth Valley of central Idaho, and it is one of the best-situated villages in the American West. The jagged granite wall of the Sawtooth Range stands directly behind it, tall and sharp, with snow in most months, while the infant Salmon River, the River of No Return, runs past the edge of the town. There are log buildings, a few lodges and cafes, a rodeo ground, and a sky so clear that the whole range seems to hang in the air.
