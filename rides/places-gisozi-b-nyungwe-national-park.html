Why this ride

From Kigali it runs 350 km to Kigali; allow about 6 h 02 of riding. On the way it passes Nyamata, Memorial sites of the Genocide: Nyamata, Nyungwe National Park and back through Memorial sites of the Genocide: Nyamata. The highest point is about 2,200 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Gisozi B

    Gisozi is a hillside district of Kigali, and for most visitors it means the memorial. The Kigali Genocide Memorial, on a ridge in Gisozi, is the burial place of more than two hundred and fifty thousand victims of the 1994 genocide against the Tutsi, and it also houses a museum that documents the killing, its causes and its aftermath, with photographs, clothing and testimonies. It is sobering, honest and carefully made, and it is the most important stop for anyone who wants to understand modern Rwanda. Allow at least two hours and go with an open heart.

  2. 2. Nyamata

    The church at Nyamata is a plain brick building with a corrugated iron roof, and what it holds is the memory of one of the worst weeks of the twentieth century. In April 1994, during the genocide against the Tutsi in Rwanda, thousands of people from the surrounding villages took refuge in the church, as they had in earlier waves of violence, assuming that a house of God would protect them. It did not. Killers entered with grenades, machetes and clubs, and in the days that followed some ten thousand people died in and around the church. Today it is a memorial. The clothes of the victims lie in piles on the pews, the altar cloth is still stained, and in an underground vault the remains of tens of thousands from the district are kept in coffins and on shelves.

  3. 3. Memorial sites of the Genocide: Nyamata

    This is a place to arrive quietly. Both belong to the group of four genocide memorials, Nyamata, Murambi, Gisozi in Kigali and Bisesero, that UNESCO inscribed on the World Heritage list in 2023, recognising them as places of remembrance of the genocide against the Tutsi, in which some eight hundred thousand to a million people were murdered in about a hundred days.

  4. 4. Nyungwe National Park

    Nyungwe is one of the oldest rainforests in Africa, a mountain forest spread over the ridges of south-western Rwanda between Lake Kivu and the Burundi border. It is high, wet and green in a way that makes the rest of the world feel dry: tree ferns taller than a house, orchids on the branches, mist pouring through the valleys in the morning, and the sound of water everywhere. The ridges here form part of the great divide between the Congo and Nile basins, and Rwanda proudly claims one of the remotest sources of the Nile in these hills.
