  1. 1. Aiud

    Aiud has a split reputation in Romania. For historians it is an old Transylvanian market town on the Mureș with a fortified citadel at its heart and one of the most famous Protestant colleges of the region. For an older generation it means something much darker: the prison on the edge of town, where the communist regime held and broke thousands of political prisoners in the 1950s and 1960s. Both sides of that story are part of the place, and a memorial near the prison remembers the people who died there.

  2. 2. Bucium

    Bucium is a commune of scattered hamlets in the Golden Quadrilateral, the old gold-mining heart of the Apuseni, and the land around it has been dug, tunnelled and washed for metal for at least two thousand years. The villages cling to the slopes of deep valleys above Abrud, with small farms, orchards and haystacks among the forests, and the population has thinned out over the decades as the mines closed and younger people left. It is a quiet, rural corner, more remote in feel than its distance from the main road suggests.

  3. 3. Abrud

    Abrud is the old capital of the gold country, a small mining town on the river of the same name at the eastern foot of the Apuseni, and it has the faded grandeur of a place that once had more money than it does now. Its streets have churches of several faiths, Orthodox, Catholic, Reformed and Unitarian, built by the Hungarians, Germans and Romanians who came here for the mines, and some of the houses on the main square still show the ornate facades of the nineteenth century. Today it is a quiet town of around six thousand people, surrounded by green hills that hide the scars of centuries of mining.
