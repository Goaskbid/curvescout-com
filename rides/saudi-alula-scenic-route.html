Why this ride

56 km round from Al-`Ula - plan on about 54 min of riding. On the way it passes Elephant Rock, Hegra and Harrat Uwayrid. The highest point is about 1,150 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. AlUla

    AlUla lies in a broad sandstone basin in north-west Saudi Arabia, and the first thing you notice when you arrive is the shape of the rock. Cliffs of red and ochre stand straight up from a flat valley floor, carved by wind into mushrooms, arches and buttresses, and the oasis of date palms at their base looks almost unreal against the stone. The town is around thirty thousand people, with the old mudbrick quarter still standing on the hillside above the modern streets.

  2. 2. Elephant Rock

    Jabal AlFil rises out of the desert floor north of AlUla as a lone sandstone pillar, some fifty metres tall, balanced on a narrow neck of rock so that from the right angle it looks like an elephant lifting its trunk to the sky. Wind and sand have been working at it for millions of years, and the result is so convincing that people stop their cars in the middle of the road. It is the symbol of this part of Saudi Arabia, and sunset here is a ritual.

  3. 3. Hegra

    Hegra, also known as Mada'in Salih, was the southern capital of the Nabataeans, the same desert traders who carved Petra in Jordan, and it holds more than a hundred tombs cut into sandstone outcrops scattered across a flat plain. It was Saudi Arabia's first UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 2008, and the sight of the carved facades, standing alone among the rocks with the desert behind them, is a powerful one.

  4. 4. Harrat Uwayrid viewpoint

    The Harrat Uwayrid is a dark lava field, a tilted plateau of black basalt that rises along the western edge of the AlUla valley, and the viewpoint here looks down from its escarpment over the oasis and the sandstone country below. Harrats are the lava fields that spread across western Arabia, laid down by volcanic activity over millions of years, and their dark, rubbly surfaces contrast sharply with the pale and orange sandstone beneath. That colour contrast is the whole picture: a black lid on a rusty cake.
