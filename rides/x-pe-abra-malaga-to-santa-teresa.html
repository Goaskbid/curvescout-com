Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ollantaytambo

    Ollantaytambo is an Inca town in the Sacred Valley of Peru, about 2,800 metres above sea level, and unlike most Inca sites it is not a ruin: people live here in houses laid out on the original Inca street plan, and the streets, with their stone channels of running water, are some of the oldest continuously inhabited in South America. Above the town, a huge stepped terrace climbs the mountainside to a temple complex, built for the emperor Pachacuti in the fifteenth century, with enormous stones fitted so tightly that a knife blade will not pass between them.

  2. 2. Abra Malaga

    You cross Abra Málaga in one climate and descend in another. On the Urubamba side the valley is dry, dusty and Andean, with terraces, eucalyptus and the white shoulders of Nevado Verónica high above. Then the road tops out at 4,316 metres, a cold, windswept saddle of grass and rock, and plunges into the cloud forest on the eastern slope, where the air gets thick, wet and green and the temperature climbs a degree with every switchback. By the time you reach Santa María, a thousand-odd metres lower, you are in the edge of the Amazon basin, with orchids, bamboo, coffee bushes and mist hanging on every ridge.

  3. 3. Santa Teresa Peru

    Santa Teresa lies in the cloud forest on the eastern slopes of the Andes, in the valley of the Urubamba River, and it is one of the places where the mountains give way to the jungle. At about fifteen hundred metres, the air is warm and damp, the hillsides are covered with coffee, bananas and avocados, and the sound of water is everywhere. The village is small and rough around the edges, and it is best known as a back door to Machu Picchu, which lies over the ridge to the east, reached by walking along the railway from the hydroelectric station nearby.
