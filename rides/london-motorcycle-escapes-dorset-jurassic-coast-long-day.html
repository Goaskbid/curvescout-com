Why this ride

A proper escape from London motorcycle escapes with roads worth waking up for. A 633 km loop that starts and ends near Horsted Keynes, about 11 h 31 of riding. On the way it passes Scenic stretch near Reading, near Buckingham and in Thorpe le Soken. near Storrington is the high point. The line favours views over speed.

  4. 4. Photo stop near Buckingham

    Buckingham is a small, handsome market town in the north of the county that bears its name, wrapped in a loop of the river Great Ouse. Its centre is a pleasing jumble of Georgian brick, coaching inns and a castellated old gaol that now houses a little museum. The town also has the distinction of being home to the University of Buckingham, the first independent university in Britain, founded in the 1970s.

  6. 6. Richmond

    From the top of Richmond Hill, the Thames bends below you through meadows and trees, and on a clear day you can see far across the river valley towards Windsor. This view is so admired that it was protected by an Act of Parliament in 1902, the only view in England with that honour, and painters like Turner and Reynolds came here to capture it. It is a fine place to end a long day, looking back towards the west where you have been.

  7. 7. Back to Dorset

    The lanes around Horsted Keynes and Forest Row run along the edge of Ashdown Forest, the great sandy heath in East Sussex that A. A. Milne turned into the Hundred Acre Wood. He lived at nearby Hartfield, and his son Christopher Robin played here with the toys that became Winnie the Pooh. The forest itself is not a forest in the modern sense; the word meant a royal hunting ground, and the land is largely open heathland with gorse, heather, bracken and clumps of Scots pine, a rare habitat that is looked after by grazing. Nightjars and Dartford warblers live there, and in July the heather turns purple.
