  1. 1. Near Reykjavík

    Reykjavík is the only real city in Iceland, and for most riders it is where the Ring Road begins and ends. The capital spreads around a bay on the southwest coast, with colourful corrugated-iron houses, the tall concrete church of Hallgrímskirkja on its hill and the glass honeycomb of the Harpa concert hall by the harbour. Across the water rises the flat-topped mountain of Esja, often streaked with snow, and on a clear day the glacier of Snæfellsjökull can be seen far across the bay to the north.

  2. 2. Golden Circle point 2

    Þingvellir is the one place in Iceland where geology and history are inseparable. The national park sits in a rift valley, a wide, grassy trough bounded by cliffs, where the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates are slowly pulling apart. You can walk between the walls of the fissure and feel the gap, and in the lake beside it divers swim between continents.

  3. 3. Golden Circle point 3

    Geysir is the word that gave the world its geysers, and the original is still here, in the Haukadalur valley, a basin of steaming ground, blue pools and mineral-stained earth. The Great Geysir itself has been mostly dormant for many years, with only occasional activity, but its neighbour Strokkur obliges the crowds every few minutes, throwing a column of water high into the air with a deep gurgle and a sigh.

  4. 4. Golden Circle point 4

    Gullfoss is the waterfall that sums up Iceland: a huge volume of glacial water from the Hvítá river dropping in two steps into a narrow canyon, with spray hanging in the air and rainbows appearing on sunny days. The upper fall is wide and the lower one plunges into a crevice at right angles, so the water seems to vanish into the earth, and the noise is a constant, low roar that you feel in your chest.

  5. 5. Near Selfoss

    Kerið looks at first like a quarry someone forgot to fill in, until you reach the rim. Then the whole thing opens below you: an oval crater with steep walls of rust-red and black volcanic gravel, streaked with green moss, and at the bottom a small lake of astonishing colour, deep blue-green and perfectly still on a calm day. It is a volcanic crater, formed a few thousand years ago when a cone of scoria collapsed into its own emptied magma chamber, and the lake is simply groundwater filling the bottom.
