Why this ride

109 km from Alicante to Alicante, about 1 h 53 of riding. It goes by way of La Sarga- Abric I, La Sarga- Abric II, La Sarga- Abric III and Port de Penaguila- Abric II.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Alicante

    Alicante is a bright, easy city built on a rock. The Castillo de Santa Bárbara sits on top of Mount Benacantil and watches the harbour from about 170 metres up, and on a clear day the view runs all the way along the coast. The promenade below, the Explanada de España, is paved with a wave pattern in red, cream and black marble, with palms on both sides, and it is the natural place to start an evening: the whole town seems to be on that walk.

  2. 2. La Sarga- Abric I

    Alcoy, or Alcoi in Valencian, is a city of bridges, built where three rivers meet in deep ravines, in the mountains of northern Alicante. It was one of the cradles of Spain's industrial revolution, with textile and paper mills powered by its rivers, and its streets have fine modernist buildings from that era. It is also famous for its Moors and Christians festival in April, one of the most spectacular in Spain, with parades, music and mock battles in honour of Saint George. The rock shelters of La Sarga lie in the hills about eight kilometres from the city, toward Ibi.

  3. 3. La Sarga- Abric II

    La Sarga is a deep, rocky ravine that cuts into the hills north of Alcoi, and it is one of the most important rock art sites in eastern Spain. The paintings here include both the lively red figures of the Levantine tradition and the simpler schematic signs of later peoples, and some show deer, goats and human figures with striking detail. The site belongs to the Mediterranean Basin rock art that UNESCO listed in 1998, and it is carefully managed, so check access conditions before travelling. Abric II, one of the shelters, is part of the cluster.

  4. 4. La Sarga- Abric III

    Alcoy sits among mountains on every side, and every road out of it is a good one. To the north-west lies the Sierra de Mariola, a natural park of aromatic herbs, whose thyme, rosemary and mountain tea are used in the local liqueur. To the south is Aitana, and to the east the long ridge of the Serra de Penàguila. The Barranc de la Sarga, where the painted shelters lie, cuts through the hills a few kilometres from the town, and its rock art has been studied since the early twentieth century.

  5. 5. Port de Penaguila- Abric II

    The Port de Penàguila is a mountain pass in the interior of Alicante province, on the road between Alcoy and the Vall de Guadalest, where the hills of the Comtat rise toward the Serra d'Aitana, the highest range in the province. The village of Penàguila, below the pass, is a small, ancient place huddled beneath a castle on a rocky crest, and Benilloba, Cocentaina and Alcoy are all close. The second shelter of the Port de Penàguila is one of several painted sites in the area.
