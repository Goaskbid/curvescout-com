  1. 1. Meseșenii de Sus - Coasta Ciungii 1

    Poarta Meseșană, the Meseș Gate, is the narrow pass through the Meseș Mountains that has carried travellers between Transylvania and the Hungarian plain for two thousand years. The Romans knew it, and watched it. Not far north of Zalău, on the heights above the pass, the Roman town and fort of Porolissum guarded this entrance to the province of Dacia, and its amphitheatre, customs post and the remains of the frontier wall can still be seen on the hill at Moigrad. The ridge to the south, where Meseșenii de Sus lies, was part of the same defensive landscape, with watchtowers on the high points.

  2. 2. Tihău - Dealul Cucului

    Dealul Cucului means cuckoo hill, and the name fits the place: a low, wooded rise above the village of Tihău, close to the town of Jibou in the Someș valley of Sălaj county, where in spring the cuckoo's call echoes across the meadows. The point belongs to the network of archaeological sites along the Roman frontier of Dacia in the northwest of the province, near the confluence of the Almaș and Someș rivers, a strategic spot where valleys meet and roads cross. As with many of these sites, there may be little to see on the surface, but the location makes sense once you look at the map.

  3. 3. Dăbâceni - Coama Pietrar 1

    The Someș is the river of northern Transylvania, gathering its waters in the mountains and winding west through a broad, green valley towards Hungary. Around Rus and Ileanda, in the northeast of Sălaj county, the valley narrows between wooded hills, and the villages cling to the slopes above the river. Dăbâceni and the ridge called Coama Pietrar lie in this landscape, one of many recorded archaeological sites in the area, and like most of them it is more about the setting than about visible remains.

  4. 4. Dumbrăveni - Dealul Podului 2

    Above the valley of the Someș Mare in Bistrița-Năsăud county, the hill called Dealul Podului carries the faint remains of a Roman watch post, one of a string of small installations that kept an eye on the northern edge of the province of Dacia. Like many points on this frontier, inscribed as a World Heritage Site in 2024, it is less a monument than a viewpoint with a story, a place where soldiers once scanned the horizon for movement in the free lands to the north. Today the horizon holds forested ridges, orchards and villages strung along the river.

  5. 5. Zagra - Dealul Lupului

    Zagra is the last farming village before the land begins to climb in earnest toward the Rodna mountains, the highest range in the Eastern Carpathians north of the Prislop pass. Pietrosul Rodnei, at about 2,300 metres, is the summit, and the whole massif is protected as a national park and biosphere reserve, with glacial lakes, rocky ridges and alpine meadows that stay snowy into June. From the Dealul Lupului, the Wolf Hill, you look across a patchwork of hayfields toward those peaks, which look closer than they are.
