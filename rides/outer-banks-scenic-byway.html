Why this ride

From Nags Head it runs 304 km to the finish; allow about 4 h 10 of riding. The stops in between: Cape Hatteras Lighthouse, Hatteras Village and Ocracoke. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Whalebone Junction

    Whalebone Junction takes its name from a whale skeleton that once stood here, and it is not much to look at now: an intersection and a few shops on a narrow strip of sand between the Atlantic and Roanoke Sound. But it matters, because this is the point where the roads from the mainland meet NC 12, the highway that runs down the Outer Banks, and from here you head south into Cape Hatteras National Seashore, with ocean on one hand and sound on the other.

  2. 2. Cape Hatteras Lighthouse

    Cape Hatteras Lighthouse is the tallest brick lighthouse in the United States, 198 feet of black and white spiral stripes that look like a barber's pole drawn by someone with ambition. It was finished in 1870 to warn ships off the shoals that stretch far out from the cape. This is the Graveyard of the Atlantic, where the Gulf Stream and the Labrador Current meet and the shoals have claimed hundreds of vessels. The lighthouse is the answer that the age of sail gave to a very practical problem.

  3. 3. Hatteras Village

    Hatteras Village sits at the very southern end of Hatteras Island, where NC 12 runs out of land and the road continues only by boat. It is a fishing community first, with charter boats at the docks, tackle shops, small restaurants, and the sort of quiet streets where locals nod from their porches. The island has lived off the sea for centuries, from early settlers who salvaged wrecks and fished to the modern sportfishing fleet, and the museum nearby tells the story of the Graveyard of the Atlantic, where shifting shoals and storms wrecked countless ships.

  4. 4. Ocracoke

    Ocracoke is only reachable by water, and that single fact shapes everything about the place. The island is a long sliver of sand at the bottom of the Outer Banks, with one village, one main road, and an atmosphere of being slightly cut off from the rest of the country. Most visitors arrive by the free ferry from Hatteras, a crossing of roughly an hour, and the arrival is a small event: the boat swings into the harbour, the brown pelicans watch, and the village appears with its white-painted houses and a little lighthouse.

  5. 5. Cedar Island

    Cedar Island is the end of the road, in a way that is surprisingly pleasant. It is a quiet, flat, marshy tongue of land on the far eastern edge of the North Carolina mainland, at the end of a long road through pine woods and salt marsh, and it faces the Pamlico Sound. Here the highway meets the ferry terminal, where boats leave for Ocracoke island, and the whole place feels like a pause between two worlds.
