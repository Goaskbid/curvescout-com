Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Nan

    Nan is a small, calm provincial capital in a mountain-ringed valley in the far north of Thailand, close to the Laos border, and it has stayed quieter than Chiang Mai or Chiang Rai. The Nan river runs through the plain, and the old kingdom here was a separate Lanna state with its own princes until the early twentieth century. The temples keep the northern style, with steep roofs, carved gables and gilded flourishes, and the town has the unhurried air of a place that visitors are only beginning to find.

  2. 2. Pua

    Pua lies in a broad valley in the far north of Thailand, about sixty kilometres from the Laotian border, and in the rainy season the whole basin turns a vivid, almost electric green. The rice fields stretch up to the foothills, with farmhouses on stilts and buffalo grazing along the dykes, and at harvest in November they turn gold. Pua is the market town of the district, and the people include Thai Lue, Hmong and Mien, whose silver jewellery, indigo cloth and embroidered tunics fill the stalls on market days. Chiang Klang, the bigger town fourteen kilometres on, supplies most of the district's fuel and shops.

  3. 3. Near Bo Kluea

    High in the Luang Prabang Range, on the road through Doi Phu Kha National Park, the air at around 1,715 metres is cool enough to need a jacket even in the hot season, and in the cool months of December and January it can be properly cold at dawn. The road here runs along ridges and through montane forest of oaks, chestnuts and tall trees draped in moss and orchids, with views across rolling blue ranges towards Laos, only a few dozen kilometres to the east. It is one of the highest and most remote roads in northern Thailand.

  4. 4. Bo Kluea

    Bo Kluea means salt well, and the name is the whole story. In a narrow mountain valley close to the Laotian border, ancient springs push up brine from deep underground, and for centuries villagers have boiled it down in shallow pans over wood fires to make salt. They still do. The salt was once traded far into the hills and across the river to Laos, and the village was an important stop on the caravan routes. Today the brine wells sit beside the road, surrounded by stalls selling the grey-white crystals, along with local honey, dried mushrooms and woven cloth. Chiang Klang is the nearest large town, thirty-five kilometres back.

  5. 5. Near Santi Suk

    North-east of Nan city, the hills begin in earnest, and the district of Santi Suk is where the flat farmland of the Nan valley gives way to the forested ridges that run towards Bo Kluea and the Lao border. The country here is a patchwork of rice paddies in the valley bottoms, corn fields on the slopes, and patches of forest on the ridges, with small villages of wooden houses on stilts. The landscape is green in the rainy season and brown and smoky in the dry months, when farmers burn their fields.
