Why this ride

From Pego it runs 273 km to Pego; allow about 4 h 42 of riding. It goes by way of Port de Penaguila- Abric II, Abric de la Creu, Barranc del Salt- Abric l and Abric de la monja. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc de l'Infern Conj. 3

    Hikers in the Marina Alta speak of the Barranc de l'Infern with a mix of affection and fear. This dramatic gorge between Vall de Ebo and Fleix carries a path of thousands of stone steps built by the Moriscos, the converted Muslims who farmed these valleys until their expulsion from Spain in 1609. Rock shelters along the ravine hold prehistoric paintings, some of which belong to the UNESCO listing of Mediterranean rock art, and the cliffs themselves are spectacular, with layers of grey and orange limestone glowing in the late afternoon sun.

  2. 2. Port de Penaguila- Abric II

    The Port de Penàguila is a mountain pass in the interior of Alicante province, on the road between Alcoy and the Vall de Guadalest, where the hills of the Comtat rise toward the Serra d'Aitana, the highest range in the province. The village of Penàguila, below the pass, is a small, ancient place huddled beneath a castle on a rocky crest, and Benilloba, Cocentaina and Alcoy are all close. The second shelter of the Port de Penàguila is one of several painted sites in the area.

  3. 3. Abric de la Creu

    Ontinyent and Bocairent sit close together in the southern part of Valencia province, in the upper valley of the Clariano river, surrounded by the mountains of the Serra de Mariola and the Serra Grossa. Ontinyent is a textile town with a fine old quarter on a hill above the river, crowned by the tall bell tower of the church of Santa Maria. Bocairent, a few kilometres away, is a medieval village perched on a rocky outcrop above a ravine. The Abric de la Creu, the shelter of the cross, lies in the hills between the two.

  4. 4. Barranc del Salt- Abric l

    Benilloba is a tiny village in the Comtat, the mountainous district north of Alcoy in the province of Alicante, with fewer than a thousand people, a church and a cluster of stone houses among olive and almond groves. The Barranc del Salt, the waterfall ravine, cuts through the hills nearby, and its first shelter is one of the many painted sites in the region that are part of the UNESCO listing of Mediterranean rock art. The Comtat and the neighbouring Alcoià are particularly rich in this heritage, with all three main styles of prehistoric painting represented: Levantine, schematic and the older Macro-schematic, unique to this small area, with large, enigmatic figures.

  5. 5. Abric de la monja

    Ontinyent sits in the Vall d'Albaida, in the south of Valencia, a town of about thirty-eight thousand people with a long textile tradition that goes back to wool, blankets and, more recently, home textiles. The Clariano river runs through it, and the hills around are wrinkled limestone, covered in pine, holm oak and wild herbs. In the shelter of those hills, small rock overhangs, called abrics here, carry paintings made by prehistoric people, and the Abric de la Monja is one of them, part of the Mediterranean Basin rock art that UNESCO listed in 1998.
