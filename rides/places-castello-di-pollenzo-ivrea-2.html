Why this ride

From Bra it runs 247 km to Bra; allow about 4 h 16 of riding. On the way it passes Palazzo Carignano, Ivrea and back through Palazzo Carignano. The highest point is about 1,150 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Castello di Pollenzo

    The village of Pollenzo was a Roman town, Pollentia, long before it was a Savoy estate, and in 402 a Roman army under Stilicho fought Alaric's Visigoths nearby in one of the last great battles of the Western Empire. Today the name means food. The castle and the neo-Gothic agricultural complex built around it by King Carlo Alberto in the nineteenth century, the Agenzia, now house the University of Gastronomic Sciences, founded by the Slow Food movement, which was born in nearby Bra. The estate is part of the UNESCO-listed Residences of the Royal House of Savoy.

  2. 2. Palazzo Carignano

    Turin has a palace whose front is bent like a sail, and it is the sort of building that makes architecture students go quiet. Palazzo Carignano was designed by Guarino Guarini, a priest, mathematician and architect, and completed in the late seventeenth century. Its brick facade swells outward in a great curve, with decoration pressed into the clay in patterns, and the building seems to breathe. Guarini was one of the great figures of the baroque, and his work in Turin, including the chapel that holds the Holy Shroud, is some of the most daring in Italy.

  3. 3. Ivrea

    Ivrea is the town Olivetti built, or at least rebuilt. In the twentieth century the Olivetti company, famous for typewriters, calculators and some of the first personal computers, turned this small Piedmontese town into a showcase of what industry could be: factories with glass walls and natural light, worker housing, libraries, nurseries and social services designed by the best architects of the day. Adriano Olivetti believed a company should serve its community, and the result was listed by UNESCO in 2018 as an industrial city of the twentieth century. Walk along Corso Jervis and you can see the buildings still standing, some reused, some waiting.
