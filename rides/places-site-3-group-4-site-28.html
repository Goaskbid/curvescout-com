Why this ride

A 122 km round trip from Muang Phônsavan - roughly 2 h 06 in the saddle. It goes by way of Site 21, Site 28 and back through Site 21. The highest point is about 1,250 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Site 3 - Group 4

    Group 4 is one of the quieter parts of Site 3, and what makes it worth the walk is its position. The jars here sit on a slight rise with open views over the valley, so that on a clear day you can see across the rice fields and low hills to the line of the horizon. It feels more private than other sites on the plain, with fewer visitors and less sense of being on a trail. The jars are scattered across the grass in an irregular pattern, and a couple are heavily tilted or cracked, which gives the place a worn, old feeling.

  2. 2. Site 21

    The Plain of Jars is one of Southeast Asia's great riddles: across the Xieng Khouang plateau in northern Laos, thousands of huge stone jars lie scattered over hillsides, in groups of a few or a few hundred, some taller than a person. They were carved from sandstone and other rock during the Iron Age, roughly between 500 BC and AD 500, and the most widely accepted theory is that they were linked to funerary practices, perhaps used to let bodies decompose before final burial. Local legend has a happier explanation: the jars were made to brew rice wine for a victory celebration of a mythical king. The sites were added to the World Heritage list in 2019, and Site 21 is one of the many numbered groups in the countryside around Phonsavan.
