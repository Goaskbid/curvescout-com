Why this ride

From Braşov it runs 345 km to Braşov; allow about 5 h 57 of riding. On the way it passes Reci - Cetate, Boroșneu Mare - Pe Dealul Cetății, Codrul secular Șinca and Moieciu - Drumul Carului. The highest point is about 1,300 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Brasov

    Braşov is the tidiest of the Saxon cities of Transylvania, a medieval town of red roofs and colourful baroque façades, ringed by wooded mountains and watched over by the bare rock of Tâmpa. The German settlers who built it in the thirteenth century called it Kronstadt, and it grew rich as a trading post at the crossing of routes between the Danube plains and Central Europe. The Black Church, the largest Gothic church between Vienna and Istanbul, dominates the old centre, and got its name after a great fire in 1689 blackened the walls. It houses an enormous organ and a collection of Anatolian carpets, given by merchants who traded in the East.

  2. 2. Reci - Cetate

    Reci lies on the flat floor of the Târgu Secuiesc basin, in Covasna county, where the Olt river meanders between mountains in the heart of the Szekler Land, the Hungarian-speaking region of eastern Transylvania. The site known as Reci - Cetate, the fortress, belongs to the eastern line of the Roman frontier in Dacia, part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire inscribed in 2024. The Romans guarded the passes through the Carpathians with forts and towers here, though today the site is little more than a raised area in the fields.

  3. 3. Boroșneu Mare - Pe Dealul Cetății

    Southeast of Sfântu Gheorghe, in Covasna county, the plain of the Râul Negru, the Black River, opens out between the mountains, a broad basin of fields, meadows and villages enclosed by the forested ridges of the Eastern Carpathians. Boroșneu Mare is one of the larger villages in this basin, a Székely community with a church and a cluster of farms, and the hill above it, Dealul Cetății, the Fortress Hill, takes its name from old earthworks or a fortification recorded there. It is one of many such sites in the area, more a landmark than a monument.

  4. 4. Codrul secular Șinca

    Codru is an old Romanian word for a deep forest, and the Codrul Secular Șinca, the ancient forest of Șinca, is exactly that: a stand of old-growth beech and fir on the northern slopes of the Făgăraș mountains in Brașov county, where trees have grown, died and fallen for centuries without being cut. It is part of the UNESCO listing of the ancient and primeval beech forests of Europe, a chain of protected forests across many countries that together tell the story of how beech spread across the continent after the last ice age. Walking among trees that are hundreds of years old, with fallen trunks covered in moss and fungi and the canopy closing high above, is a humbling experience.

  5. 5. Moieciu - Drumul Carului

    Between Bran and Rucăr, the road climbs over the saddle that separates the Bucegi mountains from Piatra Craiului, through a landscape of high meadows, hay barns, scattered farms and limestone peaks. The name Drumul Carului, the cart road, recalls the old route across this pass, which has linked Transylvania and Wallachia for centuries, and archaeologists have recorded a Roman-era site here, part of the network of frontier and road posts in Dacia. Fundata, at the top, is one of the highest villages in Romania, at around 1,350 metres, and the scattered houses of Moieciu, Peștera and Șirnea spread across the hills around it.
