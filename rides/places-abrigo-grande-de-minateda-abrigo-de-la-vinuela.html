Why this ride

322 km from Hellín to Hellín, about 5 h 17 of riding. It goes by way of Los Rumies, Cueva de las Conchas, Fuensanta II and Abrigo de la Viñuela. It tops out around 1,400 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Abrigo Grande de minateda

    Start here.

    The Abrigo Grande de Minateda is the big one, the name says so, and for once the name is accurate. It is among the most important rock art shelters in the south-east of Spain, a wide overhang in the soft rock outside Hellín in Albacete, where layers of painting from different periods sit one on top of the other. Hunters, deer, goats and strange, elongated human figures were drawn here over thousands of years, and the site is covered by World Heritage protection. The country around it is dry, broken and beautiful, a land of badlands, gullies and flat-topped hills that looks almost like another continent.

    Read the complete Abrigo Grande de minateda write-up.

  2. 2. Los Rumíes

    44 km in.

    The name Los Rumíes looks like the Arabic word rumi, which was once used across the Muslim world for Romans, then for Christians, and the Spanish landscape is full of names like it, left behind by eight centuries of Andalusi presence. The place lies in the dry hills north of the Segura valley, near Calasparra, and the rides that visit it also pass through the wider district of the Abrigo Grande de Minateda, one of the most famous painted shelters in the whole Levantine tradition. The rock art of this corner of Spain is substantial and varied.

    Read the complete Los Rumies write-up.

  3. 3. Cueva de las Conchas

    81 km in.

    Cehegín's old town rises in a tight cluster of ochre houses and church towers on a hill above the Argos river, in the northwest of Murcia, and it is one of the best-preserved historic centres in the region, with narrow streets, noble houses and churches dating back to the sixteenth century. The Cueva de las Conchas, the cave of the shells, lies less than a kilometre from the town, and it is part of the rich network of prehistoric sites in this area, included in the UNESCO listing of Mediterranean rock art.

    Read the complete Cueva de las Conchas write-up.

  4. 4. Fuensanta II

    119 km in.

    Fuensanta II is the sort of name only an archaeologist could love, a numbered rock shelter in the hills of northwest Murcia where prehistoric painters left a handful of figures on the stone. It is one of the hundreds of sites grouped under UNESCO's listing of rock art in the Mediterranean Basin of the Iberian Peninsula, and the Moratalla area is unusually rich in them. The paintings are small, faint to the untrained eye and protected, so the real value of coming is to understand the landscape that drew people here thousands of years ago: springs, shelter and game in rough, broken country.

    Read the complete Fuensanta II write-up.

  5. 5. Abrigo de la Viñuela

    159 km in.

    Segura de la Sierra is a village that looks like a stage set, and it is real. It sits on a conical hill above the olive groves of the upper Guadalimar valley, crowned by a castle, with narrow lanes of whitewashed houses winding up to the walls, and from the top the view takes in miles of olives, pine-covered ridges and distant sierras. The castle was a stronghold of the Order of Santiago, the knightly order that held much of this frontier in the Middle Ages. It has a Moorish bath house, restored and open to the public, a bullring built into the rock, and a medieval keep.

    Read the complete Abrigo de la Viñuela write-up.
