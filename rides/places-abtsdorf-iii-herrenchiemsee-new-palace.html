Why this ride

From Attersee it runs 252 km to Attersee; allow about 3 h 25 of riding. On the way it passes Historic Centre of the City of Salzburg, Herrenchiemsee New Palace and back through Historic Centre of the City of Salzburg. It tops out around 1,000 m. It crosses from Austria into Germany - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Salkammergut Lakes Road

Historic Centre of the City of Salzburg is 50 km from Abtsdorf III on West Autobahn. A proper up-and-over: 340 m of climbing, 670 m at the top, 350 m down. Watch for sand and spray near the water.

The stops

  1. 1. Abtsdorf III

    Start here.

    The Attersee is the biggest lake in the Salzkammergut, and Abtsdorf sits on its western shore, a settlement so small the label can get away with a Roman numeral. In this case the III means a third spot among a cluster of lake access points, so what you are really visiting is the shoreline between Attersee village and Weyregg, where the water is startlingly clear and the mountains stand up behind the opposite bank.

    Read the complete Abtsdorf III write-up.

  2. 2. Historic Centre of the City of Salzburg

    50 km in.

    Salzburg's old town is a stage set that happens to be real. Squeezed between the river Salzach and the steep rock of the Mönchsberg, it packs baroque domes, church towers and tall merchants' houses into a few dense streets, while the white walls of Hohensalzburg fortress sit high above everything like a lid on a jewellery box. The city has around a hundred and fifty thousand people, yet the centre still feels small enough to cross on foot in twenty minutes.

    Read the complete Historic Centre of the City of Salzburg write-up.

  3. 3. Herrenchiemsee New Palace

    123 km in.

    King Ludwig II wanted to build Versailles on an island, and for the most part he did. The New Palace on Herrenchiemsee, the largest island in the Chiemsee, is his answer to Louis XIV, a Bavarian homage to the French sun king that took years to build and was never finished. Ludwig spent barely ten days there in total. The money ran out, the king's reputation among his ministers fell, and in 1886 he was dead in Lake Starnberg under circumstances people still argue about.

    Read the complete Herrenchiemsee New Palace write-up.
