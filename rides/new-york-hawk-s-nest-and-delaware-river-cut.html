Why this ride

Cliffside sweepers, river road and a proper day away from Manhattan. 236 km round from Middletown - plan on about 4 h 03 of riding. The stops in between: Port Jervis, Hawk’s Nest cliff road and Delaware River view. It keeps to quiet secondary roads.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Middletown fuel gate

    Onions are the quiet fame of Orange County, New York, and the fields lie just north of Middletown. The black dirt region, a flat basin of ancient lakebed soil dark as coffee grounds, was drained in the nineteenth century and turned into some of the richest farmland in the Northeast, growing onions, lettuce, corn and sweet potatoes, and in autumn the roads are lined with trucks loaded with sacks. Middletown itself, a small city of about twenty-eight thousand, grew up as a railway junction and a manufacturing town, and its old brick downtown has a faded but friendly feel.

  2. 2. Port Jervis food stop

    Port Jervis is a river town at the point where three states meet, and the meeting is the first thing the place wants to tell you about. At a spot where the Neversink River runs into the Delaware, New York, New Jersey and Pennsylvania come together, and a stone monument, set up in the 1880s, marks the point. The town itself lies on the New York side, with the pale hills of the Kittatinny Ridge in New Jersey rising to the south and the Pennsylvania bank across the water at Matamoras. About nine thousand people live here, and there is all the fuel, coffee and breakfast any rider could ask for.

  3. 3. Hawk’s Nest cliff road

    Route 97 between Port Jervis and Barryville is one of those roads that every rider in the north-east seems to know, and the section called Hawk's Nest is the reason. The road was cut into the face of the cliff high above the Delaware River in the 1930s, and for a few kilometres it clings to the rock, with the rock wall on one side and a stone parapet on the other, and the river a long way below. The curves are tight and sinuous, the views through the trees are wonderful, and the whole stretch has the air of a miniature Alpine road. There is a small pull-off at the overlook, where you can park and look down at the river and the forested hills beyond.

  4. 4. Delaware River view

    The Delaware is one of the quiet success stories of American rivers. From its sources in the Catskills it runs for hundreds of miles to the sea at Delaware Bay, and for most of that length it is free-flowing, without a major dam on its main stem, which makes it the longest undammed river east of the Mississippi. The stretch here, part of the Upper Delaware Scenic and Recreational River, a unit of the national park system, runs through a forested valley of low ridges and small farms, with the river broad and green in summer and grey and icy in winter. The pull-off here gives a view down onto the water, with a bridge or two and the hills beyond.
