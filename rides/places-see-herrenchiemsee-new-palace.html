Why this ride

227 km from Innerschwand to Innerschwand, about 3 h 15 of riding. The stops in between: Historic Centre of the City of Salzburg, Herrenchiemsee New Palace and back through Historic Centre of the City of Salzburg. It tops out around 1,050 m. It crosses from Austria into Germany - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Salkammergut Lakes Road

42 km from See to Historic Centre of the City of Salzburg on West Autobahn. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 425 and 626 m. Water alongside for much of it - pick one good pull-out and stop.

The stops

  1. 1. See

    Start here.

    At the southeastern tip of the Mondsee, where the lake narrows toward the Attersee and the cliffs of the Drachenwand and the Schafberg crowd in on both sides, lies a hamlet with the shortest name you will find on any map: See. Just offshore, in the shallow water where the lake drains, divers found the remains of a prehistoric pile-dwelling settlement, and the finds were so characteristic that archaeologists named a whole Neolithic culture after the lake: the Mondsee culture, known for its decorated pottery and early copper working. The site is part of the UNESCO listing of Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps, and as with most of them, there is nothing to see except the water, which is part of the point.

    Read the complete See write-up.

  2. 2. Historic Centre of the City of Salzburg

    42 km in.

    Salzburg's old town is a stage set that happens to be real. Squeezed between the river Salzach and the steep rock of the Mönchsberg, it packs baroque domes, church towers and tall merchants' houses into a few dense streets, while the white walls of Hohensalzburg fortress sit high above everything like a lid on a jewellery box. The city has around a hundred and fifty thousand people, yet the centre still feels small enough to cross on foot in twenty minutes.

    Read the complete Historic Centre of the City of Salzburg write-up.

  3. 3. Herrenchiemsee New Palace

    114 km in.

    King Ludwig II wanted to build Versailles on an island, and for the most part he did. The New Palace on Herrenchiemsee, the largest island in the Chiemsee, is his answer to Louis XIV, a Bavarian homage to the French sun king that took years to build and was never finished. Ludwig spent barely ten days there in total. The money ran out, the king's reputation among his ministers fell, and in 1886 he was dead in Lake Starnberg under circumstances people still argue about.
