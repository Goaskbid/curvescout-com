Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. De Rust

    De Rust means the rest, and the village earns the name by being a pleasant place to stop before or after a day in the Swartberg. It lies in the Little Karoo between Oudtshoorn and the mouth of the Meiringspoort gorge, a small settlement of about three and a half thousand people, with a single main street of low whitewashed houses, shady trees and gardens, and the long wall of the Swartberg mountains standing dark to the north. The land around is dry, with farms along the river and fields of lucerne laid out like green patches on the red earth.

  2. 2. Meiringspoort

    Few roads in South Africa cross the same river as many times as this one. Meiringspoort is a gorge cut by the Groot River through the Swartberg range, and the N12 follows it for about twenty-five kilometres, crossing the stream some twenty-five times on low bridges and causeways, with cliffs that rise on both sides in tilted, twisted layers of red and grey rock. The road was built in the 1880s by Thomas Bain, and the gorge takes its name from a farmer called Petrus Meiring, who lived in the area in the early nineteenth century.

  3. 3. Prince Albert

    Prince Albert sits at the foot of the Swartberg mountains on the edge of the Great Karoo, a small, whitewashed town with Cape Dutch gables, irrigation furrows running down the main street, and an air of having been forgotten on purpose. Water from the mountains is the secret. It runs through the town in channels, feeds orchards and olive groves, and turns a patch of desert into a place that grows apricots, figs, grapes and olives on a surprising scale. Nobody comes here for speed, and the town is very good at that.
