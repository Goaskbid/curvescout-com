Why this ride

A 309 km round trip from Hwanam - roughly 5 h 20 in the saddle. The stops in between: Historic Villages of Korea: Hahoe and Yang, Buseoksa Temple and back through Historic Villages of Korea: Hahoe and Yang. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Beopjusa Temple

    Look up from the car park at Beopjusa and you see an enormous bronze Buddha, thirty-three metres tall, standing in the open air against a backdrop of granite peaks. He is a modern figure, finished at the turn of the millennium, but the temple he guards is very old: tradition says the monk Uisin founded it in 553, bringing Indian scriptures back to Korea in a donkey's pack. Its main attraction, Palsangjeon, is a five-storey wooden pagoda with eight panels depicting scenes from the Buddha's life, rebuilt in the 1960s on an older design, and one of the few such wooden pagodas in Korea.

  2. 2. Historic Villages of Korea: Hahoe and Yang

    The Nakdong river makes a wide loop around Hahoe, almost turning it into an island, and inside that loop a village of tiled-roof mansions and thatched cottages has stood for some six centuries. Hahoe is the ancestral home of the Pungsan Ryu clan, a lineage of scholar-officials during the Joseon dynasty, and its layout follows Korean principles of geomancy, with houses arranged to face the river and the cliffs of Buyongdae across the water. It was added to the World Heritage list in 2010, together with Yangdong village near Gyeongju, as the finest surviving examples of clan villages of the Joseon period.

  3. 3. Buseoksa Temple

    The name Buseoksa means temple of the floating stone, and the legend behind it involves a monk, a princess and a dragon. When the monk Uisang founded the temple in 676, after returning from studies in China, a young woman named Seonmyo who had fallen in love with him there is said to have followed him in the form of a dragon, and when local bandits opposed the new temple she lifted a huge rock into the air to frighten them off. A large boulder behind the main hall is still pointed out as the floating stone, and you will be told that a thread can be passed beneath it.
