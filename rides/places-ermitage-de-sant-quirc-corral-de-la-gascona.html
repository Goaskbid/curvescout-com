Why this ride

A 566 km round trip from Bonansa - roughly 10 h 49 in the saddle. The stops in between: Cueva de Revilla, Cueva Peña Miel I, El Remosillo and Corral de la Gascona. It tops out around 1,950 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

The roads

Pyrenees N260 Route

Cueva de Revilla to Cueva Peña Miel I: 55 km, mostly on A-2205. Downhill for the most part, 520 m in all, never above 1,121 m. This is the bit the ride is built around.

The stops

  1. 1. Ermitage de Sant Quirc

    Start here.

    Durro is a village of about a hundred people and a church tower too tall for its size, which is typical of the Vall de Boí. The hermitage of Sant Quirc stands on a rise just outside the village, a plain Romanesque building from the same period as the great churches of the valley, and it looks out over the Noguera de Tor and its steep, fir-covered sides. The Nativity church in the village below shares a map with it, so the two work well together as a short walk.

    Read the complete Ermitage de Sant Quirc write-up.

  2. 2. Cueva de Revilla

    115 km in.

    Revilla is one of the most spectacularly placed hamlets in the Spanish Pyrenees, perched on a ledge above the Garganta de Escuaín, a deep gorge cut into the limestone of the Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park. It looks down into the canyon from a terrace of meadows, with the high peaks behind. The Cueva de Revilla is a cave in the cliffs near the hamlet, and the area between Bielsa, Plan and Boltaña is some of the wildest country in Aragón.

    Read the complete Cueva de Revilla write-up.

  3. 3. Cueva Peña Miel I

    170 km in.

    The Sierra de Guara, in the province of Huesca, is a limestone range riddled with canyons, and the river Vero cuts one of the most beautiful of them, a deep, narrow gorge of grey and ochre cliffs where vultures nest and the water runs clear over pale stones. Along its walls, prehistoric people left paintings in dozens of rock shelters, and the Cueva Peña Miel I is one of them, part of the UNESCO listing of rock art of the Mediterranean Basin. The Río Vero Cultural Park protects the area, with an interpretation centre in Colungo and guided visits to some of the shelters.

    Read the complete Cueva Peña Miel I write-up.

  4. 4. El Remosillo

    231 km in.

    If you ride north from Barbastro toward Graus, the Ésera river eventually forces its way through a ridge of limestone, and the result is the Congosto de Olvena, a short, deep gorge with the road squeezed between rock and water. Up on the cliffs around here are rock shelters with prehistoric paintings, and El Remosillo is one of the best known in this corner of Aragón, between Estadilla, Graus and Fonz.

    Read the complete El Remosillo write-up.

  5. 5. Corral de la Gascona

    279 km in.

    Alquézar is one of the most dramatic villages in Spain: a cluster of ochre houses piled on a rocky spur above the canyon of the Vero river, crowned by a castle and a collegiate church built on the site of a Moorish fortress. It lies at the southern edge of the Sierra de Guara, a limestone range in Aragon cut by deep canyons, and the area is famous across Europe for canyoning, with people abseiling, jumping and swimming through the narrow gorges in summer. The Corral de la Gascona is one of the rock-art sites in the Vero canyon and its surroundings, part of the UNESCO-listed rock art of the Mediterranean Basin.

    Read the complete Corral de la Gascona write-up.
