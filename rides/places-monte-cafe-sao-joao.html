Why this ride

From Trindade it runs 70 km to Trindade; allow about 1 h 12 of riding. It goes by way of Agua Izé, Sao Joao and back through Agua Izé.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Monte Café

    Sao Tome is a tiny island on the equator, in the Gulf of Guinea, and for over a century it was one of the biggest cocoa and coffee producers in the world. Monte Cafe, in the hills above the capital at about six hundred metres, was one of the great plantations, called rocas, that covered much of the island. The Portuguese began planting coffee here in the nineteenth century, and the estate grew to include a processing yard, drying terraces, a hospital, a church and rows of workers' housing, all in a cool, cloudy valley under thick forest. The roca system, built on forced and poorly paid labour, was harsh, and the buildings remain as a monument to both the island's wealth and its suffering.

  2. 2. Agua Izé

    Agua Izé was once the grandest of São Tomé's roças, the great plantation estates that made this tiny island, just north of the equator, one of the world's leading cocoa producers in the early twentieth century. It lies on the southeast coast, about thirteen kilometres from the capital, and the buildings tell the story: a big administrative house, long workers' quarters, a hospital, storehouses and drying platforms, all now peeling and softened by tropical growth. Walls are flaking yellow and ochre, and roofs sag, but the scale is unmistakable.

  3. 3. Sao Joao

    Sao Tomé is a green volcanic island on the equator, and for a century and a half it was a cocoa empire. The Portuguese planted vast estates called roças across the land, each one a small town with a manor, a hospital, a church, drying platforms and rows of workers' housing, and by 1900 the islands were one of the largest cocoa producers in the world. The labour was brutal. Workers contracted from Angola, Cape Verde and Mozambique worked in conditions little better than slavery, and the scandal broke in Europe in the early twentieth century, prompting boycotts by chocolate makers.
