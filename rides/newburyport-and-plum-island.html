  2. 2. Plum Island

    Plum Island is a barrier island, nine miles of sand and dune lying parallel to the coast of northeastern Massachusetts, with the Atlantic on one side and a vast expanse of salt marsh on the other. Most of it is the Parker River National Wildlife Refuge, one of the best places on the East Coast to watch birds, and the road that crosses the narrow causeway from Newburyport feels like a transition from town to wilderness. On a clear day the smell of salt and marsh grass hits you before the first dune.

  3. 3. Ipswich

    Ipswich can claim, and does, to have more first-period houses than any other town in America. The term covers buildings put up before 1725, in the cramped, steep-roofed, small-windowed style of the first English settlers, and a walk along the streets near the river brings you past dozens of them, dark with age and leaning a little with the centuries. The town was settled in 1633 and has been rebuilt, chewed up and left alone in about equal measure ever since, which is why so much of it survives.
