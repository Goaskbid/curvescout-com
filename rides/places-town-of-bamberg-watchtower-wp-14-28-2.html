Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Town of Bamberg

    Bamberg is built on seven hills, like Rome, and its townspeople are proud of the comparison. The old town survived the Second World War almost unscathed, which makes it unusual in Germany, and its jumble of medieval and Baroque streets, spread between the Regnitz river and the hills, is a World Heritage Site, listed in 1993. At the top of the cathedral hill stands the Kaiserdom, a four-towered Romanesque cathedral that holds the tomb of the Emperor Henry II, who founded the bishopric in the early eleventh century, and his wife Kunigunde. It also holds a mysterious medieval equestrian statue known as the Bamberg Horseman, whose identity is still debated.

  2. 2. Margravial Opera House Bayreuth

    Bayreuth's Margravial Opera House is the sort of building that makes you wonder how a small provincial court could afford it. It was built between 1745 and 1750 for Margrave Frederick of Brandenburg-Bayreuth and his wife, Wilhelmine, the sister of Frederick the Great, who was a composer, writer and patron and the driving force behind the court's cultural life. The interior, designed by the Italian theatre architect Giuseppe Galli Bibiena and his son Carlo, is a Baroque jewel box of carved and painted wood, with gold and blue tiers, a royal box like a throne and a stage deep enough for elaborate scenery.

  3. 3. Watchtower WP 14/28

    Near the Franconian town of Pleinfeld, the Raetian Limes, the stone wall that marked the frontier of the province of Raetia, crossed a landscape of woods, fields and small rivers, and a watchtower stood here, one of hundreds along the line. Watchtower WP 14/28 is now a low mound or reconstructed foundation, but it is part of the Upper Germanic-Raetian Limes World Heritage listing. The frontier here was a stone wall, known locally as the Teufelsmauer, the devil's wall, because medieval villagers could not believe that humans had built it.
