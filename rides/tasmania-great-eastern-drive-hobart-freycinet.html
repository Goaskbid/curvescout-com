Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hobart

    Hobart tucks itself against the foot of Mount Wellington, with the wide Derwent estuary at its feet, and it has the compact, sandstone-and-timber feel of a port that never grew too large. It is the capital of Tasmania and Australia's second oldest city, founded in 1804 as a convict settlement, and the old warehouses along Salamanca Place and the cottages of Battery Point survive from the early years of the colony. On Saturdays, Salamanca Place fills with a crowded market of stalls selling food, crafts and plants.

  2. 2. Orford

    Orford is a small seaside town on the east coast of Tasmania, at the mouth of the Prosser River, and it has the easygoing feel of a place that has always been a bit off the main road. The river runs through a pretty valley and into a sheltered bay, where fishing boats and kayaks bob in the water and walkers follow a path along the bank. Across the water lies Maria Island, a national park with no cars, where wombats, Forester kangaroos and Tasmanian devils roam among ruins of a convict settlement and a short-lived cement works.

  3. 3. Swansea

    Swansea sits on the shore of Great Oyster Bay, halfway up Tasmania's east coast, and its position is one of the best on the island. The bay curves away to the east, and across the water the pink granite peaks of the Hazards rise above the Freycinet Peninsula, glowing in the sunset like a stage set. Behind the town the land climbs to the Eastern Tiers, and the Meredith River runs through farmland to the sea. It is one of the oldest settlements on this coast, and the stone buildings, old warehouses and quiet streets reflect its long history of farming, whaling and sheep.

  4. 4. Freycinet

    Freycinet is the peninsula on Tasmania's east coast where a granite spine called the Hazards rises pink and orange out of the sea, the kind of rock that looks stained by the last sunset, and the colour comes from the feldspar in the stone. The famous curve of Wineglass Bay lies on the far side, a white arc of sand reached by a steep walking track of about an hour from the car park, and a good viewpoint is on the way up; it is a fair climb in motorcycle boots, so plan to change shoes.
