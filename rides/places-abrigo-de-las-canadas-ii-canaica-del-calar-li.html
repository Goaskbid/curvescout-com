Why this ride

From Nerpio it runs 133 km to Nerpio; allow about 2 h 18 of riding. On the way it passes mingarnao I, Cañaica del Calar l, Las Cabritas and Cañaica del Calar lI. The highest point is about 1,450 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abrigo de las Cañadas II

    Nerpio is about as remote as mainland Spain gets. The village sits in the far southwest corner of Albacete province, in the Sierra de Segura, where the mountains of Andalusia, Murcia and Castilla-La Mancha meet, and the rock shelters scattered around it make up one of the richest concentrations of prehistoric art in the region. The Abrigo de las Cañadas II belongs to that group, an overhang in the hills between Nerpio and the high plateaus toward Huéscar and Yeste.

  2. 2. mingarnao I

    Everything about Nerpio feels remote, and Mingarnao pushes the feeling a little further. The shelter sits in the Sierra de Segura, where the road ends and tracks wander over limestone ridges, through pine woods and past small farms. A scattering of hamlets, such as Yeste, Letur and Nerpio itself, are the nearest signs of life, and the nearest big town in the data, Moratalla, is a long 38 km away. The sense of space is the point of coming here.

  3. 3. Cañaica del Calar l

    Very few people get here. The Cañaica del Calar lies in the far north-west of the region of Murcia, near the border with Albacete and Andalusia, in a wild landscape of limestone plateaus, pine forests and deep valleys around Moratalla and Nerpio. The calar is the local word for a limestone plateau, and the rock shelters on its edges hold prehistoric paintings in the Levantine style, part of the World Heritage rock art of the Mediterranean. The area around Nerpio is one of the richest in Spain for rock art.

  4. 4. Las Cabritas

    Nerpio sits at the edge of the world, or at least at the edge of Albacete province, a small village in the Sierra de Taibilla close to where Murcia, Granada and Jaén come together. Its walnuts are famous, grown in old orchards along the river, and the hills around it hold some of the finest Levantine rock art in Spain. Las Cabritas, the little goats, is a shelter just over a kilometre from the village, named after the animals painted on its walls, and it is part of the World Heritage Site of Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin.

  5. 5. Cañaica del Calar lI

    Cañaica del Calar lies in the south of Albacete province, on the edge of a rugged corner of the Sierra del Segura where Murcia, Albacete and Jaén nearly meet. The painted shelter that bears its name, listed here as Cañaica del Calar II, is in a landscape of dry limestone ridges, pines and sudden ravines that drain into the Río Segura. The village of Nerpio is eleven kilometres away, and Moratalla, the nearest sizeable town, is twenty-six, so this is a long way to go on the strength of a single rock face.
