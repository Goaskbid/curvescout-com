Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Saint Raphael

    Saint-Raphaël is the starting point of one of the most colourful roads in France, and the colour is its subject. The Esterel massif, a low range of volcanic rock behind the town, is made of red porphyry, which glows an almost unreal brick-red against the blue Mediterranean and the dark green of the pines. The town itself is a relaxed seaside resort of about thirty-five thousand people, with a promenade, a casino, a harbour and a handsome church in Neo-Byzantine style, Notre-Dame-de-la-Victoire, built in the nineteenth century.

  2. 2. Anthéor

    Anthéor is a tiny seaside hamlet on the Corniche d'Or, the coast road between Saint-Raphaël and Cannes, and it sits in a spot where the red rock of the Esterel meets the Mediterranean in a way that no guidebook has ever made seem exaggerated. The cliffs here are a volcanic rock, rust-red and purple, ancient lava that cooled around three hundred million years ago, and they plunge straight into water so blue it seems inked. Above the hamlet the peak of Cap Roux rises, a bare, rose-coloured crag over the sea.

  3. 3. Le Trayas

    Le Trayas is the point where the Var gives way to the Alpes-Maritimes on the Corniche d'Or, and for a hamlet with very few buildings it carries a lot of weight in the memory of anyone who has ridden here. The little station on the coastal railway, the scatter of villas on the slopes and the tiny harbour bring a sleepy feel, and the main event is the scenery: red porphyry cliffs, dark green forest and a sea that shifts between turquoise and deep blue as the clouds move.

  4. 4. Theoule sur Mer

    Théoule-sur-Mer is a little town squeezed between the red rocks of the Esterel and the sea, and it looks like a painting from the early days of the French Riviera. The houses are pink and ochre, the harbour is small and crowded with boats, and the hills behind are covered with pines and cork oaks. A fortified château with round towers, dating from the fourteenth century and now used as the town hall, stands near the shore, and the old fishing quarter has narrow streets and small terraces. The population is only about thirteen hundred, though it swells in summer.
