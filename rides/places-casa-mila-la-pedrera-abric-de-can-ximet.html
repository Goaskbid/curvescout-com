Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Casa Milà \La Pedrera\

    La Pedrera, the stone quarry, is what Barcelonans call Casa Milà, and the nickname is fair. The building, designed by Gaudí for Pere Milà and his wife Roser Segimon and built between 1906 and 1912, has a facade of pale limestone that rolls along the corner of Passeig de Gràcia and Carrer de Provença like a frozen wave, with balconies of twisted wrought iron that look like seaweed. There are no straight lines on the facade, and no load-bearing walls inside, since the structure is carried by columns and the floors can be arranged freely.

  3. 3. Triumphal Arch of Berá

    For centuries traffic on the main coastal road of Catalonia drove straight through a Roman triumphal arch. The Arc de Berà stands on the line of the Via Augusta, the great Roman road from the Pyrenees to Cádiz, about twenty kilometres northeast of Tarragona, and until the road was diverted around it in the late twentieth century, cars and trucks passed under its single span. It was built at the end of the first century BC, and an inscription records that it was erected according to the will of Lucius Licinius Sura, a figure whose identity historians still discuss. It is part of the UNESCO listing for the Roman remains of Tarraco.

  4. 4. Roca Roja

    Roca Roja, the red rock, is a fitting name for this corner of inland Catalonia, where sandstone and conglomerate cliffs glow rust-coloured in the evening light above pine forest and small terraced fields. The point lies in the hills between Querol and Mediona, where the comarques of Alt Camp, Alt Penedès and Anoia meet, a rugged, thinly populated upland that most people speed past on the motorway between Barcelona and Lleida without realising it exists.

  5. 5. Abric de Can Ximet

    The Penedès is a broad, gently rolling basin between the coastal ranges and the Montserrat massif, and it is one of the great wine regions of Catalonia. Vilafranca del Penedès, its capital, is a lively town with a Gothic church, a wine museum and a passion for castells, the human towers that teams of men, women and children build in the main square on feast days. UNESCO added the castells to its list of intangible heritage in 2010. Can Ximet is a farm name on the fringe of this landscape, and the shelter nearby is a modest rock overhang associated with prehistoric art.
