German Fairy Tale Route: Marburg to Hamelin

Why this ride

From Marburg an der Lahn it runs 399 km to Hameln; allow about 5 h 30 of riding. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Marburg

    Marburg climbs a steep hillside above the Lahn river, with a castle at the top, a Gothic church on the slope and a student at nearly every corner. It is a university town in the real sense: the university was founded in 1527 as the first Protestant university in the world, and roughly a quarter of the population is made up of students. That gives the narrow lanes of the old town an energy that few places in Hesse can match, with bookshops, cafes and bars crowded into every nook.

  2. 2. Kassel

    Kassel is a city that rebuilt itself after being flattened by wartime bombing, so the first impression from the ring road is more concrete than fairy tale. Give it an hour and the other Kassel appears. This is where the Brothers Grimm lived and worked for years in the early nineteenth century, collecting the tales that would make them famous, and the city is the start of the German Fairy Tale Route to Bremen. A museum in the city, the Grimmwelt, tells their story and shows original manuscripts.

  3. 3. Hamelin

    In Hamelin, the rats are on the pavement. A trail of little brass rats leads through the old town to the Rattenfängerhaus, the house of the Pied Piper, and the town has built its identity around the legend. According to the story, in 1284 a stranger in colourful clothes came to Hameln, as the Germans spell it, and offered to rid the town of its rats. He led them into the Weser with his pipe, the citizens refused to pay him, and he returned and led away their children. The earliest records speak of a loss of a hundred and thirty children, and historians still argue over what actually happened.
