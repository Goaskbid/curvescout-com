Why this ride

215 km from Žatec to Žatec, about 3 h 42 of riding. On the way it passes Mědník Hill Mining Landscape, Annaberg-Frohnau Mining Landscape, Erzgebirge/Krušnohoří Mining Region and back through Annaberg-Frohnau Mining Landscape. It crosses from Czechia into Germany - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Saaz Hop Landscape

    Smell comes first here. Late in summer the air around Žatec turns green and faintly bitter, because this is the heart of the Saaz hop country and the harvest fills the lanes with the scent of cones being picked and dried. The hops grown on these slopes and flats along the Ohře river are the ones that give Czech lager, and Pilsner above all, its clean, spicy bite. The cultural landscape of the town and its fields earned UNESCO status in 2023, and the old hop-drying houses and brewery buildings in Žatec are the reason.

  2. 2. Mědník Hill Mining Landscape

    Mědník is a small, steep hill on the Czech side of the Ore Mountains, or Krušné hory, the range that forms the border between Bohemia and Saxony. It rises above the village of Měděnec, whose name, like the hill's, comes from the Czech word for copper. For centuries, miners tunnelled into the hill for copper and iron ore, and the hill is now part of the Erzgebirge/Krušnohoří Mining Region, inscribed by UNESCO in 2019 as a cross-border site.

  3. 3. Annaberg-Frohnau Mining Landscape

    Silver built Annaberg. In the late fifteenth century rich silver ore was found in the Erzgebirge, the Ore Mountains on the border between Saxony and Bohemia, and a town was founded almost overnight in 1496 to house the miners. Within a few decades Annaberg was one of the largest towns in Saxony, and its wealth paid for the St. Annenkirche, one of the most beautiful late Gothic hall churches in Germany, with slender pillars branching into a star-ribbed vault like the trunks of trees.

  4. 4. Erzgebirge/Krušnohoří Mining Region

    The word dollar comes from these hills. In the early sixteenth century, a silver rush at Joachimsthal, today's Jáchymov, made the town one of the largest in Bohemia, and the coins minted there, the Joachimsthaler, shortened to thaler, gave their name to currencies around the world. The Erzgebirge, or Krušné hory, the Ore Mountains on the Czech-German border, were mined for silver, tin, cobalt, iron and finally uranium for eight centuries, and the Erzgebirge/Krušnohoří Mining Region was inscribed on the UNESCO list in 2019.
