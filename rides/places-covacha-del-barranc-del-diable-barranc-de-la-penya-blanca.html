Why this ride

A 464 km round trip from Puçol - roughly 10 h 36 in the saddle. It goes by way of Barranc de la falaguera- Abric I, Abrigo de las Cañas II, Cova de la Petxina and Barranc de la Penya Blanca. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Covacha del Barranc del Diable

    Start here.

    Puçol is a modest, busy town on the coast north of Valencia, famous locally for its orange groves, its beach and its long sandy shore. Inland from it the land rises quickly into the Sierra Calderona, a low range of pine, rosemary and dry gullies. The Barranc del Diable, the Devil's Ravine, is one of many small ravines cut into the foothills, and the covacha, a shallow cave, holds a few rock paintings from the Levantine tradition. These belong to the Mediterranean-arc rock art UNESCO listed in 1998, and they are typically small, red and easy to miss unless a guide shows you where to look.

    Read the complete Covacha del Barranc del Diable write-up.

  2. 2. Barranc de la falaguera- Abric I

    51 km in.

    Alginet sits in the Ribera Alta, the flat, fertile plain south of Valencia where the Júcar river spreads out among orange groves, rice fields and small towns. To the west, the plain meets the first hills of the interior, low ridges of limestone covered in pine and scrub. The Barranc de la Falaguera, the ravine of the fern, cuts into these hills near Alginet, Alfarp and Benifaió.

    Read the complete Barranc de la falaguera- Abric I write-up.

  3. 3. Abrigo de las Cañas II

    126 km in.

    The Macizo del Caroig is one of the emptiest corners of the Valencian region, a high, rugged limestone massif cut by deep gorges, covered with pine, scrub and rosemary, and dotted with a few small villages. Bicorp, Millares and Quesa lie on its edges, and in the ravines around them are dozens of rock shelters with prehistoric paintings, part of the UNESCO-listed rock art of the Mediterranean Basin. The Abrigo de las Cañas is one of them, and the area as a whole is among the richest in Spain for this kind of art.

    Read the complete Abrigo de las Cañas II write-up.

  4. 4. Cova de la Petxina

    191 km in.

    Bellús is a small village in the valley of the Albaida river in southern Valencia, just upstream from where the river flows into a reservoir and then joins the Júcar. The old village was partly flooded by the reservoir, and the new one stands on higher ground. The Cova de la Petxina, the cave of the shell, is in the hills near the village, close to Guadasequies and Genovés.

    Read the complete Cova de la Petxina write-up.

  5. 5. Barranc de la Penya Blanca

    231 km in.

    Planes is a small village in the hills of the Comtat in northern Alicante province, crowned by a castle and looking out over a valley of olive and cherry orchards. The Barranc de la Penya Blanca, the ravine of the white rock, lies close by, among the folded limestone ridges between Planes, Benimarfull and the reservoir at Beniarrés.

    Read the complete Barranc de la Penya Blanca write-up.
