  1. 1. Devin

    Most Bulgarians meet Devin first on a bottle label. Its mineral springs are bottled and sold in every corner shop in the country, and the small spa town that produces them lies in a tight green valley of the western Rhodopes, on the Vacha river, with steep slopes of pine and spruce rising on all sides. It is a mixed place, home to Christian Bulgarians and Pomaks, Bulgarian-speaking Muslims, and in summer it fills with holidaymakers soaking in the warm mineral pools of its spa hotels. Out of season it is very quiet indeed.

  2. 2. Smolyan

    Smolyan is a town that does not quite know where it begins. It runs for roughly ten kilometres along the narrow valley of the Cherna river, a thin ribbon of houses and apartment blocks hemmed in by pine-covered slopes, because it was stitched together in the 1960s from several separate villages and never really filled in the gaps. It is the capital of the central Rhodopes and the largest town in these mountains, and once you accept its odd shape, the setting is superb: ridges on every side, forest climbing to the skyline, and the air noticeably fresher than down on the Thracian plain.
