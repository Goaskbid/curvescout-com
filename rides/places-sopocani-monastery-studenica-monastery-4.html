Why this ride

A 130 km round trip from Novi Pazar - roughly 2 h 14 in the saddle. The stops in between: St. Peter's Church, Studenica Monastery and back through St. Peter's Church. The highest point is about 1,200 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sopoćani Monastery

    Sopoćani is one of the quietest and most beautiful monasteries in Serbia, and you may well be the only person in the church. It stands in a green valley a few kilometres west of Novi Pazar, near the springs of the Raška river, in a region that was the heartland of the early Serbian kingdom. King Stefan Uroš I founded it in the 1250s, and he is buried here, and the church was decorated with frescoes between about 1260 and 1270, painted by artists whose name we do not know but whose command of colour and space put them in the first rank of medieval European painting. UNESCO listed Sopoćani together with the nearby Old Ras in 1979.

  2. 2. St. Peter's Church

    On a rocky hill above the town of Novi Pazar stands a small, round church, and it may be the oldest in Serbia. Petrova crkva, the church of St Peter, is a rotunda from the ninth or tenth century, built on the site of earlier foundations, and the medieval ruler Stefan Nemanja, who founded the Serbian state and the dynasty that followed, is said to have been baptised here. Its stone walls are worn, its interior is dim and its setting, with the town spreading out below and the hills behind, makes you feel the age. The surrounding area was the heart of the early Serbian kingdom, called Ras, and the area is protected as a World Heritage site, together with the nearby monastery of Sopocani.

  3. 3. Studenica Monastery

    The monastery of Studenica lies in a green gorge of the Golija mountains in central Serbia, where a clear river runs down through beech and oak woods toward the Ibar. Stefan Nemanja, the founder of the medieval Serbian state, began building it in the 1190s, and it became the model for all the monasteries that followed. Nemanja himself abdicated in 1196 and later became a monk, dying on Mount Athos in 1199. His son, Saint Sava, brought his father's remains back here, and they rest in the church today.
