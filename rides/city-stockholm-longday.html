Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Stockholm

    Stockholm is built on fourteen islands where Lake Mälaren meets the Baltic, and water is always in view. The old town, Gamla Stan, sits on a small island at the centre, a knot of cobbled lanes, ochre and red façades and narrow courtyards that dates back to the thirteenth century, with the huge royal palace and the cathedral at its heart. In 1520 the Danish king had dozens of Swedish nobles beheaded in the main square, the Stockholm Bloodbath, and the event helped to spark the rebellion that placed Gustav Vasa on the throne. These days the square is a pretty place for coffee, and the shop windows are full of Dala horses and candles.

  2. 2. Engelsberg Ironworks

    Central Sweden's Bergslagen district is a country of lakes, spruce forest and old iron, and the Engelsberg Ironworks stands in the middle of it, a few kilometres from Fagersta. It is one of the best-kept examples in Europe of a pre-industrial iron foundry, with its blast furnace, the forge buildings, the workers' houses and the manor all grouped around a pond where the water that once drove the machinery still flows. UNESCO added it to the World Heritage list in 1993 as a place where the whole system of an old Swedish works, from smelting to community, can still be seen at once.
