Ruta de la Plata: Seville to Salamanca

Why this ride

From Sevilla it runs 1034 km to Salamanca; allow about 14 h 12 of riding. The stops in between: Merida and Caceres. The highest point is about 850 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Seville

    The Ruta de la Plata sounds like a silver road, and generations of travellers have assumed it carried silver from the Americas. In fact the name probably comes from the Arabic word balata, meaning a paved way, and the road itself is much older: a Roman route running north from Seville to Mérida and on to Astorga, which pilgrims and drovers kept using for two thousand years. Seville is where it begins, and the city makes a fine place to start any ride.

  2. 2. Merida

    In the year 25 BC the Romans founded a colony here for veterans of their wars in Hispania, named it Emerita Augusta, and made it the capital of the province of Lusitania. Two thousand years later, Mérida has more Roman remains than anywhere else in Spain, and they are not roped off in a museum. They are in the streets. A long Roman bridge, around eight hundred metres, still crosses the broad Guadiana on dozens of arches and is walked daily by locals. A theatre and an amphitheatre sit side by side near the centre, and in summer actors perform classical plays in the theatre in the open air. On the edge of town the tall brick piers of an aqueduct, called Los Milagros, the miracles, march across a field.

  3. 3. Caceres

    Cáceres has two faces. The modern city spreads over the plain with shopping streets, apartment blocks and ring roads, and it is a normal busy Spanish provincial capital. Inside it, behind medieval walls, lies the old quarter, which is one of the best-preserved in Europe and has been a World Heritage Site since 1986. Here stand towers, palaces and churches of golden stone, built between the thirteenth and sixteenth centuries by noble families who had made money in the wars of the reconquest and later in the Americas. Many of the towers were cut down to the level of the roofs on the order of the Catholic Monarchs, who wanted to curb the power of the nobility, and the stumps are still visible.

  4. 4. Salamanca

    Salamanca glows. Its buildings are built from a golden sandstone that takes on a honey colour in low sun, and the effect, especially in the late afternoon in the Plaza Mayor, is memorable. The city stands on the Tormes river in western Spain, about 800 metres above sea level, and is one of the great university towns of Europe, with a student population that gives the old streets life well into the night.
