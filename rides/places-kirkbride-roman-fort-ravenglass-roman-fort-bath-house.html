Why this ride

A 271 km round trip from Wigton - roughly 4 h 26 in the saddle. On the way it passes Brownrigg milefortlet 22, The English Lake District, Ravenglass Roman fort bath-house and back through The English Lake District. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Hardknott and Wrynose Passes

It is 36 km from The English Lake District to Ravenglass Roman fort bath-house. Downhill for the most part, 300 m in all, never above 326 m. Water alongside for much of it - pick one good pull-out and stop.

Check the pass is open before you commit to it.

The stops

  1. 1. Kirkbride Roman fort

    Start here.

    The tide turns the Solway Firth into an enormous, shifting plain of sand and water, and the Roman fort at Kirkbride stood at the edge of it. Here, at the western limit of the frontier in the lower Eden valley, the Romans built a fort to guard the crossing of the estuary, and the earthworks show a rectangular platform in a field by the village. The fort belonged to the Wall system, which continued along the coast, and the first thing a visitor realises is how big the view is. On a clear day you can see the Scottish hills across the water, and the ridges of Criffel and Burnswark, and the line of the shore bending away to the west.

    Read the complete Kirkbride Roman fort write-up.

  2. 2. Brownrigg milefortlet 22

    35 km in.

    The wall did not stop at the Solway. Along the Cumbrian coast, south of Bowness, the Romans built a chain of small forts, milecastles and watchtowers to guard against raiders arriving by sea from across the Irish Sea, and Brownrigg milecastle 22 is one of them. It stands on a low ridge above the shore near Maryport, with views across the Solway Firth to the hills of Scotland, a line of blue on a clear day. Little remains but a platform and some stonework, but the situation says everything.

    Read the complete Brownrigg milefortlet 22 write-up.

  3. 3. The English Lake District

    102 km in.

    Wordsworth wandered lonely as a cloud round here, and anybody who has ridden the Lake District on a wet bank holiday will understand the appeal of being lonely. The English Lake District, listed by UNESCO in 2017 as a cultural landscape, is a compact knot of mountains, lakes and valleys in Cumbria, shaped by glaciers and then by centuries of sheep farming, slate quarrying and, from the late eighteenth century, by poets, painters and tourists who came looking for the sublime.

    Read the complete The English Lake District write-up.

  4. 4. Ravenglass Roman fort bath-house

    137 km in.

    Walls Castle is the odd name for a Roman bath house, and the building still stands to a height of about four metres, one of the tallest Roman structures in northern England. It stood outside the fort of Glannoventa at Ravenglass, a harbour station at the meeting of three rivers, the Irt, the Mite and the Esk, and in the dry shelter of the dunes it has survived because its walls were thick and well built. You can walk around it on a short path from the village, and see niches and a doorway in the thick walls. The fort itself lies mostly under farmland and the railway.
