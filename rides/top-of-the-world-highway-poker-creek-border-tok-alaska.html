Why this ride

The highest point is about 900 m. It crosses from Canada into United States - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Poker Creek border

    Poker Creek is as far north as most riders will ever cross an international border by road, and it has the feel of a place at the end of the world. The tiny US customs post sits on a bare ridge on the Top of the World Highway, a road that follows the crest of the hills between Dawson City in the Yukon and Chicken in Alaska, with views that stretch for a hundred kilometres across a sea of rolling, treeless mountains. Here the boreal forest thins out into tundra, and the light in summer is long and golden.

  2. 2. Chicken Alaska

    Chicken, Alaska, is spelled that way because the miners could not agree how to spell ptarmigan. The story goes that the early prospectors on Walker Fork wanted to name the settlement after the willow grouse that gathered there, gave up arguing over the spelling, and settled on something they could all manage. The village has kept its sense of humour ever since, with a gift shop, a cafe and a good deal of cheerful chicken-themed signage.

  3. 3. Tok Alaska

    Tok is the first real town you meet in Alaska after crossing the border at Beaver Creek, and for riders who have spent a long day on the Alaska Highway it is a small and welcome sight. About twelve hundred people live here, spread along the highway at the junction of the Alaska Highway and the Tok Cutoff, which leads south towards Glennallen and Anchorage. The place calls itself the sled dog capital of Alaska, and the dogs, kennels and mushing history are genuinely part of local life.
