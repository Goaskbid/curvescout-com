Why this ride

162 km from Confrides to Confrides, about 2 h 48 of riding. The stops in between: Barranc de l'Infern Conj. 2, Cueva de la Clau, Cova de la Petxina and back through Cueva de la Clau. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Port de Confrides- Abric II

    The Port de Confrides is the kind of pass that never makes a poster but fills a rider's notebook. It carries the road over the shoulder of the Serra d'Aitana between the coast valleys and the inland basin of Alcoi, and from the top you look both ways, towards the sea haze on one side and the pale Mariola country on the other. Aitana itself, at 1,558 metres the highest summit in Alicante province, stands just north of the road, bald and stony and often wearing a little cloud.

  2. 2. Barranc de l'Infern Conj. 2

    Local walkers call it the cathedral of hiking, and once you have seen the Barranc de l'Infern you understand why. This deep limestone gorge, between the Vall de Laguar and the Vall d'Ebo in the mountains behind Dénia, is crossed by an old Moorish mule path that descends and climbs again by thousands of stone steps, carved and built into the rock walls over centuries. The route, a classic day walk, is spectacular and exhausting, with the gorge narrowing at its deepest point to a passage between towering walls. The gorge also holds prehistoric rock art in its shelters, and the sites grouped as Conjunt 2 are among them, part of the wider family of Mediterranean rock art protected by UNESCO.

  3. 3. Cueva de la Clau

    Behind Gandia, the orange groves climb into the folded limestone hills of La Safor, and among the tiny villages there, Palma de Gandía and Ador sit almost touching each other at the foot of the slopes. Cueva de la Clau is one of the many rock shelters in the Valencian hills that form part of the UNESCO-listed Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula, a scattered collection of hundreds of sites where prehistoric people painted hunters, animals, dancers and abstract signs on limestone overhangs.

  4. 4. Cova de la Petxina

    Bellús is a small village in the valley of the Albaida river in southern Valencia, just upstream from where the river flows into a reservoir and then joins the Júcar. The old village was partly flooded by the reservoir, and the new one stands on higher ground. The Cova de la Petxina, the cave of the shell, is in the hills near the village, close to Guadasequies and Genovés.
