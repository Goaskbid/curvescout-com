Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Valkenburg-De Woerd | North

    On the coast between Leiden and Katwijk, Valkenburg is a village of fewer than four thousand people with a Roman past it rarely mentions. The Oude Rijn, the old Rhine, flows through here on its last stretch toward the North Sea, and for several centuries this river was the northern edge of the Roman Empire. A fort called Praetorium Agrippinae stood on the bank at Valkenburg, one of a string of forts that now form part of the Lower German Limes, which joined the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2021.

  2. 2. Works along the IJ before Durgerdam

    East of Amsterdam, where the IJ widens into the IJmeer and the city gives way to water and sky, the old dike village of Durgerdam sits on a strip of land between the sea wall and the polders. Its wooden houses, painted in strong colours and leaning on each other along the dike, look out over a lake that was once the Zuiderzee, open to the North Sea until the Afsluitdijk closed it in 1932. The works along the IJ here belong to the Defence Line of Amsterdam, now part of the Dutch Water Defence Lines World Heritage Site.

  3. 3. Schokland and Surroundings

    Schokland was once an island in the Zuiderzee, a long, narrow strip of peat and clay surrounded by an inland sea that constantly threatened to swallow it. For centuries, its inhabitants lived on raised mounds and fought a losing battle against the water, as storms ate away at the island's edges. By the mid-nineteenth century, life had become so precarious that in 1859 the Dutch government ordered the island to be evacuated. Then, in the twentieth century, the Dutch did something extraordinary: they enclosed the Zuiderzee with a dam, the Afsluitdijk, completed in 1932, and drained large parts of it to create new land. The Noordoostpolder, drained in 1942, swallowed Schokland, and the former island now rises slightly above the surrounding fields, a long green ridge in a flat landscape of farmland. In 1995, Schokland became the first Dutch site to be listed by UNESCO, as a symbol of the Netherlands' heroic struggle against the water.
