Why this ride

82 km one way from Altdorf - plan on about 1 h 24 of riding. It goes by way of Klausen Pass and Urnerboden. The highest point is about 1,950 m. Klausen Pass is subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Klausen Pass

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 1948 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance — cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Rider note

Check the pass is open before you commit to it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Altdorf

    Altdorf is the capital of the canton of Uri, a small town at the head of Lake Lucerne's southern arm where the Reuss river leaves the mountains and the Gotthard route begins. It is a practical, low-key place, flat and sheltered, with a pretty centre and a roar of traffic nearby from the motorway, and it is the sort of town where riders refill the tank, buy a coffee and look up at the wall of peaks above.

  2. 2. Klausen Pass

    The Klausen Pass was built for horse-drawn coaches in the 1890s, and a generation later it was being used for motor racing. Between 1922 and 1934, the Klausenrennen, a hillclimb from Linthal to the summit, was one of the great events of European motor sport, with drivers such as Rudolf Caracciola and Hans Stuck throwing huge cars up the hairpins at average speeds that the road had never been designed for. The race was dropped in the 1930s, but the road has never quite lost the association, and on a quiet morning you can imagine the noise.

  3. 3. Urnerboden

    Urnerboden is a long, flat valley floor on the Glarus side of the Klausen Pass, and it is the largest alpine pasture in Switzerland. From June to September, hundreds of cows, calves and goats graze on its meadows, watched by herders and by a handful of farm buildings, and the cowbells drift across the grass like a distant orchestra. Its walls of rock rise on every side, streaked with waterfalls after rain, and the tiny church and the scattered chalets give it a human scale.

  4. 4. Linthal

    Linthal is the last real village at the head of the Linth valley in canton Glarus, a long way into the mountains and a place that the modern world mostly passes by. About a thousand people live here, among steep meadows, old wooden houses and the dark shapes of textile mills from the days when cotton printing ruled the valley. Above the village rises the Tödi, the highest peak of the Glarus Alps, at over 3,600 metres, with ice on its flanks and a long history of mountaineering. From the valley floor, in the right light, the glaciers look like they are poured down the rock.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Altdorf to Klausen Pass

Altdorf → Klausen Pass climbs about 1948 m in 18 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Klausen Pass to Urnerboden

Klausen Pass → Urnerboden drops about 1948 m over 8 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Urnerboden to Linthal

Urnerboden → Linthal is 10 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
