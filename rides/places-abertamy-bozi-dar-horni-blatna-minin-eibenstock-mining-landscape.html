Why this ride

From Abertamy it runs 71 km to Abertamy; allow about 1 h 13 of riding. It goes by way of Rother Berg Mining Landscape, Eibenstock Mining Landscape and back through Rother Berg Mining Landscape. It crosses from Czechia into Germany - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abertamy - Boží Dar - Horní Blatná - Minin

    Boží Dar claims to be the highest town in the Czech Republic, at about a thousand metres on the ridge of the Ore Mountains, and it has the climate to prove it: long winters, deep snow, and a cool, damp feel even in summer. The name means God's Gift, and the settlement was founded in the sixteenth century by miners who came after silver was found on the slopes. Abertamy, Horní Blatná and Mining Landscape are all part of the same story, a string of hill villages that grew up around the mines.

  2. 2. Rother Berg Mining Landscape

    Schwarzenberg, in the western Erzgebirge, is a town of steep streets, slate roofs and a castle and church perched on a rock above the Schwarzwasser river, and the hills around it are riddled with old mine workings. The Rother Berg, the red mountain, takes its name from the iron ore that colours its soil, and it is one of the components of the Erzgebirge/Krušnohoří Mining Region, the cross-border UNESCO site listed in 2019. For eight centuries, miners in these mountains dug for silver, tin, iron, cobalt and later uranium, and their work shaped not only the landscape but the culture of the whole region.

  3. 3. Eibenstock Mining Landscape

    Eibenstock sits high in the western Erzgebirge, at about six hundred metres above the sea, in a country of spruce forests and cold, clear streams. It owes its origins to tin: miners worked the tin deposits here from the Middle Ages, and the mining landscape around the town, with old spoil heaps, shafts and water channels, is part of the Erzgebirge/Krušnohoří Mining Region, which UNESCO recognised in 2019. Today, the traces are quiet, softened by moss and birch.
