Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Spreetshoogte Pass point 1

    Solitaire is hardly a place at all, which is exactly why riders remember it. A petrol station, a general store, a small campsite, a scatter of rusted car wrecks half buried in sand and a bakery whose apple pie is spoken of from Windhoek to Walvis Bay: that is the whole settlement, planted on the gravel plains where the Namib meets the foot of the escarpment. Everything around it is space, pale grass, red dunes in the west and the long blue line of the mountains in the east.

  2. 2. Spreetshoogte Pass point 2

    Northeast of Solitaire the road starts its argument with the escarpment. The Spreetshoogte Pass is a gravel track that climbs from the Namib plains to the highlands of the Khomas Hochland, and it is famous for its gradient. Locals describe it as one of the steepest passes in Namibia, and the engineers who built it were not bashful about taking the shortest line up the hill.

  3. 3. Spreetshoogte Pass point 3

    Up on the rim, the world changes shape. The highlands above the Spreetshoogte Pass are cool, open and surprisingly green after the Namib, a high plateau of grass and thorn scrub that rolls away toward Windhoek. Cattle and game farms spread across it, separated by long fences and gravel roads, and the light has a clean, thin quality that is easy to love after the heat of the desert.
