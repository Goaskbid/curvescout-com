Why this ride

curated scenic road. Atlantic coast, lighthouses, cliffs. From A Coruña it runs 101 km to Fisterra; allow about 1 h 50 of riding. It goes by way of Malpica, Laxe, Camariñas and Mugia. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. A Coruña

    A Coruña sits on a thumb of land poking into the Atlantic, with a harbour on one side and open ocean beaches on the other, so the city has two moods depending on which side of the isthmus you stand. The Orzán and Riazor beaches face the wind and surf, while the old town and the glass-fronted houses of the Marina look in over calmer water. It is Galician to the core, which means the weather can change four times in an afternoon and nobody will remark on it.

  2. 2. Malpica

    Malpica is where the Costa da Morte, the Coast of Death, begins in earnest. The name sounds dramatic and it is earned. This long stretch of Galician coast, bristling with granite headlands, shoals and fog, has seen more shipwrecks than almost anywhere in Europe, and the people here have a long history of watching the sea for trouble and for the cargo that it sometimes delivered. Today Malpica is a working fishing port of about seven thousand people on a deep bay, with a harbour full of brightly coloured boats and a small town of white houses climbing the hill behind it.

  3. 3. Laxe

    Laxe is easy to like because it does not try very hard. A fishing town of a few thousand people tucked into a curved bay on the Costa da Morte, it has a harbour where boats unload the day's catch, a long sandy beach that curves round the bay, and a low white skyline of houses. In summer, Galician families spread out on the sand, and in winter the Atlantic throws huge swells against the headland, so that the town feels like the last outpost before the open sea.
