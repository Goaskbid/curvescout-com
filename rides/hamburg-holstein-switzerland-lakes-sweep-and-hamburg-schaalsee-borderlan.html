Why this ride

A one-way ride of 226 km, Bad Segeberg to Mölln - roughly 4 h 14 in the saddle. It goes by way of Plön lake balcony, Malente forest lanes, Eutin castle roads and Segeberg.

The stops

  1. 1. Segeberg fuel and exit

    Start here.

    Bad Segeberg is known to every German of a certain age for one reason: Karl May. Every summer since the early 1950s, an open-air theatre cut into the side of a hill in the town stages dramas based on the adventure novels of the Saxon writer, with real horses, stunt riders, gunfights and a cast of cowboys and Native American characters in a mock Wild West setting. Hundreds of thousands of people come for it. The setting is an unusual hill, the Kalkberg, a block of gypsum and limestone that rises about ninety metres above the flat land around, and that is itself a rarity in this part of the country.

    Read the complete Segeberg fuel and exit write-up.

  2. 2. Plön lake balcony

    40 km in.

    Plön has a castle on a hill, and the hill is what makes the town. The big, white, many-windowed building stands on a rise between two lakes, and from its terrace the view runs over the water, the islands and the wooded shore. The castle was built in the seventeenth century as a residence for the dukes of Holstein-Plön, and later served as a Prussian military school, where sons of the Kaiser were among the pupils, and then as a boarding school. It is not open in all parts, but the grounds and the terrace give a fine look at the lake.

    Read the complete Plön lake balcony write-up.

  3. 3. Malente forest lanes

    53 km in.

    Away from the cafés and promenades, Malente has another face, and it is made of beech trees. The forests around the town, between the Dieksee and the Kellersee, are among the finest broadleaf woods in Schleswig-Holstein, tall and grey-barked, with a high canopy that turns the light green in summer and copper in autumn. The ground beneath is soft with leaf litter, and the quiet is broken only by woodpeckers, jays and the sound of wind in the crowns.

    Read the complete Malente forest lanes write-up.

  4. 4. Eutin castle roads

    60 km in.

    Eutin had its literary moment long before it had a reputation for roses. In the late eighteenth century the little residence town of the prince-bishops of Lübeck became a quiet meeting place for poets and painters, and people spoke of it as the Weimar of the north. Johann Heinrich Voss, who gave Germany its beloved translation of Homer's Odyssey, taught at the Latin school here, and the painter Wilhelm Tischbein, famous for his portrait of Goethe in the Roman countryside, spent his last years in the town and died here in 1829.

    Read the complete Eutin castle roads write-up.

  5. 5. Back to Segeberg

    99 km in.

    Bad Segeberg is the town with a limestone hill in the middle of a flat plain, and everything about it follows from that odd fact. The Kalkberg, a lump of gypsum and limestone pushed up by an ancient salt dome, rises sharply from the surrounding fields, and for a thousand years it has been the natural place for a fortress. Dukes and kings built castles on it and fought over it, and the stones of the last one are still scattered about its flanks. Today the hill's chief purpose is theatre: since 1952 the open-air stage at its foot has staged the Karl May Festival every summer, with galloping horses, gunfights and Winnetou, drawing hundreds of thousands of spectators from across Germany.

    Read the complete Segeberg write-up.

  6. 6. Mölln lake town

    156 km in. The day opens at Mölln lake town.

    Mölln is the town where Till Eulenspiegel is said to have died, and the town has never let anyone forget it. The medieval trickster, a figure from German folk tales who made fools of everybody he met, is supposedly buried near the church of St Nicolai, and a gravestone with an owl and a mirror is a favourite photograph spot. The tales are of uncertain origin, but the town is proud of the legend, and you will find owls and mirrors on souvenirs, fountains and signs. It gives the little lakeside town a sense of humour that many places lack.

    Read the complete Mölln lake town write-up.

  7. 7. Zarrentin water stop

    178 km in.

    Zarrentin is the kind of lake town where you finish a ride sooner than planned because a bench by the water makes a better argument than the map does. It sits at the southern tip of the Schaalsee, on the Mecklenburg side of a line that for four decades was the most heavily guarded boundary in Europe. Riders coming from Hamburg cross it without noticing, apart from a subtle shift in the roads, which are a little rougher, a little emptier and lined with the tall avenue trees that Mecklenburg does so well.

    Read the complete Zarrentin water stop write-up.

  8. 8. Schaalsee biosphere edge

    188 km in.

    The edge of a biosphere reserve is not a line on the ground, it is a change in mood. Somewhere around here the farmland of western Mecklenburg stops being merely quiet and starts being protected, and you notice it in small ways: hedgerows left wild, meadows cut late, ponds with frogs in them, and the sight of cranes stalking across a stubble field as if they owned it. Which, for part of the year, they do.

    Read the complete Schaalsee biosphere edge write-up.

  9. 9. Ratzeburg island photo

    215 km in.

    Ratzeburg sits on an island, and you notice it only when you ride onto it. The old town lies on a long, narrow strip of land in the middle of the Ratzeburg Lake, joined to the shores by three causeways, and as you come in along one of them the water opens out on both sides, with the cathedral rising ahead like a ship's bridge. Photographers love it, with good reason: from the western shore the whole town hangs on the water, brick and green copper, with the reflection doubling it.

    Read the complete Ratzeburg island photo write-up.
