Why this ride

101 km from Idstein to Idstein, about 1 h 50 of riding. The stops in between: Watchtowers WP 3/45 to 3/52 including the, Watchtowers WP 4/1 to 4/19a including fort and back through Watchtowers WP 3/45 to 3/52 including the. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Watchtower WP 3/23*

    Start here.

    On the wooded ridges near Idstein, the Taunus Limes crossed some of its highest and most remote ground, and Watchtower WP 3/23 is one of the many sites along this stretch. The asterisk in the official label suggests a site whose exact location is uncertain or reconstructed from survey work, which is common along the frontier, where many towers survive only as faint mounds in the forest. Still, walking the Limes trail here, with the bank and ditch running through the beech woods, gives a real sense of the Roman border.

    Read the complete Watchtower WP 3/23* write-up.

  2. 2. Watchtowers WP 3/45 to 3/52 including the

    25 km in.

    High in the Taunus hills northwest of Frankfurt, the Upper German Limes runs along the main ridge, and here, between Glashütten and Schmitten, a series of watchtower sites and the remains of a small fort follow the frontier through dense beech and spruce forest. The bank and ditch of the Limes are still clearly visible, winding over the hills like a long scar, and the area is part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire World Heritage site. Nearby, on the Kleiner Feldberg, lie the remains of a Roman fort, and a few kilometres east, the Saalburg near Bad Homburg is a fully reconstructed fort with a museum, the best place in Germany to imagine life on the frontier.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 3/45 to 3/52 including the write-up.

  3. 3. Watchtowers WP 4/1 to 4/19a including fort

    51 km in.

    Kaiser Wilhelm II took a personal interest in the Roman frontier, and at the beginning of the twentieth century, he ordered the complete reconstruction of the Saalburg, a Roman fort on the crest of the Taunus near Bad Homburg. The result, with its walls, gates and buildings rebuilt on the original foundations, is the most complete reconstruction of a Roman fort in Germany and houses a museum of Roman life on the frontier. A few kilometres north-east, near Rosbach vor der Höhe, the ruins of the Kapersburg fort lie in the forest, and the watchtowers numbered WP 4/1 to 4/19a, on the section of the Limes running from the Taunus ridge toward the Wetterau, are part of the UNESCO listing of the Upper Germanic-Raetian Limes.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 4/1 to 4/19a including fort write-up.
