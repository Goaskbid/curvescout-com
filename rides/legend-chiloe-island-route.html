  2. 2. Ancud

    Ancud is the northern door to Chiloé, an island of rain, mist and wooden churches off the coast of southern Chile. You reach it by ferry from the mainland, crossing the Chacao Channel from Pargua, and the first thing you notice on arrival is how soft everything is: low green hills, grey-shingled houses, boats on the water and clouds that seem to rest on the roofs. The town of about twenty-eight thousand people sits on a bay on the north coast and has the practical services of a port, including fuel, supermarkets, banks and restaurants.

  3. 3. Dalcahue

    Dalcahue is a small port on the east coast of Chiloé, the big island off the coast of southern Chile, and its name means, in the Huilliche language, a place of dalcas, the plank boats that islanders once used. It sits on a channel facing the smaller island of Quinchao, and a short ferry crosses the water every few minutes, which gives the place its restless harbour life. On a grey morning the sea and sky blend in pewter, with gulls, fishing boats and the smell of salt and woodsmoke.

  4. 4. Castro

    Castro is the capital of Chiloé and has a character that is unlike anything else on mainland Chile. It is a port town of about thirty thousand people, climbing a hillside above a long inlet, and its most famous image is the row of palafitos, wooden houses built on stilts above the tidal water, painted in faded blues, yellows and reds. At high tide they seem to float; at low tide the mud flats show and the stilts are exposed, and boats lean on the shore at odd angles.

  5. 5. Quellón

    Quellón sits at the southern end of Chiloé, a working port town of about twenty-two thousand people, where the road simply stops. This is the end of Route 5, the Chilean section of the Pan-American Highway, which runs more than three thousand kilometres from the northern desert to here, and a sign on the waterfront marks the spot. For anyone who has ridden down the long thin country, the moment of reaching it is oddly emotional, even if the town itself is practical rather than pretty.
