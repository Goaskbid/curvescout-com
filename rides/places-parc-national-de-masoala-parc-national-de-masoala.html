Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Parc National de Masoala

    Madagascar's north-east corner is a great green thumb pushed into the Indian Ocean, and the Masoala peninsula is the wildest part of it. The national park covers a vast area of lowland rainforest, mangroves, and coast, and it holds the largest protected tract of the island's eastern rainforest, which is why it is bundled into the Rainforests of the Atsinanana World Heritage listing. Behind the beaches, the forest runs up steep hills in layer after layer of palms, vines, orchids and giant trees.

  2. 2. Parc National de Masoala

    Masoala is the great green thumb of Madagascar, a peninsula pushing into the Indian Ocean in the northeast of the island, and the national park that fills it is the biggest protected area in the country. The rainforest here comes down to the beach, and the border between trees and sea is almost nonexistent, so you can stand on white sand with palms behind you and coral in front. Antongil Bay, on the western side, is deep and wide, and humpback whales come there to breed between about July and September.

  3. 3. Parc National de Masoala

    At the northern tip of the Masoala peninsula, the land swings toward Cap Est, the eastern point of Madagascar, and the landscape changes. The rainforest here meets the Indian Ocean directly, with white beaches, coral reefs and rocky headlands, and in places the trees reach almost to the waterline. This is the exposed side of Masoala, battered by cyclones and strong swells, and it is a wilder coast than the sheltered bay on the west.
