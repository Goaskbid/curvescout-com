Why this ride

curated scenic road. hairpins, forest, lake, fall colors. A one-way ride of 20 km, Imaichi to Nikkō - roughly 22 min in the saddle. The highest point is about 1,350 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Imaichi

    Imaichi is the low-key gateway to Nikkō, a town on the plain where three old roads meet before the mountains begin. Officially it has been part of Nikkō city since a municipal merger in the 2000s, but locally it keeps its own name and character: a railway town, a market town, lots of ordinary streets and convenience stores, with the Nikkō mountains rising to the north-west and the Kinugawa river running nearby. For riders heading to the Irohazaka hairpins, it is where you sort out fuel, lunch and the last coffee before the climb.

  2. 2. Near Nikkō

    Forty-eight hairpins, each one marked with a sign bearing a letter of the old Japanese alphabet poem, the Iroha: that is Irohazaka, the road that climbs from the town of Nikkō to Lake Chūzenji. There are two roads, one for going up and one for going down, each a series of tight, steep bends through dense forest, and in autumn, when the maples turn red and gold, the scene is so beautiful that the traffic jams stretch for hours.
