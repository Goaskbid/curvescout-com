Why this ride

From Aldeaquemada it runs 332 km to Aldeaquemada; allow about 5 h 43 of riding. On the way it passes Renaissance Monumental Ensembles of Úbeda, Abrigo del.melgar, Letreros de los mártires and back through Abrigo del.melgar. The highest point is about 1,400 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. morciguilla de la Cepera

    Aldeaquemada means burnt village, which tells you something about the history of this lonely corner of Jaén, and the shelter named Morciguilla de la Cepera sits in the hills about ten kilometres away, in the Sierra Morena. That range forms the long dark edge between the high plateau of La Mancha and the Andalusian lowlands, and its slopes are some of the richest places in Spain for rock painting. Shelters here include both Levantine figures and the simpler schematic ones, and a number are protected as part of the UNESCO-listed rock art of the Iberian Mediterranean basin.

  2. 2. Renaissance Monumental Ensembles of Úbeda

    Úbeda stands on a ridge above an ocean of olive trees. In every direction, the province of Jaén rolls away in silver-green rows, millions of trees planted in geometric lines up to the horizon, and on this sea floats a town of honey-coloured stone that looks as if it had been lifted from Florence. In the sixteenth century, powerful local families who served the Spanish crown, above all Francisco de los Cobos, secretary to Emperor Charles V, poured their wealth into palaces and churches here, and the architect Andrés de Vandelvira gave them a Renaissance style of rare purity. Úbeda and its smaller neighbour Baeza were inscribed together on the UNESCO list in 2003.

  3. 3. Abrigo del.melgar

    Quesada is a white town at the foot of the Sierra de Cazorla, in the province of Jaén, surrounded by an ocean of olive trees. The groves stretch to the horizon in every direction, a landscape that makes Jaén the world's largest producer of olive oil. Behind the town, the mountains rise steeply, with pine forest and limestone cliffs, and the Guadalquivir river has its source not far away. The Abrigo del Melgar lies in the hills just outside the town.

  4. 4. Letreros de los mártires

    Out in the high north-east of Granada province, Huéscar feels a long way from the Alhambra and the beaches of the Costa Tropical. This is the altiplano, a broad upland of wheat fields, almond trees and pale badlands at around a thousand metres, ringed by mountains, with the great limestone hump of La Sagra rising to the north. The rock shelter called Letreros de los Mártires lies in this country, a dozen or so kilometres from the town.
