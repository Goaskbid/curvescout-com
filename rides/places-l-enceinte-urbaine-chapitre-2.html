Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. L’enceinte urbaine

    Briançon sits at a height of more than thirteen hundred metres, which makes it the highest city in France, and it has the defences to match. The old town on its hilltop is ringed by walls built to the plans of Vauban, Louis XIV's military engineer, in the decades after 1700, and together with a fort across the valley they make a showpiece of the works that UNESCO listed in 2008. The walls are still intact, the gates still work, and from the ramparts the view over the meeting of valleys is spectacular.

  2. 2. La place forte de Mont-Dauphin

    Vauban built this in a hurry, and you can feel the urgency in the plan. After the Duke of Savoy invaded Dauphiné in 1692, Louis XIV's great military engineer chose a spur of rock above the meeting of the Durance and the Guil rivers and laid out a fortified town on a strict grid, with ramparts, bastions and a parade ground. Mont-Dauphin was meant to close the Durance valley to any army coming from Italy, and it is one of the twelve groups of Vauban fortifications that UNESCO listed in 2008. The buildings are in a rosy-grey marble from the nearby quarries of Guillestre, and in evening light they glow like embers.

  3. 3. Chapitre

    Le Chapitre is a small name on a map of the southern Alps, in the Hautes-Alpes department, in the green trough of the Champsaur where the Drac river gathers itself before heading to Grenoble. Saint-Bonnet-en-Champsaur and La Roche-des-Arnauds are the nearest villages, each under two thousand people, and Gap, the departmental capital with forty-odd thousand, is about ten kilometres away.
