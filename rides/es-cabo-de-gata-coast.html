  1. 1. Almería

    Almería has a dry, bright, slightly North African feel that makes it unlike most Spanish cities, and the reason is simple: it gets less rain than almost anywhere in Europe. The city lies on a wide bay with the arid Sierra Alhamilla behind it, and the whole landscape is in shades of sand, ochre and pale grey. In summer it is hot and bright, in winter it is gentle, and the sea breeze keeps the air moving.

  2. 2. San José

    San José is a small whitewashed settlement on the dry southeastern tip of Spain, tucked inside the Cabo de Gata-Níjar Natural Park. This is not the green Andalusia of the postcards. The landscape is volcanic, bare and honey-coloured, with agave and prickly pear, dry riverbeds and ragged coastal hills that fall into the sea in dark cliffs. It is one of the driest places in Europe, and the park is also a UNESCO biosphere reserve, which says something about its odd natural value.

  3. 3. Carboneras

    Carboneras does not try to look pretty, and that is partly why I like it. It is a working town on the Almería coast, with a fishing harbour, a cement works and a power station on its edge, and a long beach that locals actually use. Seven thousand or so people live here, and on most days it feels like a normal Spanish town that happens to be on the edge of a very beautiful natural park.
