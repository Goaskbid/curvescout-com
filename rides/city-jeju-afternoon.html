Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Jeju

    Jeju is a volcanic island off the south coast of Korea, and everything on it is shaped by that fact. Hallasan, the central cone and the highest mountain in South Korea, rises to about 1,950 metres and is visible from nearly every corner of the island on a clear day, and the whole land slopes from it to the sea in long, easy lines. Jeju City, on the north coast, is the main gateway, with an airport that ranks among the busiest in the world and a harbour with ferry links to the mainland.

  2. 2. Hallasan Natural Reserve

    Hallasan rises from the middle of Jeju like a shield laid face-up on the sea. At 1,947 metres it is the highest mountain in South Korea, a massive shield volcano with a crater lake at the top, called Baengnokdam, and a ring of smaller cones on its flanks, perhaps three hundred in all, which the islanders call oreum. The upper slopes were declared a nature reserve and are part of the World Heritage listing of the island's volcanoes and lava tubes. Camellias, Korean fir, azaleas and a great variety of mosses grow in layers by altitude, and in winter the summit is under snow, a surprise on an island with palm trees at its coast.

  3. 3. Jeju Volcanic Island and Lava Tubes

    Jeju is a volcano that has decided to be an island. Rising from the sea about ninety kilometres south of the Korean mainland, it is crowned by Hallasan, an extinct volcano of 1,947 metres, the highest peak in South Korea, with a crater lake at the top and a skirt of about three hundred smaller cones scattered across the landscape. UNESCO listed the island's volcanic landscapes and lava tubes in 2007, and the combination of black basalt, green fields and blue sea is the island's signature.

  4. 4. Geomunoreum Lava Tube System - 2

    Underneath the green hills on the north side of Jeju run some of the longest lava tubes in the world, and Manjanggul is the best known. When the volcano of Geomunoreum erupted, in the distant past, rivers of molten rock flowed north toward the sea, and as the surface cooled and hardened, the liquid inside drained away, leaving long hollow tunnels. The Manjanggul tube runs for more than seven kilometres, and the section open to visitors, about a kilometre, is vast, wide enough for a bus and as tall as a house.

  5. 5. Geomunoreum Lava Tube System - 1

    Under the northeastern slopes of Jeju there is a hollow world made by fire. Geomunoreum is a small volcanic cone, one of the hundreds that dot the island's flanks, and in the long prehistory of the island it spilled lava that ran downhill in rivers. The surface of each flow cooled and hardened while the molten rock inside kept moving, and when it finally drained away it left long empty tunnels. The Geomunoreum lava tube system, a family of caves including Manjanggul, Gimnyeonggul and others, is among the finest in the world, and it is the core of the Jeju Volcanic Island and Lava Tubes World Heritage site, inscribed in 2007.
