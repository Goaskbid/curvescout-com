Why this ride

From Davos it runs 488 km to Davos; allow about 7 h 47 of riding. The stops in between: Rhaetian Railway in the Albula / Bernina L, Swiss Tectonic Arena Sardona, back through Swiss Tectonic Arena Sardona and back through Rhaetian Railway in the Albula / Bernina L. It tops out around 2,500 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

The roads

Grand Tour of Switzerland

From Davos to Rhaetian Railway in the Albula / Bernina L is 67 km on Via Engiadina. Up about 980 m to 2,208 m, then 740 m back down the far side. Cold up there even in July - pack a warm layer and check it is open.

The stops

  1. 1. Davos

    Start here.

    At about 1,560 metres, Davos is often called the highest town in Europe, and it is certainly one of the most unusual. It lies in a long, broad valley of Graubünden, with ski slopes on both sides, a lake, a main street that looks like a motorway, and a mix of grand old sanatoria, apartment blocks and hotels that tells the story of the town.

    Read the complete Davos write-up.

  2. 2. Rhaetian Railway in the Albula / Bernina L

    67 km in.

    The Rhaetian Railway's Albula and Bernina lines were built in the early twentieth century to connect the Engadine with the rest of Switzerland and with Italy, and they are masterpieces of mountain railway engineering, with spiral tunnels, viaducts and gradients that seem impossible for a train. They were listed by UNESCO in 2008 together with the landscapes they cross, and St. If you ride in this area, you will see the red trains everywhere, and you will often be riding beside them.

    Read the complete Rhaetian Railway in the Albula / Bernina L write-up.

  3. 4. Swiss Tectonic Arena Sardona

    243 km in.

    Twice a year, in spring and autumn, the sun shines through a hole in a mountain ridge and lights up the church tower of Elm, a village in the Sernf valley of canton Glarus. The hole is the Martinsloch, a natural window in the Tschingelhörner, and it sits right on one of the most famous geological features in the world: a sharp line along the cliffs, clearly visible from the valley, where older rock lies on top of much younger rock. This is the Glarus thrust, and it helped geologists understand how mountains are built by the stacking of enormous slabs of the earth's crust. The Swiss Tectonic Arena Sardona was listed by UNESCO in 2008.
