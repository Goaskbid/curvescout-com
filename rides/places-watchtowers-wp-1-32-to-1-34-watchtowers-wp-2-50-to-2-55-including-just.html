Why this ride

157 km from Melsbach to Melsbach, about 2 h 43 of riding. It goes by way of Watchtower WP 1/73, Watchtowers WP 2/17 to 2/19 including fort, Watchtowers WP 2/50 to 2/55 including Just and back through Watchtowers WP 2/17 to 2/19 including fort. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Watchtowers WP 1/32 to 1/34

    Start here.

    Just north of Neuwied, the Westerwald begins to climb out of the Rhine valley, and the Roman frontier ran up through the woods from its starting point on the river, rising and falling over the low ridges. The Limes here is the Upper German section in its earliest stretch, where watchtowers 1/32 to 1/34 stood close together on the hillside above the Wied, a small river that runs through a pretty, wooded valley to join the Rhine. Melsbach, a village of two thousand people next to the point, lies in this valley, and the surrounding hills are a mix of beech forest, meadow and the fields of the Neuwied basin.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 1/32 to 1/34 write-up.

  2. 2. Watchtower WP 1/73

    26 km in.

    The Roman frontier in Germany starts at the Rhine near Rheinbrohl and climbs into the hills of the Westerwald, and Watchtower WP 1/73, near Hillscheid, belongs to that first stretch. The numbers tell you where you are: section one, tower seventy-three. All that survives is a low mound in the woods, but in the second and third centuries it was a stone tower with a view over the frontier line and, behind it, the palisade and ditch that ran across the landscape. The Upper Germanic-Raetian Limes has been part of the UNESCO Frontiers of the Roman Empire since 2005, and this is where it all begins.

    Read the complete Watchtower WP 1/73 write-up.

  3. 3. Watchtowers WP 2/17 to 2/19 including fort

    57 km in.

    In the Taunus and Westerwald country of Rhineland-Palatinate, the Limes crosses hills so wooded that it is easy to miss. The numbering here records three watchtower positions and a fort, and from the start of the sequence you can follow the frontier as a line of mounds through forest and field.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 2/17 to 2/19 including fort write-up.

  4. 4. Watchtowers WP 2/50 to 2/55 including Just

    80 km in.

    Bad Schwalbach is a spa town that never quite lost its nineteenth-century manners: a long, leafy valley, a Kurpark with pavilions, and iron-rich springs that once drew the fashionable from across Europe to take the waters. Just above it, on the high ground of the Taunus, the Roman frontier ran through the forests, and the stretch catalogued as watchtowers WP 2/50 to 2/55 passes within a few kilometres of the town. Here the Upper German Limes followed the ridge, with wooden and later stone towers set at intervals within sight of one another, a palisade in front, and a ditch and bank added later. In the beech woods you can still trace the low line of the bank in places, a quiet scar running through the trees for kilometre after kilometre.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 2/50 to 2/55 including Just write-up.
