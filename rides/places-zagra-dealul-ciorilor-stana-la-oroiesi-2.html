Why this ride

From Nimigea de Sus it runs 248 km to Nimigea de Sus; allow about 4 h 17 of riding. The stops in between: Bârsău Mare - Cetate, Stâna - La Oroieși and back through Bârsău Mare - Cetate. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Zagra - Dealul Ciorilor

    Zagra lies on the southern skirts of the Rodna Mountains, in the north-east of Transylvania, and the first thing you notice is the size of the range behind it. The Rodna massif is the highest part of the Eastern Carpathians, a long ridge of crystalline rock with many peaks above two thousand metres, the highest of them, Pietrosu Rodnei, reaching 2,303. A national park protects its glacial lakes, alpine meadows and a rich flora that includes edelweiss and several species that grow nowhere else. The villages at its foot are quiet, with wooden fences and haystacks.

  2. 2. Bârsău Mare - Cetate

    In the north of Transylvania, the Someș river winds through a valley of green hills, woods and villages with wooden churches and haystacks, and on a hilltop near the village of Bârsău Mare, the faint remains of a Roman fortification overlook the valley. This was part of the northern frontier of Roman Dacia, the province created after Trajan's conquest in the early second century, and it is one of the sites included in the Frontiers of the Roman Empire, Dacia, listed by UNESCO in 2024.

  3. 3. Stâna - La Oroieși

    On the hills above Zalău, in the county of Sălaj in northwestern Romania, the Roman Empire reached its northern limit in Dacia. After Trajan conquered the Dacian kingdom in 106, the Romans built a frontier system along the edges of the new province, with forts, watchtowers, walls and ditches, and in 2024 UNESCO listed it as the Frontiers of the Roman Empire, Dacia. The site at Stâna, La Oroieși, is one of many small elements of that frontier: a watchtower or defensive line on a ridge, where soldiers watched over the approaches from the free Dacian lands beyond. There is little to see on the ground, but the location, with views over the hills, explains why it was chosen.
