Why this ride

From Ronchamp it runs 339 km to Ronchamp; allow about 5 h 39 of riding. The stops in between: Fortifications of Vauban, Lac de Chalain and back through Fortifications of Vauban. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Fortifications of Vauban → Lac de Chalain

From Fortifications of Vauban to Lac de Chalain is 77 km, mostly on N 83. Mostly uphill - about 440 m, topping out near 580 m. Water alongside for much of it - pick one good pull-out and stop.

The stops

  1. 1. Chapelle Notre-Dame-du-Haut de Ronchamp

    Start here.

    Notre-Dame-du-Haut sits on a hill above Ronchamp, at the foot of the Vosges, and it is one of the strangest and most beautiful buildings of the twentieth century. Le Corbusier designed it in the early 1950s and it was consecrated in 1955. The white walls curve and lean, the roof sweeps up like a hull or a hat, and the thick walls are pierced by small deep windows filled with coloured glass. Inside, light falls in coloured pools on the floor, and the whole room feels carved rather than built. There has been a pilgrim chapel on this hill for centuries, and the earlier church was destroyed in the war, which is what led to the commission.

  2. 2. Fortifications of Vauban

    94 km in.

    Victor Hugo was born in Besançon in 1802, and so were the Lumière brothers, the pioneers of cinema, a generation later. The capital of Franche-Comté sits in a tight loop of the river Doubs, with the old town on the peninsula inside the loop and the citadel on a rock at the neck, closing the only side not protected by water. The citadel was built by Vauban, Louis XIV's great military engineer, after the French conquest of the region in the late seventeenth century, and it is one of the twelve groups of Fortifications of Vauban listed by UNESCO in 2008.

    Read the complete Fortifications of Vauban write-up.

  3. 3. Lac de Chalain

    171 km in.

    Lac de Chalain is the largest natural lake in the French Jura, a long, green-blue lake set in a valley among forested limestone cliffs, and its shores were home to farming villages built on piles thousands of years ago. The remains of these villages, preserved in the lake bed, are part of the Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps, listed by UNESCO in 2011. In the 1900s, when the lake level was lowered for a hydroelectric project, a dugout canoe and the remains of houses were found, and excavations since then have revealed a remarkable picture of life in the Neolithic.
