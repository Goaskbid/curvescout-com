Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Blairgowrie

    Blairgowrie is where the Perthshire farmland ends and the Highlands begin to show their hand. The town sits on the River Ericht, which comes tumbling out of Glenshee and, in the old days, turned the wheels of dozens of jute and flax mills; the stone mill buildings still line the river. Behind it the hills close in, and in front lies Strathmore, a broad, red-soiled plain that grows most of Scotland's raspberries. In July the fields are full of pickers, and you can smell the fruit from the road with your visor up.

  2. 2. Snow Roads Scenic Route point 2

    Braemar is a small Highland village at the head of Royal Deeside, set among pines and heather hills about three hundred and fifty metres up, and it is known for cold. It has recorded some of the lowest temperatures in Britain, and winter here is serious, with snow often lying on the ground for months. In summer it is a pleasant, busy village, with outdoor shops, cafes, a couple of pubs and a petrol station, though the stock and hours are limited.

  3. 3. Snow Roads Scenic Route point 3

    The Lecht road, the A939 over the high moor between Cock Bridge and Tomintoul, is the star of the Snow Roads. It climbs to about 640 metres, with a steep gradient on the Cock Bridge side that was among the steepest in Britain, and its ski area is one of Scotland's smallest. The moors above are brown and purple in summer, bare and white in winter, and the wind blows constantly. The nearest town of any size is Ballater, about twenty kilometres back, and services are few, so keep the tank full.

  4. 4. Snow Roads Scenic Route point 4

    Out on the moors between Tomintoul and Grantown the Snow Roads takes on a different tone, quieter and less dramatic than the Lecht but just as wide. The land here is gentle, with rolling hills, scattered farms and the occasional stand of birch, and the River Avon winds through the valley, cold and clear. The high point of the whole route is behind, and this is a stretch for relaxed riding, with views to the Cairngorm plateau away to the south.

  5. 5. Grantown on Spey

    Grantown-on-Spey was a planned town, founded in 1765 by Sir James Grant of Grant, who wanted a tidy settlement for weavers and a base for his estate. It is the end of the Snow Roads, and it is a good one.
