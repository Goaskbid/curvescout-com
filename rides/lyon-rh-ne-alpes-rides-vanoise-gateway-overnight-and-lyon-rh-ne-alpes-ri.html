Why this ride

From Tignieu it runs 812 km to Montluel; allow about 15 h 27 of riding. It goes by way of in Ceyzériat, Scenic stretch near Tarare and Boën-sur-Lignon. The climbs: in Neuville-les-Dames and near Belleville.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fuel stop near Tignieu

    A little north of Tignieu-Jameyzieu, the land runs down to the Rhône, and the river makes a broad, braided sweep across the plain before it reaches Lyon. On the far bank lies the Plaine de l'Ain, a wide gravel terrace of farms and industrial zones, and upstream the hills of the Bugey begin to rise. It is a landscape of crossings: bridges at Loyettes and Lagnieu, gravel pits turned into lakes, and long views towards the mountains.

  2. 2. Food stop in Ceyzériat

    Ceyzériat lies at the foot of the Revermont, just east of Bourg-en-Bresse, where the flat Bresse plain meets the first limestone ridge of the Jura. Behind the village, the wooded slopes rise steeply, and the hill of Mont July above it offers a viewpoint over the Bresse, with the Alps sometimes visible far to the south-east on clear days.

  3. 3. High point in Neuville-les-Dames

    The name gives away the story. Until the Revolution, Neuville-les-Dames was the seat of a chapter of noble canonesses, a community of aristocratic women who lived here under a religious rule but without the strict vows of nuns. To be admitted, a young woman had to prove several generations of noble ancestry on both sides of the family. They lived in comfortable houses around the church, wore elegant habits and could leave to marry if they wished. The chapter was dissolved in the 1790s, but the dames stayed in the village's name.

  4. 4. Scenic stretch near Tarare

    Muslin made Tarare's fortune. From the eighteenth century onwards the town wove fine, light cotton cloth that was sold across Europe, and every few years it still celebrates the fact with the Fête des Mousselines, when the streets and façades disappear under kilometres of draped fabric in a riot of colour. Outside festival time, Tarare is a quiet valley town in the hills between the Beaujolais and the Lyonnais, with old mill buildings and a long main street.

  5. 5. Near Boën-sur-Lignon, on the way back

    The Lignon du Forez comes out of the Monts du Forez with a cold, clean hurry, and by the time it reaches the plain around Boën it has cut itself a shallow gorge through old volcanic rock. This is the country west of Lyon and north of Saint-Étienne that most people cross on the autoroute without noticing, and that is exactly why it is pleasant. The Forez plain is a wide, flat bowl of pasture and orchards, and the mountains stand along its western edge like a wall with the road folded into the foot of it.

  6. 6. Photo stop near La Ricamarie

    La Ricamarie is a former mining town on the south-western edge of Saint-Étienne, where the coal basin meets the first slopes of the Pilat. For more than a century, the pits here produced the coal that fed the factories of the Stéphanois region, and the town still carries the marks of that history: workers' houses, old slag heaps grown over with trees, and a strong sense of community.

  7. 7. Neuville-sur-Saône

    Julius Caesar noticed the Saône. In his account of the Gallic Wars he wrote that the river flows with such incredible slowness that the eye cannot tell in which direction it is moving, and two thousand years later, standing on the quay at Neuville, you can see what he meant. The river here is wide, brown-green and almost still, and the reflections of the plane trees on the bank barely ripple.

  9. 9. Fuel stop near Montluel

    Montluel is the kind of small French town that most people only see as a sign on the A42, which is a shame. It climbs a slope above the little River Sereine, on the escarpment that the locals call the Côtière, the step where the Rhône plain to the south rises up onto the plateau of the Dombes. For centuries this was border country: Montluel belonged to the House of Savoy and only became French with the Treaty of Lyon in 1601, and the old centre still has the narrow, defensive feel of a frontier town.

  10. 10. Food stop near Saint-Didier-sur-Chalaronne

    Saint-Didier-sur-Chalaronne sits just above the Saône, where the little Chalaronne river ends its slow journey across the Dombes and slips into the bigger river near Thoissey. From the edge of the village the land drops to the Val de Saône, a broad floodplain of meadows and poplar rows, and across the water the slopes of the Beaujolais rise in neat green ranks of vines.

  11. 11. High point near Belleville

    Belleville-en-Beaujolais lies on the Saône at the foot of the Beaujolais hills, and it has one treasure that surprises most visitors: an eighteenth-century Hôtel-Dieu, a charitable hospital with great wards lined with curtained beds, a chapel and an apothecary with its original jars. It served the sick for more than two centuries and is now a museum, one of the best-preserved hospitals of its kind in France.

  12. 12. Scenic stretch near Aveize

    High in the Monts du Lyonnais, Aveize looks out over a rolling plateau of meadows, hedgerows and small woods, where dairy farming has shaped the landscape for centuries. It is a modest village with a church and scattered farms, typical of these hills west of Lyon, where the air is fresher than in the city and the pace of life slower.

  13. 13. Near Sury-le-Comtal, on the way back

    The Forez plain is one of those places you do not notice until you are in it: a wide, flat basin of the upper Loire, ringed by the Monts du Forez to the west and the Monts du Lyonnais to the east. This stop lies on its southern half, between Précieux, Sury-le-Comtal and Saint-Romain-le-Puy, in a country of fields, hedges, scattered farms and, here and there, the glint of a pond.

  14. 14. Photo stop near Le Péage-de-Roussillon

    In 1564, during a royal tour of France, the young king Charles IX stayed at the château of Roussillon, on the hill above what is now Le Péage-de-Roussillon, and signed an edict there that fixed the start of the year on 1 January across the kingdom. Until then, different regions began the year at Easter, Christmas or other dates. The château still stands, a Renaissance building with an elegant courtyard, overlooking the Rhône valley.

    On the west bank of the Saône, opposite Neuville, the land rises quickly from the river into the Monts d'Or. This stop sits on that slope between Chasselay and Albigny-sur-Saône, where the last suburbs of Lyon fade into orchards, stone walls and small vineyards, and the hills begin to glow golden in the evening light.

  16. 16. Back to Jura Wine

    The Dombes plateau, north-east of Lyon, is the strangest piece of farmland in France. Spread across it are some thousand-odd ponds, made by hand over the centuries, filled in winter, fished in autumn, and then planted with cereals in a rotation that has been going for hundreds of years. Medieval monks and landowners dug them to farm carp, and the system still works, which is why the horizon here is a patchwork of reed, water and sky, full of herons and migrating ducks.
