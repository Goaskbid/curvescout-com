Why this ride

From Socovos it runs 286 km to Socovos; allow about 4 h 56 of riding. It goes by way of Lavadero Tello III, maina and back through Lavadero Tello III. The highest point is about 1,200 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Benizar IV

    Water tells you where to look in this country. Benizar is a hamlet in the municipality of Moratalla, and the ravine below it is one of the many seasonal streams that run down towards the Segura, dry for most of the year and a torrent for a day or two after heavy rain. The shelters on its sides are small hollows in the rock, shaded in the afternoon and warm in the morning, which is just what you would want if you were a hunter looking for somewhere dry to sit and paint.

  2. 2. Lavadero Tello III

    Los Vélez is a corner of Almería that most visitors to the coast never see: a high, dry country of pine forest, almond groves and pale limestone peaks, with the Sierra de María rising above two thousand metres. The Lavadero Tello, the washing place at Tello, is on the edge of that mountain land, near María and Vélez-Blanco, and the shelters of the area hold some of the most famous paintings in southern Spain. At the Cueva de los Letreros, near Vélez-Blanco, a small figure with outstretched arms holding a rainbow-like shape is the Indalo, adopted as the symbol of Almería and painted on houses across the province.

  3. 3. maina

    Maina is an obscure name on the map in the Murcian hills north-west of Totana, where the Sierra Espuña rises above the valley of the Guadalentín. It refers to a rock shelter or painted site in the dry limestone country, part of the cluster of prehistoric art sites around here included in the World Heritage listing of Levantine rock art. As with many such places, there is often little to see without a guide, but the landscape around it is worth the ride.
