Why this ride

From Donostia / San Sebastián it runs 599 km to Donostia / San Sebastián; allow about 9 h 48 of riding. It goes by way of Collégiale de Ziortza, Chaussée et Tunnel de San Adrian, Santimamiñe and Cathédrale de Saint Jacques Apôtre. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

The roads

Basque Coast Route

The 70 km between San Sebastián and Collégiale de Ziortza on Kantauriko autobidea. You climb most of the way: 360 m, up to 292 m. Water alongside for much of it - pick one good pull-out and stop.

The stops

  1. 1. San Sebastián

    Start here.

    San Sebastián, Donostia to the Basques, wraps itself around one of the loveliest urban bays in Europe. The Concha, a near-perfect shell of golden sand, is enclosed by two green hills, Urgull on the east with its old fort and statue of Christ, and Igueldo on the west, reached by a vintage funicular. A promenade with white balustrades runs the full length of the beach, and on a fine evening half the town seems to be walking it. In the nineteenth century the Spanish royal family made the town their summer retreat, which explains the elegant belle époque buildings along the seafront.

    Read the complete San Sebastian write-up.

  2. 2. Collégiale de Ziortza

    70 km in.

    High on a green hillside above the village of Bolibar, in the Biscayan interior, the collegiate church of Ziortza looks out over a valley of meadows, farmhouses and eucalyptus and pine plantations. It is a tranquil, solid place, a church and cloister built of grey stone with a fortified look, and it stands on the Camino del Norte, the coastal route to Santiago, so you will often see pilgrims resting on the benches outside.

    Read the complete Collégiale de Ziortza write-up.

  3. 3. Chaussée et Tunnel de San Adrian

    154 km in.

    Imagine a mountain with a hole through it, and a medieval road running straight through the hole. The tunnel of San Adrián is a natural cave passage near the crest of the Aizkorri range, on the border of Gipuzkoa and Álava, and for centuries it carried one of the main routes between the Castilian plateau and the Basque coast. Pilgrims on the inland Camino de Santiago, merchants, armies and kings all passed through it, and a small chapel was built inside the cave. The old paved road leading up to it survives in sections, worn smooth by centuries of hooves and boots.

    Read the complete Chaussée et Tunnel de San Adrian write-up.

  4. 4. Santimamiñe

    260 km in.

    In 1916, a group of boys exploring a hillside in the Basque Country found paintings deep inside a cave, and Santimamiñe became one of the most important prehistoric sites in the region. The cave, near Kortezubi in the Urdaibai valley, contains Magdalenian paintings of bison, horses, goats and deer, made around fourteen thousand years ago, and it is part of the UNESCO listing of cave art of northern Spain. To protect the paintings, the cave itself is closed to the public, but a visitor centre offers a virtual tour that recreates the interior in detail, with guides explaining the art and the people who made it.

    Read the complete Santimamiñe write-up.

  5. 5. Cathédrale de Saint Jacques Apôtre

    299 km in.

    Down in the Casco Viejo of Bilbao, where the old streets run in a tight grid beside the Nervión, the cathedral of Santiago stands at the heart of the Siete Calles, the seven original streets of the medieval town. It is a Gothic church, rebuilt after a fire in the sixteenth century, with a neo-Gothic façade and spire added later, and it sits so snugly among the tall houses that you often see it only when you are standing right in front of it.
