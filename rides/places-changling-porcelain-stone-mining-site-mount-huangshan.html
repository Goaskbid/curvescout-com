Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Changling Porcelain Stone Mining Site

    The word kaolin comes from Gaoling, a village in the hills north-east of Jingdezhen, in Jiangxi province, where the white clay used in making porcelain was mined for centuries. Porcelain needed two ingredients: kaolin and porcelain stone, a rock that, when crushed and refined, gave the paste its translucency. The hills around Jingdezhen were full of both, and the Changling porcelain stone mining site is one of the places where the stone was quarried, crushed by water-powered hammers and refined into bricks that were carried to the kilns of the porcelain capital of the world.

  2. 2. Jingdezhen Handicraft Porcelain Industry S

    For a thousand years, the best porcelain in China, and for much of that time the best in the world, came from Jingdezhen. The city, in the north east of Jiangxi province, grew up around imperial kilns that produced fine ceramics for the emperors, and its blue and white porcelain was exported across Asia, the Middle East and Europe. The secret of its success lay partly in the hills around the city, where the white clay known as kaolin was mined. The word kaolin itself comes from Gaoling, a village in the hills north east of Jingdezhen, where the clay was first dug. The point on this ride lies in that countryside, between the city and the old clay mines, in a landscape of wooded hills, tea plantations, rivers and villages.

  3. 3. Ancient Villages in Southern Anhui - Xidi

    Xidi is a village that seems to have been left alone by the twentieth century. It lies in Yi county, on the southern side of the Yellow Mountains in Anhui, a landscape of green hills and rice fields where merchants of the Huizhou region made fortunes in salt, tea, timber and ink, and spent them at home on houses. The Hu clan, who founded the village, built lanes of whitewashed, black-tiled houses with high walls, carved wooden screens, small courtyards, and stone-framed doors, and in the sixteenth century they put up a great stone memorial arch in honour of an official of the clan. The name means west stream, after the water that runs through the village in channels beside the lanes.

  4. 4. Mount Huangshan

    Huangshan, the Yellow Mountain, rises in the south of Anhui province from a flat region of tea fields and villages, and its granite peaks, gnarled pines and seas of cloud have made it one of the most painted mountains in China. The highest of its seventy-two peaks, Lotus Peak, stands at about 1,864 metres. What makes the mountain special is the combination of tall, sharp, bare rock and the pine trees that grow out of the cracks, shaped by wind and weather into strange, twisted forms, each of which has a name, such as the Welcoming Guest Pine. UNESCO listed it in 1990.
