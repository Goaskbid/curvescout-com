Why this ride

From Zalău it runs 79 km to Zalău; allow about 1 h 22 of riding. The stops in between: Ciglean - Vârful Cigleanului 2, Cliț - Fața Chicerii and back through Ciglean - Vârful Cigleanului 2. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Zalău - Poieniță

    Poieniță is a small poiană, which is the Romanian word for a clearing in the forest, and a clearing is a good place to begin talking about mushrooms. Around Zalău, from late summer to the first frosts, the woods are full of people with baskets and plastic bags, wandering slowly between the beech trunks with their eyes on the ground. The Poieniță point lies a few kilometres outside the town, on the wooded edge of the Meseș foothills, and it belongs to a tradition of foraging that has not died out with the arrival of supermarkets.

  2. 2. Ciglean - Vârful Cigleanului 2

    The Roman province of Dacia had a northern frontier that ran along the hills of what is now Sălaj county, in north-western Romania, around the great fortress of Porolissum. Here the Limes was not a continuous wall but a network of watchtowers, small forts and earthworks set on hilltops with views over the valleys. Vârful Cigleanului, a hilltop near the village of Ciglean, close to Jibou, held one of these watchtowers, and the number in the name marks it as one of several in the area.

  3. 3. Cliț - Fața Chicerii

    Northern Sălaj is quiet country: low wooded hills, hay meadows, plum orchards and villages strung along the Someș river and its side valleys, with wooden churches and haystacks built around poles in the fields. Cliț is a small village near Băbeni, and the point named Fața Chicerii lies in the hills above it. Names in this list follow the pattern of the Romanian archaeological record, a village and then the local name of a hillside, and in this part of Sălaj many such points belong to the line of watchtowers and small forts of the Roman frontier of Dacia, which UNESCO recognised in 2024.
