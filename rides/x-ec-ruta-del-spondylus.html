  1. 1. Guayaquil

    Guayaquil is the biggest city in Ecuador, a hot, noisy and energetic port on the Guayas river, a few degrees south of the equator, and the place where a rider's journey up the coast begins. It is a commercial city, and proud of it. In the nineteenth century, cocoa made fortunes here, and the grand houses and the old customs building along the river recall that boom. The waterfront walk, the Malecón, runs for more than two kilometres along the river with gardens, fountains and a lighthouse, and on a warm evening it is full of families.

  2. 2. Montanita

    Montañita is the surf village of Ecuador's Pacific coast, a small place of sand streets, bamboo bars and beach shacks where the main sounds are waves and music. It lies on the Ruta del Spondylus, the coastal highway that runs from the south of Ecuador's coast to its northern shores, named for the spiny oyster shell that pre-Columbian peoples valued above gold. Traders carried Spondylus shells from here all the way to the Andes and Mexico, where they were used in rituals and offerings, and the shell has given the road its name.

  3. 3. Puerto Lopez

    Puerto López is a fishing town on a wide bay, with a long beach, a pier and brightly painted boats pulled up on the sand, and the sort of slow, cheerful atmosphere that settles over a place whose business is the sea. The town sits on the Ruta del Spondylus and is the main base for visiting the Machalilla National Park, which protects a stretch of dry coastal forest, beaches and the offshore Isla de la Plata. From here, small boats take visitors out to the island, which is sometimes called the poor man's Galápagos for its blue-footed boobies, frigatebirds and nesting albatrosses.
