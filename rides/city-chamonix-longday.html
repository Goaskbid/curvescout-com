Why this ride

385 km from Chamonix-Mont-Blanc to Chamonix-Mont-Blanc, about 6 h 38 of riding. It goes by way of Le Crêt de Chatillon, Secteur des Mongets, The Architectural Work of Le Corbusier and Petite villa au bord du lac Léman. The highest point is about 2,150 m. It crosses from France into Switzerland - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Chamonix

    Mont Blanc does not so much loom over Chamonix as lean on it. The valley is a deep, narrow trench of the Arve river with the highest peak in the Alps, just under 4,810 metres, standing right at the top of it, and the glaciers come down so close that you can see the cracks in the ice from the main street. Modern mountaineering has a birthday here. In 1786 two local men, Jacques Balmat and Michel Paccard, reached the summit for the first time, and the town has been selling the idea of going up high ever since. In 1924 it also hosted the first Winter Olympics.

  2. 2. Le Crêt de Chatillon

    The Crêt de Châtillon is the summit of the Semnoz, the long wooded mountain that rises above the western shore of Lake Annecy, and from the top, at around 1,700 metres, the view on a clear day is one of the finest in the French Alps: the whole lake below, the Bauges and the Aravis around, and, to the east, Mont Blanc in all its glory. Sites with names like Châtillon and Les Mongets along this shore preserve the remains of villages built on the edge of the lake thousands of years ago.

  3. 3. Secteur des Mongets

    Sevrier, on the western shore of Lake Annecy, is home to an unusual industry: bells. The Paccard foundry, which has been casting bells since the late eighteenth century and moved to Sevrier in the 1980s, has made bells for churches and carillons all over the world, and its museum explains how they are designed, cast and tuned. It is a curious contrast to the quiet lakeshore just below the village, where the Secteur des Mongets marks the remains of a prehistoric village built on the edge of the lake, now under water. It is one of the sites included in the World Heritage listing of the Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps, and divers have found wooden piles, pottery and tools in the lake bed.

  4. 4. The Architectural Work of Le Corbusier

    On the shore of Lake Geneva at Corseaux, just west of Vevey, stands a low white house, long and narrow, with a single strip window running along its lakeside facade. It looks modest, almost plain, but it is one of the key buildings of modern architecture. Le Corbusier, born in La Chaux-de-Fonds in the Swiss Jura, designed it in the early 1920s for his parents, and the Villa Le Lac, also called the Petite Maison, was an early demonstration of his ideas: a ribbon window framing the view, a roof garden, an open plan. Since 2016 it has been part of the UNESCO World Heritage site of the Architectural Work of Le Corbusier, which includes seventeen sites in seven countries.

  5. 5. Petite villa au bord du lac Léman

    In the 1920s Le Corbusier built a house for his parents on the shore of Lake Geneva, and he called it, a little ruefully, the little house, la petite maison. It stands at Corseaux, just east of Vevey, and it is tiny, only about sixty square metres of living space on a strip of garden between the railway and the water. The design is a manifesto in small. A single long window frames the lake and the mountains of Savoy like a painting, the garden wall has a table and a window cut into it, and every cupboard and corner has a purpose. His mother lived there into her nineties, and he wrote a little book about it. The house is part of the UNESCO listing of his works, and it can be visited in the season, with timed guided tours.
