Why this ride

41 km from Eckernförde to Eckernförde, about 44 min of riding. On the way it passes Main Wall Area 1 and back through Main Wall Area 1. The coast is the point of it.

The stops

  1. 1. East Wall Area 2E to 2F

    Start here.

    The Danevirke is a wall, or rather a series of walls, that once stretched across the neck of the Jutland peninsula, and this stretch is the eastern end, near the Windeby lake and the shore of the Baltic. The first earthworks went up in the seventh century, and later kings of Denmark extended them in several phases, culminating in a brick-faced wall built in the twelfth century under Valdemar the Great. The purpose was to protect the southern border against the Franks and the Saxons, and it worked more or less for several hundred years. A few steps along the grassy bank, and you can see a low ridge and a ditch, easily mistaken for a hedgerow.

  2. 2. Main Wall Area 1

    21 km in.

    The Danevirke is the longest and most important archaeological monument in northern Europe, a network of earthworks and walls that stretches across the neck of the Jutland peninsula, and the Main Wall is its backbone. At this spot, close to the village of Dannewerk near Schleswig, you are standing on a grassy ridge that looks like nothing much until someone tells you it was built to stop armies. It runs for kilometres through fields and woods, and over a stretch of centuries it was raised, repaired and extended by Danish kings, from the early Viking age into the twelfth century.
