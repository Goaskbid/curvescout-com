Why this ride

A 337 km round trip from Klein-Pöchlarn - roughly 5 h 41 in the saddle. On the way it passes St. Lorenz - Wachtturm, Zwentendorf - Kastell, Klosterneuburg - Kastell und Vicus and Frontiers of the Roman Empire - The Danube. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Pöchlarn - Kastell Hufeisenturm Ost

    Start here.

    The word Hufeisenturm means horseshoe tower, and it is the sort of detail that tells you about the Roman world when it was under pressure. In the fourth century, with raiders crossing the Danube more often, the Romans rebuilt many of their frontier forts with a new style of defence. Instead of square corner towers, they built tall towers in a U shape that projected out from the walls, giving archers and artillery a wider field of fire. The east tower at Pochlarn, the Roman fort of Arelape, belongs to that period.

    Read the complete Pöchlarn - Kastell Hufeisenturm Ost write-up.

  2. 2. St. Lorenz - Wachtturm

    33 km in.

    On the quiet south bank of the Danube in the Wachau, opposite the wine village of Weißenkirchen, the little church of St. Lorenz has a secret in its walls. Part of the building stands on, and incorporates, the masonry of a Roman watchtower, one of a chain of small fortifications that guarded the river frontier of the province of Noricum in late antiquity. The Danube was the edge of the empire here, and soldiers in the tower kept watch over the far bank for raiders. Today the site is part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire, Danube Limes listing, inscribed by UNESCO in 2021.

    Read the complete St. Lorenz - Wachtturm write-up.

  3. 3. Zwentendorf - Kastell

    70 km in.

    The Danube flows broad and brown past Zwentendorf, a quiet village on the Tullnerfeld plain west of Vienna, and for once the most famous building in the area is not the oldest one. Zwentendorf is where Austria built a nuclear power station in the 1970s and then, after a national referendum in 1978, decided never to switch it on. The plant still stands by the river, complete and unused, now a training site and a curiosity.

    Read the complete Zwentendorf - Kastell write-up.

  4. 4. Klosterneuburg - Kastell und Vicus

    109 km in.

    Klosterneuburg Abbey rises on a terrace above the Danube just north of Vienna, its baroque domes topped with an imperial crown and an archducal hat, and few visitors realise that beneath its walls lie the remains of a Roman fort. The Romans guarded this stretch of the Danube frontier, the Limes, with a chain of forts, and Klosterneuburg's position on a hill overlooking the river made it an obvious choice. The fort and the civil settlement around it are part of the UNESCO site of the Danube Limes, inscribed in 2021.

    Read the complete Klosterneuburg - Kastell und Vicus write-up.

  5. 5. Frontiers of the Roman Empire - The Danube

    166 km in.

    Marcus Aurelius, emperor and philosopher, spent several years at Carnuntum during his wars against the Germanic tribes north of the Danube, and he is thought to have written part of his Meditations here; the third book carries the note that it was written at Carnuntum. In the second century, this was one of the most important places on the Roman frontier: a legionary fortress, an auxiliary fort and a civil town that grew into the capital of the province of Upper Pannonia, with tens of thousands of inhabitants. Today, the archaeological park at Petronell-Carnuntum and Bad Deutsch-Altenburg shows reconstructed houses, a bath and streets built using Roman methods, two amphitheatres, and the Heidentor, a great ruined triumphal arch standing alone in the fields. The Danube Limes became a World Heritage site in 2021.
