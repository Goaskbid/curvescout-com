Why this ride

A mountain byway from Tellico Plains, Tennessee to Robbinsville, North Carolina, via Santeetlah Gap. Key character: long sweepers; forest; high overlooks. Surface: paved.

  1. 1. Cherohala Skyway point 1

    The town sits on the Tellico River at the foot of the mountains in Monroe County, Tennessee, a quiet place of a few hundred people with a general store atmosphere, a small main street and one or two stations. The sensible habit is to fill the tank here, buy something to eat, and only then turn onto Tennessee 165, the road that becomes the Cherohala Skyway as it climbs.

  2. 2. Cherohala Skyway point 2

    Up here the skyway earns its name. You are on the high spine of the Unicoi Mountains, somewhere near the line where Tennessee gives way to North Carolina, with the road running along the ridge at well over five thousand feet. The air is noticeably cooler than in the valleys on either side, and on a summer day you might swap a sticky ninety degrees at Tellico Plains for a damp sixty here. In winter the same ridge can be sheathed in rime ice while the valleys are dry.

  3. 3. Near Robbinsville

    Robbinsville is a quiet mountain county seat of fewer than six hundred people, and for a lot of riders it is the stop where a very good day gets summed up. It lies at about 620 metres in western North Carolina, in Graham County, one of the least populated corners of the state, and it makes a convenient place to finish or to start because it has fuel and a few places to eat, which the mountains around it do not.
