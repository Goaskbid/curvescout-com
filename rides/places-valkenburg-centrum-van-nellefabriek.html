Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Valkenburg-Centrum

    A Roman fort is a surprising thing to find in a Dutch village, but Valkenburg has one buried underneath it, and the old Rhine still runs past its doorstep. The centre of the village is a small cluster of houses, a church and a few shops around a bend of the Oude Rijn, a few kilometres from the dunes of the North Sea coast. Roman soldiers built a fort here, Praetorium Agrippinae, to guard the river frontier of the empire, and the site is part of the Lower German Limes, which is now a UNESCO World Heritage property.

  2. 2. Voorburg-Arentsburg

    Under the suburbs between The Hague and Delft lie the remains of Forum Hadriani, the northernmost Roman town on the continent, founded in the early second century AD and named after the emperor Hadrian. At Arentsburg, a country estate in Voorburg, excavations revealed streets, houses, a harbour and a canal, the Fossa Corbulonis, dug by Roman soldiers to link the Rhine and the Meuse. The site is part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire, Lower German Limes World Heritage Site, listed in 2021, which follows the Rhine frontier through the Netherlands and Germany.

  3. 3. Van Nellefabriek

    The Van Nelle factory, on the edge of Rotterdam, is one of the most beautiful industrial buildings ever made. Built between 1925 and 1931 for a company that processed coffee, tea and tobacco, it is a long, light building of glass and steel, with curtain walls that let daylight flood into the workspaces. The designers, Johannes Brinkman and Leendert van der Vlugt, created what became an icon of modernist architecture, a factory of light and air for its workers. It was added to the UNESCO list in 2014.
