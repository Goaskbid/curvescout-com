Why this ride

A 312 km round trip from Cusco - roughly 5 h 23 in the saddle. On the way it passes Segment Portada Rumiqolqa-ch’Uspitacana, Segment Patacancha-Huacahuasi, Segment Machucucho-Choquecancha and Segment Choquecancha-Paucarpata. The highest point is about 4,200 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cusco

    Cusco sits at about 3,400 metres in a high Andean valley, and the first thing it asks of you is patience. The air is thin, so even a short walk uphill can leave you breathless, and visitors from sea level often feel it as a headache or a strange tightness for a day or two. Locals will offer you coca tea, which helps a little; rest, water and a light dinner help more. On a motorcycle, thin air also saps engine power, a carburetted bike may run rich and a fuel-injected one will cope better, so do not be surprised if your machine feels a little tired.

  2. 2. Segment Portada Rumiqolqa-ch’Uspitacana

    South-east of Cusco, the Vilcanota valley narrows at a place called Rumicolca, and a massive stone gateway stands across the old road. Built originally by the Wari, the empire that preceded the Inca, as part of an aqueduct, and later converted by the Inca into a controlled entrance to the Cusco valley, the gateway of Rumicolca marked the checkpoint where travellers and goods were inspected. The segment of the Qhapaq Ñan between Rumicolca and Chuspitacana is part of the Andean road system inscribed on the UNESCO list in 2014, and its stones still carry the marks of the people who walked it.

  3. 3. Segment Patacancha-Huacahuasi

    Above Ollantaytambo, where the Sacred Valley of the Incas narrows, a side valley climbs north toward the high passes of the Urubamba range, following the Patacancha river. The segment between Patacancha and Huacahuasi is part of the Qhapaq Ñan, the Inca road system, and it crosses some of the most beautiful country in the Cusco region: steep slopes covered with potato fields and terraces, small villages of stone and adobe houses, herds of llamas and alpacas grazing in high pastures, and snow-capped peaks above. The communities of this area, like Patacancha and Huacahuasi, are known for their weaving, with intricate designs and bright colours, and the women's traditional dress is among the most striking in the Andes.

  4. 4. Segment Machucucho-Choquecancha

    The Urubamba river is the great artery of Inca country, and everything near it feels like a rehearsal for Machu Picchu. This segment of the Qhapaq Nan, the Inca road network listed by UNESCO in 2014, runs between two places named Machucucho and Choquecancha in the highlands north-east of Cusco, where side valleys climb from the main river towards high passes and snow peaks.

  5. 5. Segment Choquecancha-Paucarpata

    Along the heights above the Sacred Valley of the Incas, a footpath of stone and earth winds across the mountains, and this is one tiny part of one of the greatest road systems ever built. The Qhapaq Ñan was the road network of the Inca empire, some thirty thousand kilometres of trail linking the coast, the Andes and the forests of what are now six countries. UNESCO listed it in 2014, and the Cusco region, the heart of the empire, has some of the best-preserved stretches, many of them with the original stone paving, steps cut into the rock and retaining walls that have survived five centuries of rain.
