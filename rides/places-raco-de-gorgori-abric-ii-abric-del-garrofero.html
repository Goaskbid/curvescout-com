Why this ride

188 km from Famorca to Famorca, about 3 h 14 of riding. On the way it passes Penya de Benicadell, Abric del Gegant, Abric del Garrofero and back through Abric del Gegant. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Racó de Gorgorí- Abric II

    Spring comes early to the Vall de Castells. By late January the almond trees on the terraces turn pink and white, and the whole valley is brushed with blossom while the peaks above still hold the cold. The Racó de Gorgorí, a ravine corner in the hills above Castell de Castells, is worth seeing in that season, when the air is crisp and the rock paintings seem to belong to a landscape waking up.

  2. 2. Penya de Benicadell

    From almost anywhere in the southern half of Valencia province you can see it: a long, jagged ridge of grey limestone with a sharp tooth at its eastern end, standing on the skyline like a broken saw. The Benicadell, at 1,104 metres, separates the Vall d'Albaida to the north from the Comtat to the south, and the sharp summit, the Penya, is the mountain's signature. At its foot lie the villages of Beniarrés, Ràfol de Salem and Otos.

  3. 3. Abric del Gegant

    Ontinyent is the kind of Valencian town that does not bother selling itself to tourists, which is part of its charm. It grew rich on textiles, and the old quarter of La Vila, perched above the gorge of the Clariano river, still has a fine bell tower that is among the tallest in the region. The Abric del Gegant, a rock shelter recorded among the prehistoric painted sites of eastern Spain, sits in the hills just outside town towards Bocairent, where the valley narrows and the Serra de Mariola begins to rise.

  4. 4. Abric del Garrofero

    Bicorp is a small village in the Canal de Navarrés, a valley in the interior of Valencia province walled in by rugged mountains and deep ravines. The surrounding Macizo del Caroig is one of the largest wild areas in the Valencian Community, a vast tableland of pine, scrub and gorges cut by the Júcar's tributaries. The Abric del Garrofero, the shelter of the carob tree, lies in the hills near Bicorp, Quesa and Navarrés.
