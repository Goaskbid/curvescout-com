Why this ride

A 340 km round trip from Ankara - roughly 5 h 52 in the saddle. On the way it passes Gordion, Sivrihisar Ulu Mosque and back through Gordion. The highest point is about 1,100 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ahi Şerefeddin

    Ankara is a city of eras laid over each other, and the old quarter around the citadel is where you feel them most. This mosque, named after Ahi Şerefeddin, belongs to the thirteenth-century Seljuk and early Anatolian beylik period in the hilly Ulus district, close to the citadel walls. The name Ahi points to something characteristic of medieval Anatolia: the Ahi brotherhoods, associations of craftsmen and tradesmen that combined guild, charity and a code of honour, and that held real influence in cities where the central power was weak. I can say less about the building than about the neighbourhood, so treat this as a stop for atmosphere.

  2. 2. Gordion

    King Midas is the name everyone remembers, and this mound of earth near the village of Yassıhöyük is where his world really sat. Gordion was the capital of the Phrygians, a people who ruled much of central Anatolia in the centuries after the Hittite collapse, and the ruins lie on the flat floodplain of the Sakarya river, west of Ankara and about fifteen kilometres from Polatlı.

  3. 3. Sivrihisar Ulu Mosque

    Sivrihisar means pointed fortress, and the name fits: the little town clusters around a rocky crag in the middle of the high, open country west of Ankara, in what used to be the borderland between Phrygia and Galatia. The Ulu Mosque, the great mosque, is the main attraction, a building of the late thirteenth century with a forest of wooden pillars inside holding up a flat timber roof. This style, in which a prayer hall is filled with carved columns and beams rather than stone arches, was common in medieval Anatolia, and only a few good examples survive. The wood is dark with age, the carvings are worn smooth by centuries of touch, and the effect is a hush that feels very different from the grand stone mosques of Istanbul.
