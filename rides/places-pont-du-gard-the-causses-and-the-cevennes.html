Why this ride

From Vers-Pont-du-Gard it runs 404 km to Vers-Pont-du-Gard; allow about 6 h 58 of riding. The stops in between: Roman Theatre and its Surroundings and the, Decorated Cave of Pont d’Arc, The Causses and the Cévennes and back through Decorated Cave of Pont d’Arc. The highest point is about 950 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Pont du Gard

    Built without mortar, three tiers of arches carry a water channel across the gorge of the Gardon nearly fifty metres above the river, and the stones still fit together as if they had been placed last week. The Pont du Gard was one section of an aqueduct some fifty kilometres long that brought spring water from near Uzès to Roman Nîmes in the first century, with a total drop of only a few metres over the whole distance. It is a masterpiece of surveying as much as building, and it has stood through floods that have destroyed far younger bridges.

  2. 2. Roman Theatre and its Surroundings and the

    Orange has one of the best Roman theatres still standing anywhere, and what makes it special is the stage wall. It is a tall, plain facade, thirty-seven metres high and more than a hundred metres long, which in most other theatres has fallen or been taken apart for stone. Here it survives, and Louis XIV is supposed to have called it the finest wall in his kingdom. The theatre was built in the first century, under Augustus or soon after, and a statue of the emperor still stands in the central niche. The nearby triumphal arch, on the edge of town, is also Roman and also in good shape. UNESCO listed the theatre and arch together in 1981.

  3. 3. Decorated Cave of Pont d’Arc

    In December 1994, three cavers felt a draught coming from a pile of rocks above the Ardèche gorge, dug their way in and found themselves in a cave that nobody had entered for thousands of years. On the walls were lions, rhinoceroses, mammoths, horses and bears, drawn with a mastery that changed what we thought we knew about early art. The paintings of the Chauvet cave, named after one of the trio, Jean-Marie Chauvet, are around thirty-six thousand years old, roughly twice the age of Lascaux. The original cave is sealed to protect it, but a full-scale replica, the Grotte Chauvet 2, opened nearby in 2015.

  4. 4. The Causses and the Cévennes

    Most visitors to the Cévennes come for the gorges, but the real character of this country lies on top of them. The causses are high limestone plateaus, treeless and wind-scoured, where sheep have grazed for millennia and the horizon runs on for fifty kilometres. Cut deep into them are the gorges of the Tarn and the Jonte, with their cliffs and caves, and to the east the Cévennes mountains rise in ridges of schist and chestnut forest. The whole area is listed by UNESCO as a cultural landscape of Mediterranean agriculture and pastoralism, and much of it falls within the Cévennes National Park, set up in 1970.
