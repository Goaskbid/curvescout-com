Why this ride

The Great Dolomite Road links Giau, Falzarego, Pordoi and Sella in one day. From Selva di Cadore it runs 19 km to Cortina d'Ampezzo; allow about 21 min of riding. The highest point is about 2,236 m. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Passo di Giau · Dolomites

The 6 km between Near Selva di Cadore and Selva di Cadore. Short, so take your time with it.

The stops

  1. 1. Near Selva di Cadore

    Start here.

    Passo Giau, at 2,236 metres, is one of the great passes of the Dolomites, and many riders say it is the best. It links Cortina d'Ampezzo with Selva di Cadore and the Fiorentina valley, climbing through larch forests and high meadows to a broad, grassy saddle surrounded by spectacular peaks: the jagged tower of Ra Gusela, the Nuvolau, the Averau and, a little further, the famous Cinque Torri. To the south, the huge rock faces of Monte Pelmo and the Civetta rise above the valleys.

    Read the complete Near Selva di Cadore write-up.

  2. 2. Selva di Cadore

    About 6 km in.

    Selva di Cadore is a quiet village on the road between two big names, in the Fiorentina valley, with Monte Pelmo on one side and the great wall of the Civetta on the other. It is not a place that most people choose as a destination, and that is part of its appeal. A handful of houses, a church, a few hotels and a ski lift sit in a green bowl, and above them the Pelmo, with its flat, throne-like summit, dominates the view. The mountain is known locally as the throne of God, and when the light catches it at the end of the day, it is easy to see why.

    Read the complete Selva di Cadore write-up.

  3. 3. Cortina d'Ampezzo

    The end: about 19 km.

    The peaks of the Tofane, Cristallo, Pomagagnon and Sorapiss surround it, and the whole area is part of the UNESCO Dolomites World Heritage Site. The town has about five thousand residents, and many more in the holidays, with elegant shops, bars and a long history as a mountain resort.

    Read the complete Cortina d Ampezzo write-up.
