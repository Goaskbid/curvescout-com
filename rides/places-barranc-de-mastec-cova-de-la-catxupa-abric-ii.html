Why this ride

274 km from Cocentaina to Cocentaina, about 4 h 43 of riding. It goes by way of Pla de Petracos- Abric I, Cova de la Catxupa- Abric I, Abric de les Torrudanes and Cova de la Catxupa- Abric II. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc de mastec

    Between Cocentaina and Alcoy, in the hilly interior of Alicante province, a small ravine called the Barranc de Mastec holds rock shelters with prehistoric paintings, part of the UNESCO listing of rock art of the Mediterranean Basin. This corner of Spain is particularly rich in it, with styles ranging from the naturalistic Levantine figures of hunters and animals to the strange, abstract macro-schematic art that seems unique to these valleys. The ravine is surrounded by terraced olive and almond groves, pine woods and limestone crags.

  2. 2. Pla de Petracos- Abric I

    Pla de Petracos is one of the most important prehistoric art sites in Spain, and it looks like nothing much: a row of shallow shelters in the side of a ravine near Castell de Castells, in the high interior of the Marina Alta. What makes it special is the style. The paintings here are among the clearest examples of what archaeologists call Macroschematic art, large, bold, almost abstract human figures with raised arms and serpentine forms, painted by the first farming communities of the region in the early Neolithic. Elsewhere in the Valencian mountains you find Levantine hunting scenes; here you find something stranger and more symbolic. A short path and viewing platforms make the panels accessible, and the information boards help you see what the eye misses at first.

  3. 3. Cova de la Catxupa- Abric I

    The Marina Alta is the northern Costa Blanca at its most mixed: villas on every slope, a ribbon of coast running from Moraira to Jávea, and just behind it, farm terraces, dry stone walls and small rock shelters where prehistoric people left their mark. The Cova de la Catxupa, near Teulada and Benitachell, is one of them, a shelter in the hills a few kilometres from the sea, listed with the other Mediterranean rock art of the Iberian Peninsula.

  4. 4. Abric de les Torrudanes

    In the Vall d'Ebo, a small valley in the mountains of northern Alicante, the Abric de les Torrudanes is a rock shelter with prehistoric paintings, one of many in the area that form part of the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin World Heritage listing. The paintings include human figures and animals in the Levantine style, and the shelter's location, on a cliff above the valley, offers views over the mountains. Nearby, the Cova del Rull is a show cave with stalactites and stalagmites.

  5. 5. Cova de la Catxupa- Abric II

    Teulada is the town, and Moraira is its seaside annexe, and between them they hold most of what people know about this slice of the Costa Blanca. The Catxupa shelter lies just outside the town, which has some fourteen and a half thousand residents, many of them northern Europeans who came for the sun and stayed. The old centre of Teulada, though, remains a Valencian one, with a fortified parish church and narrow lanes that fill with the smell of coffee and baking at midday.
