Why this ride

A 172 km round trip from Newburn - roughly 2 h 36 in the saddle. On the way it passes Limestone Corner Roman temporary camp, Throp Roman fortlet, Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 66 and back through Throp Roman fortlet. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Hadrian's Wall and vallum from East Town H

    Start here.

    Heddon-on-the-Wall announces its pedigree in its name, and just east of the village, on the slope down towards Newcastle, you find the longest surviving stretch of the Wall in the eastern sector. It is a section of broad wall, built to the original specification of about ten Roman feet thick, before the builders further west were told to save time and stone by making it narrower. Standing beside it, with fields falling away towards the Tyne, you get a strong sense of the scale of the project: this curtain once ran unbroken for eighty Roman miles.

    Read the complete Hadrian's Wall and vallum from East Town H write-up.

  2. 2. Limestone Corner Roman temporary camp

    27 km in.

    Limestone Corner is the most northerly point on Hadrian's Wall, and it is famous for a job left unfinished. The Romans dug a defensive ditch in front of the Wall along most of its length, but here they hit a band of hard whinstone, and after cutting some of the huge blocks free and splitting others with wedges, they simply gave up. The blocks still lie where they were left, some with the holes for the wedges visible, nearly nineteen centuries later. Nearby, the earthworks of a Roman temporary camp, where troops on the move once pitched their tents behind a ditch and bank, are part of the same Frontiers of the Roman Empire World Heritage site.

    Read the complete Limestone Corner Roman temporary camp write-up.

  3. 3. Throp Roman fortlet

    54 km in.

    Throp is a farm name on the edge of Gilsland, in the narrow gap where the River Irthing slips between the northern Pennines and the moors that roll north towards the Scottish border. A small Roman fortlet stood here, close to the Stanegate, the road that ran along the valley before the Wall was built, and it is one of the many features that make up the Frontiers of the Roman Empire World Heritage site. Not much remains to see, but the setting is classic Wall country.

    Read the complete Throp Roman fortlet write-up.

  4. 4. Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 66

    86 km in.

    The western end of Hadrian's Wall runs right through Carlisle, and the stretch counted as wall mile 66 lies on the edge of the city, close to where the wall crossed the River Eden. The Roman town of Luguvalium grew up here, and the fort at Stanwix, across the river, housed a cavalry regiment and was the largest on the wall. Very little of the stone wall survives in the city, much of it having been robbed for buildings, but the line of the wall and vallum can be traced in places, and the Tullie House museum tells the story of Roman Carlisle with excellent displays.

    Read the complete Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 66 write-up.
