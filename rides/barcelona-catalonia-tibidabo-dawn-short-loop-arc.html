Why this ride

Out of Castelldefels, one way: 215 km, roughly 3 h 55 in the saddle. On the way it passes Sant Vicenç dels Horts, Scenic stretch near Caldes de Montbui and near Centelles. near Sant Pere de Ribes is the high point.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fuel stop near Castelldefels

    Castelldefels is where Barcelona goes to the beach, and where the coast suddenly stops being flat. Behind the town's long sandy shore, the limestone hills of the Garraf massif rise abruptly, and just south of town the coast road begins its famous run along the cliffs to Sitges. A castle on a small hill, rebuilt many times, gives the town its name and keeps watch over the plain of the Llobregat delta to the north.

  2. 2. High point near Sant Pere de Ribes

    Behind the beaches of Sitges, the Garraf massif rises in a block of bare, pale limestone, covered in scrub, dwarf palms and pine, cut by dry ravines and riddled with caves. It is one of the most surprising landscapes near Barcelona, wild and empty, and Sant Pere de Ribes sits on its southern edge, a town of old farmhouses, vineyards and modern homes a few kilometres inland from the sea.

  3. 3. Sant Vicenç dels Horts

    Sant Vicenç dels Horts takes its name from the market gardens, the horts, that once covered the fertile plain of the Llobregat river, just south-west of Barcelona. Many of those gardens survive today in the Baix Llobregat Agricultural Park, a protected area of farmland squeezed between motorways, factories and suburbs, which supplies Barcelona with vegetables, fruit and the famous artichokes of El Prat. It is an odd, fascinating landscape, where tractors and cabbages share the view with airliners and cranes.

  4. 4. Scenic stretch near Caldes de Montbui

    Hot water bubbles out of the ground at Caldes de Montbui, so hot that it steams in the town square at the Font del Lleó, the lion fountain. The Romans built baths here two thousand years ago, and their remains can still be seen in the centre of town, a remarkable survival. The springs have made Caldes a spa town for centuries, with hotels and baths where visitors still come to bathe in the mineral water.

  5. 5. Photo stop near Centelles

    Centelles stands at the southern entrance to the plain of Vic, where the Congost valley opens up after its narrow passage through the hills from Barcelona. The town has a long history: it was the seat of the powerful Counts of Centelles, and its old centre still has a medieval gate, narrow lanes and a palace. Above the town, the hill of Puigsagordi offers views over the plain and the surrounding mountains.

  6. 6. Canet de Mar

    The Maresme coast north of Barcelona has a particular rhythm: sea on one side, a railway line hugging the beach, then the N-II, then a strip of town climbing up towards wooded hills. Canet de Mar fits the pattern exactly. It is a working town rather than a resort, with a long sandy beach reached through underpasses beneath the tracks, and that is the reason it appears on these rides: it is one of the easier places along this coast to stop, swim and dry off in the sun before carrying on.

  7. 7. la Marina del Prat Vermell

    This is not the Barcelona of postcards, and that is half its charm for a rider. La Marina del Prat Vermell sits on the flat ground between the southern slope of Montjuïc and the Zona Franca, the city's great industrial and logistics zone, with the port cranes on one side, warehouses and new apartment blocks on the other, and the motorway ring roads that make escape from the city fast. It is a neighbourhood in transition, long a pocket of workers' housing among factories, now slowly being rebuilt.
