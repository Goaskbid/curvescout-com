Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Caraz

    Caraz is a small, sunny town at the northern end of the Callejón de Huaylas, the long valley that runs between the Cordillera Blanca and the lower Cordillera Negra, and it is one of the pleasantest bases in the Peruvian Andes. The town sits at about 2,250 metres, where the air is mild and the sky usually clear, and its low houses and quiet plaza look up to a wall of snow peaks, among them Huandoy and Huascarán, both above six thousand metres. The Santa River runs through the valley, and orchards and fields cover the lower slopes.

  2. 2. Llanganuco

    The Llanganuco lakes, Chinancocha and Orconcocha, lie in a steep glacial valley in Huascarán National Park at about 3,850 metres, and their colour, a deep, shining turquoise, is one of the great sights of the Peruvian Andes. Sheer walls of granite and ice rise on all sides, with the twin peaks of Huascarán, the highest mountain in Peru, to the south and the pyramid of Pisco and the massive Huandoy to the north. The names come from Quechua, and they are often explained as the female and male lakes, with Chinancocha the lower and more tranquil of the pair.

  3. 3. Huaraz

    Huaraz sits at about three thousand metres in the Callejon de Huaylas, a long, high valley between the Cordillera Blanca and the Cordillera Negra in the Peruvian Andes. The Cordillera Blanca rises along the east side, white with glaciers, and holds the highest tropical mountains in the world, including Huascaran, which at 6,768 metres is the highest peak in Peru. On a clear morning the whole range shows over the roofs of the town, an almost unreal wall of ice. Huaraz was largely destroyed in the great earthquake of 1970, which killed tens of thousands across the region and buried the nearby town of Yungay under an avalanche from Huascaran. The modern town is mostly new, a busy, practical place for trekkers and climbers.
