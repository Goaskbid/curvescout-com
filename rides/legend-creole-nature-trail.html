Why this ride

All-American Road. bayous, Gulf coast, wildlife. From Lake Charles it runs 183 km to Lake Charles; allow about 3 h 20 of riding. It goes by way of Sulphur, Cameron and Creole Nature Trail. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Lake Charles

    Lake Charles is the gateway to the Creole Nature Trail, an All-American Road that loops south from the city across the marshes of southwest Louisiana to the Gulf. It is a flat, low, watery place where the horizon is mostly sky and the road often runs on a raised bank between bayou and rice field. This is Cajun country, and you notice it first in the language on the radio and the boudin sold at gas stations, a rice-and-pork sausage that locals eat on the way to work and consider a proper breakfast.

  2. 2. Sulphur

    Sulphur is the working front door of Louisiana's Cajun coast, a refinery and chemical town in Calcasieu Parish a few minutes from Lake Charles. It may not look like the start of a nature trail, and that is part of the point. The industrial flares and stacks give way, a short ride south, to marsh, bayou, and open Gulf, and the contrast is the story of this corner of Louisiana.

  3. 3. Cameron

    Cameron is a small town with a very large story. It sits on the Louisiana coast where the Calcasieu Ship Channel reaches the Gulf, and the first thing most visitors do is cross the water on the free ferry, a short, slightly comic ride that carries cars, trucks, and motorcycles over to the east bank. On a clear day you can see the shipping lanes and the long, low shape of the marsh, and a pelican usually appears as if on cue.
