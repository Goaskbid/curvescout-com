Why this ride

From Prato Allo Stelvio it runs 80 km to Bormio; allow about 1 h 21 of riding. The stops in between: Trafoi and Stelvio Pass. The highest point is about 2,750 m. Stelvio Pass is open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Stelvio Pass

For 34 km this is Stelvio Pass, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it — expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast.

Stelvio Pass

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2757 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance. Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Prato allo Stelvio

    Prato allo Stelvio, or Prad am Stilfserjoch in German, is a village of around two and a half thousand people in the Val Venosta, in South Tyrol, at 915 metres above sea level. It is the doorstep of the Stelvio Pass from the eastern side, and the place where most riders who approach from the Vinschgau valley fill up, have a coffee and gather their courage. Almost everyone speaks German first and Italian second, and the village looks more Austrian than Italian, with painted house fronts and flower boxes.

  2. 2. Trafoi

    Trafoi is the last village before the Stelvio gets serious. It sits at about fifteen hundred metres in a narrow valley in South Tyrol, and above it the wall of the Ortler massif rises in a dazzling block of snow and ice, the highest peak of the Eastern Alps outside the Bernina range, at 3,905 metres. German is the first language here, with Italian a close second, and the church, the wooden balconies and the dumplings on the menu all say Tyrol rather than Lombardy. A short walk up the valley takes you to the Three Holy Springs, a pilgrimage spot where, the story goes, the Virgin appeared to a local shepherd girl in the nineteenth century, and a small chapel has stood beside the water ever since.

  3. 3. Stelvio Pass

    Few roads in Europe have a reputation as large as the Stelvio, and for once it is deserved. At 2,757 metres, it is one of the highest paved passes in the Alps, and the road up from the Trafoi side is a staircase of forty-eight numbered hairpins stacked against the mountainside, while the Bormio side has about forty of its own, longer and more open. Engineers of the Austrian empire built it in the 1820s to link Lombardy with the Tyrol, under the direction of Carlo Donegani, and it was meant for horse-drawn carts, which explains why the bends are so tight.

  4. 4. Bormio

    Bormio lies in a high valley of Lombardy, a town of narrow lanes, stone arches and church bells at around 1,200 metres, with mountains rising steeply on every side. It has been a spa since Roman times, and the warm springs that bubble up from deep in the rock still feed thermal baths where you can soak after a long day. In winter it is a ski resort with a long tradition, and the famously steep Stelvio downhill run, used for World Cup races, starts above the town.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Prato allo Stelvio to Trafoi

Prato allo Stelvio → Trafoi is 13 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Trafoi to Stelvio Pass

Trafoi → Stelvio Pass climbs about 2757 m in 6 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Stelvio Pass to Bormio

Stelvio Pass → Bormio drops about 2757 m over 11 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
