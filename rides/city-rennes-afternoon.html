Why this ride

A 162 km round trip from Rennes - roughly 2 h 48 in the saddle. It goes by way of Ancien moulin de Moidrey, Mont-Saint-Michel and its Bay, Le Mont Saint-Michel et sa baie and back through Mont-Saint-Michel and its Bay.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Rennes

    Rennes is the capital of Brittany, a handsome, youthful city of two hundred thousand people on the river Vilaine, with a large student population and a lively street life. The old quarter has a surprising number of half-timbered houses from the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, leaning at crazy angles, which survived a great fire in 1720 that destroyed much of the rest of the town. After that disaster, the centre was rebuilt in an orderly grid of stone, and the contrast between the two styles is part of the city's charm.

  2. 2. Ancien moulin de Moidrey

    If you have ever seen a photograph of Mont-Saint-Michel standing alone over flat green fields, with a small windmill in the foreground, there is a fair chance it was taken here. Moidrey is a village on the Couesnon, close to Pontorson, and its old mill stands in the polders that were reclaimed from the bay over centuries. The mount sits on the horizon, a few kilometres off, shrunk to the size of a thumbnail and a little unreal, and in the early morning when mist lies in the hollows it floats above the grass.

  3. 3. Mont-Saint-Michel and its Bay

    The tide here is one of the great shows of Europe. In the bay of Mont-Saint-Michel the sea can retreat for kilometres and return with a speed that has caught out many walkers, and at the biggest tides the difference between high and low water is around fourteen metres. On those days the mount, a rocky island crowned with a Benedictine abbey, can be surrounded by water, or stand alone on a plain of wet sand, depending on the hour. The causeway that used to link it to the shore has been replaced by a bridge so that the water can flow freely again.

  4. 4. Le Mont Saint-Michel et sa baie

    The Mont-Saint-Michel is a rock that thinks it is a cathedral. It rises from a flat bay of sand and silt on the border between Normandy and Brittany, crowned by an abbey whose spire is topped by a golden statue of the Archangel Michael. According to the old story, the archangel appeared to a bishop of Avranches in the early eighth century and told him to build a sanctuary on the rock, and Benedictine monks settled there in the tenth century. The abbey that grew over the next centuries became one of the great pilgrimage sites of medieval Europe.
