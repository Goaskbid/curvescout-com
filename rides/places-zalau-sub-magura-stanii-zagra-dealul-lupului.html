Why this ride

From Zalău it runs 248 km to Zalău; allow about 4 h 17 of riding. The stops in between: Bârsău Mare - Cetate, Zagra - Dealul Lupului and back through Bârsău Mare - Cetate. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Zalău - Sub Măgura Stânii

    Sub Magura Stanii means below the sheepfold hill, and the stop sits on the lower slopes, where the hill gives way to farmland and the first houses. This is the part of the landscape where people live and work: small plots of potatoes and maize, plum orchards, vegetable patches behind the houses, and a few cows grazing on the verge. The label offers no monument, so the best description is a day in the country, in a place where life goes on at the speed it always has.

  2. 2. Bârsău Mare - Cetate

    In the north of Transylvania, the Someș river winds through a valley of green hills, woods and villages with wooden churches and haystacks, and on a hilltop near the village of Bârsău Mare, the faint remains of a Roman fortification overlook the valley. This was part of the northern frontier of Roman Dacia, the province created after Trajan's conquest in the early second century, and it is one of the sites included in the Frontiers of the Roman Empire, Dacia, listed by UNESCO in 2024.

  3. 3. Zagra - Dealul Lupului

    Zagra is the last farming village before the land begins to climb in earnest toward the Rodna mountains, the highest range in the Eastern Carpathians north of the Prislop pass. Pietrosul Rodnei, at about 2,300 metres, is the summit, and the whole massif is protected as a national park and biosphere reserve, with glacial lakes, rocky ridges and alpine meadows that stay snowy into June. From the Dealul Lupului, the Wolf Hill, you look across a patchwork of hayfields toward those peaks, which look closer than they are.
