Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Auckland / New Zealand

    North of Auckland the coast road thins out and the land becomes greener and wetter, with dairy farms, tidal creeks and little harbours cut deep into the hills. The ride is the start of a long weekend towards the Bay of Islands.

  3. 3. Waiuku

    Waiuku sits at the head of a long, muddy arm of the Manukau Harbour, south-west of Auckland, and for centuries it was a crossroads. Māori travellers hauled canoes across the short portage between the Manukau and the Waikato River here, linking the harbours of the north with the great river system of the interior, and early European traders used the same route. Today it is a quiet country town serving farms in the surrounding hills, with a main street of shops and cafés and an easy, unhurried rhythm.

  4. 4. Near Titirangi

    Titirangi calls itself the gateway to the Waitākere Ranges, and it fits the role: a bohemian village of artists, writers and bush-clad houses, built on steep slopes above the Manukau Harbour, where the suburbs of Auckland end and the native forest begins. The finish is a few kilometres from the village, on the harbour side towards Lynfield and Blockhouse Bay, and it serves as a calm finish for long rides back from Northland.

  5. 5. High point near Pukekohe East

    The soil around Pukekohe is a deep chocolate red, the gift of old volcanoes, and it grows a startling share of New Zealand's vegetables. Market gardens, many started by Chinese and Indian immigrants in the twentieth century, cover the slopes with neat rows of onions, potatoes and greens, and the area remains one of the most productive horticultural districts in the country. Pukekohe East is a rural community on the low hills to the east of the town, with long views over the fields.

  6. 6. Scenic stretch near Papakura

    Papakura grew up on the Great South Road, built by British troops in the early 1860s to move soldiers and supplies south for the invasion of the Waikato, a key episode of the New Zealand Wars. Redoubts and military settlements dotted the route, and Papakura was one of them. Today it is a busy town on the southern edge of Auckland, with a main street, a railway station and expanding suburbs.
