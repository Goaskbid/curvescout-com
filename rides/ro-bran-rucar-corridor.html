Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Braşov

    Brașov is a city built against a mountain. The wooded slope of Tâmpa rises straight from the old town, topped by a giant white sign in the style of Hollywood, and a cable car takes you to the summit for the view over red tiled roofs and the Carpathian foothills. Behind the city the high peaks of the Postăvarul and Piatra Mare ranges ring the horizon, and winter snow lies on them from late autumn.

  2. 2. Bran

    Bran is the village with the castle that the whole world calls Dracula's, and that is both its fortune and its problem. The castle, perched on a rock above the road, was built in the late fourteenth century by the Saxons of Brașov as a fortress and customs post guarding the pass between Transylvania and Wallachia. There is no solid evidence that Vlad the Impaler, the real figure behind the legend, ever spent much time here, and Bram Stoker never visited Romania, but the castle looks the part, and the tourist industry has run with it. In the twentieth century it was a royal residence, much loved by Queen Marie, and its rooms are furnished in her style.

  3. 3. Rucăr

    Rucăr is a long village in the upper Dâmbovița valley, on the Wallachian side of the old pass from Bran, and for centuries it was the frontier post where merchants paid their dues before crossing into Transylvania. It sits in a broad green valley between the Piatra Craiului to the north and the hills of the Muscel to the south, and it has the feel of a mountain village that has been lived in for a long time, with old stone and timber houses, orchards and a big Orthodox church.

  4. 4. Câmpulung

    Câmpulung, often called Câmpulung Muscel to tell it apart from its namesake in Bukovina, has a claim that most Romanians learn in school: it was the first capital of Wallachia. In the early fourteenth century, the first princes of the new state ruled from here before moving to Curtea de Argeș and later to Târgoviște and Bucharest. The town sits at the foot of the Carpathians on the Râul Târgului, at the southern end of the corridor that leads up to the Bran pass, and it has a long history as a market town and trading post between Wallachia and Transylvania.
