  1. 1. Church of the Holy Paraskeva

    In 1780 a painter named Radu Munteanu covered the inside of the wooden church at Desești with scenes that still shock and delight: a Last Judgment in which sinners are marched off to a hell full of grinning devils, alongside saints, prophets and lively village figures. The church, dedicated to the Holy Paraskeva and built in 1770 from oak logs, rises above the village with a tall, slender tower and a steep double roof of shingles. It is one of the eight wooden churches of Maramureș inscribed by UNESCO in 1999, and its paintings are among the best preserved of them all.

  2. 2. Wooden Churches of Maramureş

    Maramureș is a region in the far north of Romania, along the Ukrainian border, where traditions have survived more strongly than almost anywhere else in Europe. Its villages are known for their carved wooden gates, their haystacks and their wooden churches, eight of which form a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Bârsana, in the valley of the Iza river, has one of them, a church built in the eighteenth century, with a tall slender spire and a steep shingled roof.

  3. 3. Church of Saint Nicholas

    Budești is one of the old villages of the Cosău valley in Maramureș, the far northern corner of Romania, where traditional life has survived longer than almost anywhere else in Europe. Its wooden church of St Nicholas, built in the seventeenth century, is a masterpiece of the local carpentry tradition: a dark timber body with a tall, steep shingled roof and a slender spire that seems to pierce the sky. Inside, the walls are covered with painted scenes from the Bible, and among the church's treasures is a coat of chain mail said to have belonged to Pintea the Brave, a legendary outlaw hero of the region. The church is one of eight in the UNESCO listing of the Wooden Churches of Maramureș.

  4. 4. Church of the Presentation of the Virgin a

    In Maramures, they build in wood as if wood were stone. The valley of the Iza, in the north of Romania, near the Ukrainian border, is a country of green hills, haystacks, carved gates and tall, steep-roofed churches of dark oak and spruce, and eight of the wooden churches here, built between the seventeenth and nineteenth centuries, are on the World Heritage list. The church of the Presentation of the Virgin at Barsana, dated to 1720, is one of them. It stands in the cemetery of the village, slender and dark, with a high, shingle-covered roof and a tower that rises like a pencil. Inside, the walls and ceiling are covered with paintings of saints and biblical stories in a naive and moving style.

  5. 5. Church of the Holy Parasceve

    Maramureș is one of the few corners of Europe where wood is still the language of the sacred, and the church of Saint Parascheva at Poienile Izei is a good place to learn it. It stands on a small rise above the Izei valley, a dark timber church with a steep, double-tiered shingled roof and a slender spire, built in the early seventeenth century and moved to its present spot later. Inside, the walls and ceiling carry painted scenes from the eighteenth century, and the one that always gets quoted is a vivid Last Judgment in which sinners get remarkably specific punishments.
