Why this ride

Victoria: the Twelve Apostles and the Otways. 196 km from Torquay to Warrnambool, about 3 h 34 of riding. On the way it passes Lorne, Apollo Bay and Great Ocean Road. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Torquay

    Torquay is where the Great Ocean Road begins, or ends, depending on which way you ride. It is a town of about thirteen thousand people on the edge of Bass Strait, and it has a reputation that has little to do with motorcycles: this is the capital of Australian surfing. Bells Beach, a few minutes down the coast, hosts one of the world's oldest surf competitions, held every Easter, and the town gave birth to brands such as Rip Curl and Quiksilver that have since gone global. Surf World, the museum in town, tells the story with boards, photographs and a lot of sun-bleached nostalgia.

  2. 2. Lorne

    Lorne is where the Great Ocean Road stops being a seaside drive and starts behaving like a mountain road. The town sits on Louttit Bay with the Otway Ranges standing right behind it, steep and thick with eucalypt, tree ferns and the odd waterfall. About twelve hundred people live here, a number that swells enormously in the Australian summer holidays, when Melbourne decamps to the beach and the main street fills with surfboards and ice cream.

  3. 3. Apollo Bay

    Apollo Bay is where the Great Ocean Road stops being a headline and starts being a town. It sits in a curve of bay on the south coast of Victoria, with the forested Otway Ranges standing directly behind and Bass Strait rolling in from the front. A population of about fifteen hundred swells in summer when holidaymakers arrive, and you notice the change in how busy the main street and the foreshore are.

  4. 4. Great Ocean Road point 4

    The last stretch of road to Cape Otway runs for kilometres through tall forest, and the trees are full of koalas. Manna gums line the lane to the lighthouse, and the animals can be spotted sleeping in the forks of the branches, grey furry lumps with their heads tucked in. Stop on the verge and look up, and you will soon see one, though the ride is slow because other drivers do the same. Apollo Bay is some eighteen kilometres back along the road, the only real town for a long way.

  5. 5. Great Ocean Road point 5

    The Twelve Apostles were never twelve, and there are not even that many now. The limestone stacks rising from the Southern Ocean off the Port Campbell coast were once called the Sow and Piglets, then renamed to attract tourists, and there were never more than nine. One collapsed in 2005 in front of watching visitors, leaving seven. They stand a short way offshore, golden and pink in the evening light, with waves breaking at their feet. Port Campbell is a short ride to the west.

  6. 6. Great Ocean Road point 6

    Port Campbell is a small fishing village in a cove, where a creek cuts a notch in the limestone cliffs, and it is the natural base for the Shipwreck Coast. The jetty and the little beach are protected from the Southern Ocean, and the main street has a few cafes, a pub and a general store. A few hundred people live here, and Timboon is the nearest sizeable village inland.

  7. 7. Warrnambool

    Warrnambool is the western gateway to the Great Ocean Road, or the end of it, depending on which way you ride, and it is a solid, comfortable regional city of about thirty thousand, where the dairy farms of south-west Victoria meet a cold, wild coast. The town spreads along the shoreline, with long beaches, a breakwater and good sea views, and the centre has all the practical comforts of a place that does not depend entirely on tourism.
