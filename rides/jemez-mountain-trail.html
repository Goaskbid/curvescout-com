Why this ride

Out of Bernalillo, one way: 141 km, roughly 2 h 48 in the saddle. On the way it passes Jemez Pueblo, Jemez Springs and Valles Caldera. It tops out around 2,750 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

The roads

Jemez Mountain Trail

From Valles Caldera to Los Alamos is 33 km. Mostly downhill - about 590 m down, from a high point of 2,756 m. Cold up there even in July - pack a warm layer and check it is open.

The stops

  1. 1. Bernalillo

    Start here.

    Bernalillo is an old Spanish and Pueblo town on the Rio Grande just north of Albuquerque, and it has a quiet identity of its own, a mix of adobe walls, cottonwoods, cafes, a few vineyards and a long history. The Sandia Mountains stand to the east, with a ridge that turns watermelon pink at sunset, which is what the name Sandia means in Spanish. The town has about nine thousand people and holds a festival of San Lorenzo each August, with traditional dances.

    Read the complete Bernalillo write-up.

  2. 2. Jemez Pueblo

    47 km in.

    Jemez Pueblo is a living community, not a monument, and the first thing to understand about it is that the people who live here call it Walatowa, meaning this is the place in their language, Towa. The village sits at the foot of the Jemez Mountains, in a valley of red sandstone cliffs along State Route 4, and the pueblo's lands run up the canyon of the Jemez River toward the high country. The Jemez people have lived in this region for many centuries, and are the only remaining speakers of Towa, a language they share with the people of the former Pecos Pueblo.

    Read the complete Jemez Pueblo write-up.

  3. 3. Jemez Springs

    67 km in.

    Jemez Springs is a small village strung along the Jemez River in a canyon of red and cream cliffs in northern New Mexico, and it feels a long way from anywhere. State Route 4 follows the river here, winding through a gorge of banded sandstone, with cottonwoods along the water and juniper on the slopes. It is a land of hot springs, tucked into the base of the Jemez Mountains, and the village has baths and a few small inns where tired riders can sit in mineral water.

    Read the complete Jemez Springs write-up.

  4. 4. Valles Caldera

    108 km in.

    Pull over where State Route 4 opens onto the Valle Grande and the first reaction is usually silence. The meadow in front of you is the floor of a volcano, a vast grassy bowl some thirteen miles across, ringed by forested domes and ridges, with elk grazing in the distance and the sky arching over it all. About 1.25 million years ago a colossal eruption emptied a magma chamber here, and the ground collapsed into the void, leaving a caldera. The ash from that blast settled into the pale tuff that forms the cliffs of Bandelier and the Pajarito Plateau nearby.

    Read the complete Valles Caldera write-up.

  5. 5. Los Alamos

    The end: 141 km.

    Los Alamos is a town that exists because of a secret. In 1943 the US government chose this remote mesa, once the site of a boys' ranch school, for the Manhattan Project, and J. Robert Oppenheimer's team built the first atomic bombs in the pine forest at the end of a single winding road. The laboratory is still the biggest employer, which gives the town an odd, educated, slightly fenced-in feel. Street names, museums and a surprising number of PhDs in the grocery queue all hint at it.

    Read the complete Los Alamos write-up.
