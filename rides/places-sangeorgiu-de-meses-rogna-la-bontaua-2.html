Why this ride

159 km from Cizer to Cizer, about 2 h 44 of riding. On the way it passes Stâna - La Oroieși, Tihău - Grădiște, Rogna - La Bontauă and back through Tihău - Grădiște. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sângeorgiu de Meseș

    The village of Sangeorgiu de Meses lies on the southern flank of the Meses mountains, in the old Hungarian-Romanian borderland of Salaj, and its name carries the saint of horsemen, George, and the mountain that gives the area its character. It is a place that rides begin and end, which suits it well: a quiet village with a church, a shop, a couple of bars, and an easy exit onto roads that climb in several directions. A few kilometres away is Cizer, and a little further, the small settlements of Starciu and Mal, each with its own cluster of orchards and farm tracks.

  2. 2. Stâna - La Oroieși

    On the hills above Zalău, in the county of Sălaj in northwestern Romania, the Roman Empire reached its northern limit in Dacia. After Trajan conquered the Dacian kingdom in 106, the Romans built a frontier system along the edges of the new province, with forts, watchtowers, walls and ditches, and in 2024 UNESCO listed it as the Frontiers of the Roman Empire, Dacia. The site at Stâna, La Oroieși, is one of many small elements of that frontier: a watchtower or defensive line on a ridge, where soldiers watched over the approaches from the free Dacian lands beyond. There is little to see on the ground, but the location, with views over the hills, explains why it was chosen.

  3. 3. Tihău - Grădiște

    The Someș river makes its way through the hills of north-western Romania between Cluj and Baia Mare, and near the small town of Jibou, in Sălaj county, it passes through a narrow valley guarded on its slopes by the remains of a Roman fort. The site, known locally as Grădiște, which simply means old fort, lies above the village of Tihău, close to Surduc and Gâlgău Almașului.

  4. 4. Rogna - La Bontauă

    Rogna is a scatter of farms in the hills of northern Sălaj, close to the Someș valley and the border with Maramureș, and La Bontauă is the name the locals give to a piece of high ground where a Roman frontier site has been recorded. The northern edge of Roman Dacia ran through these hills, and the Romans placed watchtowers and small forts on high points to keep an eye on the river valleys and the free Dacians to the north. Today there is little to see apart from the shape of the land, but the view over the rolling hills makes it easy to imagine a sentry looking out across the valley.
