Why this ride

Salt air first, soft hills after coffee. 117 km round from Bad Doberan - plan on about 2 h 01 of riding. On the way it passes Kühlungsborn beach road, Rerik cliffs and Neukloster inland. It keeps close to the shore for long stretches.

  2. 2. Kühlungsborn beach road

    Kühlungsborn is really three villages that were married in 1938 and given a name that sounds like a spa. Brunshaupten, Arendsee and Fulgen had all been fishing hamlets and then bathing resorts, and today the merged town runs along the Baltic for some three kilometres, with a long promenade, a sea bridge that reaches out over the water, and a line of white villas in the old resort style with carved balconies and little towers.

  3. 3. Rerik cliffs photo stop

    The coast between Kühlungsborn and Rerik is the one stretch of this part of Mecklenburg that has any drama. The land here rises into a low cliff of clay and sand, a metre-by-metre record of the ice age, and the Baltic keeps chewing at it so that trees lean out over the edge and every storm drops a slice onto the beach. Stand back from the lip when you take a photo. The ground is softer than it looks, and the sea does not care about a view.

  4. 4. Neukloster inland sweep

    Neukloster is a small town of just over four thousand people, tucked between woods and a lake south of the Baltic coast, and it is named for the thing that made it. In the early thirteenth century a Cistercian nunnery was founded here, and its brick church still stands at the centre, a good example of the Gothic building style that spread along this part of the Baltic. Brick instead of stone because there is no stone here: the glaciers left clay, so the whole region learned to bake its own building material and became very good at it.

This Rerik cliffs photo stop → Neukloster inland sweep stretch is for rhythm and scenery. Keep your head up, let the view breathe, and resist turning every open bend into a target. Stop once properly rather than half-stopping everywhere.
