  3. 3. Rocky Mountain National Park Trail

    This is the broad ridge that separates the South Platte drainage from the Arkansas, a plateau of ponderosa pine, scrub oak and hay meadows at roughly two thousand metres, with the Front Range a blue wall away to the west. On a clear day, Pikes Peak stands out to the south as a great pale dome.

    4. Rocky Mountain National Park Trail

    Parker is one of the fast-growing suburbs on the south-east side of Denver, a town of many thousands of people that used to be little more than a stagecoach stop. Despite the label, nothing here is a pass; the land is gently rolling and the nearest high peaks are thirty miles west, a blue-grey line of the Front Range along the horizon.

  5. 5. Back to Rocky Mountain

    This one is a gate, not a viewpoint, and it deserves an honest description. There are no mountains here. On a clear day you can see the Front Range as a thin blue-white line far to the west, a hundred kilometres away or more, and it is a good reminder of how abruptly the Rockies rise from the plain.
