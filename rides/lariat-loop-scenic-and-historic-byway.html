Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Golden Colorado

    Golden has a bit of everything a rider wants at the end of a day. It is wedged into a gap where Clear Creek slips out of the Front Range and onto the plains, with flat-topped Table Mountain on one side and Lookout Mountain on the other, so the town feels like it was dropped into a doorway. For a few years in the 1860s it was even the capital of Colorado Territory, until Denver took the title and the weight along with it.

  2. 2. Lookout Mountain

    A few minutes from Golden's brewery smoke and red-brick downtown, the road tilts upward and begins a series of switchbacks that make Lookout Mountain Road feel like a proper mountain pass. The views build quickly: first the town and the creek below, then the whole Front Range plain spread out to the east, Denver's towers a faint cluster in the haze. On a clear evening the light on the city is worth the ride on its own.

  3. 3. Evergreen

    Evergreen is the mountain town that Denver residents escape to, and you can feel it as soon as you arrive. Pine trees close in around the road, the elevation has crept above seven thousand feet, and the lake in the middle of town reflects aspen and sky. After the open prairie and the busy suburbs, this feels like a different state, and it takes only forty minutes by bike to get from one to the other.

  4. 4. Morrison

    Morrison is a small town squeezed into the mouth of a canyon, with high red sandstone walls on one side and the flat plain on the other. It is easy to ride through quickly, and that would be a mistake. The town sits at the edge of one of the most distinctive geological spots in Colorado, where the great tilted slabs of the Front Range come right up to the prairie like a row of rust-red sails.
