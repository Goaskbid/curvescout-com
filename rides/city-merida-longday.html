Why this ride

A 257 km round trip from Mérida - roughly 4 h 26 in the saddle. The stops in between: Pre-Hispanic Town of Uxmal, Uxmal, Kabah and Sayil.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Merida

    Mérida is the capital of Yucatán and the cultural heart of the peninsula, a flat, hot, well-kept city of more than three-quarters of a million. It was founded in 1542 by Francisco de Montejo the Younger on the site of a Maya city called T'hó, and the Spaniards used the stones of the Maya pyramids to build their cathedral and houses. The cathedral, begun in 1561 and finished at the end of the century, is one of the oldest on the American mainland.

  2. 2. Pre-Hispanic Town of Uxmal

    Uxmal is the most elegant of the Maya cities, and the quality of its stonework is the first thing visitors notice. Built mainly between the seventh and tenth centuries, it belongs to the Puuc region of Yucatán, named for the low range of hills that rise here, and its buildings show a style distinct from the temples of the jungle: smooth, flat lower walls topped by intricate friezes of stone mosaic, with lattice and serpent patterns and hundreds of masks of Chaac, the rain god, with his curling nose.

  3. 3. Uxmal

    Getting to Uxmal is half the pleasure. Highway 261 leaves Mérida and runs south for about eighty kilometres through flat, green scrub, past haciendas that once grew henequen and small Maya villages with oval thatched houses and painted churches. The land is a limestone shelf, with a thin red soil, and then the Puuc hills appear, low ridges that seem tall after the plain. The road is good, straight and fast, with occasional topes at village entrances.

  4. 4. Kabah

    Kabah is the sister of Uxmal, about eighteen kilometres to the south, and it was linked to it by a raised stone causeway, a sacbe, that ran straight through the forest. A monumental arch with a corbelled vault still stands at the point where the road once left Kabah toward Uxmal, a lonely frame in the trees, and it marks the start of a story about power and ritual that few visitors fully take in.
