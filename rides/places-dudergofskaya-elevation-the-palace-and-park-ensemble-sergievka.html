Why this ride

165 km from Krasnoye Selo to Krasnoye Selo, about 3 h of riding. It goes by way of The Monastery Troitse-Sergieva Pustin, The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag, The Palace and Park Ensemble «Sergievka» and back through The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

It is 25 km from The Monastery Troitse-Sergieva Pustin to The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag on Ропшинское шоссе. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 16 and 77 m. Watch for sand and spray near the water.

The stops

  1. 1. Dudergofskaya Elevation

    Start here.

    The land around St Petersburg is almost perfectly flat, so any hill gets noticed. The Duderhof Heights, a pair of wooded hills rising south of the city near Krasnoye Selo, are the highest ground for many kilometres around, with the higher one, Voronya Gora or Crow Hill, reaching a little under two hundred metres above sea level. From the top, on a clear day, you can see the gilded domes of the city and the waters of the Gulf of Finland.

    Read the complete Dudergofskaya Elevation write-up.

  2. 2. The Monastery Troitse-Sergieva Pustin

    28 km in.

    On the southern shore of the Gulf of Finland, between St Petersburg and Peterhof, the old road runs past a string of palaces and estates that the Russian elite built in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries to be near the sea and the court. Among them, near Strelna, stands the Trinity-Sergius Hermitage, a monastery founded in the eighteenth century as a dependency of the great Trinity Lavra of St Sergius near Moscow. It is part of the wide UNESCO listing of St Petersburg's historic centre and the monuments around it.

    Read the complete The Monastery Troitse-Sergieva Pustin write-up.

  3. 3. The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag

    53 km in.

    Ropsha is one of those places where the scenery is gentle and the history is anything but. The village sits on the low Izhora Upland southwest of St Petersburg, between Krasnoe Selo and the coast road at Lomonosov, and the palace that gives the estate its name looks down over a chain of spring-fed ponds and a slow, wooded park. It was here, in July 1762, that the freshly deposed Emperor Peter III was held after his wife Catherine seized the throne, and here that he died a few days later under circumstances nobody at court cared to explain. Catherine became "the Great"; Ropsha became a footnote with a chill on it.

    Read the complete The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag write-up.

  4. 4. The Palace and Park Ensemble «Sergievka»

    82 km in.

    Peterhof is the tsars' answer to Versailles, strung along the southern shore of the Gulf of Finland about thirty kilometres west of St Petersburg, and Sergievka belongs to its quieter western fringe. Think of it less as one famous building than as a country estate in the old Peterhof manner, with a house, an overgrown park, ponds and lawns, laid out when noble families built summer retreats close to the court.

    Read the complete The Palace and Park Ensemble «Sergievka» write-up.
