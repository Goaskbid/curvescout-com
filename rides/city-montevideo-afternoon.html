  1. 1. Montevideo

    Montevideo is a city that faces the water and sits down beside it. A long waterfront road, the Rambla, runs for about twenty-two kilometres along the river Plate, and on any evening the whole city seems to be on it, with people walking, jogging, fishing or sitting with a thermos of hot water and a gourd of mate, the bitter herbal tea that Uruguayans carry everywhere. The river is so wide here that it looks like the sea, though it is brown with silt, and the Atlantic begins farther east.

  2. 2. The work of engineer Eladio Dieste: Church

    A concrete wave stands beside the beach road in Atlántida, and it is a church. The Iglesia de Cristo Obrero y Nuestra Señora de Lourdes, in the Estación Atlántida district, was designed by the Uruguayan engineer Eladio Dieste and finished in 1960. It is made entirely of brick, and its undulating walls ripple like fabric, rising to a sinuous roofline that seems to defy the material. Dieste used a technique he called reinforced ceramic, which allowed thin brick shells to span huge spaces with almost no thickness. The result is light, strong and beautiful, and in 2021 UNESCO added the church to the World Heritage list.
