Why this ride

On the way it passes Parc National de Masoala and back through Parc National de Masoala. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Parc National de Marojejy

    Marojejy is a mountain wrapped in cloud. It rises in the north-east of Madagascar above the Andapa basin, reaching 2,132 metres at its highest peak, and the climb from the lowland rice fields to the summit passes through several different worlds: lowland forest with huge buttressed trees, then montane forest dripping with moss and orchids, and finally a stunted cloud forest and open rock at the top. The park is part of the Atsinanana World Heritage group, and it is considered one of the most diverse corners of the island.

  2. 2. Parc National de Masoala

    Masoala is the biggest national park in Madagascar, a huge peninsula of rainforest, mangrove and coral reef in the north-east of the island, and it is listed by UNESCO as part of the Rainforests of the Atsinanana, a group of eastern reserves that hold some of the richest wildlife on earth. Rain falls here all year, and the forest is thick, dark and loud with life. Red ruffed lemurs, found nowhere else, move high in the canopy, and the aye-aye, the strange nocturnal lemur with long fingers, lives in the trees. Chameleons, tomato frogs, leaf-tailed geckos and a dazzling collection of birds fill the undergrowth.

  3. 3. Parc National de Masoala

    Deeper into the Masoala peninsula, away from the bay, the land climbs into steep ridges covered in wet forest, and here the sense of remoteness grows. The park has no real roads through this interior, and rivers and footpaths are the arteries. Heavy rain, which can exceed five or six meters a year, feeds streams that rush down the slopes, and the forest is thick with tree ferns, climbing bamboo and epiphytes. It is a hot, humid world where leaves drip all night and the ground is covered in moss.
