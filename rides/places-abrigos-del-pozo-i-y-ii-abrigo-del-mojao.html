Why this ride

A 256 km round trip from Calasparra - roughly 4 h 25 in the saddle. On the way it passes Abrigo de la Ventana I, Abrigo del mojao and back through Abrigo de la Ventana I. The highest point is about 1,300 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abrigos del Pozo I y II

    Calasparra sits where the Segura river leaves the hills of the Murcian interior, and anyone who has ordered a paella with proper grains has probably eaten its rice without knowing it. The town has its own protected denomination for rice, grown in terraces watered from the river, and the pale fields in the valley are an odd and welcome sight in country otherwise made of rock and scrub. Eleven thousand people live here, which means fuel, a bakery that opens early and plenty of bars where lunch is not a negotiation.

  2. 2. Abrigo de la Ventana I

    High in the Sierra de Segura, where Albacete, Murcia and Jaén meet, the Taibilla river cuts a deep valley through limestone mountains, and the cliffs and shelters along its banks hide some of the finest prehistoric paintings in Spain. The Abrigo de la Ventana, the window shelter, is one of them, near the village of Nerpio, and it is part of the World Heritage site of Levantine rock art.

  3. 3. Abrigo del mojao

    Sometimes a name on a map is all you have, and this is one of those places. The Abrigo del Mojao belongs to the cluster of painted rock shelters in the high north-east of Almería, in the sierra country that spills across towards Murcia, and it sits some sixteen kilometres from the nearest village of any size. The shelters here are part of the Mediterranean Basin rock art tradition that UNESCO recognised in 1998, and their paintings are usually schematic, simple red signs and figures drawn by people who lived in this dry upland thousands of years ago.
