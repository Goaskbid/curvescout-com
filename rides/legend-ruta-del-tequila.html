Why this ride

official tourism route. agave landscape, distilleries, volcanic hills. From Guadalajara it runs 71 km to Magdalena; allow about 1 h 17 of riding. It goes by way of Amatitán and Tequila. The highest point is about 1,650 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Near Guadalajara

    Guadalajara is Mexico's second city in many ways, and the heart of its mariachi and tequila traditions. Mariachi music, the charro horsemanship and the tequila spirit all trace their cultural roots to the state of Jalisco, and the capital is where they come together. The historic centre has big colonial buildings, wide plazas and a cathedral with two towers that were rebuilt after an earthquake in the nineteenth century. Murals by José Clemente Orozco, one of the great Mexican muralists, cover the walls and dome of the Hospicio Cabañas, a vast neoclassical complex that is a UNESCO site.

  2. 2. Amatitán

    Amatitán is a small agave town west of Guadalajara that sits in the middle of the blue fields you came for. The landscape around it, rows of grey-blue agave plants like spiked rosettes on red volcanic soil, with the cone of Tequila volcano rising behind, is the central picture of the Ruta del Tequila. The plants take six to eight years to ripen before they are cut, so each field is a calendar of the owner's patience. In 2006 UNESCO recognised the agave landscape and the old distilleries and haciendas of the area as a World Heritage Site, and Amatitán is one of the places where you can feel why.

  3. 3. Tequila

    Tequila is both a town and a drink, and it is rare for the two to be so closely bound. The town, with about thirty thousand people, lies at the foot of the volcano of the same name, and its centre is a tidy grid of low houses painted in strong colours around a main plaza and a parish church. Mexico gives such places the title Pueblo Mágico, and here it fits more honestly than usual, because the thing the town is famous for is still produced a few streets away. The air in places smells of cooked agave, sweet and a little smoky, and the big distillery names, Cuervo and Sauza among them, have their old facilities in town.

  4. 4. Magdalena

    Magdalena is a small town in the highlands of Jalisco, in the heart of the blue agave country, and it has a quieter, more rural feel than the busy tequila towns nearby. It lies west of Guadalajara, in a landscape of dry hills and fields where rows of spiky blue-grey agave plants run to the horizon. About sixteen thousand people live here, and the town is known for opal and obsidian, which are mined in the surrounding hills and sold in small shops around the plaza. It sits on the Ruta del Tequila, which links Guadalajara with Tequila and its distilleries and haciendas.
