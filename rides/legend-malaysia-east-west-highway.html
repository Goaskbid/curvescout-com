Why this ride

curated scenic road. rainforest, mountain lake, elephant country. A one-way ride of 161 km, Kuala Kangsar to the finish - roughly 2 h 56 in the saddle. The highest point is about 1,050 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kuala Kangsar

    Kuala Kangsar is the royal town of Perak, the seat of the sultan, and it carries its status lightly. The town sits on the Perak river, Malaysia's second longest, and about forty thousand people live here. The Ubudiah Mosque, with its golden onion dome and white minarets, is among the most beautiful in the country, built in the early twentieth century after the sultan vowed to build one if an elephant that was unable to walk recovered. The Istana Iskandariah, the palace, stands on a hill, and the Perak Royal Museum is housed in a wooden palace built without nails. It is a quiet, dignified place with tree-lined avenues.

  2. 2. Malaysia East West Highway point 2

    Gerik is the last town in Perak before the East-West Highway heads off into the jungle, and it has the feel of a frontier: a single main street, a few petrol stations, shops selling everything from fishing nets to motorcycle parts, and roadside restaurants serving nasi campur and roti canai to truckers and travellers. The town sits in a broad valley surrounded by forested hills, and the Perak river flows nearby. Thailand is less than forty kilometres to the north, and the border town of Betong is the nearest large settlement.

  3. 3. Malaysia East West Highway point 3

    Around 1,050 metres up, the East-West Highway crosses the spine of the peninsula, the Titiwangsa Range, which divides Perak from Kelantan and the west coast from the east. Up here the forest is dense and green, the air is cool and damp, and the views stretch over endless forested ridges with no sign of human activity, apart from the road itself. It is one of the most remote places in Peninsular Malaysia, and it feels it: no towns, no villages, just jungle and the occasional viewpoint where people stop to look.

  4. 4. Malaysia East West Highway point 4

    Coming down off the Titiwangsa on the Kelantan side, the jungle slowly loosens its grip. The road drops towards the town of Jeli, rubber and oil palm plantations begin to replace the forest, and kampung houses on stilts appear among fruit trees. Here the highway meets the east coast world: a different dialect, a different cuisine, a more conservative culture, and in Kelantan the weekend falls on Friday and Saturday rather than Saturday and Sunday, which can catch out anyone planning around shop hours.
