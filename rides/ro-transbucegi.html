  1. 1. Sinaia

    Sinaia is the royal resort of Romania, a mountain town on the Prahova Valley where the first king built his summer palace and the aristocracy followed with villas, a casino and a railway station fit for visiting monarchs. It sits at around eight hundred metres on the eastern side of the Bucegi, with the limestone wall of the massif rising straight above the town, and it is one of the places where Romania's turn-of-the-century ambitions are still visible in stone and timber.

  2. 2. Piatra

    If you came looking for mountains, this Piatra will surprise you. The village sits down on the edge of the Romanian Plain, in Olt county, a long way south of the Bucegi, next to the small town of Piatra Olt and just a short run from Slatina. The landscape around it is wide and flat to gently rolling: fields of maize, wheat and sunflowers that stretch to the horizon, lines of acacia and poplar along the roads, and the broad Olt river flowing south toward the Danube. It is the Romania of farming villages and long horizons rather than castles and hairpins.
