Why this ride

From Nerpio it runs 247 km to Nerpio; allow about 4 h 16 of riding. The stops in between: Cañada de la Cruz, Cueva de la Hiedra and back through Cañada de la Cruz. The highest point is about 1,400 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fuente del Sabuco II

    Sabuco is the old word for the elder tree, and a fuente is a spring, so this shelter is named for the water that makes the place possible: the Elder Spring. Fuente del Sabuco II lies about eleven kilometres from Nerpio in the high country of south-west Albacete, and it is one of the many shelters of this district that hold paintings. The ride also links it to a site called Diosa Madre, the Mother Goddess, a name given by archaeologists to a type of schematic figure that appears across the prehistoric art of the Iberian Peninsula. The Levantine art of the region is protected by UNESCO as part of the Mediterranean rock art of Spain.

  2. 2. Cañada de la Cruz

    The Cañada de la Cruz is a high valley or drovers' pasture in the Segura mountains; cañada is the word for the traditional livestock routes and grazing grounds used by shepherds moving their flocks between summer and winter pastures, and this part of Spain has a long history of transhumance.

  3. 3. Cueva de la Hiedra

    Ivy is a modest plant to give a cave its name, but the Cueva de la Hiedra, the Cave of the Ivy, tells you something about the place. On a dry limestone hillside above Huesa, any cave that holds damp enough to grow ivy stands out, and the plant may mark the mouth of a shelter. The cave lies about four kilometres from Huesa and a little more from Quesada, in the folded limestone country between the olive plain and the wooded ridges of the Cazorla natural park.
