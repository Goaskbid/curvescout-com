Why this ride

Out of Buşayrā, one way: 273 km, roughly 4 h 37 in the saddle. On the way it passes Petra, back through Dana, back through Karak and back through Wadi Mujib. The highest point is about 1,150 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Dana

    Dana is a village that clings to the edge of a cliff, and from its terrace the land falls away in a great gulf of rock, with the desert plain of Wadi Araba shimmering far below. It stands in the highlands of southern Jordan at about 1,200 metres, a cluster of old stone houses that were abandoned in the twentieth century and are now partly restored, at the heart of the Dana Biosphere Reserve, the largest nature reserve in the country.

  2. 2. Petra

    Petra is the most famous ruin in Jordan, and it earns the fame. The Nabataeans, an Arab people who grew rich on the incense and spice trade, carved their capital into the rose-coloured sandstone cliffs of a hidden valley more than two thousand years ago. You approach through the Siq, a narrow gorge about a kilometre long, with walls rising on either side until, at the end, the Treasury appears in a slice of light, a monumental facade cut straight out of the rock.

  4. 4. back through Karak

    Karak is dominated by its castle, which sits on a spur above the town and looks as stern now as it did eight centuries ago. The Crusaders built it in the 1140s, under the kings of Jerusalem, to control the road between Damascus and Egypt and the pilgrim route to Mecca, and for a few decades it was one of the most important strongholds of the Latin East. Its most notorious lord, Reynald of Châtillon, raided Muslim caravans from here, and the dark vaulted corridors and cellars are a good place to imagine the sieges. In 1188, after a long blockade that followed the Battle of Hattin, Saladin took the castle, and it later passed to the Mamluks, who added their own towers and halls.

  5. 5. back through Wadi Mujib

    Wadi Mujib is a deep gorge in the Jordanian plateau, cut by the Mujib river on its way to the Dead Sea. The King's Highway crosses it on a dramatic descent, dropping from the high farmland of Moab down into the canyon and climbing out again on the far side, and the change of altitude is striking. The gorge is about four kilometres wide at the top and the floor lies close to the level of the Dead Sea, one of the lowest places on Earth. In the Bible this canyon was known as the Arnon, and it marked the border of Moab.

  6. 6. Madaba

    Madaba is the town of mosaics, and the reason is a floor. In the Church of St George, a nineteenth-century Greek Orthodox building, lies a sixth-century Byzantine mosaic map of the Holy Land, made from some two million tiny stones, which shows Jerusalem, the Nile delta and the Dead Sea with astonishing detail. It came to light again in 1884 when the church was being built, and it remains the oldest known cartographic depiction of the region. Other churches and houses in town contain fine mosaics too, and a small archaeological park displays some of the best, so you can easily spend a morning looking at the floors.
