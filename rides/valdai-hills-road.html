Roadbook

  1. Valdai start · 200 m

    Valdai is a lake town on high, wet, wooded ground halfway between Moscow and Saint Petersburg, and for three centuries travellers have stopped here because they had to. The Valdai Hills, which are really more of a rolling upland about two hundred metres above sea level, are the birthplace of rivers: the Volga, the Western Dvina and the Dnieper all start within a short distance. The town of eighteen thousand sits on the shore of Lake Valdai, a deep, clear lake in a bowl of forest, and the surrounding national park protects a mix of pine, spruce and bog.

    The lake's island holds the Iversky Monastery, founded in the 1650s by Patriarch Nikon, who wanted a place to rival the great Orthodox sites on Mount Athos and modelled the buildings on them. Its white walls and golden domes reflect in the water, and it is reached by a short boat ride or a causeway. Nikon later fell from power, but the monastery stayed and was restored after periods of neglect.

    Valdai's other claim to fame is its bells. In the eighteenth century, craftsmen here cast small bronze bells for the horse-drawn mail coaches on the road between the capitals, and the bells became so closely connected with the town that they are still called Valdai bells. They rang on the harness of troikas, and a small museum shows how they were cast. You can buy a modern copy in a shop, and its sound is bright and clear, a ringing that carries across the lake.

    The old Tsarist road, known as the Sovereigns' Road, ran through here, with post stations where horses were changed. The riding in the area is easy, along tree-lined roads past villages with wooden houses, over low hills, with lakes appearing and vanishing between the pines. The surface is mixed, with good asphalt on the main road and patches of rough tarmac on the lanes. Elk and deer are common at dusk, and trucks use the highway at speed.

    There is fuel in town, and it is a good place for a meal, with fish from the lake, mushroom dishes in season and the sweet baked goods Russians love. Summer is warm and green, with swimming in the lake, and autumn colours are fine from mid September. In winter the snow lies deep and the lake freezes, which turns the whole landscape into a white silence.

  2. Iversky Monastery scenic

    Most monasteries are built on hills; this one is on an island. The Iversky Monastery stands on Selvitsky Island in Lake Valdai, a few kilometres from the town of Valday, and the sight of its white walls and gilded domes across the water is one of the great images of the Russian north-west. Patriarch Nikon founded it in 1653, copying the Iviron Monastery on Mount Athos, and the same ambition that created New Jerusalem is visible here, in the conviction that the Russian landscape could host a holy place as grand as any in the East.

    The country around it is the Valdai Hills, the highest ground in the region, though that is not saying much, with the summits barely reaching three hundred metres. What makes them important is the water: the Volga, the Dnieper and the Western Dvina all rise in the district, and the many lakes, among them Valdai itself, are the legacy of glaciers. The land is thick with spruce, birch and aspen, and much of it is protected as a national park. The air is clean and cold, the lakes are clear, and in autumn the forest goes through every shade of red and gold.

    Valday has another claim to fame: bells. When Ivan the Great crushed Novgorod in 1478 and carried off its veche bell, the symbol of the city's freedom, the bell was sent into exile in Valdai, and the town became a centre of bell founding. Valdai bells, small and clear, were once hung on the harnesses of post-horses, and they have been made here ever since. There is a museum of them in the town.

    The road from Valday to the monastery is short and easy, and the highway that serves the area, the old Moscow to St Petersburg route, passes nearby, so traffic can be heavy with lorries. The side roads that wind between the lakes are far quieter and have a comfortable, rolling character, with decent surfaces and long views across the water. Valday has fuel, shops and cafes, and a population of about eighteen thousand. Boats cross to the island in the warmer months, so check before you plan. Early autumn is the time to come, when the forest is alight and the lake is calm.
