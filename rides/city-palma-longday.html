Why this ride

From Palma it runs 351 km to Palma; allow about 6 h 03 of riding. On the way it passes C2: Southwest Area, Talayotic Menorca, C1: Plains of Ciutadella and C3: Western Migjorn area. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Palma

    Palma is a city built around a cathedral that looks like a stone cliff. La Seu, as everyone calls it, rises directly above the seafront, a Gothic mass of pale limestone begun in the early fourteenth century by King James II of Mallorca, with a huge rose window and buttresses that seem to leap toward the sea. It took centuries to build, and in the twentieth century the architect Antoni Gaudí was called in to rework the interior, hanging a great canopy above the altar. The reflection of the building in the long pond in front, at dawn, is one of the great sights of the Mediterranean.

  2. 2. C2: Southwest Area

    Ciutadella is the old capital of Menorca, a handsome town of nearly thirty thousand people at the western end of the island, with a small harbour lined with fish restaurants and a cathedral built on the site of a mosque. The south-west corner of the island, below the town, holds some of the finest prehistoric remains on Menorca, including the burial chamber known as the Naveta des Tudons, a stone structure shaped like an upturned boat that is among the oldest roofed buildings in Europe. It belongs to the talayotic culture that built towers and T-shaped sanctuaries across the island over many centuries.

  3. 3. Talayotic Menorca

    Menorca's prehistory is stranger and more abundant than its tourist image suggests. From around 1000 BC, the island's people built huge monuments of unmortared stone, and the big circular towers called talaiots, which probably served as lookout posts, houses or meeting places, dot the island by the hundred. The culture that built them is called Talayotic, and the sites were listed by UNESCO in 2023 as a group of outstanding prehistoric settlements and sanctuaries. This stretch of land, west of Ciutadella, is not far from some of the best examples, among them the Naveta d'es Tudons, one of the oldest roofed buildings in Europe.

  4. 4. C1: Plains of Ciutadella

    West Menorca is a low, open plateau of limestone, and the plains around Ciutadella are the island at its most pastoral. The land is divided into small fields by dry-stone walls, thousands of kilometres of them, built over centuries by hand without mortar, and the walls give the whole landscape a stitched, patchwork look. Cows graze in the meadows, and farmhouses called llocs, painted white, sit among clusters of pines and wild olive trees. The soil is red in places, grey in others, and the sea shows at the end of almost every lane.

  5. 5. C3: Western Migjorn area

    Menorca has more prehistoric monuments per square kilometre than almost anywhere in Europe, and the south-west of the island is a good place to see why. The people who lived here in the Bronze and Iron Ages built talayots, round towers of stacked stones, and taules, T-shaped stone altars, and navetas, burial chambers shaped like upturned boats, in a culture that lasted for over a thousand years. In 2023 UNESCO added the Talayotic Culture of Minorca to its list, and the western Migjorn area, the south-west of the island, is one of the zones included.
