Why this ride

A 130 km round trip from Brampton - roughly 2 h 22 in the saddle. On the way it passes Hadrian's Wall between the track to Cockmo, Hadrian's Wall and vallum between the road, Hadrian's Wall and vallum between the B630 and back through Hadrian's Wall and vallum between the road. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Written Rock of Gelt: Roman quarry inscrip

    Start here.

    Beside the river Gelt, a small stream that runs through a wooded sandstone gorge east of Carlisle, a smooth face of rock bears an inscription cut by Roman soldiers some eighteen centuries ago. This is the Written Rock of Gelt. It marks the quarry where legionaries cut stone for the western part of Hadrian's Wall, and it records their work, with names and dates, in neat Latin letters that are still readable though weathered by the rain.

    Read the complete Written Rock of Gelt: Roman quarry inscrip write-up.

  2. 2. Hadrian's Wall between the track to Cockmo

    25 km in.

    The stretch of Wall near Cawfields is where the whole thing makes the most sense, because the Romans used the landscape. Here the Great Whin Sill, a hard ridge of dark dolerite, rises in a line of crags along the north, and the Wall runs along the top of it, looking down over a drop on one side and a long slope on the other. Close to Cockmount Hill, the Wall and its southern ditch, the vallum, are both visible, and the milefortlet at Cawfields is one of the best preserved on the line, a square enclosure with a gateway still standing. Nearby, an old quarry has filled with water to make a small, dark lake at the foot of the crags.

    Read the complete Hadrian's Wall between the track to Cockmo write-up.

  3. 3. Hadrian's Wall and vallum between the road

    46 km in.

    Between Hexham and Haydon Bridge, the river Tyne runs through a wide green valley, and on the ridge to the north the line of Hadrian's Wall can be traced across fields and woods in a series of ditches, banks and stretches of masonry.

    Read the complete Hadrian's Wall and vallum between the road write-up.

  4. 4. Hadrian's Wall and vallum between the B630

    65 km in.

    Out at the eastern end of the frontier, the Wall leaves the moors behind and runs through gentle farmland toward Newcastle, and most of what survives here is not stone but earth: the vallum, a broad ditch flanked by banks that the Romans dug just south of the Wall along almost its entire length. Between the Tyne and the Military Road, near Heddon-on-the-Wall and Rudchester, the vallum can be traced in the fields as a long hollow, and the Wall itself lies largely under the road or as low courses in the grass. Its purpose is still debated, but most scholars see it as a boundary marking the military zone, controlling who could approach the Wall.
