Why this ride

554 km from Chur to Santa Cristina Valgardena, about 8 h 55 of riding. The climbs: Ofenpass approach, Pordoi Pass, Campolongo Pass and Gardena Pass. It tops out at 2,239 m. It crosses from Switzerland into Italy - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It is a high-mountain day.

Great Dolomites Road

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2136 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance. Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Chur eastbound gate

    Chur likes to call itself the oldest town in Switzerland, and the claim holds up better than most: people have lived on this spot, where the Plessur meets the Rhine, for several thousand years. It became the seat of a bishop in late Roman times, and the cathedral on the hill still keeps that tradition alive. Today the city has around thirty thousand residents and sits at 593 metres, the gateway to Graubünden, with fuel, supermarkets, bakeries and restaurants everywhere a rider could want. As a start point it is practical and charming at once.

  2. 2. Ofenpass approach

    The Ofenpass, which the Romansh call Pass dal Fuorn, climbs through the only national park in Switzerland. The park was founded in 1914 and the road is the single way to see it by bike, so the rules matter: stay on the road, stop only where parking is allowed, and leave the ibex, red deer and marmots to their own devices.

    Read the complete Ofenpass approach write-up.

    You top out at 2149 m here. The road climbs 1556 m on the way up to it, steadily. Reckon on about 10 °C colder than the valley, and more in the wind. At this height the road is a summer one: snow gates can stay shut into June and shut again in October. Next is Pordoi Pass.

  3. 3. Pordoi Pass

    Pordoi is the high point of the Sella Ronda, a pass of 2,239 metres between the Fassa valley and Arabba, and for many people the one with the best view in the Dolomites. The road climbs from Canazei in about thirty hairpins to the saddle, where a big car park, a few restaurants and the cable car to Sass Pordoi cluster beneath the pale cliffs of the Sella massif. On a clear day, the rock turns pink and gold at dusk, the effect known locally as the enrosadira.

    Read the complete Pordoi Pass write-up.

  4. 4. Campolongo Pass

    Campolongo is the quiet pass in a family of loud ones. At 1,875 metres it links Corvara in Alta Badia with Arabba and the upper valley of Livinallongo, and on the Sella Ronda it is the stretch that most riders ride without thinking, between the more famous climbs of Pordoi and Gardena. That undersells it. The road winds up through pasture and scattered spruce from Corvara with a long, relaxed run of hairpins, and from the top the whole of the Sella massif stands over you, a pale, castellated block of limestone that looks as if it was built rather than eroded.

    Read the complete Campolongo Pass write-up.

    The high point of this section, 1875 m up. You lose 364 m on the way down to it, steadily. Reckon on about 8 °C colder than the valley, and more in the wind. High enough to hold ice in the shaded corners early and late in the year. Next is Gardena Pass, climbing 261 m.

  5. 5. Gardena Pass

    Gardena Pass joins two Ladin valleys: Val Gardena on the north side, with Selva and Ortisei, and Alta Badia on the south, around Corvara. At roughly 2,100 metres it is one of the four passes that circle the Sella massif, and the road runs between the great rock walls of the Sassolungo and the Sella, with their pale dolomite faces that glow pink at sunset.

  6. 6. Val Gardena overnight base

    Val Gardena is the valley where the Dolomites speak Ladin. The language, a Romance tongue descended from the Latin of Roman soldiers, survives here in a handful of valleys, and in villages like Selva, Santa Cristina and Ortisei you will see three sets of names on every sign: German, Italian and Ladin, so Selva is also Wolkenstein and Sëlva. The altitude of 1,560 metres puts you in Selva, at the top end of the valley, where the road to the Sella and Gardena passes begins and the walls of the Dolomites are close enough to touch.

Chur eastbound gate to Ofenpass approach

Chur eastbound gate → Ofenpass approach climbs about 1556 m in 78 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Ofenpass approach to Pordoi Pass

Ofenpass approach → Pordoi Pass is 147 km of getting across. Ride it tidily and keep the enthusiasm for what comes after.

Pordoi Pass to Campolongo Pass

Pordoi Pass → Campolongo Pass is 7 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Campolongo Pass to Gardena Pass

Campolongo Pass → Gardena Pass is 8 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Gardena Pass to Val Gardena overnight base

Gardena Pass → Val Gardena overnight base drops about 576 m over 8 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
