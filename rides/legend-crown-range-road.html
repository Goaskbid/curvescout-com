Why this ride

New Zealand's highest sealed road, Queenstown to Wanaka. 58 km from Queenstown to Wanaka, about 1 h 03 of riding. The highest point is about 1,100 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Queenstown

    The town sits on the shore of Lake Wakatipu, a long, dark, glacier-carved lake shaped like a lightning bolt, with the Remarkables mountain range rising straight from the far side in a wall of grey rock and snow. A Māori legend says the lake is the burnt outline of a giant, Matau, whose heart still beats under the water, and the lake level does rise and fall in a slow rhythm, a seiche caused by wind and pressure, which is the scientific explanation for the legend. The old coal-fired steamship TSS Earnslaw, launched in 1912, still crosses the lake to a high-country farm.

  2. 2. Near Arrowtown

    Lake Hayes is the small lake that every photographer in the Queenstown area seems to know about, and on a still morning you will see why. The water mirrors the brown and gold hills around it, and in autumn, from April into May, the poplars and willows along the shore turn a brilliant yellow. It sits in the Wakatipu basin between Queenstown and Arrowtown, along State Highway 6, and a walking track loops around the shore in a couple of hours, a fine way to loosen up after a morning in the saddle.

  3. 3. Near Arrowtown

    The air gets thin and cold quickly on the Crown Range. At this point, at about 1,121 metres, you are high on the zigzag section of the road between Arrow Junction and the saddle, where it climbs out of the Wakatipu basin in a series of tight hairpins. Look back over your shoulder: the whole basin spreads out below, with Lake Hayes, Arrowtown, the Shotover River, the mountains around Queenstown, and on a clear day the Remarkables catching the light. There is a viewpoint near the top of the zigzags where most people stop, and it is worth it.

  4. 4. Near Arrowtown

    Over the top of the Crown Range, the road falls into the Cardrona Valley and the mood changes completely. After the switchbacks on the Queenstown side, this is long, open country: a broad trough of tussock and schist with the Cardrona River braiding along the bottom, a few sheep stations, and almost nothing else for kilometre after kilometre. The road descends at a gentle grade with sweeping bends you can read well ahead, and on a calm day it is one of those stretches where you find yourself riding slower than you need to, just to make it last.

  5. 5. Wanaka

    Wānaka is a lakeside town that grew up without quite deciding to. A few thousand people live here, on the southern shore of a long glacial lake, with mountains in every direction and the white bulk of the Southern Alps closing the northern horizon. The great peak of Mount Aspiring, nearly three thousand metres high, is a pyramid you may glimpse on a clear day beyond the lake, and it is a rewarding sight after the climb from Queenstown.
