  1. 1. Mount Iboundji

    Iboundji is a name that carries over a large, quiet area of central Gabon, a country that is more than eighty percent forest. The mountain of that name rises from the rainforest of the Ogooué-Lolo region as an isolated massif, a green dome among low ridges, and for those who live nearby it has long been a landmark and a place of local stories.

  2. 2. Ecosystem and Relict Cultural Landscape of

    In central Gabon, where the rainforest breaks up into patches of open savanna, the Ogooué river bends through a landscape that has been a meeting place for about as long as people have crossed Africa. The long official name belongs to the Ecosystem and Relict Cultural Landscape of Lopé-Okanda, a UNESCO mixed site listed in 2007 for both its nature and its human story. The forest here is not a solid green wall. It shifts between thick canopy and grassy clearings, giving animals an unusually rich mix of habitats.

  3. 3. Elarmékora

    Elarmékora is a name to look for on a map of central Gabon, and most people will not find it. It belongs to the Lopé-Okanda landscape, a UNESCO World Heritage site where the equatorial rainforest breaks into patches of open savanna along the Ogooué River. This odd mosaic is a relic of ancient climate swings, when the forest shrank and grassland spread, and the people who lived here left a record of it in iron-working sites and rock engravings dated to thousands of years ago.
