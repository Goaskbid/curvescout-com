Why this ride

A 341 km loop that starts and ends near Rome, about 6 h 12 of riding. It goes by way of Narni and Assisi.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Rome

    Rome is the end of the line for the Mille Miglia, or at least the halfway mark, because the old race ran out from Brescia to Rome and back again, a thousand Italian miles in all, which is where the name comes from. It ran from 1927 until 1957, mostly through towns and villages with the roads left open and crowds standing at the kerb, and the drivers flew down the Via Cassia and through the Apennine passes at speeds that make the whole thing seem barely believable now. It ended after a crash in 1957 that killed spectators, and was later revived as a rolling classic-car parade, which still comes through Rome every spring.

    Read the complete Rome write-up.

  2. 2. Narni

    In Latin the town was called Narnia, and C. S. Lewis is widely believed to have taken the name for his magical land from an old atlas where he found it. Whether or not that is true, Narni has embraced the connection, and it does have something of a storybook quality: a medieval hill town perched on a rocky spur above the gorge of the Nera river, with towers, churches, narrow streets and a fortress on the summit. Some claim it is the geographical centre of Italy, and a monument in the valley marks the spot.

    Read the complete Narni write-up.

  3. 3. Assisi

    From the valley floor Assisi looks like a long pale ribbon draped across the lower slope of Monte Subasio, and the colour comes from the mountain itself. The town is built of Subasio stone, a limestone that ranges from cream to rose, and in the evening light the whole place glows pink. It is, of course, the town of Francis, the merchant's son who gave away everything in the early thirteenth century and founded the Franciscan order, and of Clare, who followed him. The great basilica built over his tomb, begun in 1228, has two churches one above the other, and the frescoes inside, including the cycle of the saint's life traditionally attributed to Giotto, changed the course of European painting.

    Read the complete Assisi write-up.
