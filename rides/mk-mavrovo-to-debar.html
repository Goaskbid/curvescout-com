  1. 1. Gostivar

    Gostivar sits at the head of the Polog valley in the west of North Macedonia, at the foot of the Šar mountains, and it works as the gateway from the main lowlands into the high country of Mavrovo. The town is large, around fifty thousand people, and mixed in character, with a mainly Albanian and Macedonian population, minarets and church towers, busy markets and the sound of two languages in the street. The Vardar river rises just outside the town, springing from a karst source below the mountains, and that is the start of the longest river in the country, which flows eastwards to Skopje and eventually to the Aegean.

  2. 2. Debar

    Debar sits in the far west of North Macedonia, close to the Albanian border, in a bowl of hills at the northern end of an artificial lake. It is a town of eighteen thousand people with a mixed population, mostly Albanian and Macedonian, a long history of trade and a reputation for craft: woodcarving, silverwork and weaving. Its old bazaar, its mosques and churches and a cluster of small spas on the outskirts give it a layered feel, and the pace of life is easy. The lake was created in the 1960s by the Spilje dam on the Black Drin.
