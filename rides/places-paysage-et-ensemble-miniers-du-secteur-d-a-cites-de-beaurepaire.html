Why this ride

From Vieux-Condé it runs 73 km to Vieux-Condé; allow about 1 h 16 of riding. It goes by way of Chevalement de la fosse Dutemple, Cités de Beaurepaire and back through Chevalement de la fosse Dutemple. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Paysage et ensemble miniers du secteur d’A

    Coal made this corner of northern France, and for a century and a half it made it black. The mining landscape listed by UNESCO runs for a hundred and twenty kilometres across the old Nord and Pas-de-Calais coalfield, and the sector at this stop lies in the Valenciennes end of it, close to the Belgian frontier and the river Escaut, which the Belgians call the Scheldt. The pits of Anzin, the company that started the whole business in the middle of the eighteenth century, were the first great mines in France.

  2. 2. Chevalement de la fosse Dutemple

    A steel tower with a big wheel at the top: once you have seen one headframe, you start to spot them all across the north of France, but each one marks a place where men went down into the dark. The headframe of the Fosse Dutemple, between Valenciennes and Petite-Forêt, is one of the survivors, kept as part of the UNESCO-listed Nord-Pas de Calais mining basin. Under it, cages once dropped miners hundreds of metres to the coal seams; now it stands as a monument, its pulleys still against the sky.

  3. 3. Cités de Beaurepaire

    Somain exists because of the railway. Several lines meet here, and the town grew up around the station, which still has the busy feel of a junction. The Cités de Beaurepaire were built by the Aniche mining company, one of the great concerns of the northern coalfield, and they show how a company could shape a landscape: neat brick rows, small squares, a few shared buildings, and gardens laid out to a plan. A local will tell you who lived in which house and which families still do; an outsider notices the symmetry first, and then the small signs of individuality, a painted door, a trellis, a satellite dish pointing the wrong way.
