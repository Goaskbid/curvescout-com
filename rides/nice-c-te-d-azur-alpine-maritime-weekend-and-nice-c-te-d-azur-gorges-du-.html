Why this ride

From Le Cannet it runs 542 km to Lucéram; allow about 9 h 52 of riding. It goes by way of Vence, Scenic stretch near Roquebillière and near Roquebillière. The climbs: near Mandelieu-la-Napoule and Cap-d'Ail. It tops out at about 1,700 m. It crosses France, Italy and Monaco - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules.

  1. 1. Fuel stop in Le Cannet

    Le Cannet climbs the hills directly behind Cannes, and from its upper streets you look down over the rooftops of the city to the bay, the Lérins islands and the red Esterel mountains to the west. The old village at its heart, with narrow lanes, painted walls and small squares, still has a Provençal charm that the busy streets below have largely lost.

  2. 2. High point near Mandelieu-la-Napoule

    Mandelieu-la-Napoule sits at the western end of the bay of Cannes, where the river Siagne meets the sea, and behind it rise two very different massifs. To the south-west, the Esterel is a jagged range of red volcanic rock, plunging into the sea along one of the most dramatic stretches of coast in France. To the north, the Tanneron is a softer range of hills famous for its mimosa, which blooms in brilliant yellow in late winter.

  3. 3. Vence

    Vence is where the Riviera stops pretending to be the seaside and starts to become the mountains. The old town sits on a rocky spur about ten kilometres inland from the coast, still largely enclosed by its medieval walls, and behind it the land rises in steps of limestone, the baous, towards the high plateaus of the Préalpes. Finishing a ride here means ending among fountains, narrow lanes and terraces in the shade.

  4. 4. Scenic stretch near Roquebillière

    Roquebillière sits in the valley of the Vésubie, one of the deep valleys that cut into the Alpes-Maritimes north of Nice. The river rushes down from the high peaks near the Italian border, and the valley is steep, wooded and dramatic, with villages perched on the slopes and mountains rising on every side.

  5. 5. Photo stop near Roquebillière

    Roquebillière has had to rebuild itself more than once. In 1926, a landslide swept down onto the old village, killing many of its inhabitants, and much of the population moved to a new site on the other side of the river. Then, in October 2020, Storm Alex unleashed catastrophic floods down the Vésubie valley, destroying roads, bridges and houses, and leaving scars that are still visible today. The resilience of the valley's people is remarkable, and the road has been rebuilt with new bridges and protections.

  6. 6. Ospedaletti

    Palms, pastel houses and a bay that faces due south: Ospedaletti is the Riviera dei Fiori at its most relaxed, a small Ligurian town tucked between Sanremo and Bordighera with its feet in the sea and terraced hills behind it. The old coast railway has been moved inland into a tunnel, and its former line along the shore is now a cycle and walking path, so the seafront is quieter than you might expect on such a crowded coast.

  7. 7. Cap-d'Ail

    Cap-d'Ail is the last French town before Monaco, and you can feel the principality pressing against it. The Basse Corniche winds in from Èze and Beaulieu between villas and pines, the rocks drop to clear water below, and then, almost without warning, you are among the towers of Fontvieille. Cap-d'Ail itself is quieter than its neighbour: a scatter of Belle Époque villas, gardens and newer apartments on the slopes above a small cape with coves and a little beach.

  10. 10. Scenic stretch near Menton

    Menton is the last town in France before Italy, the finish of the Route des Grandes Alpes, and after seven hundred kilometres of mountains it feels like being tipped into a warm bath. The old town tumbles down a hill to the harbour in tones of ochre, apricot and cream, topped by the baroque Basilica of Saint Michael with its glowing facade. The climate is the mildest on the French Riviera, sheltered by the mountains behind, and the local lemons are famous, celebrated each February in the Fête du Citron, when huge sculptures made of fruit and flowers fill the gardens and people parade through the streets.

  12. 12. Antibes

    Old ramparts rise straight from the sea at Antibes, and behind them a tight knot of lanes, markets and ochre houses climbs toward a castle on the shore. The town sits between Nice and Cannes on the bay of the same name, facing the snowy line of the Alps on clear winter days, with the long wooded peninsula of Cap d'Antibes to the south and the jazz town of Juan-les-Pins on its western flank.

  13. 13. Near Saint-Laurent-du-Var

    Until 1860, the river at Saint-Laurent-du-Var marked an international frontier. On the west bank lay France; on the east bank lay the County of Nice, part of the Kingdom of Sardinia, and travellers along the coast had to cross the wide, shifting bed of the Var to pass from one to the other. For centuries there was no proper bridge, and the village made its living from the men who carried travellers across on their backs or guided them through the shallows. When Nice became French, the border vanished, but the river is still the dividing line between the town and the city.

  15. 15. Near Puget-Théniers, on the way back

    The upper Var valley around Puget-Théniers is a strange and beautiful corridor: a wide bed of grey gravel with the river braiding through it, steep wooded slopes on either side, and above them the bare limestone of the Pre-Alps catching the light. The N202 runs through it from Nice towards Digne, and for riders coming back from the Verdon it is the long, satisfying homeward leg, quick and open with the river beside you.

  16. 16. Back to Gorges

    North of Nice, the valley of the Paillon cuts up into the mountains, and Lucéram, Berre-les-Alpes and L'Escarène are small, stone-built villages along its length. The landscape is a little wilder than the coast, with olive terraces on the lower slopes giving way to pine forest and bare limestone higher up, and the air carries a scent of thyme and warm rock. The Paillon is a seasonal river, often almost dry in summer and surprisingly fierce after autumn storms.
