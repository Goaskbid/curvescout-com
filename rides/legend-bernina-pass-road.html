Why this ride

curated scenic road. glaciers, lakes, railway heritage. From St. Moritz it runs 43 km to Tirano; allow about 47 min of riding. The stops in between: Pontresina and Poschiavo. The highest point is about 2,500 m. It crosses from Switzerland into Italy - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. St. Moritz

    St. Moritz is not subtle, and the world's most famous winter resort does not intend to be. It sits at about 1,800 metres in the Upper Engadine, on a sunlit shelf above a lake, with ranges of white peaks on every side and a record of sunny days that the tourist board advertises with some pride. Its mineral springs were known in the Bronze Age, and in the Middle Ages pilgrims came to drink the water. Winter tourism, legend says, started in 1864 when the hotelier Johannes Badrutt bet English summer guests that they would enjoy the Alps in the snow. The town has hosted the Winter Olympics twice, in 1928 and 1948, and the Cresta Run, an ice toboggan track, still runs every winter.

  2. 2. Pontresina

    Pontresina sits at the mouth of two glacial valleys on the Upper Engadin, and the village has been a mountaineers' base since the nineteenth century. The first ascent of Piz Bernina, the highest peak in the eastern Alps at 4,049 metres, was made in 1850 by a Graubünden surveyor, Johann Coaz, and the village grew rich guiding foreigners up the neighbouring summits. Old photographs of men in woollen coats and hobnailed boots, roped together on a glacier, still hang in the cafes.

  3. 3. Near Poschiavo

    The Bernina Pass, at 2,328 metres, crosses the high Alps between the Engadine and the Valposchiavo in south-eastern Switzerland, and it is a place of water and ice. On the broad, rocky summit lie two lakes, Lago Bianco and Lago Nero, separated by a low ridge that is also a great continental watershed: water from one side flows to the Inn and the Danube and eventually to the Black Sea, while water from the other flows to the Adda, the Po and the Adriatic.

  4. 4. Poschiavo

    Poschiavo is the Italian-speaking surprise on the far side of the Bernina. After the cold, bare grandeur of the pass, you drop through a series of hairpins and arrive in a warm, green valley where the language changes, the houses change and the food changes, even though you are still in Switzerland. The valley, the Valposchiavo, belongs to canton Graubünden, but its people speak a Lombard dialect and its old town looks like a village in Piedmont or Lombardy.

  5. 5. Tirano

    Tirano is where the Alps run out of snow and start thinking about wine. The town of about eight thousand sits in the Valtellina, the long east-west valley of the Adda, in Lombardy, at the foot of the Bernina Pass, and its mood is distinctly Italian: arcaded streets, warm stone, an espresso at every corner. It is the southern terminus of the Bernina Express, the railway that climbs over the pass from Switzerland, and the station square has red Swiss trains next to Italian ones, a peculiar and pleasing sight.
