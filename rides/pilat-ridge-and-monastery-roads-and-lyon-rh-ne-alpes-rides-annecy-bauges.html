Why this ride

From Courzieu it runs 679 km to Tarare; allow about 13 h 46 of riding. It goes by way of Sainte-Croix-en-Jarez, Pilat and Yzeron. The climbs: Col de la République and near Neulise. It tops out at about 900 m. It is a high-mountain day.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Yzeron ridge roads

    Lyonnais like to escape to the hills on Sundays, and the Yzeron valley is one of their favourite exits. It lies to the west of the city, in the Monts du Lyonnais, a range of rounded, wooded hills that reach about nine hundred metres at their highest, and the roads here, little more than lanes, climb out of the suburbs into a landscape of meadows, chestnuts and old stone farms. The village of Yzeron gives its name to the stream and the valley, and it has been a weekend retreat for generations.

  2. 2. Sainte-Croix-en-Jarez

    Sainte-Croix-en-Jarez is a village inside a monastery, which is a sentence that does not make sense until you see it. Béatrix de Roussillon founded a Carthusian charterhouse here in 1280, in a hollow of the Pilat hills, and for five centuries, monks lived in solitary cells around a cloister, as the Carthusians do. When the Revolution dispersed the community, the buildings were sold, and the villagers moved into the monks' cells and the service buildings, so today ordinary houses, a bakery and a café share the old walls, and the village's streets follow the cloister. The church, the chapter house and the great gate are still there, and some of the cells are open to visitors.

  3. 3. Pilat viewpoint

    The Pilat is the last high ground before the Rhône, a rounded massif of granite and forest that rises abruptly from the river valley to its highest point, the Crêt de la Perdrix, at about fourteen hundred metres. It is a regional nature park, a place of beech and fir woods, heather moors and open pastures, and it feels more remote than its position, between Lyon and Saint-Étienne, would suggest.

  4. 4. Col de la République

    The Col de la République is a pass with a political name and an unexpected role in cycling history. At about eleven hundred and forty-five metres, it carries the old main road from Saint-Étienne to Annonay across the Pilat massif, and it has been climbed by the Tour de France more than once, while the steady grade through the beechwoods has made it a training favourite for local clubs. The pass lies in the Parc naturel régional du Pilat, between the industrial valley of Saint-Étienne and the Rhône basin.

  5. 5. Back to Yzeron

    Yzeron sits on a hillside high in the Monts du Lyonnais, a village of stone houses and a church spire among pasture and spruce woods, with views over the folded hills toward Lyon. It is a cooler, quieter place than the city, which is only about twenty-five kilometres away. In summer, people from Lyon drive up to escape the heat, and in winter a dusting of snow sometimes falls on the high ground, giving the villages an Alpine air. The landscape is made of rounded ridges and wooded gorges, cut by little rivers like the Yzeron itself, which runs down toward the city through a long green valley.

  6. 6. Fuel stop near Tarare

    Muslin made Tarare's fortune. From the eighteenth century onwards the town wove fine, light cotton cloth that was sold across Europe, and every few years it still celebrates the fact with the Fête des Mousselines, when the streets and façades disappear under kilometres of draped fabric in a riot of colour. Outside festival time, Tarare is a quiet valley town in the hills between the Beaujolais and the Lyonnais, with old mill buildings and a long main street.
