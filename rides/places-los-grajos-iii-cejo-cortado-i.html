Why this ride

From Cieza it runs 92 km to Cieza; allow about 1 h 35 of riding. It goes by way of Abrigo de las Enredaderas, Cejo Cortado I and back through Abrigo de las Enredaderas. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Los Grajos III

    Cieza is a big town for this part of Murcia, with over thirty-five thousand people on the banks of the Segura, and its fame in Spain rests on fruit. In February, the peach and apricot orchards of the river valley burst into pink and white blossom, and visitors come from all over the region to see the hills turn soft with it. The name of the shelter, Los Grajos, means the jackdaws, or rooks, the black birds that nest in the cliffs, and the ride ties it to Maina and to the Abric de la Paella. The paintings belong to the Levantine tradition protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain, and Cieza has some of the most important sites in this part of the country.

  2. 2. Abrigo de las Enredaderas

    Where the Río Segura squeezes through the sandstone and limestone ridges of northern Murcia, between Calasparra and Cieza, the valley turns into a canyon of orange and grey rock with narrow green strips of reeds and orchard along the water. The Abrigo de las Enredaderas, a rock shelter, lies twelve kilometres or so from both towns, in dry hills of pine, rosemary and esparto grass that smell sharply of resin on a hot day.

  3. 3. Cejo Cortado I

    Mula is a town in the heart of the Murcia region, built on the slopes of a hill crowned by a sixteenth-century castle, with an old quarter of narrow streets, noble houses and churches. Around it, a dry landscape of hills, ravines and badlands opens out, with orchards and olive groves along the river. The Cejo Cortado, a name meaning the cut brow, refers to a rocky escarpment with prehistoric shelters about seven kilometres from the town, between Mula, Albudeite and Ricote.
