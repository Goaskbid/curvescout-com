Why this ride

A 114 km round trip from Calasparra - roughly 1 h 58 in the saddle. On the way it passes Cejo Cortado I, Los Grajos and back through Cejo Cortado I. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abrigos del Pozo I y II

    Calasparra sits where the Segura river leaves the hills of the Murcian interior, and anyone who has ordered a paella with proper grains has probably eaten its rice without knowing it. The town has its own protected denomination for rice, grown in terraces watered from the river, and the pale fields in the valley are an odd and welcome sight in country otherwise made of rock and scrub. Eleven thousand people live here, which means fuel, a bakery that opens early and plenty of bars where lunch is not a negotiation.

  2. 2. Cejo Cortado I

    Mula is a town in the heart of the Murcia region, built on the slopes of a hill crowned by a sixteenth-century castle, with an old quarter of narrow streets, noble houses and churches. Around it, a dry landscape of hills, ravines and badlands opens out, with orchards and olive groves along the river. The Cejo Cortado, a name meaning the cut brow, refers to a rocky escarpment with prehistoric shelters about seven kilometres from the town, between Mula, Albudeite and Ricote.

  3. 3. Los Grajos

    Jackdaws gave this place its name, grajos in Spanish, and they still wheel and chatter around the limestone cliffs where prehistoric people once sheltered and painted. Los Grajos lies in the hills near Alhama de Murcia, in the valley of the Guadalentín, where dry mountains rise above a plain of orchards, greenhouses and towns. The rock shelters here hold paintings in the Levantine style, and access is generally restricted to protect them.
