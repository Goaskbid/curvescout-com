Why this ride

A 318 km round trip from Quehue - roughly 5 h 29 in the saddle. On the way it passes Segment Cusipata-Santa Cruz de Occobamba N, Belén Square and Church and back through Segment Cusipata-Santa Cruz de Occobamba N. The highest point is about 4,450 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Segment Chiwuanquiri

    The Qhapaq Ñan, the great road system of the Inca Empire, stretched over thirty thousand kilometres across the Andes, linking Cusco with the farthest corners of the empire, from southern Colombia to central Chile. In 2014, UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site across six countries, and the listing includes hundreds of individual segments, each a fragment of the network. The Chiwuanquiri segment lies in the high province of Canas, south of Cusco, near the village of Cahuanuyo, on the high puna at around four thousand metres. Here, the old road crosses open grassland, with stone paving, retaining walls and drainage channels still visible in places, and it is a moving experience to walk on a road built more than five centuries ago.

  2. 2. Segment Cusipata-Santa Cruz de Occobamba N

    South of Cusco, the Vilcanota River runs through a broad, fertile valley flanked by high mountains, and along it ran one of the most important stretches of the Qhapaq Ñan, the Inca road system that linked an empire stretching from Ecuador to Chile. The route from Cusco to Lake Titicaca followed this valley, and sections of the original road, some paved with stone, some cut into the hillside, still survive. The segment between Cusipata and Santa Cruz de Occobamba went onto the World Heritage list in 2014 as one strand of the Qhapaq Ñan.

  3. 3. Belén Square and Church

    Belén is a small plaza on the south side of Cusco's centre, with a church that dates from the colonial era and a neighbourhood that has long been home to potters and tradespeople. Like much of the city, it was built on Inca land, and the name recalls the hospital and chapel of Bethlehem that the Spanish founded for the care of the sick. The church is modest by Cusco's standards, but its location, away from the busiest tourist streets, gives it an everyday air that many visitors miss.
