  1. 1. Cottonwood Falls

    The courthouse in Cottonwood Falls looks as if somebody dropped a piece of France onto the prairie. It is a small limestone building in the Second Empire style, topped with a mansard roof and a clock tower, finished in the 1870s and still used by Chase County, which makes it one of the oldest working courthouses in Kansas. Around it are a handful of limestone streets with fewer than a thousand people living on them, and the whole place feels like a village that was built to last and then politely not expanded.

  2. 2. Council Grove

    Wagon trains did their last serious shopping in Council Grove. In the nineteenth century, this was the final reliable stop on the Santa Fe Trail, the place where traders headed for New Mexico cut timber for spare axles, stocked up and waited for enough company to travel safely onward. A treaty with the Osage was signed here in 1825 under a great oak, and the Kaw, or Kansa people, were later settled nearby, so the place has a long record as a meeting point, which is what the name means.
