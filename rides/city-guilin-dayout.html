Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Guilin

    Guilin gets its name from the osmanthus trees that line its streets, and when they flower in autumn, the whole city smells of apricot and honey. The name means forest of cassia, and the city of about six hundred and fifty thousand people sits in a bowl of limestone hills in the north-east of Guangxi, on the banks of the Li River. The hills are the point: they rise in sharp, steep-sided cones and towers right in the middle of the town, so that streets, bridges and parks have to find their way between them. Elephant Trunk Hill, on the river bank, has an arch at its base that makes it look like an elephant drinking.

  2. 2. Guilin Karst - Lijiang Fengcong Karst Sect

    There are two kinds of limestone scenery around Guilin, and the difference is the key to understanding what you see. Fenglin, or peak forest, is a plain studded with isolated towers, each standing alone like a tree in a clearing. Fengcong, or peak cluster, is where the towers are joined at the base, forming rows and clumps of hills with deep hollows between them. The section along the Li River south of Guilin, down towards Yangshuo, has mostly the clustered kind, and the stretch where the river threads through them is the most famous landscape in China.
