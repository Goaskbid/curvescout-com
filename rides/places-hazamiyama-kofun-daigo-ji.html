Why this ride

148 km from Kashihara to Kashihara, about 2 h 33 of riding. On the way it passes Kôfuku-ji, Daigo-ji and back through Kôfuku-ji. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hazamiyama Kofun

    South-east of Osaka, in the low country between the Yamato river and the Nara hills, the earth is full of tombs. These are the kofun, great mounds raised for rulers and nobles between roughly the third and the sixth centuries, and the most famous have a distinctive shape, a keyhole, with a round rear mound and a trapezoid front, surrounded by a moat. The Mozu and Furuichi groups, with forty-nine mounds between them, were listed by UNESCO in 2019, and Hazamiyama, as far as I can tell, belongs with the Furuichi mounds.

  2. 2. Kôfuku-ji

    In the early eighth century, when Nara became Japan's first permanent capital, the powerful Fujiwara clan moved their family temple here, and Kofuku-ji became one of the great temples of the city, wealthy enough to maintain its own warrior monks. Its five storey pagoda, rebuilt in the fifteenth century after one of several fires, is the second tallest wooden pagoda in Japan and a symbol of Nara, reflected in the Sarusawa pond below. The Central Golden Hall was rebuilt and reopened in 2018, and the National Treasure Museum holds the famous statue of Ashura, a three faced, six armed guardian with a strikingly human expression. Kofuku-ji is part of the Historic Monuments of Ancient Nara World Heritage Site. The pagoda is undergoing a long restoration and may be wrapped in scaffolding when you visit.

  3. 3. Daigo-ji

    The oldest building in Kyoto does not stand in the city centre but in the southeastern hills, at the temple of Daigo-ji: a five storey pagoda completed in 951, which has survived fires, earthquakes and wars that destroyed almost everything else of its age in the city. The temple was founded in the ninth century on Mount Daigo, and its grounds spread from the foot of the mountain to the summit, linked by a steep path, with halls, gardens and sub temples along the way. It is part of the Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto World Heritage Site.
