Why this ride

Historic mountain villages on the outward leg and the Rio Grande corridor on the return. The road: NM-76, NM-68 and US-64. Distance and riding time are estimates from the checkpoints until the road is measured on this page.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Santa Fe

    Route 66 used to go to Santa Fe, and then in 1937 it did not. The original alignment of the Mother Road ran over Glorieta Pass into the old Spanish capital of New Mexico, before dropping south to Albuquerque, and then engineers cut a straight line across the desert from Santa Rosa to Albuquerque, shaving about a hundred miles off the journey and leaving Santa Fe on a bypassed loop. The ride from Amarillo to Santa Fe follows that early route, and this is the end of it, in a city at about seven thousand feet in the foothills of the Sangre de Cristo Mountains.

    Read the complete Santa Fe write-up.

  2. 2. Chimayo

    Chimayo is a village in a fold of the high desert foothills of northern New Mexico, at about 1,800 metres, and it holds one of the most important pilgrimage sites in the United States. The Santuario de Chimayo, a small adobe church completed in 1816, stands in a courtyard shaded by cottonwoods, with twin bell towers and a simple, dignified interior. Inside a side room, a small hole in the floor, the pocito, contains soft earth said to have healing properties, and every year tens of thousands of pilgrims come, many on foot, to take a pinch of the soil, with the greatest numbers on Good Friday.

    Read the complete Chimayo write-up.

  3. 3. Taos

    Taos is a small town with a big reputation among painters, writers and people who came for a visit and stayed. It lies at about 7,000 feet on a high plateau at the southern end of the Sangre de Cristo Mountains in northern New Mexico, with the dark ridge of the range rising to the east and the dry sagebrush plain dropping to the Rio Grande gorge to the west. The light is extraordinary, clear and golden, and early in the twentieth century it drew a group of artists, the Taos Society of Artists, who painted the landscape and the people. D. H. Lawrence also lived near here for a time in the 1920s, and his ranch can be visited.

    Read the complete Taos write-up.
