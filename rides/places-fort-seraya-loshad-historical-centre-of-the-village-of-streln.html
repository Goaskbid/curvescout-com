Why this ride

From Lebyazh’ye it runs 283 km to Lebyazh’ye; allow about 5 h of riding. The stops in between: The Datcha of the Hospital, The Monastery Troitse-Sergieva Pustin, Highway Pushkin - Gatchina and Historical Centre of the Village of Streln. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

38 km from The Datcha of the Hospital to The Monastery Troitse-Sergieva Pustin, mostly on КАД. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 15 and 65 m. Watch for sand and spray near the water.

The stops

  1. 1. Fort «Seraya Loshad»

    Start here.

    Along the southern shore of the Gulf of Finland, west of Saint Petersburg, a low strip of pine forest and sand runs for kilometres between the road and the sea. Fort Seraya Loshad, the Grey Horse, stands in this coastal country, eleven kilometres from the village of Lebyazhye, among a group of forts built for the defence of the capital and the naval base at Kronstadt.

    Read the complete Fort «Seraya Loshad» write-up.

  2. 2. The Datcha of the Hospital

    30 km in.

    Somewhere between Bolshaya Izhora and Lomonosov, along the southern shore of the Gulf of Finland, stood a summer house known as the Datcha of the Hospital. The name suggests one of the many country retreats built along this coast in the nineteenth century, when the road west from St Petersburg was lined with the estates of the aristocracy, merchants and institutions. Few of these houses survive intact, and the exact remains of this one are hard to pin down; it is better to take it as a pause on one of the most historic stretches of shore in Russia.

    Read the complete The Datcha of the Hospital write-up.

  3. 3. The Monastery Troitse-Sergieva Pustin

    68 km in.

    On the southern shore of the Gulf of Finland, between St Petersburg and Peterhof, the old road runs past a string of palaces and estates that the Russian elite built in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries to be near the sea and the court. Among them, near Strelna, stands the Trinity-Sergius Hermitage, a monastery founded in the eighteenth century as a dependency of the great Trinity Lavra of St Sergius near Moscow. It is part of the wide UNESCO listing of St Petersburg's historic centre and the monuments around it.

    Read the complete The Monastery Troitse-Sergieva Pustin write-up.

  4. 4. Highway Pushkin - Gatchina

    104 km in.

    Two imperial palaces lie at either end of this road, and between them the flat country south of Saint Petersburg still carries the scars of the twentieth century's worst siege. At the northern end is Pushkin, the former Tsarskoye Selo, where the Catherine Palace stretches its blue and white façade for hundreds of metres and the reconstructed Amber Room glows inside. At the southern end is Gatchina, Paul I's palace, grey and fortress-like, in a park of lakes and islands. The historic road between them is one of the many components of the World Heritage Site of the historic centre of Saint Petersburg and its related monuments.

    Read the complete Highway Pushkin - Gatchina write-up.

  5. 5. Historical Centre of the Village of Streln

    141 km in.

    Strelna is where Peter the Great first looked at the Gulf of Finland from the south shore and decided that his new capital needed a country palace worthy of Versailles. He began a residence here in the early eighteenth century, before settling on Peterhof, a few kilometres to the west, and the estate was later developed by his daughter Elizabeth and then given to the Grand Duke Konstantin, whose name it still bears in the Konstantinovsky Palace. In the 2000s the palace was restored and today it is used for state receptions and conferences, with the grounds open to visitors on a part-time basis. The historic centre of the village is a small area of old streets near the shore.

    Read the complete Historical Centre of the Village of Streln write-up.
