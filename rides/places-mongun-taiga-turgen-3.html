Why this ride

It goes by way of Tsagan shuvuut, Turgen and back through Tsagan shuvuut. The highest point is about 2,950 m. It crosses from Russia into Mongolia - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mongun Taiga

    Mongun-Taiga is the highest peak in Tuva, a snow-capped mountain of nearly four thousand metres on the border with Mongolia, where the Altai ranges tangle with the Sayan and the steppe fades into rock and ice. The name means roughly the silver mountain, which suits it, and the area is a wild upland of glacial valleys, alpine lakes and wide, windy pastures that are grazed in summer by yaks, sheep and horses belonging to Tuvan herders.

  2. 2. Tsagan shuvuut

    Tsagan Shuvuut means roughly white mountain-range in Mongolian, and the name fits. High in the far north-west, near the meeting point of Tuva and Mongolia, the range rises into snow and rock, and the slopes are dark with larch and cedar taiga lower down. This is the Altai-Sayan country, where Siberian forest meets Central Asian steppe, and it feels like both at once.

  3. 3. Turgen

    Far to the north-west of Mongolia, past the salt shores of Uvs Nuur, the country folds into a high rim of mountains where the steppe meets the Siberian forest. Turgen is the name of a river and a mountain massif here, in the Uvs basin, a region that UNESCO treats as one of the great closed drainage basins of Central Asia, with a lake so salty that fish barely live in it and an extraordinary spread of habitats, from desert to tundra, in a short distance.
