Why this ride

A one-way ride of 429 km, Bourail to Koumac - roughly 5 h 52 in the saddle. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bourail

    Bourail is the main town of the middle section of New Caledonia's west coast, a cattle town of about 4,800 people that feels more like rural Australia than the Pacific. The surrounding plain is ranch country, dotted with niaouli trees whose pale peeling bark gives the savanna a ghostly look, and every August the town holds its big agricultural fair, a sort of country show with horses, cattle and a lot of barbecue. The Pacific side is only a few minutes away, and the coast here has long white beaches and a famous rock arch, the Roche Percée, with a standing stone beside it called the Bonhomme.

  2. 2. Kone

    Koné is where New Caledonia stops looking like a holiday brochure and starts looking like a working island. The town sits on the west coast of the Grande Terre, about halfway up, in a dry plain of savanna and cattle ranches, and it has been the administrative centre of the Northern Province since the province was created in the 1980s.

  3. 3. Koumac

    Koumac is the last proper town on the road north, and it knows it. A few thousand people live here, spread around a main street with a supermarket, a bakery or two, a petrol station and a handful of restaurants, and beyond it the sealed road thins out and the Grande Terre narrows toward its northern tip. The town sits on the dry side of the island, where the mountains hold back the clouds and the plains turn brown in the dry season, and the Tiébaghi massif rises to the west with its red mining scars, once the site of a large chrome mine that gave the district its livelihood.
