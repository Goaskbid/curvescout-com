Why this ride

From Petrodvorets it runs 302 km to Petrodvorets; allow about 4 h 51 of riding. It goes by way of Tolbukhin Lighthouse on Tolbukhin Island, Terioki, Koltushskoe Highway and back through Terioki. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

27 km from The Mordvinov's Estate to Tolbukhin Lighthouse on Tolbukhin Island, mostly on КАД. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 2 and 22 m. Water alongside for much of it - pick one good pull-out and stop.

The stops

  1. 1. The Mordvinov's Estate

    Start here.

    Peterhof's fountains get the attention, but the coast road west of it, running along the Gulf of Finland toward Oranienbaum, is the better introduction to the landscape. The Mordvinov estate, named for a family of Russian statesmen and naval officers, belongs to the string of country houses that nobles built along this shore in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, when Peter the Great's new capital pulled the whole aristocracy to the Baltic.

    Read the complete The Mordvinov's Estate write-up.

  2. 2. Tolbukhin Lighthouse on Tolbukhin Island

    27 km in.

    Ships have been guided by a light on Tolbukhin island for about three centuries, and it is one of those places that makes more sense on a chart than on a road. The island lies in the Gulf of Finland, south-west of Kronstadt and out in the shallow channel that leads to Saint Petersburg, and the lighthouse on it marks the southern passage into the Neva Bay. The surrounding waters are shallow, and in the grey light of autumn the sea and the sky merge into a single pale band.

    Read the complete Tolbukhin Lighthouse on Tolbukhin Island write-up.

  3. 3. Terioki

    90 km in.

    Terioki, or Terijoki in Finnish, is the old name of Zelenogorsk, a resort town on the northern shore of the Gulf of Finland, about fifty kilometres from St Petersburg. Until the Winter War, it was part of Finland, and before the revolution it was one of the favourite dacha destinations for the wealthy of the imperial capital, who came by train to spend summers among the pine trees and sandy beaches. After the Soviet Union took the Karelian Isthmus in 1940 and again in 1944, the Finnish population left, and in 1948 the town was renamed Zelenogorsk, the green hill.

    Read the complete Terioki write-up.

  4. 4. Koltushskoe Highway

    164 km in.

    East of Saint Petersburg, the Koltushskoe highway runs through suburban sprawl and countryside toward the village of Koltushi, and it belongs to a memorial landscape that ties the whole region together: the Green Belt of Glory, a chain of monuments marking the defensive line around Leningrad during the siege of 1941 to 1944, when nearly nine hundred days of blockade cost the lives of hundreds of thousands of civilians. Monuments, steles and memorials stand along the roads where the front lines and supply routes ran, including the Road of Life across Lake Ladoga.

    Read the complete Koltushskoe Highway write-up.
