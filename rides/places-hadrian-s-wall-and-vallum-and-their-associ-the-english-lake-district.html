Why this ride

From Greenhead it runs 288 km to Greenhead; allow about 4 h 20 of riding. It goes by way of Herd Hill North, The English Lake District and back through Herd Hill North. Lakes are the scenery here.

The roads

Herd Hill North → The English Lake District

Herd Hill North to The English Lake District: 84 km, mostly on A591. It gains around 300 m and ends high, near 215 m. Best in the early light, before the traffic.

The stops

  1. 1. Hadrian's Wall and vallum and their associ

    Start here.

    Gilsland is a village with a split personality. The boundary between Northumberland and Cumbria runs right through it, along the Poltross Burn, so the houses on one side are in one county and those on the other in another. Hadrian's Wall runs through it too, and the stretch of Wall, vallum and associated features around the village is part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire World Heritage site. The Wall here crosses the narrow gorge of the burn and the railway line, with a milecastle right next to the old station.

    Read the complete Hadrian's Wall and vallum and their associ write-up.

  2. 2. Herd Hill North

    60 km in.

    Bowness-on-Solway is the western end of Hadrian's Wall, a quiet village on a low headland above the estuary, and Herd Hill lies a little farther along the shore on the Cardurnock peninsula. Roman engineers built the wall to Bowness, where the fort of Maia stood, and then continued the defences south along the Cumbrian coast with milefortlets and towers. The northern watchtower on this hill was one of the chain that watched across the water to Scotland, at a place where the Solway is narrow enough to be forded at low tide.

    Read the complete Herd Hill North write-up.

  3. 3. The English Lake District

    144 km in.

    Wordsworth wandered lonely as a cloud round here, and anybody who has ridden the Lake District on a wet bank holiday will understand the appeal of being lonely. The English Lake District, listed by UNESCO in 2017 as a cultural landscape, is a compact knot of mountains, lakes and valleys in Cumbria, shaped by glaciers and then by centuries of sheep farming, slate quarrying and, from the late eighteenth century, by poets, painters and tourists who came looking for the sublime.

    Read the complete The English Lake District write-up.
