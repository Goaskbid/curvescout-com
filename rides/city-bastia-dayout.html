  1. 1. Bastia

    Bastia has always been the working port of Corsica. Genoa made Bastia the island's capital for centuries, until 1791, and the town retains a busy, slightly rough charm that is quite unlike Calvi's polish or Ajaccio's grandeur.

  2. 2. Gulf of Porto: Calanche of Piana

    The Calanques of Piana are red. Not a polite pink, but a deep rust-orange, the granite eroded by wind and sea into spires, arches and strange figures that people have been naming for centuries, such as the heart, the dog's head and the bear. They rise hundreds of metres straight out of the sea along the western coast of Corsica, with the maquis, the dense scrub of strawberry tree, myrtle and rock rose, clinging to every ledge. The scent on a warm day is overwhelming, and the whole area is part of a UNESCO site that also covers the Gulf of Girolata and the Scandola reserve.
