Why this ride

Short, clean and good when the west wind is not joking. The best part sits around Danish Wohld lanes, Kiel Canal view, while Gettorf coffee keeps the day practical instead of turning it into a fuel or food hunt. Check the OSM map, Google preview and GPX checkpoint order before departure; they use the same waypoint sequence. Ride it with legal pull-offs, patience through villages and enough daylight to stop without rushing.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Danish Wohld lanes

    The Danish Wohld is a rolling peninsula of farmland and beech woods between Kiel Fjord and the Bay of Eckernforde, an unassuming corner of Schleswig-Holstein that rewards a motorcyclist with exactly what a short evening ride should offer: quiet lanes, long views and the smell of the sea on the breeze. The name is old, with Wohld meaning forest, and it recalls the days when this land formed a border region between the Danish and German worlds. The ride starts in the lanes near Osdorf, a village of a couple of thousand people, with Felm a little farther on, and Kiel's busy fuel stations and cafes a short ride behind you.

  2. 2. Gettorf coffee

    Gettorf is the kind of town that makes a ride feel like a normal day. It sits on the Danish Wohld, the green peninsula between Kiel and Eckernförde Bay, roughly six thousand people around a church, a main street and a handful of cafés and bakeries. The name Danish Wohld goes back to a time when this was borderland between Danish and German influence, and the land was forested and thinly settled. Today it is farmland, hedgerow and quiet manor estates, with the sea close but rarely in view.

  3. 3. Kiel Canal view

    The Kiel Canal is one of the busiest artificial waterways in the world, and you can stand beside it on a quiet country lane and feel entirely alone, until a container ship the size of an apartment block slides past at head height, a few metres from the bank. Opened in 1895 as the Kaiser Wilhelm Canal, it cuts across the base of the Jutland peninsula from the Elbe estuary to the Baltic at Kiel, a distance of about ninety-eight kilometres, saving ships a long and stormy trip around Denmark. Today, thousands of ships use it each year.
