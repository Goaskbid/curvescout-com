Why this ride

curated scenic road. sea cliffs, tunnels, volcanic coast. From Funchal it runs 49 km to São Vicente; allow about 53 min of riding. The stops in between: Câmara de Lobos and Porto Moniz. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Funchal

    Funchal climbs the sides of a natural amphitheatre on the south coast of Madeira, and from the harbour the houses rise in tiers into the green mountains behind. It is the island's capital, with about a hundred thousand people, and the name comes from funcho, the Portuguese word for fennel, which grew wild here when the first settlers arrived in the fifteenth century. Sugar made the island rich, then wine, and the legacy of both still shows in the tall merchant houses and the church interiors covered in gilded wood.

  2. 2. Câmara de Lobos

    Câmara de Lobos is the fishing harbour everyone paints. Winston Churchill sat here in 1950 to do exactly that, on a holiday to Madeira, and the place still trades on the connection with a viewpoint named after him. Below the road, a small bay of brightly painted wooden boats, drying nets and stone steps looks very much as it must have done a century ago, and above it white houses climb the slope in terraces.

  4. 4. Porto Moniz

    Porto Moniz sits at the north-western corner of Madeira, where the island runs out of land and meets the Atlantic with a wall of black volcanic rock. The village is small, with just under three thousand inhabitants, and its fame rests on the natural swimming pools, a series of basins formed in solidified lava by ancient eruptions, which fill with clear seawater at high tide and make a sheltered place to swim among the rocks while the swell crashes just beyond. Fishermen still work the coast, and the village has a small fort, a handful of cafes and a lively fish restaurant tradition. The name comes from an early settler family, the Monizes, who received land here in the fifteenth century.

  5. 5. São Vicente

    São Vicente lies on Madeira's north coast, in a narrow green valley where the mountains drop almost straight into the sea, and it is the sort of place where a road has to be persuaded into existence. The old road along the north coast, the ER101, runs between the sea cliffs and the steep slopes, in and out of tunnels carved through the rock, and over small bridges that cross waterfalls. The result is one of the most dramatic coastal roads in the Atlantic, wild, wet and green.
