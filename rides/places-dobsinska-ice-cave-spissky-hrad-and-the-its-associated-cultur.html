Why this ride

A 255 km round trip from Dobšiná - roughly 4 h 24 in the saddle. On the way it passes Levoča and the work of Master Paul in Spiš, Levoča, Kezmarok and Spišský Hrad and the its Associated Cultur. The highest point is about 850 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Dobšinská Ice Cave

    Step out of a warm summer day into the Dobšinská Ice Cave and the temperature drops to just below freezing within a few metres. The cave, on the northern edge of the Slovak Paradise in eastern Slovakia, holds a vast mass of ice, tens of thousands of cubic metres, with frozen floors, walls and columns that have built up over thousands of years. It was explored in 1870 by Eugen Ruffinyi, opened to visitors soon after, and became one of the first caves in Europe to be lit by electricity. It is part of the UNESCO-listed Caves of Aggtelek Karst and Slovak Karst, shared with Hungary.

  2. 2. Levoča and the work of Master Paul in Spiš

    Master Paul of Levoča carved the tallest Gothic wooden altar in the world, and you can stand in front of it in the church of St James in the centre of this walled town in eastern Slovakia. Completed in the early sixteenth century, the altar rises almost nineteen metres, with life-size figures of the Virgin and saints in limewood, painted and gilded, under a forest of pinnacles. It is the masterpiece of a sculptor about whom little is known beyond his work, and it is part of the UNESCO listing of Levoča, Spiš Castle and the associated cultural monuments.

  3. 3. Levoča

    Spread across a ridge in eastern Slovakia, with the jagged peaks of the High Tatras rising on the northern horizon, the Spiš region holds one of Europe's great clusters of medieval monuments. The town of Levoča, the old regional capital, was a wealthy royal free town, and its walls, burgher houses and Renaissance town hall still surround a long central square. Inside the church of St James stands Master Paul of Levoča's carved Gothic altarpiece, finished in the early sixteenth century and the tallest of its kind in the world, an astonishing tower of limewood saints and filigree.

  4. 4. Kezmarok

    Kežmarok sits at the foot of the High Tatras in northern Slovakia, and on a clear day the jagged peaks of the smallest high mountain range in Europe rise above the town like a wall. It is an old town with a mixed history: founded by German settlers in the Middle Ages, it was a free royal town and a centre of trade and crafts, and its wealthy citizens built a castle, churches and fine houses around the square.

  5. 5. Spišský Hrad and the its Associated Cultur

    Spiš Castle is huge, and from a distance it looks like a long white crown laid on top of a hill. It stands above the small town of Spišské Podhradie in eastern Slovakia, on a travertine outcrop about two hundred metres above the plain, and it is one of the largest castle complexes in Central Europe, with the ruined walls and towers covering some four hectares. It was first recorded in the early thirteenth century, was rebuilt and extended by Hungarian kings and noble families such as the Zápolya and the Thurzo, and fell into ruin after a fire in 1780. UNESCO lists the castle together with the nearby church, town and village of Žehra.
