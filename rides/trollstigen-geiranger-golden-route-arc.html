Why this ride

263 km one way from Åndalsnes - plan on about 5 h 21 of riding. The climb of the day is Trollstigen. It tops out at 858 m. Trollstigen is subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. The passes are the reason to go.

Scenic road chapters

Trollstigen

The ground tilts up and stays tilted for 81 km. 848 m of it, taken steadily rather than in one wall — a steady rhythm suits this better than bursts. The height arrives whether you hurry or not.

Rider note

Check the pass is open before you commit to it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Åndalsnes

    Åndalsnes is a small town at the head of the Romsdalsfjord, hemmed in by some of the most vertical mountains in Norway. The Romsdalshorn, a sharp pyramid of rock, rises above the valley to the south, and the Trollveggen, Europe's tallest vertical rock face at around a thousand metres, stands a short way up the valley. The town itself is modest, with a few thousand people, a railway terminus, a harbour and a handful of cafes, and its main business is welcoming people who come for the mountains.

  2. 2. Trollstigen viewpoint

    The Trollstigen Plateau is a bare, glacier-scoured saddle at 858 metres, ringed by peaks with names like Bispen, the Bishop, and Kongen, the King, and the viewing platform here, a sculptural structure of steel and concrete that hangs over the drop, is the best place to understand what the road does. Below, the Trollstigen, the Troll's Ladder, zigzags down the side of the valley in eleven hairpins, with the long white thread of the Stigfossen waterfall falling beside it, and beyond lies the broad green Isterdalen and the far peaks of Romsdalen.

    Read the complete Trollstigen viewpoint write-up.

    Planning note

    You top out at 858 m here. The road climbs 848 m on the way up to it, steadily. Reckon on about 6 °C colder than the valley, and more in the wind. Nothing on this route is higher. Next is Valldal, mostly downhill.

  3. 3. Valldal

    Valldal is a small town at the head of the Norddalsfjord, in the green and orderly farmland that lies at the foot of the great Sunnmøre mountains, and it is the first proper settlement you reach after the long descent from the Trollstigen. Valldal is famous in Norway for its strawberries, which ripen in July in the long summer light and are sold in roadside stands, and the local people take a great deal of pride in them.
