Why this ride

227 km round trip from Susan-ri Tomb, about 4 h 52 of riding. It runs out through Tokhung-ri Tomb, Tokhwa-ri Tombs No. 1, Homam-ri Sasin and Tomb of King Tongmyong and Jinpha-ri group and comes back by another road.

The stops

  1. 1. Susan-ri Tomb

    Start here.

    Susan-ri is a burial mound near the west coast of North Korea, a few kilometres from the Taedong River estuary and the barrage that closes it off. Under the earth lies a stone chamber painted with murals from the Goguryeo kingdom, which ruled the northern Korean peninsula and much of Manchuria from the first centuries BC until 668 AD. The tomb belongs to the Complex of Koguryo Tombs, which UNESCO listed in 2004. The wall paintings show figures, animals and the four guardian creatures of the cardinal directions, and they are among the oldest and most vivid pictures in the region.

    Read the complete Susan-ri Tomb write-up.

  2. 2. Tokhung-ri Tomb

    18 km in.

    Beneath a low grassy mound in the countryside south-west of Pyongyang lies one of the finest painted tombs of the ancient kingdom of Koguryo. Tokhung-ri was built in 408 for a high-ranking official, and its walls are covered in murals showing his life: processions, hunting scenes, officials paying respects, mythical creatures and a starry sky with the sun and moon. Unusually, it carries a long ink inscription giving the man's titles and the date, which makes it an important anchor for the history of the whole kingdom. It is part of the Complex of Koguryo Tombs inscribed by UNESCO in 2004.

    Read the complete Tokhung-ri Tomb write-up.

  3. 3. Tokhwa-ri Tombs No. 1

    57 km in.

    This is a place almost no one reading these words will be able to reach on two wheels, and it is better to say so plainly. Tokhwa-ri Tomb No. 1 lies in the countryside north of Pyongyang, in the Democratic People's Republic of Korea, among a group of burial mounds from the kingdom of Goguryeo. Independent travel in North Korea is not possible. Foreign visitors, when the country admits them at all, travel only on organised tours with official guides, follow fixed itineraries and are not allowed to drive or ride. Citizens of some countries, including the United States, are barred from travelling there by their own governments, and many others are strongly advised against it.

    Read the complete Tokhwa-ri Tombs No. 1 write-up.

  4. 4. Homam-ri Sasin

    104 km in.

    The Four Guardians, the dragon of the east, the tiger of the west, the red bird of the south and the black tortoise entwined with a snake in the north, rule the walls of the Homam-ri tomb. Painted more than fifteen hundred years ago in the Koguryo kingdom, which dominated Korea and parts of Manchuria in those centuries, they are drawn in sweeping lines and bright mineral colours, and they twist across the stone as if in motion. The tomb is in the area around Pyongyang, in North Korea, and the whole group of Koguryo tombs was listed by UNESCO in 2004.

    Read the complete Homam-ri Sasin write-up.

  5. 5. Tomb of King Tongmyong and Jinpha-ri group

    153 km in.

    This is one of the few places in a ride collection that you almost certainly cannot ride to on your own, and it is worth saying so plainly. The Tomb of King Tongmyong lies in North Korea, southeast of Pyongyang, and the country admits foreign visitors only on guided tours, with fixed itineraries, official guides and no independent driving, let alone motorcycling. Still, it is a remarkable place, and knowing about it adds a layer to understanding the Korean peninsula.
