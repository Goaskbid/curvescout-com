  1. 1. Cochrane Chile

    Cochrane is the last proper town on the southern Carretera Austral before the road runs out into the far south, and it feels like it. About three thousand people live here, in low houses around a spacious plaza, with a river valley and glacial lakes nearby and the high Patagonian steppe to the east. It was founded in the 1950s and named after Thomas Cochrane, the Scottish admiral who commanded the Chilean navy in the independence war against Spain, so there is an odd flavour of Scottish seafaring in the middle of the mountains.

  2. 2. Caleta Tortel

    Caleta Tortel has no streets. The village hangs off the rocky shore of a fjord in southern Chilean Patagonia, and everything, from the church to the post office to the family houses, is joined by boardwalks and stairways of cypress timber that rise and fall with the slope. Wheels are left at the top, and so are motorbikes, because the whole place is a footpath. The wood is the local ciprés de las Guaitecas, which resists rot in the constant rain, and the walkways give off a damp, resinous smell that you notice as soon as you step off the road.
