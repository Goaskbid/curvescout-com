Why this ride

152 km round trip from Watchtowers WP 9/33 to 9/35, about 3 h 05 of riding. It runs out through Watchtowers WP 9/70 to 9/99a including the, Welzheim-Ost civil settlement northern par and Watchtowers WP 12/39 and comes back by another road.

The stops

  1. 1. Watchtowers WP 9/33 to 9/35

    Start here.

    Öhringen had not one but two Roman forts, known today as the Bürgkastell and the Rendelkastell, both on the Upper Germanic Limes, which ran past the town on its way south. The watchtowers numbered here were part of that frontier, and the town grew partly on the remains of the Roman settlement that clustered around the forts. Little is visible above ground, but finds are displayed in the local museum, and the line of the Limes can be followed on foot or by bike through the surrounding countryside.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 9/33 to 9/35 write-up.

  2. 2. Watchtowers WP 9/70 to 9/99a including the

    25 km in.

    Mainhardt sits on a ridge in the Swabian-Franconian Forest, and the Roman frontier ran right through it, on its way south in one of the longest straight lines ever drawn across European landscape. Here, the Upper Germanic Limes runs for some eighty kilometres from Walldürn to near Welzheim almost perfectly straight, ignoring hills and valleys, as if surveyors had laid a ruler on the map. The watchtowers numbered in this section stood along that line in the forest, and Mainhardt had a fort, whose site is marked in the village, with a small museum.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 9/70 to 9/99a including the write-up.

  3. 3. Welzheim-Ost civil settlement northern par

    54 km in.

    In the Swabian Forest east of Stuttgart, the Roman frontier ran as straight as a ruler for some eighty kilometres, ignoring hills and valleys in a way that still puzzles historians. Welzheim lies on this straight stretch of the Upper German-Raetian Limes, and the town had two Roman forts, the west and east forts, with a civil settlement around them. The northern part of the settlement near the east fort is one of the components of the Limes World Heritage Site, and the Ostkastell has been turned into an archaeological park with a reconstructed gate.

    Read the complete Welzheim-Ost civil settlement northern par write-up.

  4. 4. Watchtowers WP 12/39

    84 km in.

    Iggingen is a small village on the plateau above the Rems valley, a few kilometres east of Schwäbisch Gmünd, and like many villages in this part of Württemberg it has a Roman frontier running through its fields. The site labelled Watchtower WP 12/39 sits on the Raetian Limes, the stone wall that marked the edge of Roman Raetia, and from here the line runs eastwards towards Aalen, the largest Roman cavalry fort north of the Alps, now home to an excellent Limes museum.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 12/39 write-up.
