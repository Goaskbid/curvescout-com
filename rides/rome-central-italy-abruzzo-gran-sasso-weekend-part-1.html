  2. 2. Near Ronciglione, on the way back

    Lago di Vico fills the crater of an old volcano in the Cimini hills, north of Rome, and it is one of the best-preserved volcanic lakes in Italy. The slopes around it are covered with beech and chestnut forest, and the lake is a nature reserve, with herons and grebes in the reeds. It sits a short hop from the lake, between Caprarola, Canepina and Carbognano, in a landscape of hazelnut orchards and woods.

  3. 3. Marina di Cerveteri

    The Etruscans buried their dead in cities of their own, and at Cerveteri, a few kilometres inland from this stop, the necropolis of the Banditaccia is one of the most remarkable archaeological sites in the Mediterranean. Hundreds of tombs, some shaped like round mounds, others like houses with streets between them, stretch across a plateau among cypress and grass. Inside, the tombs are carved to imitate rooms, with beds, chairs and household objects in stone.
