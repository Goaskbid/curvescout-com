Why this ride

A 325 km round trip from Velez Rubio - roughly 5 h 36 in the saddle. The stops in between: Letreros de los mártires, Zona 1, Las Covachicas and Canalejas de Abajo. The highest point is about 1,400 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Inferior Letreros

    The Cueva de los Letreros is what most people mean when they say Vélez-Blanco, and the rock shelter at Inferior Letreros is part of the same story. Famous images in the main cave include a little figure with raised arms and a bow-like shape that locals have adopted as the Indalo, now the good-luck symbol of the whole province of Almería, painted on walls and sold on keyrings from the beaches to the mountains. The paintings belong to the Levantine tradition that UNESCO lists as the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula.

  2. 2. Letreros de los mártires

    Out in the high north-east of Granada province, Huéscar feels a long way from the Alhambra and the beaches of the Costa Tropical. This is the altiplano, a broad upland of wheat fields, almond trees and pale badlands at around a thousand metres, ringed by mountains, with the great limestone hump of La Sagra rising to the north. The rock shelter called Letreros de los Mártires lies in this country, a dozen or so kilometres from the town.

  3. 3. Zona 1

    Nerpio is about as far from anywhere as you can get in mainland Spain. The village lies in the southwestern corner of Albacete province, close to the borders of Murcia, Granada and Jaén, in a high, wild landscape of limestone ridges, pine forest and deep river valleys. The area is known for two things: walnuts, which grow in huge old trees along the Taibilla river and are celebrated in local cooking, and one of the densest concentrations of prehistoric rock art in the Iberian Peninsula, part of the UNESCO-listed rock art of the Mediterranean Basin. The generic label Zona 1 points at the hills near the village, where rock shelters with painted figures are scattered across the cliffs.

  4. 4. Las Covachicas

    The Sierra del Segura, in the far south of Albacete province, is a world of its own: deep valleys, rivers lined with poplars, terraces of olive and almond trees, and white villages that look more Andalusian than Castilian. Letur is one of the most beautiful, a village of Moorish origin built on a rock above a stream, with narrow alleys, arches and gardens watered by old irrigation channels. Las Covachicas, a group of shelters, lies in the hills nearby, between Letur, Socovos and Férez.

  5. 5. Canalejas de Abajo

    Canalejas de Abajo is a name on the map rather than a place with a church and a bar: a scattering of farms and old cortijos in the upper valley of the Taibilla river, south of Nerpio, in the mountains where Albacete, Jaén and Granada meet. The Sierra de Segura and the Sierra de las Cabras rise all around, high limestone country cut by deep valleys, and the region is known among archaeologists for its painted rock shelters. The Abrigo del Sabinar and others along the same valley hold some of the most important prehistoric paintings of the Spanish south east, drawn on the soft overhangs that the river and the wind have shaped.
