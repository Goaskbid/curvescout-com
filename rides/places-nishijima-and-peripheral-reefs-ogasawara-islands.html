Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Nishijima and peripheral reefs

    The Ogasawara islands name each other like a family, and Nishijima is among the younger members of the group, a modest uninhabited island west of Chichijima, the father island. Chichijima, Anijima and Ototojima, the elder and younger brothers, and Hahajima, the mother island, make up the main cast. The names are Japanese, and yet the main harbour town on Chichijima is called Ōmura, and the bay on its northern side Futami, and the older Western names like Peel Island and Port Lloyd still turn up on charts. That patchwork tells you how the island chain was settled.

  2. 2. Ogasawara Islands

    About a thousand kilometres south of Tokyo, in the Pacific, a scatter of small volcanic islands sits on the road to nowhere. These are the Ogasawara Islands, also called the Bonin Islands, and they have never been joined to any continent. That isolation is the whole point. Snails, plants and insects have evolved here in ways found nowhere else, which is why UNESCO listed the islands in 2011 and why some people call them the Galapagos of the East.
