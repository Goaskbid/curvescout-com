Why this ride

A big-mile bridge to the real theatre. 533 km round from Bergamo - plan on about 9 h 11 of riding. The stops in between: Lake Iseo, Trento and Canazei. The highest point is about 1,900 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bergamo old town

    Bergamo is two cities in one: a modern lower town of boulevards, offices and shopping streets spread across the Lombard plain, and above it, on a steep spur of the foothills, the Citta Alta, a walled medieval city of cobbled lanes, towers and piazzas. The upper city is the point of this stop, a place to finish a long day and walk around while the stone cools. The city as a whole has about 114,000 people, and Orio al Serio, home of Milan's third airport, is just to the south.

  2. 2. Lake Iseo pause

    Lake Iseo is the quiet one among the big Lombard lakes, and the western shore, where this pause sits, shows why. The road runs close to the water between cliffs and small villages, with Monte Isola, Europe's largest lake island, rising green in the middle of the lake. Fishing boats and ferries cross to its villages, and in summer the sound of engines drifts over the water. The surroundings are less polished than Lake Como, with working harbours and unfussy cafés, and many riders find that a relief.

  3. 3. Trento food gate

    Trento is the real beginning of the Dolomites, even though the famous peaks are still a good way north. The city lies in the Adige valley, ringed by steep mountains that rise abruptly from the valley floor, and the light that falls on its walls is distinctly alpine, while the cooking and the architecture have a southern ease. It is a university city with about eighty thousand people, and the cafés on its piazzas are full of students and cyclists.

This Lake Iseo pause → Trento food gate stretch is for rhythm and scenery. Keep your head up, let the view breathe, and resist turning every open bend into a target. Stop once properly rather than half-stopping everywhere.
