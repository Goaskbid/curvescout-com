Why this ride

It goes by way of Segment Río de La Sal - Pampa del Inca, Segment Portal del Inca - Río de La Sal and back through Segment Río de La Sal - Pampa del Inca. The highest point is about 3,200 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Segment Río de La Sal - Pampa del Inca

    Here the Atacama is not a single desert but a collection of them, and the stretch around Río de la Sal sits in its high, empty heart. The land is a pale expanse of volcanic rock and sand, with salt-crusted basins, dry riverbeds and enormous distances. Nothing grows here without help. The sky is absurdly blue, the air is thin, and at night the stars come out in a density most people have never seen.

  2. 2. Segment Río de La Sal - Pampa del Inca

    Out here the Atacama stops being a postcard and becomes a working landscape: pale gravel plains, dry washes, and a horizon that stays the same colour for an hour at a time. The Río de la Sal gets its name honestly. Whatever water the Andes send down this way picks up minerals on the trip, and what survives to the surface leaves white crusts along the banks. Pampa del Inca, the broad flat beside it, is the kind of name you only get where travellers have been crossing the same ground for centuries.

  3. 3. Segment Portal del Inca - Río de La Sal

    A long way up in the Atacama, where the desert climbs into the high Andes of northern Chile, a worn path crosses the landscape between a place called the Portal del Inca and the Río de la Sal, the salt river. It is a segment of the Qhapaq Ñan, the great network of Inca roads, which UNESCO listed in 2014 across six countries. In Chile the road went south from the Inca heartland, crossing passes of over four thousand metres to reach the valleys of Copiapó and beyond, and traces of it survive in the dry air with striking clarity: stone markers, small shelters and the line of the trail itself.
