  1. 1. Petropolis

    For decades, the emperors of Brazil escaped the Rio summer by climbing into these hills, and Petrópolis still feels like an imperial retreat. The city lies in the Serra dos Órgãos at around 800 metres, cooler and fresher than the coast, and it was built in the mid-nineteenth century as a summer residence for Dom Pedro II, the last emperor. His former palace is now the Imperial Museum, where visitors shuffle across the polished floors in felt slippers to protect them, and it holds the crown jewels.

  2. 2. Teresopolis

    Look up from the streets of Teresópolis and you will see the Dedo de Deus, the Finger of God, a granite spire that points at the sky from the ridge above town. It is the emblem of the Serra dos Órgãos, a range whose jagged peaks reminded the Portuguese of organ pipes, and the city sits right beside the national park that protects it. Altitude is around 900 metres, so evenings are cool, and in winter you may need a jacket.
