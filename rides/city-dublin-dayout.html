From Dublin it runs 116 km to Dublin; allow about 1 h 40 of riding. The stops in between: Brú na Bóinne - Archaeological Ensemble of and back through Brú na Bóinne - Archaeological Ensemble of.

The stops

  1. 1. Dublin

    Start here.

    Dublin is a city built on a river and on conversation. The Liffey divides it, crossed by a string of bridges including the pretty Ha'penny Bridge, and the centre is a mix of Georgian squares, with their coloured doors, and busy modern streets. Trinity College, founded in 1592, holds the Book of Kells, a ninth-century illuminated manuscript of extraordinary delicacy, and the nearby streets are full of pubs, bookshops and music. The Guinness Storehouse, at St James's Gate, draws visitors from around the world.

    Read the complete Dublin write-up.

  2. 2. Brú na Bóinne - Archaeological Ensemble of

    58 km in.

    Brú na Bóinne means the bend of the Boyne, and the loop of the river that gives it its name holds one of the most important prehistoric landscapes in Europe. Three great passage tombs, Newgrange, Knowth and Dowth, stand on a low ridge above the water, built by farmers about five thousand years ago. That makes them older than Stonehenge and older than the pyramids of Giza, a thought that tends to silence even the chattiest visitor.
