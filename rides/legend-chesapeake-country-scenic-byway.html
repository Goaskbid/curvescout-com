Why this ride

National Scenic Byway. Chesapeake Bay, farmland, historic ports. A one-way ride of 101 km, Elkton to Easton - roughly 1 h 50 in the saddle. The stops in between: Chestertown and Rock Hall. The line favours views over speed.

  2. 2. Chestertown

    Chestertown looks like a town that decided long ago what it wanted to be and then very sensibly never changed its mind. It sits on the Chester River on Maryland's Eastern Shore, at the head of navigation, and its old brick houses, many from the eighteenth century, still line the streets leading up from the waterfront. The place was a colonial port, shipping tobacco and grain to Europe, and the wealth that built those handsome Georgian merchants' homes is still visible in the cornices and doorways.

  3. 3. Rock Hall

    Rock Hall is a working harbour town first and a tourist stop a distant second, and it likes things that way. It sits on the Chesapeake Bay side of Maryland's Kent County, a short, low-lying run west from Chestertown, and the waterfront is where the town keeps its heart: crab boats, oyster boats, marina slips, and the weathered sheds that go with them. The local people are watermen, and the town has long called itself a place for fishing and boats. Pearl of the Chesapeake is the line you will see on the signs.
