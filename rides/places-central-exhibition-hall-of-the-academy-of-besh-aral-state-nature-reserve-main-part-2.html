Why this ride

From Tashkent it runs 318 km to Tashkent; allow about 5 h 29 of riding. It goes by way of The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve, Besh-Aral State Nature Reserve - main part and back through The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve. The highest point is about 2,700 m. It crosses from Uzbekistan into Kyrgyzstan - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Central Exhibition Hall of the Academy of

    On April 26, 1966, an earthquake flattened much of old Tashkent, leaving tens of thousands homeless, and the Soviet Union responded by turning the city into a showcase. Architects and builders came from across the republics to rebuild, and the result was a capital of wide avenues, big squares and concrete modernism, with buildings that mixed Soviet geometry with Central Asian patterns, blue tiles, carved screens and domes. The Central Exhibition Hall of the Academy of Arts belongs to that world, a hall for displays of painting and sculpture, standing in a city that has since added glass towers and new mosques but keeps these older shapes as part of its character.

  2. 2. The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve

    East of Tashkent, the land rises steeply into the western ranges of the Tian Shan, and the Chatkal range forms the border with Kyrgyzstan. The Chatkal State Biosphere Reserve, established in the Soviet era, protects a stretch of these mountains, with juniper forests, walnut and wild fruit trees, alpine meadows and rocky peaks.

  3. 3. Besh-Aral State Nature Reserve - main part

    Far up the Chatkal valley in western Kyrgyzstan, where the river runs between the Chatkal and Sandalash ranges of the Western Tien Shan, lies the Besh-Aral State Nature Reserve. It protects mountain steppe, juniper woodland, walnut and apple groves and rocky high country, and it is part of the transnational World Heritage Site of the Western Tien Shan, inscribed in 2016 and shared with Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan. The reserve was created in particular to protect Menzbier's marmot, a small marmot found only in this part of Central Asia.
