Why this ride

A 92 km round trip from Ulldecona - roughly 1 h 35 in the saddle. The stops in between: Cova dels Rossegadors o del Polvorin, La fenellosa and back through Cova dels Rossegadors o del Polvorin.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abric d'Ermites V exterior

    The Montsià is a small, rocky massif that rises abruptly above the Ebro delta, the last real mountain before the river dissolves into rice paddies and lagoons, and the hills around Ulldecona are its northern neighbours. From their ridges you can look across the flat delta to the sea, a view that changes from silver in the morning to gold in the evening. Abric d'Ermites V exterior lies about four and a half kilometres from Ulldecona, on the slopes of the Serra de Godall, and carries painting in the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain. The ride links it with Valcomuna, a site name that sounds like a village meeting place.

  2. 2. Cova dels Rossegadors o del Polvorin

    The Tinença de Benifassà is one of the emptiest corners of the Valencian Community, a group of tiny stone villages scattered across rugged limestone mountains in the far north of Castellón, right where Valencia, Catalonia and Aragón meet. The Cova dels Rossegadors, also called the Cova del Polvorí, lies on the edge of this country near Rossell, with Mas de Barberans and Canet lo Roig the other nearby villages.

  3. 3. La fenellosa

    People in Aragón like to call the Matarraña their Tuscany, and for once the comparison is not entirely marketing. It is a comarca of honey-coloured stone villages on hilltops, olive groves on terraces, almond trees and vineyards, with the limestone wall of the Ports de Beseit rising behind. La Fenellosa lies in this country, between Valderrobres, Fuentespalda and Mas de Barberans across the Catalan line, in the rough hills where the farmland gives way to the mountains.
