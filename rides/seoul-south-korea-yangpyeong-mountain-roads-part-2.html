Why this ride

Out of Hwaseong-si, one way: 234 km, roughly 4 h 48 in the saddle. It goes by way of near Seonwon and Anyang-si. Much of it runs through woodland.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Photo stop in Hwaseong-si

    Hwaseong-si stretches across the western edge of Gyeonggi province, from the suburbs of Suwon to the mudflats and islands of the Yellow Sea. Its name echoes the famous Hwaseong Fortress in Suwon, but the city itself has its own treasures, both royal and coastal.

  3. 3. Near Anyang-si

    Gwanaksan rises over the southern edge of Seoul, a ridge of bare granite with a small hermitage perched on a cliff near the summit, and its rocky profile is one of the landmarks of the city. Seoul National University, the country's top university, sits at its northern foot, and the trails up the mountain are crowded with hikers in bright outdoor gear on weekends.

  4. 4. Back to Yangpyeong

    Gwangneung is the forest of kings. The royal tomb of King Sejo, the seventh king of Joseon, and his queen lies on a wooded hillside in the northern part of Namyangju, and the surrounding forest, protected for more than five hundred years, is one of the best-preserved old woods in Korea.
