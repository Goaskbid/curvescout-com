  1. 1. Quito

    Quito sits almost on the equator and almost two miles up, and the two facts together explain a lot. At about 2,850 metres the air is thin, the sun is fierce, and the days are mild whatever the calendar says, so the city lives in a permanent early spring. It stretches for kilometres along a narrow Andean valley, with Pichincha volcano rising on the western side and, on clear mornings, the snow cone of Cotopaxi and other peaks visible to the south and east.

  2. 2. Mindo

    Down the western slopes of the Andes, where cold Quito air turns into warm, wet forest, lies Mindo. It is a small village at roughly 1,200 metres on the edge of the cloud forest of the Pichincha province, and it has built a reputation as one of the best birdwatching places in Ecuador. Hundreds of species live in the surrounding reserves, from toucans and tanagers to hummingbirds that whirr at feeders, and the famous Andean cock-of-the-rock gathers here to display at dawn.
