Why this ride

A 280 km round trip from Nerpio - roughly 4 h 40 in the saddle. On the way it passes Cueva de las Palamas, Cueva del Tio Labrador and back through Cueva de las Palamas. It tops out around 1,350 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Abrigo de la Rambla de Pedro Izquierdo

    Start here.

    A rambla is a dry riverbed that only runs after storms, and the Rambla de Pedro Izquierdo is one of many that cut into the limestone hills around Nerpio, in the remote south-western corner of Albacete. In a rock shelter above it, prehistoric people left paintings in the Levantine style, small, lively figures of people and animals, and the site is one of the many shelters around Nerpio that belong to the UNESCO-listed Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula. Visits are normally arranged through the village, and a guide will know which shelters are accessible and when the light is right for seeing the faded pigment.

    Read the complete Abrigo de la Rambla de Pedro Izquierdo write-up.

  2. 2. Cueva de las Palamas

    74 km in.

    Cehegín, in the north-west of the Murcia region, is a town of two faces: a modern lower town, and an old town on a hill, with steep streets, noble houses and churches, declared a historic site. The Cueva de las Palomas, the cave of the doves, lies on the edge of the town, less than a kilometre from the centre, with the larger town of Caravaca de la Cruz about five kilometres away.

    Read the complete Cueva de las Palamas write-up.

  3. 3. Cueva del Tío Labrador

    141 km in.

    Vélez-Rubio is a larger and busier place than its neighbour Vélez-Blanco, with seven thousand people, a lively main street, and a church that is a surprise in a town this size. The Church of the Encarnación, begun in the eighteenth century, has twin towers and a massive Baroque façade, usually described as the largest Baroque church in Almería, and its bulk dominates the roofs. It is a good place to start before riding up toward the mountains, because the fuel stations here are the nearest reliable ones for a long way.
