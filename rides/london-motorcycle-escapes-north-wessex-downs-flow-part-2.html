Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. London motorcycle escapes

    Cranbrook stands in the High Weald of Kent, a hilly, wooded country of sandstone ridges and winding lanes, and in the Middle Ages it was one of the richest towns in the county, thanks to the cloth trade. Edward III encouraged Flemish weavers to settle here in the fourteenth century, and the broadcloth they made brought wealth to the town, visible today in the big timber-framed houses and the huge church known as the Cathedral of the Weald. On the edge of the town stands Union Mill, an eighteenth-century smock mill that is the tallest working windmill of its kind in Britain.

  4. 4. Near Brent

    The Hanger Lane gyratory has a fearsome reputation among London drivers, and this finish lies right next to it, between Ealing, Alperton and Park Royal. It is a sprawling roundabout system where the A40 Western Avenue meets the North Circular, with traffic lights, lanes and junctions that confuse even regulars. Keep your wits about you and know your exit before you arrive.

    Runnymede is a few minutes away, and it carries more weight than any other meadow in England. King John sealed Magna Carta there in June 1215, and a memorial stands on the riverbank, with another to John F. Kennedy on a hillside above.
