Mascún II and Cueva de los moros.

Why this ride

A 354 km round trip from Bárcabo - roughly 6 h 06 in the saddle. On the way it passes Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña, Forau del Cocho, Abrigo de Los Gitanos and Cueva de los moros.. The highest point is about 1,400 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mascún II

    Boltaña is a small town of nine hundred people in the Sobrarbe, the valley country at the foot of the high Pyrenees, and from its edge the view north is dominated by the great limestone massifs of the Monte Perdido range, often white with snow into early summer. The shelter at Mascún II lies about twenty kilometres away, in the quieter, lower hills of the Sierra de Guara. It carries painting in the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain, and the ride links it with El Remosillo, another site in the canyons.

  2. 2. Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña

    Just north of Huesca, where the flat cereal land of the Hoya suddenly rears up into the first limestone walls of the Pre-Pyrenees, lies the little village of Nueno and, above it, the rock shelter named after Santa Eulalia de la Peña. This is the southern edge of the Sierra de Guara country, a landscape of gorges, conglomerate cliffs and scrub where griffon vultures ride the thermals and the light comes hard off pale stone.

  3. 3. Forau del Cocho

    South of Huesca the land opens out. The Pyrenees are behind you, a snowy line on a clear day, and ahead stretches the gently rolling cereal country that eventually turns into the dry steppes of Los Monegros. Forau del Cocho lies in this transitional landscape, between Torres de Barbués, Almuniente and the outskirts of Huesca itself, a place where the only relief is the occasional low ridge or gully cut into soft rock.

  4. 4. Abrigo de Los Gitanos

    The road north-west out of Huesca toward Pamplona runs along the foot of a dramatic wall of rock. To the north are the Pre-Pyrenean sierras, and studded along them are some of Aragón's most striking sights: the Castle of Loarre, a superbly preserved Romanesque fortress on a spur, and the Mallos de Riglos, huge red conglomerate towers rising straight out of the hillside. The rock shelter known as the Abrigo de los Gitanos lies in this country, between Ayerbe, Loarre and Luesia.

  5. 5. Cueva de los moros.

    There is a Cueva de los Moros in half the valleys of Spain. Every unexplained cave, ruin or rock shelter used to be credited to the Moors, and this one, in the hills of the western Aragonese Pyrenees between the valleys of Hecho and Ansó, is a rock shelter with ancient traces. You should approach it as an excuse to spend time in two of the most beautiful and least spoiled valleys of the Pyrenees, rather than as a monument.
