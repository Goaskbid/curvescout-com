Why this ride

A 134 km loop that starts and ends near Pereira, about 2 h 10 of riding. On the way it passes Filandia, Salento, Cocora Valley and Manizales. The highest point is about 3,550 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 18 km this is Colombia Coffee Triangle Loop, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Pereira

    Pereira sits high in the Andes of Colombia's Eje Cafetero, about 1,400 metres up, and it feels like a city that was built on the profits of one small red fruit. It is the capital of the department of Risaralda, a busy, well-fed town of some 440,000 people, hemmed in by green ridges that are planted with coffee almost to the skyline. It was founded in the middle of the nineteenth century, young by Colombian standards, and it has the confident, slightly untidy energy of a place that has always been about trade.

  2. 2. Filandia

    Filandia sits on a ridge in the hills of Quindío, in the heart of Colombia's coffee country, and the first thing that strikes you is the colour. Houses on the main streets are painted in bright blues, reds and yellows, with carved wooden balconies and doors, and on a sunny afternoon the whole village glows. It is a town that takes pride in how it looks, and it is easy to see why it has become one of the favourites of the region.

  3. 3. Salento

    Salento is the little town that brought tourists to the coffee region, and it has the looks to justify it. The main street, the Calle Real, runs uphill from the plaza in a row of brightly painted colonial houses with balconies and tiled roofs, lined with craft shops, cafes and restaurants, and ends at a long flight of steps up to a viewpoint over the valley. The town is small, only a few thousand people, and the atmosphere is relaxed.

  4. 4. Cocora Valley

    The wax palm of Quindío is the tallest palm tree in the world, and in the Cocora Valley it stands like a forest of green-topped poles, some of them sixty metres high, rising out of steep pasture in a thin mist. It is Colombia's national tree, and seeing hundreds of them on the slopes above a cloud forest is as strange as it sounds. Cattle graze below, small streams cut through the grass, and above the palms the dark forest climbs into the high country of Los Nevados National Park. The air is cool and damp, even when the lowlands are warm.

  5. 5. Manizales

    Manizales is a city built on a ridge, and it takes some getting used to. The streets climb and fall at gradients that make a bicycle useless and a motorcycle a sensible choice, and at about 2,150 metres the air is cool and often misty. The city is the capital of Caldas, a coffee department, and it was founded in the middle of the nineteenth century by settlers from Antioquia who cut their way into the forest to plant coffee.
