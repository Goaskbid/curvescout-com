Why this ride

Repeated coastal bends, headlands and frequent viewpoint stops. It also links naturally to Florida Keys. Highlight: a ferry crossing to Marathon, about 50 km of water - check the timetable and book ahead in summer.

Access context

Check seasonal gates, park access and state 511 notices before publication. Specific flag: hurricane; congestion.

The roads

Overseas Highway

Key Largo to Marathon: 81 km. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 0 and 10 m. Best in the early light, before the traffic.

The stops

  1. 1. Key Largo

    Start here.

    Key Largo is the first of the Florida Keys, the one you reach after the long straight run down from Florida City, and it is also the biggest, stretching some thirty miles along the line of the old coral reef. The Overseas Highway, US 1, runs through its entire length, lined with dive shops, tackle stores, seafood shacks and motels. The town itself has about ten thousand people, and fuel, groceries and food are easy to find all along the road, which is marked by mile markers counted from Key West.

    Read the complete Key Largo write-up.

  2. 2. Marathon

    81 km in.

    Look west from the end of Marathon and the road simply leaves land behind. The Seven Mile Bridge starts here, carrying US 1 across open water toward the Lower Keys, and beside it runs the old bridge, built in the early twentieth century for Henry Flagler's Overseas Railroad, which reached Key West in 1912. A hurricane on Labor Day 1935 wrecked the railway, and the line was converted into a highway. Part of the old bridge is now a path for walkers and cyclists, leading out to Pigeon Key, where the workers who built the bridge once lived.

    Read the complete Marathon write-up.

  3. 3. Key West

    The end: 158 km.

    Mile Marker 0 is a small thing to stand beside, a green sign at the corner of Whitehead and Fleming streets, but after about a hundred and thirty miles of bridges and causeways over water the colour of a swimming pool, it feels like a finish line in the most literal sense. Key West is the end of US 1, and the end of the road in the continental United States, closer to Havana than to Miami's downtown skyscrapers by a surprisingly small margin.

    Read the complete Key West write-up.
