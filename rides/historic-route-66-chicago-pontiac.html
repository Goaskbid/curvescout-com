Why this ride

A one-way ride of 354 km, Chicago Loop to Pontiac - roughly 4 h 51 in the saddle. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Chicago

    Route 66 begins in the middle of the Loop, a few steps from Lake Michigan, on a downtown street where a small brown sign marks the start of the road that once ran to Los Angeles. Chicago was the logical beginning. By 1926, when the highway was commissioned, the city was already the rail and trade hub of the Midwest, and farm families, migrants and travellers poured out of here along the road westward, the one that John Steinbeck later called the Mother Road.

  2. 2. Joliet

    Joliet is not pretty and does not pretend to be, which is exactly why it makes a good Route 66 stop. It is an industrial city on the Des Plaines River, southwest of Chicago, with limestone quarries, steel mills, railway yards and a long history of making things and moving them. The old highway came through here on its way out of the metropolitan area, and the town still wears the road lightly, with a few vintage signs and diners and a welcome centre that tells you where to look.

  3. 3. Pontiac

    Pontiac, Illinois, is a courthouse town of around twelve thousand people on the Vermilion River, and for anyone with a soft spot for Route 66 it is something close to a pilgrimage. The old highway slips through the middle of town on Old Route 66, and the people here have made a point of keeping its memory alive, with museums, murals and tidy brick storefronts that make a pleasant stop in the middle of the flat Illinois farmland.
