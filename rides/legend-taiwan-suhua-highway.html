Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Taiwan Suhua Highway point 1

    Su'ao is the end of the flat land. North of the town the Lanyang plain of Yilan spreads out in rice fields and fish ponds; south of it the Central Mountain Range plunges straight into the Pacific, and the road to Hualien has to cling to the cliffs. Su'ao itself is a port town with a deep natural harbour, a naval base, a cement works and a famous cold spring, and its fishing district of Nanfang'ao, just over the hill, is one of the busiest fishing harbours on Taiwan's east coast.

  2. 2. Taiwan Suhua Highway point 2

    Just south of Su'ao, the first serious climb of the Suhua Highway lifts you above the sea, and suddenly the scale of the coast becomes clear. Below the road, the cliffs drop straight into deep blue water; ahead, ridge after ridge of the Central Mountain Range falls into the ocean, each one cut by a gorge, each one breaking the coastline into another bay. On clear days you can see far down the coast, and on many days you see only cloud and rain.

  3. 3. Taiwan Suhua Highway point 3

    The Qingshui Cliffs are the climax of the Suhua coast. For several kilometres the mountains fall almost vertically into the Pacific, rising roughly a thousand metres from the waterline, their faces made of the same pale marble and gneiss that form Taroko Gorge just inland. The sea at their base turns from turquoise to deep blue within a few metres, and on a sunny day the colours look almost unreal. The road clings to the cliff high above the water, passing through short tunnels and galleries.

  4. 4. Hualien City

    Mountains and sea crowd against each other at Hualien. The city sits on a narrow coastal plain on Taiwan's east coast, with the Pacific Ocean on one side and the Central Mountain Range rising steeply to more than three thousand metres on the other, and the view from the waterfront, with the peaks looming over the town, is one of the most striking in Taiwan.
