Why this ride

A 218 km round trip from Seascale - roughly 3 h 46 in the saddle. It goes by way of The English Lake District, Burrow Walls Roman fort, Bank Mill tower 15a and back through Burrow Walls Roman fort. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ravenglass Roman fort

    Ravenglass is the only coastal village in the Lake District National Park, a small huddle of whitewashed houses along a single street facing a tidal estuary where three rivers, the Esk, the Irt and the Mite, meet the Irish Sea. At low tide the estuary becomes a vast expanse of mud, sand and channels, with boats lying aslant on the shingle, and at high tide the water laps almost at the cottages' doorsteps. The Romans saw the same harbour and built a fort here, Glannoventa, as a supply base on the Cumbrian coast, linked to the line of coastal forts and towers that extended from Hadrian's Wall down the coast.

  2. 2. The English Lake District

    Wordsworth wandered lonely as a cloud round here, and anybody who has ridden the Lake District on a wet bank holiday will understand the appeal of being lonely. The English Lake District, listed by UNESCO in 2017 as a cultural landscape, is a compact knot of mountains, lakes and valleys in Cumbria, shaped by glaciers and then by centuries of sheep farming, slate quarrying and, from the late eighteenth century, by poets, painters and tourists who came looking for the sublime.

  3. 3. Burrow Walls Roman fort

    On the northern edge of Workington, near the mouth of the river Derwent, a fragment of stone wall stands alone in a field, a jagged piece of masonry rising above the grass. It is part of Burrow Walls, a Roman fort that guarded the Cumbrian coast, one of the chain of defences that extended from the western end of Hadrian's Wall down the shore to watch for raiders crossing from Scotland and Ireland. Much of the fort's stone was reused in a medieval building on the site, and what survives today is modest, but it is part of the Hadrian's Wall World Heritage site and has a lonely charm, with views over the sea and the river.

  4. 4. Bank Mill tower 15a

    Hadrian's Wall did not stop at the sea. West of Bowness, the Romans continued their frontier along the Cumbrian coast with a chain of milefortlets and watchtowers, guarding against raiders who might sail across the Solway Firth. Bank Mill tower 15a is one of those coastal towers, set on the low ground near Silloth, and its remains are slight, though the logic is clear: from here, a sentry could see the whole estuary, and a signal could pass quickly along the line. The coast defences belong to the same UNESCO frontier listing as the wall itself.
