Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. London

    London is the one place on the map where the best advice is to leave the bike at the edge. The centre around Charing Cross and Trafalgar Square is a maze of one-way streets, bus lanes, taxis and tourists, and parking is limited and expensive. The city also has a congestion charge and a low-emission zone, whose rules for motorcycles have changed over the years, so check the current conditions for your own machine before you ride in. For a start and finish, a quiet side street or a secure garage on the outskirts is a better plan.

    Read the complete London write-up.

  2. 2. Newmarket

    Newmarket has been the headquarters of British horse racing since Charles II made it his sporting retreat in the seventeenth century, and the town still lives and breathes thoroughbreds. Around three thousand horses are trained here, and the gallops on the open heath around the town are where they work in the mornings. If you ride through early, you will almost certainly meet strings of racehorses crossing the road on special horse walks, with traffic lights and signs warning drivers to slow down. Give them all the room and quiet they need; a startled racehorse is a very expensive problem.

    Read the complete Newmarket write-up.

  3. 3. Holt

    Holt is a small Georgian market town a few miles inland from the north Norfolk coast. Most of it was rebuilt after a fire in 1708 destroyed much of the medieval town, which is why the high street is lined with handsome brick and flint houses of a fairly uniform age. It has an air of quiet prosperity, with independent shops, delis, bookshops and cafés, and it makes a very civilised lunch stop.
