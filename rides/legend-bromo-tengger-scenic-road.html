Why this ride

curated scenic road. volcanic caldera, mountains, sunrise views. 52 km from Malang to the finish, about 57 min of riding. On the way it passes Tumpang and Bromo Tengger. The highest point is about 2,400 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Malang

    Malang is a cool, hilly city in East Java, at about 450 metres, and the altitude is the thing everyone mentions. After the heat of Surabaya, a couple of hours to the north, the evening air is a relief, and for the Dutch it was a favourite hill station, laid out in the early twentieth century with broad avenues, big trees and neat colonial villas around the central square. Some of that has survived, and the old streets near the Tugu monument still have a faded charm, though the city is now large and busy, with a university population and a lot of motorbikes.

  2. 2. Tumpang

    Tumpang is a busy market town at the eastern edge of the Malang plain, in the heart of East Java, and for most visitors it is the place where the road to Mount Bromo begins. The town is where the highway from Malang squeezes through a bottleneck of shops, stalls and minibuses, and where the jeeps wait that take travellers up to the volcanoes. Behind it, the Tengger mountains rise in blue ridges, with the smoking cone of Semeru, the highest peak on Java, hovering above on a clear day.

  3. 3. Bromo Tengger Scenic Road point 3

    At around 2,400 metres, the road from Malang over the Tengger massif reaches the rim of one of the most extraordinary landscapes in Indonesia. From here, at the southern edge of the great caldera, you look down into a vast, flat plain of volcanic sand, the Sea of Sand, with the smoking cone of Bromo rising from it and, beyond, the perfect cone of Semeru, the highest mountain on Java at 3,676 metres, puffing out regular plumes of ash. It is a view that makes people stop talking.

  4. 4. Bromo Tengger Scenic Road point 4

    Down on the floor of the Tengger caldera, the Sea of Sand stretches away in every direction, a grey-black plain of fine volcanic ash surrounded by cliffs, with Bromo smoking at its centre. It is a strange, otherworldly place: silent except for the wind and the roar of jeep engines, with dust devils swirling across the plain and a Hindu temple, Pura Luhur Poten, standing alone at the foot of Bromo like something from a dream. In the early morning, mist fills the caldera, and the cones rise out of it like islands.

  5. 5. Bromo Tengger Scenic Road point 5

    Cemoro Lawang is the village perched on the north-eastern rim of the Tengger caldera, and for most visitors it is the gateway to Bromo. It is a small place, with a single main street lined with guesthouses, warungs and shops selling warm hats and gloves, and the houses cling to the edge of the cliff, looking down into the Sea of Sand. At dawn, the village comes alive with jeeps and motorbikes heading for the viewpoints, and by mid-morning it is quiet again, as visitors return to bed or move on.
