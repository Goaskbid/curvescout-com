Why this ride

A 359 km round trip from Pushkin - roughly 5 h 43 in the saddle. It goes by way of The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag, Southern battery No 2, The I.Repin Estate «The Penates» and Fort «Krasnaya Gorka». The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

From The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag to Southern battery No 2 is 36 km, mostly on КАД. An easy profile, never above 74 m. Best in the early light, before the traffic.

The stops

  1. 1. The Palace and Park Ensembles of the Town

    Start here.

    Between 1941 and 1944 Tsarskoye Selo lay behind German lines, and when the Red Army came back the Catherine Palace was a shell. The Amber Room's panels had vanished, the park trees had been cut for firewood, and most of the interiors were ash. The restoration that followed, and the new Amber Room that finally reopened in 2003, make this one of the most remarkable rebuilding jobs in Europe. The town, now called Pushkin after the poet who studied at its Lyceum, is part of the UNESCO listing for the historic centre of Saint Petersburg, and the ensemble of palaces, pavilions and parks around it is the reason to come.

    Read the complete The Palace and Park Ensembles of the Town write-up.

  2. 2. The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag

    30 km in.

    Ropsha is one of those places where the scenery is gentle and the history is anything but. The village sits on the low Izhora Upland southwest of St Petersburg, between Krasnoe Selo and the coast road at Lomonosov, and the palace that gives the estate its name looks down over a chain of spring-fed ponds and a slow, wooded park. It was here, in July 1762, that the freshly deposed Emperor Peter III was held after his wife Catherine seized the throne, and here that he died a few days later under circumstances nobody at court cared to explain. Catherine became "the Great"; Ropsha became a footnote with a chill on it.

    Read the complete The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag write-up.

  3. 3. Southern battery No 2

    66 km in.

    Kotlin Island sits in the middle of the Gulf of Finland like a cork in a bottle, and Peter the Great knew it. He seized this stretch of coast from Sweden in 1703 and almost at once began fortifying the island to guard the new capital, Saint Petersburg, which lay at the head of the gulf. Over the next two centuries a ring of forts and batteries grew up on islands, shoals and the shore, and Kronstadt became the home port of the Baltic Fleet. The batteries named in these ride titles, Southern and Northern, belong to that defensive system.

    Read the complete Southern battery No 2 write-up.

  4. 4. The I.Repin Estate «The Penates»

    108 km in.

    In 1903 the painter Ilya Repin, the most famous Russian artist of his day, moved to a wooden house in the village of Kuokkala on the Gulf of Finland, which he named the Penates, after the Roman household gods. He lived and worked there until his death in 1930, and after Finnish independence in 1917, he found himself living abroad, refusing invitations to return to the Soviet Union. The house, with its glass-roofed studio and eccentric architecture, was destroyed in the Second World War and rebuilt afterwards, and it is now a museum with many of his paintings and possessions.

    Read the complete The I.Repin Estate «The Penates» write-up.

  5. 5. Fort «Krasnaya Gorka»

    173 km in.

    On the southern shore of the Gulf of Finland, near the village of Lebyazhye, the remains of Fort Krasnaya Gorka sit among pine woods and sand dunes. The fort was built in the years before the First World War as part of a chain of coastal defences protecting the approaches to St Petersburg, with heavy guns facing the sea and their counterparts on the opposite shore at Ino, in what was then the Grand Duchy of Finland. Together, the forts were designed to close the gulf to an enemy fleet.

    Read the complete Fort «Krasnaya Gorka» write-up.
