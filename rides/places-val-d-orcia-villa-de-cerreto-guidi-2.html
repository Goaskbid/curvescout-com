Why this ride

A 261 km round trip from Torrenieri - roughly 4 h 30 in the saddle. On the way it passes Historic Centre of Siena, Historic Centre of San Gimignano, Villa de Cerreto Guidi and back through Historic Centre of San Gimignano. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Val d'Orcia

    Every photograph you have ever seen of Tuscany with a farmhouse on a bare hill, a winding line of cypresses and a soft haze over rolling fields was probably taken somewhere close to here. The Val d'Orcia is the valley of the river Orcia, south of Siena, a farmed landscape that Renaissance landowners deliberately shaped to look like the paintings of their time. UNESCO listed it in 2004 as a landscape designed with an idea in mind, and on a clear morning that idea is very easy to grasp.

  2. 2. Historic Centre of Siena

    Siena sits on three ridges in the heart of Tuscany, a red-brick medieval city that has hardly changed its shape in seven hundred years. At its centre is the Piazza del Campo, a sloping, shell-shaped square paved in brick and divided into nine sections, a reminder of the Council of Nine that governed the city in its golden age. The Palazzo Pubblico, with the slender Torre del Mangia, stands at the bottom of the square, and inside are frescoes by Ambrogio Lorenzetti showing the effects of good and bad government, painted in the fourteenth century.

  3. 3. Historic Centre of San Gimignano

    Seen from the surrounding countryside, San Gimignano looks like a medieval Manhattan: a cluster of stone towers rising from a hilltop above vineyards and olive groves in the Val d'Elsa. In its heyday, in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, the town had dozens of them, built by rival families as symbols of power and wealth. Fourteen survive, enough to make it one of the most recognisable towns in Italy and a UNESCO World Heritage site since 1990.

  4. 4. Villa de Cerreto Guidi

    Cosimo I de' Medici liked to hunt, and in the 1560s he had a villa built on a hilltop at Cerreto Guidi, on the edge of the marshes and woods west of Florence, where the game was plentiful. From the outside it is austere, a solid block of stone facing a square, but the approach is spectacular: a pair of great brick ramps, known as the Medici bridges, zigzag up to the entrance. The villa is part of the UNESCO listing of the Medici Villas and Gardens in Tuscany, and it holds a dark legend. Cosimo's daughter Isabella died here in 1576, and the story goes that she was murdered by her husband, Paolo Giordano Orsini, for her alleged infidelity. Whether it is true or not, it is told to every visitor.
