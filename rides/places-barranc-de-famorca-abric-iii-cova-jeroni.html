Why this ride

From Castell de Castells it runs 69 km to Castell de Castells; allow about 1 h 11 of riding. The stops in between: Barraac de la Palla, Cova Jeroni and back through Barraac de la Palla.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc de famorca- Abric III

    Famorca is one of the smallest municipalities in the province of Alicante, a few dozen houses clinging to a slope in the mountains of the Marina Alta, and the ravine named after it is a deep cut in the limestone that holds several rock shelters with painted art. Abric III is one of them, in the Levantine tradition, small red figures that UNESCO protects with other sites across the eastern side of the country as the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula. The nearest village, Castell de Castells, two kilometres away, has a famous neighbour: the Pla de Petracos, a major painted shelter with a visitor trail, which shows what the area offers at its best.

  2. 2. Barraac de la Palla

    Tormos and Sagra are tiny villages at the foot of the mountains in the north of Alicante province, at the point where the plain of the Marina Alta meets the hills. They are surrounded by orange groves and orchards, with the mountains of the Vall de Laguar and the Serra del Migdia rising steeply behind. The Barranc de la Palla, the ravine of the straw, cuts into the hills near Tormos, close also to Orba and the town of Pego.

  3. 3. Cova Jeroni

    Cova Jeroni belongs to that quiet tangle of valleys behind Pego and Dénia where the Marina Alta hides its oldest stories. The shelters in this part of Alicante, along the ravines of Famorca, Ebo and Gallinera, carry some of the finest prehistoric art in eastern Spain, and they are listed together with hundreds of others as the rock art of the Mediterranean Basin. Do not expect a visitor centre and a car park; expect a dirt track, a short walk, a rock overhang and a few ochre figures that reward a long look.
