Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Historic Centre of Guimarães and Couros Zo

    Aqui nasceu Portugal, says the slogan painted on an old section of the town wall: Portugal was born here. Guimarães claims to be the cradle of the nation because Afonso Henriques, the first king of Portugal, is said to have been born here, and in 1128 he won the battle of São Mamede nearby, against his mother's forces, a key step towards independence. The castle on the hill, a compact fortress with a tall keep, and the small Romanesque church beside it, where he is said to have been baptised, are the symbols of that story.

  2. 2. Alto Douro Wine Region

    The Douro river has been carving a gorge through the north of Portugal for millions of years, and for about two thousand years people have been planting vines on its slopes. The terraces, held up by stone walls, climb steeply from the water and rise through schist and slate in rows, and the effect, in the right season, is of a vast green staircase. The Alto Douro was the first wine region in the world to be officially demarcated, by the Marquis of Pombal in 1756, and UNESCO listed it in 2001. Port wine, the sweet fortified red that made the region famous, is still made here.

  3. 3. Vale de Figueira / Teixugo

    What I can say is that this stretch of the Côa valley is the part where the story of the rock art really begins. In 1991 Nelson Rebanda, a young archaeologist working on the dam project, noticed engravings on the schist walls, and over the following years teams recorded hundreds of panels along the river.
