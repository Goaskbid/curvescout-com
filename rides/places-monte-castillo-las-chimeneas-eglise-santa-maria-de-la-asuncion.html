Why this ride

A 216 km round trip from Puente Viesgo - roughly 3 h 43 in the saddle. The stops in between: La Garma, Covalanas, Eglise Santa Maria de la Asuncion and back through Covalanas. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Monte Castillo - Las Chimeneas

    Everyone talks about Altamira, but Monte Castillo has a better claim to the title of Europe's oldest gallery. El Castillo, the cave that gives the hill its name, contains a red disc that was dated in 2012 to more than forty thousand years ago, which makes it among the oldest known cave paintings anywhere. Las Chimeneas, the cave marked here, is one of its neighbours inside the same hill, and the whole mountain is a kind of honeycomb of Ice Age art.

  2. 2. La Garma

    La Garma is a low limestone hill a few kilometres inland from the Bay of Santander, green with meadows and oak scrub, the sort of rounded Cantabrian hump you might ride past without a second glance. Inside it lies one of the most important prehistoric caves in Europe. Found by chance in the 1990s, the cave of La Garma contains not just paintings but preserved living floors from the Palaeolithic: hearths, bones, tools and the outlines of shelters left exactly as they were when a rockfall sealed the entrance thousands of years ago. It forms part of the UNESCO site of the Cave of Altamira and Palaeolithic Cave Art of Northern Spain.

  3. 3. Covalanas

    Covalanas is known as the cave of the red deer. Its walls carry red ochre paintings of hinds, drawn in a distinctive dotted technique, and they are among the finest Palaeolithic paintings in the Cantabrian region. The cave sits on a steep, wooded hillside above Ramales de la Victoria, in eastern Cantabria, and it was one of the first painted caves to be recognised in Spain, in the early twentieth century. It is part of the UNESCO-listed Cave of Altamira and Palaeolithic Cave Art of Northern Spain, and visits are limited to small groups, so booking ahead is advisable.

  4. 4. Eglise Santa Maria de la Asuncion

    Castro-Urdiales has one of the best harbour fronts on the whole Cantabrian coast, and it knows it. The Gothic church of Santa María de la Asunción stands on a rocky headland above the old port, its buttresses and pinnacles silhouetted against the sea, flanked by the remains of a castle that now holds a lighthouse and by a medieval bridge leading to a little chapel on a rock. Built from the thirteenth century onward, the church is one of the finest Gothic buildings in northern Spain, and it lies on the coastal route of the Way of Saint James, which is part of a UNESCO listing that covers the pilgrimage roads of the north.
