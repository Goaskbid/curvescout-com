Why this ride

Rainforest descent, ocean heat and a coastal reward. A 464 km loop that starts and ends near Mogi das Cruzes, about 8 h 55 of riding. It goes by way of Paraty old town and Ubatuba coast. Serra do Mar road is the high point. It tops out at about 800 m. It is a high-mountain day.

Roadbook

  1. Mogi das Cruzes fuel fuel

    Mogi das Cruzes is a big city on the eastern fringe of São Paulo, with more than three hundred thousand people, and for a rider heading toward the Serra do Mar and the coast it is the practical last stop before the road gets serious. It sits on the Tietê river valley in a landscape of low hills and market gardens, with the mountains of the Serra do Mar forming a dark wall to the south. The city has a large community of Japanese descent, who settled in the area in the early twentieth century and made it a centre of vegetable and flower growing, and you will see their mark in the markets, restaurants and festivals.

    The city's main festival is the Festa do Divino, a religious celebration with processions, music and food that has taken place for generations. The old centre has a cathedral, small squares and shops, and the surroundings include parks of Atlantic forest that survive in patches between the farms and suburbs. The air is warm and humid, and in summer afternoon thunderstorms are common, with heavy rain that can make the roads slick in minutes.

    As a fuel stop, Mogi is simple. The city has many stations along the main roads, open for long hours, and prices are in line with the São Paulo region. The traffic around the city can be heavy, especially at rush hours and on weekends, with lorries, buses and cars sharing the highways. If you are heading for the coast, an early start helps you beat the worst of it. Take care at junctions and on the multi-lane roads, where drivers can be aggressive and lane discipline is loose.

    Food is cheap and good. Small restaurants serve the Brazilian plate of rice, beans, meat and salad, and the Japanese influence gives you the chance to try sushi, noodle dishes or a pastel, a fried pastry filled with cheese or meat, bought from a market stall. Coffee is strong and sweet, and a pão de queijo, cheese bread, is a good snack for the road.

    Beyond the city, the road begins to climb toward the Serra do Mar and the coast, with forest closing in and the temperature dropping. Check your tyres and lights, carry a rain jacket and fill the tank fully, because the mountain sections have fewer stations. Brazil drives on the right, and local riders are quick, so keep a safe distance and stay alert.

  2. Serra do Mar road pass

    The Serra do Mar is the great escarpment that runs along the coast of south-eastern Brazil, a wall of forested mountains between the São Paulo plateau and the Atlantic, and crossing it is one of the main events of any ride from the city to the sea. The roads that climb or descend it, such as the Rodovia dos Tamoios, thread through dense Atlantic forest, one of the most diverse and most threatened ecosystems on earth. The trees are tall and wrapped in vines, bromeliads and ferns, and the air is thick with the smell of wet leaves and soil.

    The area near Santa Branca and Jacareí lies in the Paraíba valley, on the plateau side of the range, where reservoirs and farmland fill the broad floor of the valley. From here the roads lead up and over the mountains toward the coast, and the change in scenery is rapid. On the plateau, the air is drier and cooler, with pasture and eucalyptus, and as the road rises into the forest, the temperature drops and cloud begins to gather. At the crest, the mist can be so thick that visibility falls to a few metres, and the road emerges below it into sunshine or a downpour.

    The ride is demanding in the best way. The bends are tight and often blind, the surface can be wet and slick with moss and mud, and heavy trucks use the same lanes, so overtaking needs judgement. Wet weather is common, with sudden downpours, and fog is frequent in the mornings. Care is required on descents, where brakes can overheat and the road drops steeply, and the locals know that a clear day can turn into a grey one in minutes. Landslides sometimes close sections after heavy rain, so check the conditions.

    Santa Branca, about two kilometres away, is a small town with basic services, and Jacareí, a city of over two hundred thousand, lies about eleven kilometres off, with plenty of fuel, food and repairs. There are few stations on the mountain, so fill the tank before the climb. Roadside stalls and small restaurants appear at viewpoints and sell snacks and fruit.

    The wildlife in the forest is rich, with toucans, monkeys and many butterflies, though you will seldom see them from a bike. Stop at a viewpoint, if the clouds permit, and listen to the forest. It is a green, loud world.

  3. Paraty old town sight
  4. Ubatuba coast swim

    Ubatuba is a beach town on the northern coast of São Paulo state, and the name comes from a Tupi word often said to mean place of the canoes. The Tupinambá, who lived here before the Portuguese came, built large wooden boats and defended the coast fiercely, and the memory of them lingers in the place names. The town claims about a hundred beaches within its municipality, a stretch of coast where the Atlantic forest comes down to the sea, with steep green mountains, rocky headlands and small bays of clear water.

    The coast road, running along the foot of the Serra do Mar, links the beaches in a ribbon of curves and short climbs. In places it hugs the shore, giving views of islands and sailing boats, and in others it dips inland through forest and banana plantations. The surface is generally good, though patched in places, and the bends are moderate. Traffic can be heavy in summer and on holidays, when the town fills with visitors from São Paulo, and the road becomes slow. At other times, early morning rides are pleasant, with warm light on the water and little traffic.

    The sea is warm, usually between twenty-two and twenty-six degrees, and the water is clear on the better beaches. The surf varies from calm bays suited to a gentle swim to open beaches with rolling waves popular with surfers. Currents can be strong at some beaches, so heed local warnings and flags. Along the edge of the forest, the Serra do Mar State Park protects a huge area of Atlantic forest, with trails leading to waterfalls and viewpoints. Rain is frequent, and a sudden shower can interrupt a perfect afternoon, but it passes quickly and leaves the air fresh.

    Ubatuba, with about seventy-nine thousand people, has fuel stations, supermarkets, bakeries and many restaurants, and the nearest bigger town is Caraguatatuba, about forty kilometres to the west. Fresh fish, shrimp and moqueca, a fish stew with coconut milk, are on the menu in many places, and açaí bowls and fresh fruit juices are everywhere. Prices are higher in high season, so check before you order.

    Parking for motorcycles is usually easy near beaches, but do not leave valuables on the bike, and take care at night. Sand and salt are hard on a motorcycle, so rinse off the dust when you can. A swim at the end of the day, with the sun going down behind the mountains, is hard to beat.
