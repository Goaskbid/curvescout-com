Why this ride

From Florence it runs 411 km to Florence; allow about 7 h of riding. On the way it passes Historic Centre of Siena, Piazza del Duomo and back through Historic Centre of Siena. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Chiantigiana Wine Road

Historic Centre of Siena is 80 km from Villa de Careggi on Raccordo Autostradale 3 Siena-Firenze. It gains around 460 m and ends high, near 346 m.

The stops

  1. 1. Villa de Careggi

    Start here.

    Cosimo de' Medici chose a quiet slope just north of Florence for his country house, and in the late 1450s he had the architect Michelozzo rebuild an older castle-like farmhouse at Careggi into a villa. It was not a grand showpiece. It was a working retreat where a banker who ran a city without holding office could think, farm, and entertain scholars. Cosimo died here in 1464, and so did his grandson Lorenzo the Magnificent in 1492, which makes the walls rather weighty with Florentine history.

    Read the complete Villa de Careggi write-up.

  2. 2. Historic Centre of Siena

    80 km in.

    Siena sits on three ridges in the heart of Tuscany, a red-brick medieval city that has hardly changed its shape in seven hundred years. At its centre is the Piazza del Campo, a sloping, shell-shaped square paved in brick and divided into nine sections, a reminder of the Council of Nine that governed the city in its golden age. The Palazzo Pubblico, with the slender Torre del Mangia, stands at the bottom of the square, and inside are frescoes by Ambrogio Lorenzetti showing the effects of good and bad government, painted in the fourteenth century.

    Read the complete Historic Centre of Siena write-up.

  3. 3. Piazza del Duomo

    205 km in.

    Nothing prepares you for that first glimpse through the gateway. A wide green lawn, and set on it like ornaments on a cloth, the white marble cathedral, the round baptistery, the cemetery cloister of the Camposanto, and of course the bell tower, leaning at an angle that looks wrong no matter how many photographs you have seen. The Piazza del Duomo in Pisa, also called the Field of Miracles, was one of the first places in Italy to be listed by UNESCO, back in 1987.
