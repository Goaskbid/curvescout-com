  1. 1. Norwegian Scenic Route Rondane point 1

    The southern end of the Rondane road is the start of a different kind of mountain country. Gone are the steep fjord walls and dripping waterfalls of the west, and in their place stands a broad, high land of rounded summits, pale slopes and wide valleys, where the sky takes up most of the view. Rondane was Norway's first national park, created in 1962, and the mountains in it include ten peaks over two thousand metres, with Rondeslottet the highest at 2,178 metres. They look smooth from a distance, the product of rock that breaks into blocks and of long ages under ice.

  2. 2. Norwegian Scenic Route Rondane point 2

    To understand Rondane, it helps to know what the mountains are made of. The rock here is mostly a hard, pale, layered sandstone called sparagmite, and it weathers in a way that gives the peaks their characteristic rounded forms and their slopes of loose stone. Over millions of years the ice sheets rubbed down the summits, left boulders strewn across the valleys and scooped out hollows that became lakes. The landscape looks old, and it is.

  3. 3. Norwegian Scenic Route Rondane point 3

    There is a viewing point on this road that looks across a lake to the peaks of Rondane, named after the painter Harald Sohlberg, who painted these mountains with a stillness that few artists have matched. His best-known picture, Winter Night in the Mountains, shows a snowy peak under a deep blue sky with a single star above it, and it is one of the most loved paintings in Norway. Standing at the viewpoint in the late evening, with the light fading and the water dark, it is not hard to see what drew him.

  4. 4. Folldal

    Folldal is a small mining village in the Østerdalen district, and it carries a curious history for a place so quiet today. Copper was mined here from the seventeenth century until the 1970s, and the old workings at Folldal Verk are now a museum, with buildings and shafts that give a picture of the life of the miners. About 600 people live in the village centre, and the surrounding municipality is large, with mountains, forests and pastures. The nearest towns are Alvdal, a long way south, and the larger settlements are far enough that fuel is something to plan for.
