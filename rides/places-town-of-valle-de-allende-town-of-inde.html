Why this ride

A 423 km round trip from Valle de Allende - roughly 7 h in the saddle. The stops in between: Temple of San Miguel of the town of Villa, Town of Indé and back through Temple of San Miguel of the town of Villa. It tops out around 2,050 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

The stops

  1. 1. Town of Valle de Allende

    Start here.

    Valle de Allende is a quiet town in the high desert of southern Chihuahua, set in a broad valley at about 1,500 metres, with green orchards and fields along the river and bare brown hills beyond. The population is around four thousand, and the place feels more like a country crossroads than a tourist stop. It is a base for a ride that heads south into the mountains toward Indé, across a landscape that has been shaped by mining and ranching for four centuries.

    Read the complete Town of Valle de Allende write-up.

  2. 2. Temple of San Miguel of the town of Villa

    94 km in.

    The little church of San Miguel is the quiet heart of Villa Ocampo, which sits in the dry north of Durango, close to where the state meets Chihuahua. The settlement belongs to the long chain of towns strung along the Camino Real de Tierra Adentro, the old road that carried silver south and supplies, settlers and missionaries north, and it still has the layout you would expect: a plaza, a church at its edge, low houses of adobe and brick, and a feeling that nobody is in a hurry.

    Read the complete Temple of San Miguel of the town of Villa write-up.

  3. 3. Town of Indé

    212 km in.

    In the high desert of northern Durango, between mountain ranges and dry basins, the town of Inde lies along the old silver road that ran from Mexico City to Santa Fe in New Mexico. The Camino Real de Tierra Adentro, the Royal Road of the Interior Land, was in use from the sixteenth century to the nineteenth, carrying silver, mercury, supplies, settlers and missionaries over 2,500 kilometres, and the Mexican sections were inscribed on the World Heritage list in 2010. Inde was a stopping point on that road, with a church, a plaza and the haciendas that supplied the mining camps.
