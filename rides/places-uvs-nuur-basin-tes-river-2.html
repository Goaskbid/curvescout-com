Why this ride

On the way it passes Ubsu-Nur, Tes River and back through Ubsu-Nur. The highest point is about 1,200 m. It crosses from Mongolia into Russia - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. Rivers set the line for much of the day.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Uvs Nuur Basin

    Uvs Nuur is a lake in a basin, and the basin matters more than the lake. The depression lies astride the border between Mongolia and the Russian republic of Tuva, ringed by mountains on three sides, and it is the northern end of the Central Asian steppe and the southern edge of the Siberian taiga. Within a few hours of each other you can find desert dunes, steppe, mountain tundra, glaciers, forest and salt marsh. UNESCO listed it in 2003 as a transboundary site, and Russia and Mongolia manage their sides separately.

  2. 2. Ubsu-Nur

    Ubsu-Nur, or Uvs Nuur, is a lake in a land of extremes. It lies in a closed basin on the border between Mongolia and the Russian republic of Tuva, in the middle of Central Asia, where there is no outlet to the sea, so the water simply evaporates, leaving it too salty to drink. It is the largest lake in Mongolia by area, shallow, with wide marshes and reed beds, and it sits between the Altai mountains to the west and the Sayan range to the north. The Tes river, which feeds the lake, gives its name to a part of the basin, and the whole area was listed by UNESCO in 2003 as the Uvs Nuur Basin.

  3. 3. Tes River

    Where the Tes river comes down from the Mongolian highlands to the Uvs basin, in the far north-west of the country, the land is open, stony and almost without trees. It flows toward Uvs Nuur, the largest lake in Mongolia, a shallow body of salt water that has no outlet, shared with the Russian republic of Tuva, and the whole basin is a UNESCO site, listed in 2003 as one of the best preserved steppe ecosystems in Central Asia. The delta of the river is a wetland where reeds, marsh and pools attract migrating birds, including swans, pelicans, cranes and many ducks.
