  1. 1. Food stop near Prats de Lluçanès

    The Lluçanès is one of those Catalan districts that most visitors never find. It is a high plateau between the plains of Osona and the Berguedà mountains, a land of rolling fields, oak and pine woods, Romanesque churches and isolated farmhouses, the great stone masies that are typical of rural Catalonia. Prats de Lluçanès, a small town of a few thousand people, is its main centre.

  2. 2. Photo stop near Bonastre

    Bonastre is a small hill village in the Baix Penedès, inland from the coast between Tarragona and Barcelona, surrounded by vineyards, olive groves and pine-covered hills. It has a castle tower, a church and narrow streets, and from its edges the views stretch over the vines to the Mediterranean. It is a quiet place, a world away from the beach resorts on the coast.

  3. 3. Near El Prat de Llobregat

    The Llobregat delta is flat, green and noisy in equal measure. Jets climb out of Barcelona's airport over the sea, the city's port stretches along the coast to the north, and between them lie fields of artichokes, orchards, marshes and pine-backed beaches.
