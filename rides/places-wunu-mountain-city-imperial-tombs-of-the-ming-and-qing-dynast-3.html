Why this ride

A 244 km round trip from Huanren - roughly 4 h 12 in the saddle. On the way it passes Yongling Tomb of the Qing Dynasty, Imperial Tombs of the Ming and Qing Dynast and back through Yongling Tomb of the Qing Dynasty. Expect gradients and long views.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Wunu Mountain City

    Wunu Mountain City is a ruined fortress on a flat-topped crag in Liaoning province, in the green hills of northeastern China, and it was probably the first capital of the Goguryeo kingdom. Before that kingdom grew into a power that rivalled Chinese dynasties, its founders chose a mountain stronghold above a river for safety. Cliffs shelter the plateau on almost every side, and the builders closed the few gaps with stone walls. Inside, there are traces of buildings, a spring, a soldier's quarters and a platform that may have served as a lookout. It is part of the Capital Cities and Tombs of the Ancient Koguryo Kingdom, listed by UNESCO in 2004, which takes in sites around Huanren and Ji'an.

  2. 2. Yongling Tomb of the Qing Dynasty

    Before Beijing, before the Forbidden City, the people who would rule China as the Qing dynasty were a clan of forest-edge farmers and hunters in the hills of eastern Liaoning. Yongling is their ancestral grave. It lies near Xinbin, a Manchu autonomous county in the mountains east of Fushun, not far from Hetu Ala, the small hill town that Nurhaci made his first capital, and it holds the remains of the forebears of that remarkable man, who united the Jurchen tribes in the late sixteenth century and set in motion the conquest of China by his successors.

  3. 3. Imperial Tombs of the Ming and Qing Dynast

    Long before the Manchus took Beijing, they had a home, and it was in the green, hilly country east of Shenyang, where the Hun river comes out of the Changbai foothills. Yongling, the Qing dynasty's earliest imperial tomb, stands there in the county of Xinbin, built at the start of the seventeenth century and expanded afterwards to house the ancestors of Nurhaci, the chief who united the Jurchen tribes and founded the line that would rule China until 1912. It is an old-fashioned ceremonial complex, with walls, a stone bridge, spirit-way sculptures and a tomb mound behind, set against a wooded hillside, and it makes a clear point: before they were emperors of China, these people were mountain chieftains.
