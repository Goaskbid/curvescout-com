Why this ride

On the way it passes Dong Phayayen-Khao Yai Forest Complex, Pang Sida National Park, Dong Yai Wildlife Sanctuary and back through Pang Sida National Park. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Khao Yai National Park

    Khao Yai was the first national park in Thailand, set up in 1962, and today it forms the heart of a vast forest complex, Dong Phayayen-Khao Yai, that UNESCO listed in 2005. It stretches across mountain ridges, evergreen forest, grassland and the sources of several rivers, and it protects some of the richest wildlife in mainland Southeast Asia: wild elephants, gibbons whose calls ring across the valleys at dawn, hornbills with their huge casques, macaques, sambar deer and, in the thick of the forest, a few tigers and bears that almost nobody sees.

  2. 2. Dong Phayayen-Khao Yai Forest Complex

    Thailand's first national park lies just a couple of hours from Bangkok, and it still has wild elephants walking down its roads. Khao Yai, established in 1962, covers a large area of forested hills on the southern edge of the Khorat plateau, and together with neighbouring protected areas, it forms the Dong Phayayen-Khao Yai Forest Complex, listed by UNESCO in 2005. The forests here are a mix of evergreen and deciduous, grasslands and streams, home to elephants, gibbons, hornbills, deer, and a small number of tigers.

  3. 3. Pang Sida National Park

    Pang Sida is the eastern end of the great forest belt that stretches across north-eastern Thailand from Khao Yai to the Cambodian border, a block of jungle, waterfalls and rolling hills in Sa Kaeo province. Together with Khao Yai, Thap Lan, Ta Phraya and a wildlife sanctuary, it makes up the Dong Phayayen–Khao Yai Forest Complex, a World Heritage site that protects some of the best remaining monsoon forest in mainland South-east Asia.

  4. 4. Dong Yai Wildlife Sanctuary

    Near the Cambodian border, the forests of eastern Thailand are some of the wildest in the country, and Dong Yai is part of a great chain of them. The Dong Phayayen–Khao Yai forest complex, which UNESCO listed in 2005, covers about six thousand square kilometres of hills and valleys stretching from Khao Yai National Park to the Cambodian frontier. Dong Yai Wildlife Sanctuary is one of the five protected areas in the complex, together with Ta Phraya, Thap Lan and Pang Sida National Parks, and it forms a corridor for animals that need space.
