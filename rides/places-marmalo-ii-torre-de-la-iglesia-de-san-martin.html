Why this ride

From Villar del Humo it runs 233 km to Villar del Humo; allow about 4 h 01 of riding. The stops in between: Abrigo de la Paridera de Tormón, Cueva de Doña Clotilde, Torre de la iglesia de San Martín and back through Cueva de Doña Clotilde. The highest point is about 1,450 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. marmalo II

    There are places in Spain that sound like names from a fairy tale, and Villar del Humo is one of them, the Village of Smoke. It is a hamlet of about three hundred and fifty people in the Serranía of Cuenca, where the pine forest begins to thicken and the Cabriel river carves gorges through the limestone. Marmalo is the name of the ravine nearby, and its painted shelters are among the major Levantine sites of inland Spain, listed with the UNESCO group of 1998.

  2. 2. Abrigo de la Paridera de Tormón

    Paridera is a shepherd's word: a stone pen where ewes give birth, sheltered from the wind. Above Tormón, a tiny village in the southern Sierra de Albarracín, one such overhang has given its name to a rock shelter with prehistoric paintings, the Abrigo de la Paridera, part of the Levantine rock art sites the Iberian Peninsula shares under one UNESCO listing. The cliff here is rodeno, the red sandstone that gives this corner of Teruel its startling colour, eroded into towers, arches and overhangs among the pine trees.

  3. 3. Cueva de Doña Clotilde

    The rock art in the Cueva de Doña Clotilde, in the pine forests outside Albarracín, is a little different from the typical Levantine scenes. Painted in white or pale colours rather than the usual red, the figures include schematic human shapes and animals, and they sit on the red sandstone of the Rodeno, a landscape of rust-coloured rocks and maritime pines that is now protected as a cultural park. The shelter is part of the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin listing, and the Rodeno is full of painted shelters, several of which, like the bulls of the Prado del Navazo, can be visited along marked paths.

  4. 4. Torre de la iglesia de San Martín

    Teruel is a small provincial capital high on the Aragonese plateau, and its skyline is made of Mudéjar towers, built by Muslim craftsmen working for Christian patrons in brick and glazed ceramic tiles. The tower of San Martín, dating to the early fourteenth century, is one of the finest, a square tower straddling a street, so that traffic passes through an arch at its base. With the other Mudéjar towers of the city and the cathedral, it forms part of the World Heritage listing of Mudéjar Architecture of Aragon.
