Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Shkodër

    Shkodër is one of the oldest towns in Albania and the cultural capital of the north, a place where Catholic bell towers, mosques and Orthodox domes sit within a short walk of each other and bicycles outnumber cars in the old streets. It lies on a flat plain between the huge Lake Skadar, the largest lake in southern Europe, and the first foothills of the Albanian Alps, and the Buna river, a short, powerful stream, carries the lake's water to the Adriatic. Above the town stands Rozafa Castle on its rock, with views over both rivers and the lake. Legend says the walls kept collapsing until the builders agreed to wall up a woman alive, and the mother of a baby in the family agreed on the condition that one breast, one eye and one hand be left free to nurse the child.

  4. 4. Near Gusinje

    Gusinje is a mountain town at the foot of the Prokletije, the range whose name means Accursed Mountains, and from the main street you can look up at limestone peaks that crowd the border with Albania and Kosovo. It lies in a high valley of the Lim headwaters near the lake of Plav, a place of green meadows, old stone houses and a population that is largely Bosniak and Albanian. The town's character is more Ottoman than Slavic, with mosques, small shops and family-run cafés, and the pace of life is slow.
