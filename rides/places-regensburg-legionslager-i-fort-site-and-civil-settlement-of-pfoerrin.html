  1. 1. Regensburg - Legionslager I

    Regensburg exists because of a decision made in the year 179. The emperor Marcus Aurelius was fighting the Marcomanni and other tribes across the Danube, and a new legionary fortress was needed on the northern frontier of the province of Raetia. The site chosen was a bluff on the south bank of the Danube, near the point where the river Regen comes in from the north. The fortress was named Castra Regina, the camp by the Regen, and the Third Italic Legion, raised by Marcus Aurelius himself, was stationed there.

  2. 2. Weltenburg‐Am Galget - Kleinkastell

    The Danube does something unexpected just upstream of Kelheim. Instead of wandering across flat Bavarian farmland, it cuts straight through a band of white Jurassic limestone, squeezed between cliffs that rise steeply from the water. This is the Weltenburg Narrows, and on the hill above the abbey at Weltenburg, at the spot called Am Galget, the Romans put a small fort to watch the river and the frontier.

  3. 3. Fort site and civil settlement of Pfoerrin

    Between Ingolstadt and Kelheim, the Danube flows through a broad, flat valley in Bavaria, with hop gardens, fields of maize and asparagus beds on its banks. Pförring is a small market town on the north bank, with Neustadt an der Donau and Vohburg on the other side. In Roman times, this was the frontier: the Raetian Limes ended at the Danube just downstream, and the Romans built a fort here, known as Celeusum, with a civil settlement outside its walls.

  4. 4. Watchtowers WP 15/11 to 15/26 and fortlets

    The Altmühl valley in Bavaria is one of Germany's best kept riding secrets, a winding river valley cut deep into Jurassic limestone, lined with juniper heaths, rock towers and castles. Near where the valley meets the Danube, the Roman frontier, the Raetian Limes, ran across the land, and the stretch of watchtowers and small forts numbered WP 15/11 to 15/26 lies in the wooded hills here, between Riedenburg, Dietfurt and the Danube town of Vohburg.
