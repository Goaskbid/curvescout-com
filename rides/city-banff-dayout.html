Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Banff

    Banff in the morning has a particular feel: cold air coming off the glacier-fed Bow River, mist clinging to Mount Rundle, and the smell of pine and woodsmoke from kitchens. The town sits in the heart of the Canadian Rockies at about 1,400 metres, hemmed in by peaks, and it exists because of hot springs. In the 1880s railway workers found warm water at Cave and Basin, and the government made the area a reserve that grew into Canada's first national park.

  2. 2. Canadian Rocky Mountain Parks

    On the south side of the Trans-Canada Highway rise Mount Stephen and Mount Burgess, and a few kilometres on, the road climbs towards Kicking Horse Pass, at about 1,627 metres the boundary between Alberta and British Columbia, and the line of the continental divide. The name came from an incident in 1858, when the explorer James Hector was kicked by his horse and left for dead.
