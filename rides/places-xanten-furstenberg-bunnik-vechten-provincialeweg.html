Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Xanten-Fürstenberg

    The Fürstenberg is a low hill on the edge of the Lower Rhine plain, just south of Xanten, and two thousand years ago it was one of the most important military bases in the Roman Empire. Here the Romans built Vetera, a legionary fortress that housed up to two legions and served as a springboard for campaigns into Germania under Augustus. In AD 69 and 70, during the Batavian revolt, Vetera was besieged, starved into surrender and destroyed, and a new fortress was built nearby. The site on the Fürstenberg is part of the Lower German Limes, the Roman frontier along the Rhine from the Alps to the North Sea, which was added to the World Heritage list in 2021. On the ground there is little to see, mostly fields and woods, but the elevated position, with views over the river plain, explains why the Romans chose it.

  2. 2. Nijmegen-Kops Plateau | Kopse Hof South

    Every July, a river of walkers pours through Nijmegen. The Four Days Marches, which have been held since 1909, attract tens of thousands of people who walk thirty, forty or fifty kilometres a day for four days, and the whole city welcomes them with flowers and music. The marchers cross the great bridge over the Waal, which is one of the widest rivers in the country, and the Kops Plateau, a rise on the edge of the old town, is part of the same landscape. In the second half of the twentieth century it was a place of sandpits and playing fields, but an older story lies beneath it.

  3. 3. Bunnik-Vechten | Provincialeweg

    A modern fort and a Roman one stand within a couple of kilometres of each other here, and both exist because of the same river. Vechten, on the Kromme Rijn just east of Utrecht, was the Roman fort of Fectio, one of the best-known sites on the Lower Germanic frontier. It lay at a point where the Rhine split, with the branch that became the Vecht heading north, and that made it a perfect depot. Soldiers, grain, and building stone all went through here, and archaeologists have pulled a remarkable amount out of the ground, including altars, bronze statuettes and the debris of a busy harbour.
