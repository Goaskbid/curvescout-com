Why this ride

A 287 km round trip from Lipljan - roughly 4 h 57 in the saddle. The stops in between: Church of the Virgin of Leviša, Dlaboka Reka and back through Church of the Virgin of Leviša. The highest point is about 1,500 m. It crosses from Kosovo into North Macedonia - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Gračanica Monastery

    Gračanica is a monastery of five small domes rising from a Kosovo plain a few kilometres south-east of Pristina, and it is one of the most graceful medieval buildings in the Balkans. King Stefan Milutin of Serbia had it built around 1321 on the site of an older church, and its cross-shaped plan, tall drum-shaped domes and bands of brick and stone make it a masterpiece of the late Byzantine style. UNESCO lists it among the Medieval Monuments in Kosovo.

  2. 2. Church of the Virgin of Leviša

    In the middle of Prizren's old town, behind a wall and a fence, stands a brick-and-stone church whose bell tower rises above the rooftops. This is the Church of the Virgin of Leviša, Bogorodica Ljeviška in Serbian, one of the most important medieval monuments in the Balkans. It was rebuilt in the early fourteenth century under the Serbian king Milutin on the site of an older church, and its interior was covered with frescoes that are among the finest of their time.

  3. 3. Dlaboka Reka

    Few borderlands in the Balkans feel as folded and unhurried as the country around Peshkopi, in the Dibra mountains of north-eastern Albania. Across the frontier the same people are Macedonia's Dlaboka Reka, the Deep River, and the double name tells you the story: a Slavic and Albanian valley land cut through by a political line that locals still cross for weddings and markets. The Black Drin runs through the region, the great peaks of the Korab range stand along the border, and Mount Korab itself, at 2,764 metres, is the highest in both Albania and North Macedonia.
