Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Archaeological Site of Mystras

    Mystras is a whole Byzantine city draped down a steep hillside, a few kilometres west of modern Sparta, at the foot of the Taygetos mountains. It began in 1249, when William II of Villehardouin, a Frankish prince ruling the Peloponnese after the Fourth Crusade, built a castle on the summit. Within a few years the Byzantines had taken it back, and Mystras grew into the capital of the Despotate of the Morea, a centre of learning and art in the last centuries of the empire. The philosopher Gemistos Plethon taught here, and in 1449 Constantine XI Palaiologos, the last Byzantine emperor, was crowned here before leaving for Constantinople, where he died when the city fell in 1453.

  2. 2. Temple of Apollo Epicurius at Bassae

    At over a thousand metres in the mountains of Arcadia, on a rocky shoulder above the gorges of the Neda, stands one of the best preserved temples of ancient Greece, and since the 1980s it has stood under a vast white tent. The Temple of Apollo Epicurius at Bassae was built in the fifth century BC by the people of nearby Phigalia, who dedicated it to Apollo the helper, it is said, for saving them from plague. Ancient writers attributed its design to Iktinos, one of the architects of the Parthenon. It was the first Greek monument in Greece to be listed as a World Heritage Site, in 1986.

  3. 3. Archaeological Site of Olympia

    Every four years, for more than a thousand years, a truce was declared across the Greek world so that athletes could travel to Olympia. The sanctuary lies in a green valley in the western Peloponnese, where the Alfeios river meets a smaller stream, below the pine-covered Hill of Kronos. The traditional date of the first Games is 776 BC, and they carried on until the Roman emperor Theodosius put an end to pagan festivals at the end of the fourth century AD. The statue of Zeus made by Phidias for the temple here, a giant seated figure of gold and ivory, was one of the Seven Wonders of the ancient world, though it has long since vanished.
