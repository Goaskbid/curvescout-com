Why this ride

349 km from Bellinzona to Bellinzona, about 5 h 11 of riding. It goes by way of Isolino Virginia-Camilla-Isola di San Biag, Sacro Monte Calvario and back through Isolino Virginia-Camilla-Isola di San Biag. It tops out around 1,850 m. It crosses from Switzerland into Italy - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules.

Grand Tour of Switzerland

The stops

  1. 1. Chateau de Sasso Corbaro

    Start here.

    Bellinzona sits at the point where the valleys of the southern Alps converge before opening towards Lake Maggiore and Italy, and whoever held it controlled the routes over the Gotthard, the Lukmanier and the San Bernardino passes. In the fifteenth century the Dukes of Milan built a formidable system of defences here: three castles and a wall that ran across the whole valley floor. Castelgrande, on a rocky hill above the old town, Montebello, on the slope above it, and Sasso Corbaro, highest of all on a wooded spur, together form a World Heritage site. Sasso Corbaro was built in 1479, in a matter of months, after the Swiss Confederates defeated the Milanese at the Battle of Giornico, a short way up the Leventina valley, and the Duke wanted to plug the gap in his defences.

  2. 2. Isolino Virginia-Camilla-Isola di San Biag

    85 km in.

    Lago di Varese is the quiet one. The big lakes, Maggiore and Lugano, grab the postcards and the coach tours, while this shallow, reedy sheet of water just west of Varese gets on with being pretty in a modest way. Off its western shore, a few hundred metres out from Biandronno, floats a tiny wooded island called Isolino Virginia, and that speck of land is one of the most important prehistoric sites in the Alpine world.

    Read the complete Isolino Virginia-Camilla-Isola di San Biag write-up.

  3. 3. Sacro Monte Calvario

    174 km in.

    Domodossola's holy mountain rises immediately above the town, on the wooded Mattarella hill, and the walk or ride up gives you the whole Ossola valley at your feet. The Sacro Monte Calvario was begun in the seventeenth century as a devotional path of chapels depicting the Passion of Christ, each holding painted and sculpted scenes, leading up to a sanctuary church at the top. It is one of the nine Sacri Monti of Piedmont and Lombardy inscribed together on the UNESCO list, and it has a particular link with Antonio Rosmini, the philosopher-priest who founded his religious institute here in the nineteenth century.
