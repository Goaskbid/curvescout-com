Why this ride

From Austin it runs 350 km to Austin; allow about 6 h 02 of riding. It goes by way of Mission Valero, Mission Concepcion, Mission San José and Mission San Juan. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Austin

    Austin sits where the flat Texas blackland prairie meets the limestone Hill Country, and that line, the Balcones Escarpment, is the reason the city is a good place to start a ride. West of downtown the land rises suddenly into oak and juniper hills cut by creeks, with the Colorado River, dammed into the long Lake Austin and Lake Travis, curling between them. The city grew here in the nineteenth century as the capital of the Republic of Texas, and the pink granite Capitol, which is taller than the one in Washington, still dominates the skyline.

  2. 2. Mission Valero

    San Antonio de Valero is the formal name of what the world knows as the Alamo. The mission was founded in 1718 by Spanish Franciscans, who named it in honor of Saint Anthony of Padua and the viceroy of New Spain, the Marqués de Valero. It stood beside the San Antonio River, on land that offered water, irrigation and a place to teach indigenous families a farming life. By the end of the eighteenth century the mission was secularized, and its old chapel became a military post, whose name came from the Spanish word for cottonwood.

  3. 3. Mission Concepcion

    Mission Concepción is the quiet one, and many people think it is the best preserved. Its full name is Nuestra Señora de la Purísima Concepción de Acuña, and its stone church was completed in the 1750s, which makes it the oldest unrestored stone church in the United States still in use. It stands on a low rise a few miles south of downtown San Antonio, with two bell towers, a dome and a facade that is plain but beautifully proportioned. The Franciscans moved the mission here in 1731 from East Texas, where drought and conflict had driven them out, and here they found the river and a better chance.

  4. 4. Mission San José

    Mission San José is known as the Queen of the Missions, and it earns the title. Founded in 1720, it grew into the largest and most complete of the San Antonio missions, a walled community with a church, granary, workshops, a mill and rows of small apartments for the indigenous families who lived inside. The compound still has much of its outer wall, and walking through the gate gives a sense of the size of the settlement. The church is the centerpiece, built in the late eighteenth century with a facade of carved limestone that is more ornate than anything else in the city.
