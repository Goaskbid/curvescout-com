  1. 1. Shawinigan

    Shawinigan was once called the City of Energy, and the title still fits. In the late nineteenth century the falls on the Saint-Maurice River provided power for factories, and by the early twentieth century this was one of the biggest industrial centres in Quebec, producing aluminium, pulp, paper and chemicals. Electricity made the town, and the long lines running out to Montreal showed how much power was available. The old plants are still scattered along the river, and the Cité de l'énergie lets you look at the industry from an observation tower.

  2. 2. La Tuque

    La Tuque is a big town in a bigger forest. It sits on the upper Saint-Maurice River, in a municipality that, by area, ranks among the largest in Canada, a sprawl of lakes, spruce forest and hills that stretches far beyond what you can see from the main street. The name means woolly cap, after a rock formation near the river that early voyageurs thought resembled the hat, and the town grew around the railway, the pulp and paper mill and the timber trade.

  3. 3. Chambord

    Lac Saint-Jean appears at Chambord after a long ride north, and it is a startling sight: a broad inland sea of fresh water, so wide the far shore is just a low line on the horizon. Route 155 ends here after its long run up the Saint-Maurice valley from La Tuque, through hundreds of kilometres of forest with few towns, and it joins the road that circles the lake. Chambord, a village of about seventeen hundred people, sits on the south shore, where farms and blueberry fields open up after the endless woods.
