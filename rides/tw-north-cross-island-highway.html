Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Daxi

    Daxi is a small town in the hills of Taoyuan, on the Dahan river, and it marks the start of one of Taiwan's most popular mountain roads. The old street, with arched facades in baroque style built during the Japanese colonial period, runs down toward the river, lined with shops selling dried tofu, a local speciality that Taiwanese people travel for. The tofu is dense, savoury and a little chewy, and you can buy it in many flavours. In the evening the street fills with families and the smell of grilled food.

  2. 2. Yilan

    Yilan lies on the Pacific coast in north-eastern Taiwan, in the Lanyang plain, a green triangle of rice paddies and scallion fields that is hemmed in by steep mountains on three sides and the open ocean on the fourth. It is wetter than the rest of the island, with rain in most months, and the damp gives the landscape a soft, mossy look. Offshore, Guishan Island, shaped like a turtle, rises from the water and can be seen from the shore on a clear day, and boats go out for whale and dolphin watching from the harbour at Wushih.
