Why this ride

Out of Waimea, one way: 50 km, roughly 52 min in the saddle. It goes by way of Waimea Canyon Lookout and Kalalau Lookout. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Waimea Kauai

    Waimea means reddish water in Hawaiian, and the name is earned: the river that gives the town its name carries a good deal of red soil down from the canyon after rain. It is a small, sleepy place on the dry western side of Kauai, strung out along the shore, with low houses, a few shops and cafes, and a surprisingly big place in history. In January 1778, Captain James Cook anchored off this coast, the first recorded European contact with the Hawaiian islands, and a statue in town commemorates the visit.

  2. 2. Waimea Canyon Lookout

    Waimea Canyon is often called the Grand Canyon of the Pacific, and when you step up to the lookout, the comparison is not absurd. The canyon is some ten miles long and a mile wide, and it drops more than three thousand feet, a trench of red, orange and green cut into the west side of Kauai, with ridges and buttes rising from the floor and a thin ribbon of river far below. Waterfalls streak the walls after rain, and in the early morning the colours glow.

  3. 3. Kalalau Lookout

    Kalalau Lookout is the place on Kauai where the road ends and the cliffs begin. From the railed platform, you look down into the Kalalau Valley, a deep green amphitheatre of fluted ridges that drops more than four thousand feet to the sea, with the surf of the Nā Pali coast glinting at the bottom. It is one of the most celebrated views in Hawaii, and when the weather cooperates, it is hard to turn away from.

  4. 4. Puu O Kila

    The road finishes in a car park at a lookout, and the lookout finishes in a cliff. Puʻu o Kila sits at the high end of Kōkeʻe State Park on Kauai, at about four thousand feet, and from the railing the land drops away in one great fold into Kalalau Valley, a steep green amphitheatre of ridges and fluted walls that runs out to the Pacific and the Nā Pali coast. Even on a clear morning, a white cloud can slide in and fill the valley in minutes, then clear again just as quickly, so patience is part of the stop.
