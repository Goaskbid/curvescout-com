Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. London motorcycle escapes

    Where the Thames squeezes between the Chiltern Hills and the Berkshire Downs, the river has had to cut a passage through a chalk ridge, and the result is the Goring Gap. It is the sort of place that looks very English from a distance and even better up close: willows, pastures, narrow locks and a weir, with the brick railway bridge at Moulsford that Brunel built crossing the water.

  3. 3. Near Enfield Town

    The world's first cash machine was installed at a Barclays branch in Enfield in June 1967, and a blue plaque marks the spot. Enfield has another claim to fame for riders: the Royal Small Arms Factory at Enfield Lock made the Lee-Enfield rifle, and the town's name passed to the Royal Enfield motorcycle company, whose bikes are still built today, though now in India. The factory site has since been redeveloped into housing around the old buildings.

    Orsett is a quiet Essex village with a surprising amount of history. Tilbury, with its Tudor fort and its docks, lies about six kilometres to the south, and Grays and the big Lakeside shopping centre are to the west.
