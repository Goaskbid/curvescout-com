Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Enden

    Enden lies on the edge of the Rondane mountains in Innlandet, and the name is apt, because the road here runs close to the end of the inhabited country and the beginning of the open fell. Rondane was Norway's first national park, created in 1962, and it is a landscape of rounded peaks, ten of which top 2,000 metres, with Rondeslottet the highest at about 2,178. The slopes are covered in reindeer lichen, which gives the hills a pale, silvery look, and the valleys hold lakes and bogs. The nearest towns, such as Otta and Vinstra, are over thirty kilometres away, so this is remote territory.

  2. 2. Sohlbergplassen

    Harald Sohlberg painted his famous winter landscape of a snow-covered peak under a vivid night sky in the Rondane mountains, and the viewing point called Sohlbergplassen is named after him. It sits beside the road at the edge of the national park, and from it you look across to Rondeslottet, the highest peak of the range at about 2,178 metres, and the long line of rounded summits that make up this part of the Norwegian interior. The painter's view is familiar to many Norwegians, who know the picture from school, and the viewpoint has a sculptural platform that frames the mountains without spoiling them.

  3. 3. Atnbrua

    Atnbrua is a bridge over the river Atna, where the Fv27 scenic road crosses water on its way through the high valleys east of Rondane. Rivers in this part of Norway are clear and cold, running fast over smooth stones, and the Atna, which drains the mountains, is a good example, with fish in it that anglers travel a long way to catch. From the bridge you can look down into pools the colour of tea, and the sound of water is a constant background. There is a parking place close by, and a short walk along the bank is easy enough for anyone.

  4. 4. Folldal

    Folldal is a small mining village in the Østerdalen district, and it carries a curious history for a place so quiet today. Copper was mined here from the seventeenth century until the 1970s, and the old workings at Folldal Verk are now a museum, with buildings and shafts that give a picture of the life of the miners. About 600 people live in the village centre, and the surrounding municipality is large, with mountains, forests and pastures. The nearest towns are Alvdal, a long way south, and the larger settlements are far enough that fuel is something to plan for.
