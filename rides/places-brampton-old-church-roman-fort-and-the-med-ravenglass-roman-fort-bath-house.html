Why this ride

A 353 km round trip from Brampton - roughly 6 h in the saddle. The stops in between: Drumburgh Roman fort and Hadrian's Wall be, Low Mire, The English Lake District and Ravenglass Roman fort bath-house. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Brampton Old Church Roman fort and the med

    Start here.

    Brampton is the sort of small Cumbrian market town that makes a good place to stop for lunch without any plan to do so. A few miles east of Carlisle, in the green valley of the River Irthing, it has an octagonal Moot Hall in the market place, built in the early nineteenth century, and a main street of sandstone houses and tea rooms. Market day brings stalls to the square, and the town has the unhurried feel of a place that knows where it is. The old church that gives this stop its name stands outside the town, on a rise by the river, with the grass mounds of a Roman fort beside it.

    Read the complete Brampton Old Church Roman fort and the med write-up.

  2. 2. Drumburgh Roman fort and Hadrian's Wall be

    35 km in.

    The small Roman fort at Drumburgh sat on a low rise above the Solway marshes, guarding a stretch of Hadrian's Wall where the frontier ran along the edge of the firth towards its western end at Bowness. Nothing much survives of the fort itself, but its stones did not go far. Drumburgh Castle, a fortified farmhouse built in the sixteenth century by the Dacre family, is clearly made from reused Roman masonry, and you can still pick out dressed blocks and even the odd carved fragment set into its walls.

    Read the complete Drumburgh Roman fort and Hadrian's Wall be write-up.

  3. 3. Low Mire

    73 km in.

    Low Mire lies in the quiet farmland behind the Cumbrian coast, near Dearham and Maryport, where the land slopes gently down to the Solway Firth and the hills of southern Scotland rise across the water. It is an area of small fields, hedges and scattered farms, with the Lake District fells visible to the south-east on clear days.

    Read the complete Low Mire write-up.

  4. 4. The English Lake District

    143 km in.

    Wordsworth wandered lonely as a cloud round here, and anybody who has ridden the Lake District on a wet bank holiday will understand the appeal of being lonely. The English Lake District, listed by UNESCO in 2017 as a cultural landscape, is a compact knot of mountains, lakes and valleys in Cumbria, shaped by glaciers and then by centuries of sheep farming, slate quarrying and, from the late eighteenth century, by poets, painters and tourists who came looking for the sublime.

    Read the complete The English Lake District write-up.

  5. 5. Ravenglass Roman fort bath-house

    178 km in.

    Walls Castle is the odd name for a Roman bath house, and the building still stands to a height of about four metres, one of the tallest Roman structures in northern England. It stood outside the fort of Glannoventa at Ravenglass, a harbour station at the meeting of three rivers, the Irt, the Mite and the Esk, and in the dry shelter of the dunes it has survived because its walls were thick and well built. You can walk around it on a short path from the village, and see niches and a doorway in the thick walls. The fort itself lies mostly under farmland and the railway.

    Read the complete Ravenglass Roman fort bath-house write-up.
