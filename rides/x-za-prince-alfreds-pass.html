  1. 1. Knysna

    Knysna lies around a tidal lagoon on the Garden Route, with a pair of sandstone cliffs known as the Heads guarding the narrow channel to the sea. Forest runs down to the water, and the whole town has a green, relaxed look, with a population around sixty-nine thousand and a waterfront full of small shops and restaurants. The lagoon is an estuary, home to rare seahorses and a rich oyster industry, and every year there is an oyster festival in the winter that fills the town.

  2. 2. Prince Alfred Pass

    Thomas Bain built a great many passes, and Prince Alfred, finished in 1867 and named for Queen Victoria's son, who visited the Cape in 1860, is one of the longest. It runs from Knysna up through the Outeniqua mountains to Uniondale, over a distance of nearly eighty kilometres, and most of it is gravel. The route was built to link the timber country of the Garden Route with the farms of the Karoo, and for decades ox wagons carried wood, wool and supplies along it.

  3. 3. Uniondale

    Uniondale is a small Karoo town on the northern side of the Outeniqua mountains, a place of whitewashed houses, tin roofs and a long, wide main street that has seen little change in decades. It lies in a dry valley at the foot of the pass, on the road between the coast and the Little Karoo, and it serves as the gateway to Prince Alfred's Pass, one of the most beautiful gravel roads in South Africa. The surrounding country is open and rolling, with sheep and goats grazing among rocky hills.
