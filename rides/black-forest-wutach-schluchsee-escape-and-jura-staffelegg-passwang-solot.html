Why this ride

From Schaffhausen it runs 460 km to Bremgarten; allow about 8 h 22 of riding. On the way it passes Wutach Gorge scenic area, Schluchsee water stop, Todtnau forest roads and St. Blasien dome. The climbs: Staffelegg ridge road and Passwang road. It tops out at 943 m. It crosses from Switzerland into Germany - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. Rolling hill country.

  1. 1. Schaffhausen border gate

    Schaffhausen is the little Swiss city that wanders north of the Rhine, in a canton that pokes into Germany like a thumb, and from the border post you can hear the language change within a few kilometres. At about four hundred metres it is low country, with gentle hills, vineyards, and fields rolling towards the Black Forest.

  2. 2. Wutach Gorge scenic area

    The Wutach is a river with an identity crisis, and that is why the gorge it cut is so spectacular. Once a headwater of the Danube, it was captured, in geological terms quite recently, by a short, steep tributary of the Rhine, and since then it has been tearing a deep trench through the limestone and granite of the southern Black Forest. The result is a gorge well over a hundred metres deep, wild and wooded, and the longest and deepest in the region. Geologists come to study the river capture; walkers come for the moss, the ferns and the sound of water.

    Read the complete Wutach Gorge scenic area write-up.

  3. 3. Schluchsee water stop

    The biggest lake in the Black Forest is not natural. The Schluchsee was created in the 1930s, when the valley was dammed to supply a hydroelectric scheme, flooding farms and hamlets and lifting the water level to form a long, slim lake in the high country at about 930 metres. It looks as though it has always been there, with forested hills on every side, and the older people in the villages can still remember the old landscape.

  4. 4. Todtnau forest roads

    Todtnau sits in the Wiese valley in the southern Black Forest, and the forest roads around it are the reason people ride here. At about 650 metres, the town is tucked below the Feldberg, the highest mountain in the whole range, and the lanes that thread up through the spruce on either side belong to a landscape of steep pastures, deep side valleys and old farmsteads with their huge, low-hanging roofs.

  5. 5. St. Blasien dome coffee

    St. Blasien is the kind of town that surprises you from a distance. You come down through spruce forest, round a bend, and there is a green copper dome of tremendous size standing over a small Black Forest town as if someone had mislaid a cathedral from a much bigger city. The dome belongs to the former Benedictine abbey church, and at roughly thirty-six metres across it is one of the largest church domes in Europe north of the Alps.

  6. 6. Koblenz border return

    Koblenz is a small Swiss town on the Rhine, and its job on this ride is simple: it is where you cross back. Across the river lies Waldshut, the German side, and the pair are connected by an old bridge that has seen centuries of toll collectors and, later, customs officers. Today the border is open and relaxed, but the habit remains of stopping here to sort out the practical things before the road home.

  8. 8. Bremgarten old bridge

    Bremgarten is a medieval town on a bend, and the bend is the whole point. The river Reuss, fast, green and cold from the Alps, loops in a tight horseshoe here, and the old town is built inside the loop, on a spur of land that makes it nearly an island. Its medieval lords chose the site for its defensibility and built walls, gates and towers around it. Some of the towers still stand, and steep lanes run from them down to the water.

  9. 9. Staffelegg ridge road

    The Staffelegg is a modest pass, 621 metres, but it deserves more credit than the number suggests. It crosses the folded ridges of the Jura between the Aare valley at Aarau and the Fricktal beyond, and the road that climbs it from Küttigen is one of those quiet Swiss crossings that locals use daily and visitors rarely notice. That is its charm.

  10. 10. Passwang road

    Passwang is a Jura crossing at 943 metres between Balsthal in the Solothurn canton and the Baselland valleys to the north. It is a proper mountain road in miniature, and for many riders it is the highlight of this part of Switzerland, because it packs good bends, long views and quiet forest into a short distance. The road leaves Balsthal through the Gäu and the gorge of the Klus, then climbs through Mümliswil in a series of rising bends toward the saddle.

    Read the complete Passwang road write-up.

  11. 11. Solothurn food stop

    Solothurn has a habit of making visitors ask why they have not come sooner. It sits on the Aare at the foot of the Jura, about fifteen thousand people living in what is probably the best-preserved Baroque town in Switzerland, and for a couple of centuries it was the seat of the French ambassador to the Confederation. That gave the place a refinement out of proportion to its size: grand townhouses, fountains in every square, and a cathedral built in the eighteenth century in pale Ticinese style, dedicated to Saint Ursus.

  12. 12. Hallwilersee water stop

    Hallwilersee is one of the quieter lakes of the Swiss Plateau, a long, narrow stretch of water in the Aargau hills some forty kilometres south-west of Zürich, lying in the Seetal valley at roughly four hundred and fifty metres. It lacks the drama of the lakes near the Alps, but it has a gentle beauty that appeals to those who prefer a slower pace: green meadows, orchards, small harbours and a swimming beach on each shore. In summer, rowing boats and paddle boards dot the surface.
