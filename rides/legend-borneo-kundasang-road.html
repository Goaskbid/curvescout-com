Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kota Kinabalu

    Kota Kinabalu sits on the north-west tip of Borneo, with the South China Sea on one side and the jungle-covered Crocker Range on the other, and on a clear morning the peak of Mount Kinabalu, at 4,095 metres, floats above the clouds to the east. The city, the capital of Sabah, has about four hundred and fifty thousand people, a waterfront lined with seafood restaurants, a lively Filipino market selling dried fish and handicrafts, and a string of islands just offshore that make up the Tunku Abdul Rahman Marine Park. The sunsets over the sea are famously good.

  2. 2. Borneo Kundasang Road point 2

    Tamparuli is where the coastal plain of Sabah ends and the road to Kinabalu begins to climb. It is a small Kadazan-Dusun town on the Tuaran river, best known across Malaysia for its suspension bridge, which inspired a popular song about a lover crossing it in the rain, and the town is sung about more than it is visited. It is a practical stop: shops, fuel, eating houses, and a weekly market, or tamu, where farmers from the hills bring vegetables, fruit, rice wine and live chickens to sell.

  3. 3. Near Ranau

    Kundasang lies on the eastern shoulder of Mount Kinabalu at roughly 1,500 to 1,900 metres, high enough for the air to be cool and fresh, and from the town the mountain looks close enough to touch. The slopes around are covered with vegetable farms, cabbage, carrots, lettuce and tomatoes growing in terraced fields, and the scene, with its cool climate and green hills, has earned the area the nickname of Sabah's little New Zealand, helped by a dairy farm on the hills where Friesian cows graze against a backdrop of granite peaks.

  4. 4. Borneo Kundasang Road point 4

    Kinabalu is not just a mountain but a whole world. The massif is a granite pluton that pushed up through the surrounding rocks relatively recently in geological terms, and it was shaped by glaciers during the ice ages, which carved its summit into a jagged plateau of bare rock with spires and gullies. On its slopes, the climate changes from tropical lowland to cool montane to alpine, and the variety of life is astonishing: thousands of plant species, including hundreds of orchids, pitcher plants of extraordinary size, and rhododendrons, many found nowhere else. The park that protects it was one of Malaysia's first UNESCO World Heritage sites.
