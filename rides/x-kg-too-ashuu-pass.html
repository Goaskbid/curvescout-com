Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bishkek

    Bishkek lies at the foot of the Kyrgyz Ala-Too, the northern range of the Tian Shan, and on a clear morning the snow peaks seem close enough to touch from the city's long boulevards. The capital of Kyrgyzstan was a small fort and trading post in the nineteenth century, and was then rebuilt as a Soviet city named Frunze, after the Bolshevik commander born nearby. The legacy is a grid of broad, straight streets lined with poplars and elms, Soviet apartment blocks, wide squares, and statues, including the one of Manas, the hero of the national epic, who stands in front of the main square, Ala-Too.

  2. 2. Too Ashuu Pass

    Too Ashuu is the point on the main road south from Bishkek where the country changes character. North of the pass, the highway crosses the Chuy plain with its fields, towns and traffic. South of it, you drop into a high, empty world of grass, yurts and sheep, the Suusamyr valley, with the Ala-Too wall rolling on in every direction. The pass lies at about 3,500 metres on the Kyrgyz Ala-Too, and the road across it is the central artery of the north-south link that joins Bishkek to Osh, built in the Soviet period when nobody was counting the cost.

  3. 3. Suusamyr

    Suusamyr is a high, wide, windy valley in the heart of Kyrgyzstan, a long green bowl at more than two thousand metres between the Suusamyr-Too and Kyrgyz ranges, and in summer it is one of the best places in Central Asia to see a pastoral way of life that has hardly changed in centuries. The valley is famous for being cold: snow can fall in any month, and winter temperatures drop far below freezing.
