Why this ride

From Alacón it runs 247 km to Alacón; allow about 4 h 16 of riding. It goes by way of Abrigo del Barranco Hondo, Abric de la Tenalla and back through Abrigo del Barranco Hondo. The highest point is about 1,200 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abrigo de los Borriquitos

    Borriquitos means little donkeys, and whoever named this shelter presumably saw something long-eared and four-legged in the red paint, though Levantine painters drew deer and goats far more often than donkeys. The name is a reminder that these places were named by farmers, long before they were studied by archaeologists, and that what a shepherd sees on a wall may not be what a professor sees. The shelter is near Alacón, in the Martín valley in Teruel, and belongs to the Parque Cultural del Río Martín.

  2. 2. Abrigo del Barranco Hondo

    Castellote is one of those villages in the Teruel Maestrazgo that seems to have been built by people who wanted to be left alone. Its houses pile up beneath a great rock wall topped by the ruins of a castle associated with the Knights Templar, and the Guadalope river winds below on its way into the Santolea reservoir. The Abrigo del Barranco Hondo, a painted rock shelter in a deep ravine outside the village, is part of the extensive collection of Levantine rock art scattered through these mountains, with small figures in red ochre that have survived on the stone for millennia.

  3. 3. Abric de la Tenalla

    The Tinença de Benifassà is one of the wildest corners of the Valencian region, a cluster of tiny villages in the mountains where Valencia, Catalonia and Aragon meet, and the Abric de la Tenalla lies on its southeastern edge, near Rosell. It is one of the many rock shelters in this area with prehistoric paintings, part of the UNESCO-listed rock art of the Mediterranean Basin, and the nearby Serra de Godall and the Ulldecona shelters to the east are among the most important sites in Catalonia. The landscape is dramatic: limestone cliffs, deep gorges, pine and holm oak forests, and abandoned terraces where shepherds once grazed their flocks.
