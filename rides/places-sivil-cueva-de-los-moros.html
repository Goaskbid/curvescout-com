Why this ride

A 547 km round trip from Bárcabo - roughly 8 h 10 in the saddle. The stops in between: Pyrénées - Mont Perdu, Cueva de los moros. and back through Pyrénées - Mont Perdu. It tops out around 1,950 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Pyrenees N260 Route

Sivil to Pyrénées - Mont Perdu: 149 km on Autovía Mudéjar. It climbs 1620 m to the top at 1,550 m and drops 1330 m again. Top up fuel at the bottom - pumps thin out on the way up.

The stops

  1. 1. Sivil

    Start here.

    Sivil is one of those names you will not find on a signpost, and that is rather the point. It belongs to the dry, folded country of the Somontano in northern Aragon, where the last foothills of the Pyrenees run out into the Ebro basin. The labels attached to it speak of a Moorish cave and a tobacco cave, which tells you the real subject: shelters in the limestone where people painted animals and hunters thousands of years ago, part of the Mediterranean rock art that UNESCO lists as a single family of sites.

    Read the complete Sivil write-up.

  2. 2. Pyrénées - Mont Perdu

    149 km in. The ride passes about 6 km from it.

    Monte Perdido, the lost mountain, at 3,355 metres, is the highest limestone massif in Europe and the centrepiece of a cross-border World Heritage site shared by Spain and France. On the Spanish side, it gives its name to the Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park, with deep canyons radiating out from the summit; on the French side, it looks down on the great cirques of Gavarnie and Troumouse.

    Read the complete Pyrénées - Mont Perdu write-up.

  3. 3. Cueva de los moros.

    283 km in.

    There is a Cueva de los Moros in half the valleys of Spain. Every unexplained cave, ruin or rock shelter used to be credited to the Moors, and this one, in the hills of the western Aragonese Pyrenees between the valleys of Hecho and Ansó, is a rock shelter with ancient traces. You should approach it as an excuse to spend time in two of the most beautiful and least spoiled valleys of the Pyrenees, rather than as a monument.

    Read the complete Cueva de los moros. write-up.
