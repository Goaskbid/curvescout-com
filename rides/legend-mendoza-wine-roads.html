Why this ride

curated scenic road. vineyards, Andes views, wine estates. From Mendoza it runs 94 km to Tunuyán; allow about 1 h 43 of riding. It goes by way of Maipú and Luján de Cuyo. The highest point is about 1,650 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mendoza

    Mendoza is a city of trees. Walk or ride any of its wide avenues and you will pass under rows of old plane trees, fed by narrow irrigation channels called acequias, a system that the indigenous Huarpe people started long before the Spanish arrived. The reason is the desert: Mendoza gets very little rain, and every green leaf here exists because snowmelt from the Andes is carried down in channels and spread across the plain. It is a city of nearly nine hundred thousand people in the greater area, set against a wall of mountains that, on a clear winter morning, includes the white shoulder of Aconcagua far to the west.

  2. 2. Maipú

    Maipú is the oldest of Mendoza's wine districts, a flat, tree-lined expanse of vineyards and olive groves just south-east of the city, and it is where many visitors have their first taste of Argentine Malbec. The land lies on the plain beside the Mendoza River, irrigated by a network of channels, called acequias, that were first dug by the indigenous Huarpe people and later expanded by the Spanish. Without them, this would be desert, as the rainfall is tiny, but with them, the plain is green with vines, poplars and fruit trees, and rows of tall poplars stand like windbreaks along the lanes.

  3. 3. Luján de Cuyo

    Luján de Cuyo is the home of Argentine Malbec, and its vineyards climb in terraces up the foothills of the Andes, a little south-west of Mendoza. The district is higher than Maipú, with many of its vineyards at between 900 and 1,100 metres, and the altitude, with its strong sunshine and cool nights, gives the grapes concentrated flavour and a firm structure. Names like Perdriel, Agrelo and Vistalba are known to wine drinkers across the world, and the stony soils of the old riverbed are said to be ideal.

  4. 4. Near Tunuyán

    Tunuyán lies in the Uco Valley, about eighty kilometres south of the city of Mendoza, where the vineyards run in long green lines toward the wall of the Andes. The mountains here are enormous. The snowy mass of the Tupungato volcano, at more than six thousand metres, fills the western horizon, and on a clear morning the peaks glow pink above the vines. The valley sits at about a thousand metres above sea level, and the altitude gives cool nights, strong sunlight and wines of unusual freshness, particularly Malbec and Chardonnay.
