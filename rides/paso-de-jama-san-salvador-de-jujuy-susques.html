Why this ride

From San Salvador de Jujuy it runs 369 km to Susques; allow about 5 h 05 of riding. The highest point is about 4,000 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. San Salvador de Jujuy

    Jujuy, as everyone calls it, sits in a green bowl about 1,200 metres up in the far north-west of Argentina, where the wet forests of the eastern slope meet the dry high country behind. The city was founded in 1593, on the third attempt, after earlier settlements were wiped out or abandoned, and it still has the look of a provincial capital that has had to work for its place. About three hundred thousand people live here, with the river Xibi Xibi running through the middle of town and the hills of the Yungas closing in on three sides. It is the usual start for the long climb into the Andes.

  2. 2. Purmamarca

    Purmamarca is the village most people photograph first in the Quebrada de Humahuaca, and the reason is the hill behind it. The Cerro de los Siete Colores rises straight from the edge of the settlement in bands of pink, rust, ochre, green and grey, and the colours change hourly: pale and chalky at noon, deep and almost wet-looking when the low sun rakes across them in the late afternoon. It is a small adobe village, about two thousand three hundred metres up in Jujuy province, in the far north-west of Argentina.

  3. 3. Susques

    Susques is a small high-altitude town on the road to Chile, perched on the Argentine puna at about 3,600 metres, where the air is thin, the sky is deep blue and the horizon is made of bare rust-coloured hills. The Ruta 52 climbs here from Jujuy through the Quebrada de Purmamarca, over the Cuesta de Lipán, one of the highest and most dramatic mountain roads in Argentina, then crosses the great white Salinas Grandes, the salt flats, before arriving in Susques. The town marks the beginning of the real altiplano.
