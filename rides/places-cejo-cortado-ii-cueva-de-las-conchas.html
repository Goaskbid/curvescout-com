Why this ride

From Mula it runs 89 km to Mula; allow about 1 h 32 of riding. The stops in between: Abrigo del milano, Cueva de las Conchas and back through Abrigo del milano. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cejo Cortado II

    Mula does not announce itself to motorcyclists, which is a pity, because the roads around it are among the most enjoyable in inland Murcia. The town of nearly seventeen thousand sits on the river that bears its name, below the long grey wall of the Sierra Espuña to the south, and its old quarter climbs a hill capped by a castle. Cejo Cortado, seven kilometres from the centre, is a small crag in dry hill country where a shelter holds prehistoric paintings, the kind of place you find by following a farm track and reading the contours.

  2. 2. Abrigo del milano

    Bullas is wine country, and the vineyards of Monastrell grapes roll over the hills around the town in the northwest of Murcia, producing robust red wines under their own denomination of origin. The Abrigo del Milano, the kite's shelter, named after the bird of prey that circles over these hills, lies in the countryside between Bullas, Mula and Pliego. It is one of the many rock shelters in Murcia with prehistoric paintings, part of the UNESCO listing of Mediterranean rock art, though access to many shelters is restricted to protect them, so ask locally before trying to visit.

  3. 3. Cueva de las Conchas

    Cehegín's old town rises in a tight cluster of ochre houses and church towers on a hill above the Argos river, in the northwest of Murcia, and it is one of the best-preserved historic centres in the region, with narrow streets, noble houses and churches dating back to the sixteenth century. The Cueva de las Conchas, the cave of the shells, lies less than a kilometre from the town, and it is part of the rich network of prehistoric sites in this area, included in the UNESCO listing of Mediterranean rock art.
