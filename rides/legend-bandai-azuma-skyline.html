Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fukushima

    Fukushima is a city of fruit. Its basin, ringed by mountains, produces peaches, pears, apples and grapes, and in spring the slopes are pink and white with blossom. It is the start and the end of the Bandai-Azuma Skyline ride, and the first view of the mountains is a promise.

  3. 3. Bandai-Azuma Skyline point 3

    The high ground of the Bandai-Azuma Skyline is a lunar landscape, and you reach it after the forest drops away and the road emerges onto bare, gravelly slopes of volcanic rock. Here, at around 1,600 metres, the Azuma volcanic group stands around you, with craters, steaming vents and scoured ridges, and the famous Mount Issaikyo, which still releases gas from its crater. On a clear day, the views stretch across the whole range and down to the plains. This is the highest and most exposed part of the road, with wind that can be fierce.

  4. 4. Bandai-Azuma Skyline point 4

    At the southern end of the Skyline, the road leaves the barren high ground and comes down into a gentler landscape of forest, lakes and wide views toward Mount Bandai. This is the part of Fukushima prefecture that surrounds Lake Inawashiro, the fourth-largest lake in Japan, and the land is dotted with hot springs, ski resorts and small farms. Mount Bandai itself is a famous volcano, and its great collapse in 1888 buried villages and dammed rivers, forming the lakes and ponds of the Urabandai area, which now look like a scatter of coloured jewels in the forest.
