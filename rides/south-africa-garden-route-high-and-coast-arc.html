Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Outeniqua Pass

    The Outeniqua Pass is the quick way from the coast to the Karoo, and a motorcyclist's first lesson that South Africa's mountains come in a hurry. On the coast, George sits among green hills with the Indian Ocean on the horizon. Twenty minutes inland, the N12 climbs the face of the Outeniqua range in a series of well-engineered bends, crests at roughly eight hundred metres, and drops into the dry, rocky country of the Little Karoo toward Oudtshoorn. The pass was opened in the early 1950s, replacing the older Montagu Pass, which still exists as a gravel road a short distance away.

  2. 2. Wilderness beach

    Wilderness beach is a wide, pale curve of sand that runs east from the mouth of the Touw River toward Sedgefield, backed by dunes, lakes and wooded hills, and on a calm morning it is among the loveliest places on the Garden Route. The Indian Ocean rolls in with long, slow breakers, and the horizon is clean and blue, with the dark green flank of the Outeniqua mountains behind. The beach stretches for many kilometres, so you can walk for as long as you like and meet nobody but a few dog walkers and the odd fisherman casting a line from the shore.

  3. 3. Knysna lagoon

    Knysna sits on the edge of a lagoon, a broad, sheltered stretch of water that almost seems like a lake until you notice the tide running in and out through a narrow gap in the sandstone cliffs. These are the Knysna Heads, two great cliffs that guard the entrance, and the passage between them is one of the most dangerous harbour mouths in South Africa, with many ships lost over the years. A viewpoint on the eastern Head offers a spectacular look at the channel and the surf breaking on the rocks. The lagoon itself is a protected estuary, home to the rare Knysna seahorse, found nowhere else, and a variety of fish and birds.
