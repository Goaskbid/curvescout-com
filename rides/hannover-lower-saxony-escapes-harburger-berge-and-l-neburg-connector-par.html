Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hannover

    Hannover does not seek attention, which is part of its character. The capital of Lower Saxony, with about half a million people, is a flat, green, practical city on the Leine river, famous for its trade fairs, and for speaking what Germans consider the purest standard German. Large parts of the centre were destroyed in the Second World War, and the rebuilding is mostly plain, but there are fine things inside it: the Herrenhausen Gardens, a baroque garden of clipped hedges and fountains, the Maschsee, a big lake laid out in the 1930s where locals sail and jog, and the new town hall, a copper-domed building from 1913 with a lift that rises inside the dome.

    Read the complete Hannover write-up.

  2. 2. Warmsen

    If you pass through in October or November, listen. The moors of the Diepholz lowland, a short way north-west of here, are one of the great resting places for migrating cranes in Germany, and on autumn evenings thousands of them come in to roost with a sound like distant bugles. Warmsen sits on the southern fringe of that country, a farming village between Uchte and the Weser, in a landscape so flat that a church spire counts as a landmark.

    Read the complete Warmsen write-up.

  3. 3. Berge

    North of Osnabrück the land rises into a low, wooded ridge, the Ankumer Höhe, a moraine left by the glaciers of the penultimate ice age, and around it the countryside rolls gently with fields, hedges and old farmsteads. Berge is a village on the western side of that ridge, between Bippen and Fürstenau, in a district that Stone Age farmers settled thousands of years ago.

    Read the complete Berge write-up.

  4. 4. Morsum

    The Weser makes its last big bends here before Bremen, and Morsum sits just south of the river near Thedinghausen, in a landscape of dykes, meadows and old marsh villages. This is not the Morsum on Sylt that most Germans know but a small farming place in the flat country between Verden and Bremen, where the river shapes everything.

    Read the complete Morsum write-up.

  5. 5. Lüneburg

    Salt made Lüneburg, and the town looks as though it knows it. For more than a thousand years the brine springs under the old town were boiled down into white gold, which was carted to Lübeck and shipped across the Baltic to preserve herring, and the profits built a city of tall brick gables, grand merchant houses and a town hall that grew by accretion across six centuries.
