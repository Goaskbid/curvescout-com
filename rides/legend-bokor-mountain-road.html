Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kampot

    Kampot is a lazy river town on the southern coast of Cambodia, and it wears its faded French colonial architecture with a sort of gentle indifference. Old shophouses with peeling yellow paint and wooden shutters line the riverfront, and the wide Preaek Tuek Chhu river flows slowly past, with fishing boats heading out to sea in the evening. Across the river, the bulk of Bokor Mountain rises over the town, often wrapped in cloud. It is a place where people come for a few days and stay for weeks.

  2. 2. Near Kampot

    At the foot of Bokor, west of Kampot, the land is flat and green, and then suddenly it isn't. The mountain rises abruptly from the coastal plain, a long ridge of sandstone covered in dense forest, and the road up begins with almost no warning, leaving the rice paddies and palm trees behind and starting to climb. Here is the entrance to Preah Monivong Bokor National Park, and the beginning of one of the best mountain roads in Cambodia.

  3. 3. Bokor Mountain Road point 3

    On top of Bokor the forest opens onto a wide, windswept plateau of scrub, rock and boggy grassland, and out of the mist appear the ghosts of a French hill station. The old Bokor Palace Hotel, opened in the 1920s, stands empty and blackened, its grand rooms open to the wind, and nearby a small Catholic church, also abandoned, sits on a rocky outcrop looking out over the sea. In the fog, which comes in fast and often, these buildings look like the set of a horror film, and indeed they have been used as one.
