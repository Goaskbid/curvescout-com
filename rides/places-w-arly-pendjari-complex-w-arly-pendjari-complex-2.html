Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. W-Arly-Pendjari Complex

    This is the great wildlife country of West Africa, where three nations meet at a bend of the Niger river. The W park takes its name from the shape of the river's loops, and together with the Arly reserve in Burkina Faso and the Pendjari in Benin it forms the largest protected savannah area in the region. Elephants, buffalo, hippos and several kinds of antelope live here, and so do lions and a small, precious population of cheetahs. In the dry months from January to April animals gather at the remaining waterholes, which is when watching is best.

  2. 2. W-Arly-Pendjari Complex

    West Africa's last great savanna wilderness sits where Niger, Benin and Burkina Faso meet. The W-Arly-Pendjari complex, named after the three parks that make it up, covers more than ten thousand square kilometres of grassland, riverine forest and rocky ridges, and holds the largest population of elephants in West Africa, along with lions, cheetahs, hippos and buffalo. The W takes its name from the double bend of the Niger river that traces the shape of that letter, and the whole complex was listed by UNESCO in 1996 and extended later.

  3. 3. W-Arly-Pendjari Complex

    West Africa's last great savanna wilderness lies where three countries meet, and it is a place with a fierce beauty and a difficult present. The W–Arly–Pendjari complex spans Benin, Burkina Faso and Niger, and its name comes from the shape of the Niger river as it bends in a W through the W National Park. It is the biggest block of protected savanna in West Africa and holds the region's largest population of elephants, as well as lions, cheetahs, hippos, buffalo and many antelope. UNESCO listed it in 2017 as a transboundary site.
