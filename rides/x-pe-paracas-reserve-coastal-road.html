The roads

Peru Coastal Panamericana South

It is 15 km from Paracas to Paracas Reserve, mostly on IC-602. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 3 and 37 m. Water alongside for much of it - pick one good pull-out and stop.

The stops

  1. 1. Paracas

    Start here.

    The Paracas peninsula juts into the Pacific from the Peruvian coast, a dry, red-and-ochre tongue of sand and rock where no rain falls for years, and it is as strange and beautiful a place as any on the Panamericana. The Paracas National Reserve covers a huge area of this coast and sea, with dramatic cliffs, empty beaches, and a Red Beach whose sand takes its colour from volcanic rock.

    Read the complete Paracas write-up.

  2. 2. Paracas Reserve

    15 km in.

    The Paracas National Reserve is a stretch of desert coastline on the Pacific shore of Peru, where the dry land meets the cold, rich waters of the Humboldt Current, and the contrast between the two is startling. Pale sand dunes, red cliffs and rocky headlands drop to a sea that is full of fish, and the air is thick with seabirds. The name Paracas comes from the Quechua for sand rain, a reference to the strong winds that sweep across the desert in the afternoon, and anyone who rides here will understand why.
