  1. 1. Berlin

    The tower, finished in 1969 and 368 metres high, is the tallest structure in Germany and can be seen from nearly everywhere in the city. It was built by the East German government as a showpiece of socialist engineering, and when the sun hits its steel sphere, it throws a cross of light that Berliners cheekily called the Pope's revenge.

  2. 2. Rheinsberg

    In 1736, Crown Prince Frederick of Prussia, the future Frederick the Great, moved into the palace at Rheinsberg with his wife, and he later said the years he spent here were the happiest of his life. Away from the stern court of his father, he read philosophy, played the flute, corresponded with Voltaire and surrounded himself with friends. The palace, rebuilt for him in an elegant Rococo style on the shore of the Grienericksee, is still the jewel of this small town, and the park around it, with its avenues, sculptures and views across the water, is perfect for a slow walk.
