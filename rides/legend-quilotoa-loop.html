Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Latacunga

    Latacunga lies at about 2,750 metres in the high Andean valley that the explorer Alexander von Humboldt called the Avenue of the Volcanoes, and the name is hardly an exaggeration. On a clear morning the snowy cone of Cotopaxi, nearly 5,900 metres high, hangs above the plain to the east, with Iliniza and other peaks to the west. The town has a slightly worn, dignified look, with many buildings made from pale volcanic stone and plazas shaded by old trees.

  2. 2. Quilotoa Loop point 2

    Out in the high country west of Latacunga the farms get smaller, the fields steeper, and the road begins to climb towards the great barren plateau of the western cordillera. This section runs through open paramo and patchwork fields, with the horizon scored by deep valleys. The towns of Pujili and Saquisili lie behind, a day's walk if you had to, but at the pace of a bike they are far enough that fuel is a real concern. Check the tank, because little is on offer here.

  3. 3. Quilotoa Loop point 3

    Quilotoa is the reason people come. The crater of an extinct volcano, about three kilometres across, holds a lake of astonishing colour, a turquoise to emerald green that shifts with the light and the minerals dissolved in it. The rim sits at about 3,900 metres, and the drop to the water is some three hundred metres, with a steep path that zigzags down for those who want to touch it. The climb back up is far harder than the descent, so take it slowly, or hire a mule for the way up.

  4. 4. Quilotoa Loop point 4

    Chugchilan is a small village on the edge of a canyon, the sort of place where a day's ride ends with soup and a fire. The valley of the Toachi River drops away below the village in green folds, and the farmland on the far side is cut into a patchwork of squares, with the occasional white house and a thread of smoke. At about 3,200 metres the village is cold in the evening, and the sky clears after dusk to reveal a thick scatter of stars.

  5. 5. Quilotoa Loop point 5

    Sigchos is a modest market town set on a green ridge, and arriving feels like returning to the world of roofs, shops and things that look like a town. The road here drops from the high paramo into a warmer, softer valley, where sugarcane and fruit trees start to appear among the maize. The altitude is lower than at Quilotoa, so it is noticeably warmer and the air feels thicker, a relief after the rim.
