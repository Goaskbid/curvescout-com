Why this ride

A one-way ride of 160 km, Joshua Tree to the finish - roughly 2 h 11 in the saddle. The stops in between: Hidden Valley, Keys View and Cholla Cactus Garden. The highest point is about 1,100 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Joshua Tree Village

    Joshua Tree Village is the little desert town at the western gate of Joshua Tree National Park, a place of low buildings, rock shops, cafes, vintage stores and art galleries, where the desert light seems to have drawn a good many musicians, painters and hikers. It sits at about twenty-seven hundred feet in the high Mojave, where the air is dry and clear, the nights are cold, and the horizon is a long line of boulders and yucca. It has a relaxed, slightly eccentric feel.

  2. 2. Hidden Valley

    Hidden Valley is a small, boulder-ringed basin in Joshua Tree National Park, and it looks, appropriately enough, like a place a person might hide something. Piles of pale monzogranite, rounded by weathering into stacked blocks and domes, form a natural wall around a flat of grass and desert shrubs, with a one-mile loop trail running through it. The local legend says that cattle rustlers used the valley to hide stolen herds in the late nineteenth century, behind rock walls that made a natural corral, and that a rancher named Bill Keys later improved the site for his own cattle. The story is well worn, but the setting makes it easy to believe.

  3. 3. Keys View

    Keys View sits on the crest of the Little San Bernardino Mountains at about 1,580 metres, and the view from it is the longest in Joshua Tree National Park. Below you the land falls away sharply into the Coachella Valley, a broad, flat basin of date palms, golf courses and spreading towns, with Palm Springs on the near side and the Salton Sea glinting far off to the south on a clear day. Beyond lie the high peaks of the San Jacinto and San Gorgonio mountains, snow-capped for much of the winter.

  4. 4. Cholla Cactus Garden

    The Cholla Cactus Garden is the sort of place that makes you slow down on instinct. Over a flat expanse of the Pinto Basin, thousands of teddy bear cholla stand in dense, silvery stands, each one a spiny cluster of jointed stems that glows with a golden halo when the light hits it from behind. They look soft, which is the trap. The spines are barbed, the joints detach at a touch, and anyone who has met one will tell you it does not feel cuddly. A short loop trail winds through the thickest part, and you should stay on it.

  5. 5. Cottonwood Spring

    Cottonwood Spring is an oasis in the lower corner of Joshua Tree National Park, where the high Mojave desert gives way to the hotter Colorado Desert, and a small cluster of fan palms and cottonwood trees grows around a spring that has been used for centuries. The Cahuilla people gathered here, grinding seeds and mesquite beans in rock mortars, and the holes can still be seen in the boulders. Later, gold miners camped by the water, and ruins of their mills survive in the hills.
