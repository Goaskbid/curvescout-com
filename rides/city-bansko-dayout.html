Why this ride

A 109 km round trip from Bansko - roughly 1 h 53 in the saddle. It goes by way of \Pchelino Convent\ Complex, Rila Monastery, Rila Monastery Complex and \St. Luke Hermitage\ Complex. The highest point is about 1,700 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bansko

    Bansko lies at the foot of the Pirin Mountains in south-west Bulgaria, at the northern end of the Razlog valley, and the first sight of it is the wall behind: Pirin's jagged limestone peaks, climbing to Vihren at 2,914 metres, the second-highest mountain in the country after Musala. The range is a national park and a UNESCO site, and its high lakes, dwarf pine and rare Bosnian pine hold some of the most unspoiled alpine country in the Balkans. Winter brings snow from December well into spring, and Bansko is one of the busiest ski resorts in Bulgaria, with a gondola that runs from the edge of town.

  2. 2. \Pchelino Convent\ Complex

    The mountains of southwestern Bulgaria are among the wildest in the Balkans, and the area between Rila and Pirin holds a string of small monasteries tucked into forested valleys. During centuries of Ottoman rule these monasteries kept the Bulgarian language, the Orthodox faith and the art of icon painting alive, and the great Rila Monastery, founded in the tenth century around the hermitage of Saint Ivan of Rila, became the national symbol.

  3. 3. Rila Monastery

    The road to Rila Monastery climbs a long valley through forests of beech and spruce, and when the monastery appears the effect is theatrical. Its walls enclose a courtyard of striped arcades in black, white and red, with a church in the middle covered in frescoes, and behind it the green flanks of the Rila mountains rise towards peaks of more than two and a half thousand metres. It is the largest and most important monastery in Bulgaria, and UNESCO listed it in 1983.

  4. 4. Rila Monastery Complex

    Rila Monastery sits at around eleven hundred metres in a forested valley of the Rila mountains, the highest range in the Balkans, where Musala at nearly three thousand metres stands above all the peaks of south-eastern Europe. The monastery lies at the end of a long road that follows the Rilska river upstream through beech and spruce, and the approach feels like going back in time. Mist hangs in the trees, the air smells of resin, and the river noise grows louder as you climb.

  5. 5. \St. Luke Hermitage\ Complex

    Above Rila Monastery, in the folds of the Rila Mountains, a path leads to the hermit's cave where it all began. The monastery was founded in the tenth century by Saint Ivan of Rila, a monk who left the world to live as a recluse in these forests, and he gathered followers who built a community.
