Why this ride

571 km from Manāli to Leh, about 7 h 53 of riding. The stops in between: Atal Tunnel north portal, Baralacha La, Sarchu and Lachulung La. The highest point is about 5,350 m. Baralacha La, Lachulung La and Tanglang La are open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Leh–Manali Highway

For 45 km this is Leh–Manali Highway, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it — expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast.

Leh–Manali Highway

First and second gear more often than not. Look through each corner to the next one and let the engine do the holding. The valley floor is behind you and the road starts working for its height at about 16.8%. Corners stack above one another where the ground allows nothing straighter.

Leh–Manali Highway

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 5328 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance — cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Manali

    Manali is where the plains give up and the Himalaya begins in earnest. The town sits in the Kullu valley in Himachal Pradesh, on the banks of the Beas, among deodar forests and apple orchards, with snow peaks rising behind. Old Manali, across the river, is the quieter half, a tangle of lanes, wooden houses and cafes, and in the cedar woods above the town stands the Hadimba temple, a pagoda-like wooden shrine built in the sixteenth century and dedicated to a figure from the Mahabharata. The valley has long been a stopping place for traders, travellers and, more recently, backpackers and motorcyclists.

  2. 2. Atal Tunnel north portal

    The Atal Tunnel changed the Himalayan road map. For generations, the road north from Manali climbed over the Rohtang Pass, which was open for only part of the year, snowed in from late autumn to early summer. In 2020 a tunnel of about nine kilometres was opened under the pass, one of the longest highway tunnels in the world at such a height, and named after the former prime minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee. The north portal, where you emerge into Lahaul, is one of the great moments on the road to Ladakh.

  3. 3. Baralacha La

    Baralacha La is a pass that feels like the roof of the world, and at 4,890 metres, it nearly is. The name means the pass with crossroads on the summit, because it sits where roads from Lahaul, Zanskar and Ladakh once met. The rivers tell the story: the Chandra rises near here on the south side, and the Yunam flows north, and a few kilometres below the pass lies the clear, round lake of Suraj Tal, one of the highest lakes in India, from which the Bhaga River begins.

  4. 4. Sarchu

    Sarchu is not a town, and calling it a stop barely does it justice. It is a high, bare plateau at about 4,300 metres, on the boundary between Himachal Pradesh and Ladakh, where the Leh-Manali Highway crosses a wide, windy basin beside the Tsarap river. In the summer season, a collection of tents and camps appear on the plain, serving riders, truck drivers and travellers who need a place to sleep halfway between Manali and Leh, and in winter everything is gone and the snow takes over.

  5. 5. Lachulung La

    Lachulung La is the second great pass on the road between Manali and Leh, a bare, windswept saddle at 5,059 metres, with prayer flags snapping in the wind and the road clawing its way over a ridge of crumbling rock. It lies north of Sarchu and the Gata Loops, in the high desert plateau of southern Ladakh, where the air is thin and the land is a bare, tawny colour streaked with red and green minerals. There are no trees and almost no plants, only rock, snow and sky.

  6. 6. Tanglang La

    Tanglang La is the second highest of the great passes on the road between Manali and Leh, at about 5,300 metres, and it is a place where the air is so thin that walking up a few steps feels like climbing a stair. The pass lies on the watershed between the Indus valley and the high plateau to the south, and the landscape around it is stark and vast: bare ridges of brown and grey, snowfields in the shadows, and a sky that looks very dark and very close.

  7. 7. Leh

    Leh sits in the high desert of Ladakh at about 3,500 metres, in the broad valley of the Indus, with the Karakoram on one side and the Zanskar range on the other, and it is the end of the Manali-Leh highway, one of the great road journeys in the world. The last stage of that road is the best: you cross the Tanglang La, among the highest passes in the world, then drop to the Indus plain, through villages with whitewashed chortens and prayer flags, and the town appears below a hill crowned by the old Leh Palace, a nine-storey building of mud and wood modelled on the Potala in Lhasa. Behind it, the Namgyal Tsemo monastery and a ruined fort hang on the ridge.
