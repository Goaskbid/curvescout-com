Why this ride

Out of Lake City and back again: 55 km, roughly 53 min in the saddle. On the way it passes Engineer Pass, Animas Forks and Cinnamon Pass. The highest point is about 3,900 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Alpine Loop Colorado

Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it. The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 3901 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Lake City Colorado

    Lake City is the sort of village that never quite got the memo about the twenty-first century. Tucked into a high valley of the San Juan Mountains at around 8,600 feet, it has about 370 residents, a short main street of wooden false fronts, a handful of cafes and a county courthouse that serves one of the emptiest counties in Colorado. It was a silver-mining town in the 1870s, and the mines lasted just long enough to leave behind a pretty centre and a ring of ghost towns. The nearest bigger place is Gunnison, and the closest for fuel is in the village itself, so check your tank before heading into the backcountry.

  2. 2. Engineer Pass

    Engineer Pass is a gravel road, which matters, because up here the word gravel means rock, ruts, shale, creek crossings and exposure. At nearly 3,900 metres it is the highest point on the Alpine Loop, the long dirt circuit that links Ouray, Silverton and Lake City through the heart of the San Juan Mountains, and it is a proper high-country dual-sport ride. This is no place for a heavy tourer or an inexperienced rider on a road bike. A light adventure bike with good tyres, a competent rider and a sensible start time make it a different story.

  3. 3. Animas Forks

    Animas Forks is a ghost town that looks as though the residents stepped out an hour ago and forgot to come back. At about 3,400 metres it sits in a high basin where several creeks meet to form the upper Animas River, ringed by steep slopes, and a scatter of weathered wooden buildings still stands among the grass and wildflowers. The Duncan House, with its bay window and painted trim, is the most photographed, and the old jail and the remains of the Gold Prince mill can be seen nearby.

  4. 4. Cinnamon Pass

    Cinnamon Pass is the friendlier of the two high passes on the Alpine Loop, though friendly is relative at 3,852 metres on a dirt road. It links Lake City with Animas Forks over the shoulder of the San Juans, and its name is supposedly from the cinnamon-coloured rock of the slopes, which glow rust and orange in the right light. The summit is a broad, open saddle with a marker, a wide view of ragged peaks, and usually a few jeeps, a few motorcyclists and a few marmots arguing over the best rock.
