  1. 1. Ciudad Valles

    Ciudad Valles is the commercial capital of the Huasteca Potosina, the lush, tropical eastern part of San Luis Potosí where the dry highlands of central Mexico fall toward the Gulf coast. It is a hot, busy city of over a hundred thousand people on the Río Valles, with sugar cane, citrus and cattle country around it, and a marked change in climate and culture from the desert plateau a few hours west. The air is humid and the vegetation thick, with palms and banana plants.

  2. 2. Xilitla

    Concrete flowers, stairways leading nowhere and gothic arches dripping with ferns: Las Pozas, just outside Xilitla, is one of the strangest places in Mexico. It was built by Edward James, an eccentric English poet and patron of the surrealists, who came to this jungle-covered mountainside in the 1940s, fell in love with it and spent decades building a dreamlike garden of concrete sculptures among the waterfalls and pools of a mountain stream. Today it is open to visitors, and wandering through it, half lost in the vegetation, is unforgettable.

  3. 3. Aquismón

    At dawn above Aquismón, thousands of birds spiral up out of a vast hole in the ground. The Sótano de las Golondrinas, the Cave of Swallows, is a pit more than three hundred metres deep, one of the deepest open shafts in the world, and every morning swarms of swifts and parakeets rise from it in widening circles before flying off to feed. In the evening they return, diving straight down into the darkness like falling stones. It is one of the great natural spectacles of Mexico, and it happens just a short ride from this small Huasteca town.
