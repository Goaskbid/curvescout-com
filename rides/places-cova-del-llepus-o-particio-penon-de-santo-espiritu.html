Cova del Llepus o Partició and Peñón de Santo Espíritu

Why this ride

A 397 km round trip from Morella - roughly 6 h 51 in the saddle. On the way it passes Racó d'en Gil, Barranco de Gibert II, Castell de Villafamés and Peñón de Santo Espíritu. The highest point is about 1,400 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cova del Llepus o Partició

    Morella is the sort of town that makes riders stop in the middle of a long day just to look. It sits at about a thousand metres in the high Els Ports country of northern Castellón, ringed by more than two kilometres of medieval walls, with a castle on a crag above and a Gothic basilica below, and the whole mass of golden stone looks as if it grew out of the rock. The Cova del Llepus, about four kilometres away, is one of the painted shelters of the district, a Levantine site protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain. The ride links it with another cave, the Escoda, and with a hill the maps call the Peñón de Santo Espíritu.

  2. 2. Racó d'en Gil

    There is a small triangle of country in the Alt Maestrat, between Benassal, Catí and Albocàsser, where the hills fold into narrow ravines and the stone walls of old terraces climb every slope. Racó d'en Gil, a name meaning something like Gil's corner, lies in this landscape, and it sits within easy reach of the Valltorta gorge, the place that put Levantine rock art on the map.

  3. 3. Barranco de Gibert II

    Up on the roof of the Maestrazgo, where Teruel and Castellón meet, the land rises above fourteen hundred metres into a bleak, beautiful plateau of pasture, pine and limestone. Mosqueruela, on the Aragonese side, is one of the highest villages in the area, and Vilafranca, on the Valencian side, sits in a valley below. The Barranco de Gibert cuts into the hills between them, close also to La Iglesuela del Cid, and its second shelter holds prehistoric paintings.

  4. 4. Castell de Villafamés

    Villafamés announces itself from a long way off: a village of white and ochre houses stacked up a hill of reddish rock, with the remains of a castle crowning the top. It stands a little inland from the coast of Castellón, roughly between Borriol, Cabanes and the beach town of Benicàssim, close enough to the sea that you can smell salt on a westerly breeze turning, yet already in that rumpled hill country that leads up into the Maestrat.

  5. 5. Peñón de Santo Espíritu

    Behind the orange groves of the Camp de Morvedre, a few kilometres inland from Sagunto, the land rises into the Sierra Calderona, a low range of pine woods, scrub and red sandstone that separates the coastal plain from the dry interior. The Peñón de Santo Espíritu is a rocky outcrop in these hills above the village of Gilet, and it carries prehistoric rock art in the Levantine tradition, the style of painted hunters, animals and human figures found in shelters along the Mediterranean side of Spain and listed by UNESCO as the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula. The paintings are fragile and often protected, so do not expect to wander up and touch anything.
