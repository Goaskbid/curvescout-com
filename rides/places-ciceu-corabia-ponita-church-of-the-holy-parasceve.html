Why this ride

A 172 km round trip from Chiuiești - roughly 2 h 58 in the saddle. It goes by way of Sita - Vârful Sitii, Groșii Țibleșului - Preluci, Church of the Holy Parasceve and back through Groșii Țibleșului - Preluci. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ciceu - Corabia-Ponița

    Ciceu is a name that carries more history than its size suggests. The ruins of Ciceu Castle stand on a high hill in the north of Transylvania, in the border country of Bistrița-Năsăud and Cluj counties, in the hill country between Dej and Bistrița. In the fifteenth century the fortress and its estate were granted by the Hungarian crown to Stephen the Great, the ruler of Moldavia, along with the castle of Cetatea de Baltă, a rare case of a prince from beyond the Carpathians holding lands inside Transylvania. Little remains of the castle today, though the walls on the hilltop still give an idea of its position.

  2. 2. Sita - Vârful Sitii

    On a hilltop near the village of Spermezeu, in the Someș hills of Bistrița-Năsăud county in northern Romania, a small watchtower once stood guard over the northern edge of the Roman Empire. When the emperor Trajan conquered Dacia in the early second century, the Romans built a frontier around their new province, the Dacian limes, a chain of forts, watchtowers and earthworks running along the Carpathians and through the hills. Vârful Sitii, the summit of Sita, was one of the observation points, chosen because from here soldiers could watch the valleys to the north and send signals to the forts behind them.

  3. 3. Groșii Țibleșului - Preluci

    The Țibleș massif rises like a dark green wall between Maramureș and the Bistrița country of northern Romania, an old volcanic range topped by the Țibleș peak at around eighteen hundred metres. On its southern flanks, above the village of Groșii Țibleșului, lie the forests of Preluci, part of the Ancient and Primeval Beech Forests of the Carpathians and Other Regions of Europe that UNESCO has protected as one sprawling, multi-country site. These are woods left largely to themselves, where old beeches grow huge, fall, rot and feed the next generation without a forester in sight.

  4. 4. Church of the Holy Parasceve

    Maramureș is one of the few corners of Europe where wood is still the language of the sacred, and the church of Saint Parascheva at Poienile Izei is a good place to learn it. It stands on a small rise above the Izei valley, a dark timber church with a steep, double-tiered shingled roof and a slender spire, built in the early seventeenth century and moved to its present spot later. Inside, the walls and ceiling carry painted scenes from the eighteenth century, and the one that always gets quoted is a vivid Last Judgment in which sinners get remarkably specific punishments.
