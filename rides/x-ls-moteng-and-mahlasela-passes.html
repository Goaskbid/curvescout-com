Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Butha-Buthe

    Butha-Buthe means place of lying down, and the name comes from the story of the Basotho people retreating to the flat-topped mountain above the town in the 1820s, when war and famine were tearing through the region, before their leader Moshoeshoe moved on to found his stronghold at Thaba Bosiu. Today it is a lively northern Lesotho market town of a few thousand people in its centre, with a few more in the surrounding villages, set in a wide valley under steep brown mountains. It is the first real stop after the border crossing from South Africa, a short distance away.

  2. 2. Moteng Pass

    Northern Lesotho is a country of high, treeless ridges and deep valleys, and the Moteng Pass is a good place to meet it. The road climbs from the lowlands near the South African border into the Maloti mountains, where villages of stone and thatch cling to the slopes and every hillside seems to be terraced with strips of maize and wheat. Phuthaditjhaba, a large town on the South African side about thirty kilometres away, and Butha-Buthe, the nearest Lesotho town of any size, are the points that anchor the region on a map.

  3. 3. Mahlasela Pass

    Few landscapes are as bare as these. In the highlands of northern Lesotho, there are almost no trees, because centuries of grazing and the cold climate have left the hills covered in grass, with rocks, streams and gullies cut into the hillsides, and the Mahlasela Pass climbs over this bare country in a series of long curves. The view from the top reaches across layer after layer of ridge, each paler than the last, until the horizon fades into haze.
