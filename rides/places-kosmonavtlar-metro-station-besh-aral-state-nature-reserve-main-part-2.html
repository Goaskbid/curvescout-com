Why this ride

A 658 km round trip from Tashkent - roughly 14 h 48 in the saddle. It goes by way of The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve, Besh-Aral State Nature Reserve - main part and back through The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve. It tops out around 2,700 m. It crosses from Uzbekistan into Kyrgyzstan - carry ID and check the toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Kosmonavtlar Metro Station

    Start here.

    On the Tashkent metro, the stations are the sights. The system opened in 1977, the first in Central Asia, and the Soviet planners treated each stop as a small palace, with a theme, a colour scheme and local craftsmen to carry it out. Marble, carved alabaster, mosaics and chandeliers decorate the platforms, and the Kosmonavtlar station, the Cosmonauts, is one of the best, with a space theme: blue-grey tiles, circular lamps like planets and portraits of cosmonauts, a tribute to the space programme of which Uzbekistan was proud. For years, photography underground was banned, and the rule has now relaxed, so you can look around with a camera if you are polite.

    Read the complete Kosmonavtlar Metro Station write-up.

  2. 2. The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve

    114 km in. The ride passes about 8 km from it.

    East of Tashkent, the land rises steeply into the western ranges of the Tian Shan, and the Chatkal range forms the border with Kyrgyzstan. The Chatkal State Biosphere Reserve, established in the Soviet era, protects a stretch of these mountains, with juniper forests, walnut and wild fruit trees, alpine meadows and rocky peaks.

    Read the complete The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve write-up.

  3. 3. Besh-Aral State Nature Reserve - main part

    328 km in. The ride passes about 19 km from it.

    Far up the Chatkal valley in western Kyrgyzstan, where the river runs between the Chatkal and Sandalash ranges of the Western Tien Shan, lies the Besh-Aral State Nature Reserve. It protects mountain steppe, juniper woodland, walnut and apple groves and rocky high country, and it is part of the transnational World Heritage Site of the Western Tien Shan, inscribed in 2016 and shared with Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan. The reserve was created in particular to protect Menzbier's marmot, a small marmot found only in this part of Central Asia.
