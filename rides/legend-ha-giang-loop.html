Why this ride

Northern Vietnam, the Mã Pí Lèng pass above the Nho Quế gorge. From Hà Giang it runs 147 km to Hà Giang; allow about 2 h 40 of riding. It goes by way of Thị Trấn Tam Sơn, Thị Trấn Đồng Văn, Thị Trấn Mèo Vạc and Lũng Hồ. The highest point is about 1,500 m. The line favours views over speed.

Scenic road chapters

Ha Giang Loop

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 6 km this is Ha Giang Loop, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hà Giang

    Ha Giang is where Vietnam runs out of country. The city lies on the Lô River in the far north, just a short ride from the Chinese border, and it is the gateway to one of the most dramatic road circuits in Southeast Asia. Beyond the last streets, the road lifts into the Dong Van Karst Plateau, a UNESCO Global Geopark since 2010, where limestone peaks and gorges crowd every horizon and terraces of corn and rice climb the hillsides.

  2. 2. Thị Trấn Tam Sơn

    Climb out of Hà Giang on the QL4C and the first big reward comes at around 1,500 metres, at Quản Bạ, where the road crests a pass known as Heaven's Gate and the karst country of the far north opens in front of you. From the viewpoint you look down onto Tam Sơn, the district town, in a broad valley of rice and corn fields, and beyond it two small, perfectly rounded hills sit side by side. The locals call them the Fairy Bosom, and once you have seen them, you will not need it explained.

  3. 3. Thị Trấn Đồng Văn

    Đồng Văn is the town at the top of the loop, a small market centre high on the karst plateau, close to the Chinese border, and the place most riders spend a night. It sits in a bowl surrounded by limestone peaks, and its heart is an old quarter of traditional houses with mud walls, wooden doors and tiled roofs, built around a market hall. The houses are a hundred years old or more, and in the evening the lanterns are lit, the cafes fill up and the air smells of woodsmoke and grilled meat.

  4. 4. Near Thị Trấn Đồng Văn

    Mã Pí Lèng is the reason many people ride the Hà Giang Loop at all. Between Đồng Văn and Mèo Vạc, the road climbs to about 1,500 metres and then runs along the edge of a cliff above the Nho Quế River, which flows through a narrow canyon far below, a ribbon of jade green between walls of grey rock. The view from the top is one of the most famous in Vietnam, and it lives up to the reputation: on a clear day you can see the river snaking through the gorge, terraced fields clinging to the slopes and endless ridges stretching into China.

  5. 5. Thị Trấn Mèo Vạc

    After the drama of Mã Pí Lèng, the road drops into Mèo Vạc, a district town in a narrow valley surrounded by limestone mountains, and the first thing that hits you is how ordinary it is. It is a working town, not a tourist town: concrete houses, government buildings, a market, schools and a few guesthouses and restaurants. After the cliffs above, it is a relief to slow down, eat a bowl of phở and watch the town go about its business.

  6. 6. Near Lũng Hồ

    The southern road back from Mèo Vạc is the half of the loop that many people skip, and they miss out. Around Lũng Hồ and the village of Du Già, a few kilometres west, the country is greener and more enclosed than on the high karst plateau: deep valleys with rice terraces, forested slopes, streams and waterfalls, and scattered villages of Tày and H'Mông houses. It feels more remote, and it is. Fewer tourists come this way, and the homestays are simple and welcoming.
