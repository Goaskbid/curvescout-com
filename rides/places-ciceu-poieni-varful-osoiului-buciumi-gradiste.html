Why this ride

A 247 km round trip from Negrilești - roughly 4 h 16 in the saddle. The stops in between: Valea Leșului - Țiclău, Buciumi - Grădiște and back through Valea Leșului - Țiclău. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ciceu-Poieni - Vârful Osoiului

    Hay is still cut by hand in the hills of northern Transylvania, and the slopes near Ciceu-Poieni are as good a place as any to see why that matters. This is a country of steep meadows above small villages, with clumps of forest, plum trees along the lanes and the stone-and-wood houses of farmers who still keep a few cows and sheep. Vârful Osoiului is a hilltop name rather than a signed attraction, about four kilometres from Negrilești, and it works best as a way to understand the landscape between the Someș valley and the hills of the north.

  2. 2. Valea Leșului - Țiclău

    The Someș river does most of the work here. Flowing west and then north out of Transylvania, it has carved a valley through the hills of Sălaj county, and near the villages of Lozna and Letca the slopes close in, wooded and steep, with small clearings and hay meadows. On the ridges above, the Romans built watchtowers to guard the northern edge of Dacia, and the remains near Valea Leșului and Țiclău are among the sites included in the UNESCO frontier listing. Today they are little more than grassy hollows and stone scatters, visited by archaeologists and the occasional curious walker.

  3. 3. Buciumi - Grădiște

    Buciumi has a Roman fort, and a proper one. On a low hill above the village, the outline of a rectangular auxiliary fort has been excavated, with walls, gates and the foundations of the buildings inside, laid out to the standard Roman pattern with the headquarters in the middle and barracks to either side. It was one of the forts guarding the road and the passes of the Meseş mountains, a few kilometres from the larger fortress and town at Porolissum, and it held a unit of auxiliary troops, soldiers recruited from the provinces rather than citizen legionaries.
