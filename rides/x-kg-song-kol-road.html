Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kochkor

    Kochkor is a flat, windy, high-valley town at roughly eighteen hundred metres in Kyrgyzstan's Naryn region, and it is where riders turn from the pavement to the dirt. It sits in a broad valley between the Kyrgyz Ala-Too and the Kyoktu range, with sheep and horses in the fields around it and the long line of the mountains marked by snow in all but the hottest months. About two hundred kilometres from Bishkek, it is the usual gateway to Song-Kol, a high alpine lake at over three thousand metres where herders bring their flocks in summer.

  2. 2. Song Kol

    Song Kol is a lake at 3,000 metres, sitting in a high bowl of the Tian Shan like something left behind by the sky. Its name means last lake, or perhaps following lake; Kyrgyz speakers give different readings. It lies in Naryn province, far from any town, and for four months a year it is the summer pasture of herders who bring their sheep, horses and yaks up from the valleys. Their yurts, white domes against a green and brown landscape, are the main architecture here, and the main way a traveller stays.

  3. 3. Naryn

    It is the capital of the province and the last real town of any size before the high pastures, a low, grey Soviet-era place of apartment blocks and a few older buildings, with brown hills rising on both sides and snowy peaks beyond. Winters are bitterly cold, sometimes thirty below, while summers are dry and bright.
