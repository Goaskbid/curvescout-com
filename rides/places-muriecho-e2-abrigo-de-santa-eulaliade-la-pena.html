Why this ride

A 110 km round trip from Bárcabo - roughly 1 h 54 in the saddle. On the way it passes Abrigo de Arilla, Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña and back through Abrigo de Arilla. The highest point is about 1,050 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Muriecho E2

    There is a corner of the Vero valley where the road begins to remind you of Pyrenean country, and Muriecho, five kilometres from Alquézar and a little to the east, is on the edge of it. The shelter listed as Muriecho E2 is the second in its series in this area, and belongs with the painted rocks of the Vero that UNESCO placed on its World Heritage list in 1998. The village of Muriecho itself is one of the many small settlements in this part of Huesca that lost most of their people in the twentieth century, as the young moved to Zaragoza, Barcelona or abroad.

  2. 2. Abrigo de Arilla

    The Sierra de Guara is the southern edge of the Pyrenees, a rough range of sandstone and limestone where rivers have cut deep, dark canyons that are among the best in Europe for canyoning. The Abrigo de Arilla lies in that country, well off the main roads, in the lonely hills between Alquézar and the Sierra, and is one of the rock shelters of the Río Vero cultural park. The paintings are mostly Levantine and schematic, thousands of years old, and are part of the Mediterranean Basin rock art that UNESCO listed in 1998. Reaching the shelters generally requires a walk, and regulations apply.

  3. 3. Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña

    Just north of Huesca, where the flat cereal land of the Hoya suddenly rears up into the first limestone walls of the Pre-Pyrenees, lies the little village of Nueno and, above it, the rock shelter named after Santa Eulalia de la Peña. This is the southern edge of the Sierra de Guara country, a landscape of gorges, conglomerate cliffs and scrub where griffon vultures ride the thermals and the light comes hard off pale stone.
