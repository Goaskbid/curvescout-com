Why this ride

Out of Askim, one way: 514 km, roughly 9 h 24 in the saddle. On the way it passes Scenic stretch near Sande, near Prestfoss and near Våler. The climb of the day is in Larkollen.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fuel stop near Askim

    The Glomma, Norway's longest river, swings past Askim on its way to the sea, and the town grew up on the energy it provides. A large hydroelectric plant at Solbergfoss harnesses the river nearby, and in the twentieth century Askim became known for its rubber works, which turned out tyres and boots for half of Norway. Today it is the main town of the inland Østfold district, with a modern centre, schools and shops, surrounded by wide farmland, lakes and forested ridges.

  3. 3. Scenic stretch near Sande

    Sande is where the inner Oslofjord pinches into a quiet bay, the Sandebukta, between Drammen and Holmestrand. The E18 thunders past it, and most traffic never slows down, but turn off and you find a gentle green landscape of farmland, low wooded ridges and small boat harbours, far softer than the fjord country further west that the bigger rides are heading for.

  4. 4. Photo stop near Prestfoss

    Prestfoss is the main village of Sigdal, a long, green valley west of Drammen, where the river Simoa flows south between forested mountains. The village sits at a waterfall on the river, and around it the valley opens into farmland, with old wooden farmhouses, red barns and meadows.

  6. 6. Oslo / Norway Scenic Roads

    The country is a mosaic of spruce and pine, small farms, bogs and long, slow lakes, running east toward the Swedish border, and it has a quiet, lonely character that suits a rider who has had enough of crowds. Kirkenær, the nearest town, has barely twelve hundred people.
