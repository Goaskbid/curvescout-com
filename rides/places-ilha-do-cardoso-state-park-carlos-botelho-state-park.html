Why this ride

From Cananéia it runs 329 km to Cananéia; allow about 5 h 40 of riding. The stops in between: Chauas Ecological Station, Carlos Botelho State Park and back through Chauas Ecological Station. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ilha do Cardoso State Park

    The southern tip of São Paulo's coast frays into a maze of islands, channels and mangroves, and the largest of the islands, Ilha do Cardoso, rises from the sea as a forested mountain ridge. It has been a state park since the 1960s, protecting a remarkable range of habitats in a small space: mountain rainforest, restinga scrub on sandy soils, mangroves, dunes and long empty beaches facing the Atlantic. It is part of the Atlantic Forest Southeast reserves listed by UNESCO.

  2. 2. Chauas Ecological Station

    The south coast of São Paulo state is a world away from the megacity a few hours to the north. Here, around the old town of Iguape, the Ribeira de Iguape river reaches the sea through a maze of channels, mangroves, islands and lagoons, backed by the forested slopes of the Serra do Mar. The Chauás Ecological Station protects a piece of lowland Atlantic Forest and restinga, the sandy coastal vegetation, near Iguape, and it is part of the Atlantic Forest Southeast Reserves, listed by UNESCO in 1999. The Atlantic Forest once covered much of the Brazilian coast; today only a fraction remains, and this region holds some of the largest continuous stretches.

  3. 3. Carlos Botelho State Park

    Carlos Botelho State Park protects a big piece of the Atlantic Forest on the slopes of the Serra de Paranapiacaba, in the south of São Paulo state, and it is one of the best remaining pieces of a forest that once covered the whole coast of Brazil. Less than a tenth of the original Atlantic Forest survives, and what is left is crowded with life: tapirs, ocelots, the occasional jaguar, hundreds of bird species and the muriqui, the woolly spider monkey, the largest primate in the Americas. The park is among the few places where this endangered monkey still lives.
