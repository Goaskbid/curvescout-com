Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Thermal Baths at Alange

    The Romans, who were serious about hot water, found a warm spring at Alange in what is now Extremadura and built a bathing complex around it, and the place is still used today. The Roman baths, about twenty kilometres from Mérida, include a domed chamber with stone benches and a pool fed by the spring, and the water comes out pleasantly warm, a gift in a dry country. The baths date from the time when Mérida, then called Emerita Augusta, was one of the great cities of Roman Spain, and wealthy citizens would travel here to relax and be cured.

  2. 2. The Mithraeum House - The Columbaria Funer

    Mérida was founded in 25 BC as Emerita Augusta, a colony for veterans of Augustus's Cantabrian wars, and for centuries it was one of the greatest cities of Roman Spain, capital of the province of Lusitania. It still has a theatre and an amphitheatre side by side, a circus that could hold tens of thousands, a bridge over the Guadiana of about 790 metres that remains in use, and an aqueduct whose brick-and-granite piers rise above the road like the legs of a giant. The Casa del Mitreo and the Columbaria lie on the south-east edge of the old centre, near the bullring, and they show a more private side of Roman life.

  3. 3. Cornalvo Dam

    Nearly two thousand years after Roman engineers built it, the dam at Cornalvo still holds water. It lies in the rolling countryside north-east of Mérida, in Extremadura, and it supplied the Roman city of Augusta Emerita, capital of the province of Lusitania. The dam is an earthen embankment faced with stone, with a tower in the reservoir that controlled the outflow, and it is part of the Archaeological Ensemble of Mérida, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
