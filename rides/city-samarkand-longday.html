Why this ride

A 423 km round trip from Samarkand - roughly 7 h 18 in the saddle. On the way it passes Town of Ancient Penjikent, Sanjarshakh Settlement, Historic Centre of Shakhrisyabz and back through Historic Centre of Shakhrisyabz. The highest point is about 2,300 m. It crosses from Uzbekistan into Tajikistan - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Samarkand

    Samarkand is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, and it has been arguing with Bukhara about which is more beautiful for about a thousand years. Alexander the Great took it in 329 BC, when it was called Marakanda, and it grew rich on the Silk Road, only to be flattened by Genghis Khan in 1220. Then Timur, or Tamerlane, made it his capital in the late fourteenth century and filled it with the turquoise-domed monuments that still dominate it today.

  2. 2. Town of Ancient Penjikent

    On a dusty loess terrace just outside modern Panjakent, a few low mud ridges and pits are all that remain of one of the richest cities on the Silk Road. Fourteen centuries ago this was a Sogdian town of merchants, with two-storey houses, temples and walls covered in paintings of feasts, heroes and gods. The best frescoes went to the Hermitage in St Petersburg and to the Rudaki museum in town, so bring some imagination with you, but standing among the excavated streets with the Zeravshan glittering below, the scale of the place still lands.

  3. 3. Sanjarshakh Settlement

    Sanjarshakh lies in the Zarafshan valley of Tajikistan, a short ride from the town of Panjakent, and its low mounds and walls are the remains of a Sogdian settlement. The Sogdians were the great merchants of Central Asia, who spoke an Iranian language and carried silk, spices, paper and ideas along the Silk Road from China to Persia and beyond, and their language was the common tongue of the trade routes for several centuries. Their towns lay in the valleys of the Zarafshan and the Kashkadarya, and Panjakent, a few kilometres from here, is the best known, with houses decorated with wall paintings of epic stories, banquets and gods, that are now in the Hermitage in Saint Petersburg.

  4. 4. Historic Centre of Shakhrisyabz

    Timur, the conqueror known in the West as Tamerlane, was born in 1336 in a village near Shakhrisyabz, and when he became ruler of an empire stretching from Delhi to Damascus, he spent heavily on his home town. He made it a showpiece, and the main monument was Ak-Saray, the White Palace, begun in 1380 and never completed in his lifetime. Only two piers of the entrance portal survive, but they rise about thirty-eight metres, covered in blue, turquoise and gold tilework, and they still dwarf everything around them, a statement of power so big that it is hard to photograph from close up.
