Why this ride

A 159 km round trip from Nerpio - roughly 2 h 44 in the saddle. It goes by way of Zona 1, Guijarral, Abrigo del Engarbo and back through Guijarral. The highest point is about 1,450 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cañaica del Calar lII

    Past the village of Nerpio, the land climbs toward the Calar, a high limestone plateau with thin soil, pine woods and open pasture, and the name Cañaica del Calar tells you where you are. Cañaica sounds like a diminutive of cañada, a drove road, and old tracks of that kind cross the sierra, used for centuries by shepherds moving flocks between summer and winter pastures. The shelter here, the third of a group, lies eleven kilometres from Nerpio, among those quiet uplands, which is farther than most of the other sites in the district and gives a stronger sense of isolation.

  2. 2. Zona 1

    Nerpio is about as far from anywhere as you can get in mainland Spain. The village lies in the southwestern corner of Albacete province, close to the borders of Murcia, Granada and Jaén, in a high, wild landscape of limestone ridges, pine forest and deep river valleys. The area is known for two things: walnuts, which grow in huge old trees along the Taibilla river and are celebrated in local cooking, and one of the densest concentrations of prehistoric rock art in the Iberian Peninsula, part of the UNESCO-listed rock art of the Mediterranean Basin. The generic label Zona 1 points at the hills near the village, where rock shelters with painted figures are scattered across the cliffs.

  3. 3. Guijarral

    Spain's largest protected area is the Sierras de Cazorla, Segura y Las Villas, and it spreads across a long stretch of wooded mountains in Jaén and Albacete. The Guijarral lies on its northern edge, in country close to Yeste, Segura de la Sierra and Orcera, where black pines and holm oaks cover steep slopes and rivers cut gorges between. Here, among rock walls, shelters preserve paintings, part of the Mediterranean Basin rock art listed by UNESCO in 1998, and the sites of the area, such as Zona 1 and the Collado de la Cruz, tell of hunting peoples who lived in these mountains long ago.

  4. 4. Abrigo del Engarbo

    Deep in the Sierra de Segura in the northeast of Jaén province, close to where the Segura river is born, the Engarbo rock shelters hide a few small painted figures under limestone overhangs. They belong to the great serial UNESCO listing of Levantine rock art, prehistoric paintings found along the mountains of eastern Spain, with stylised humans and animals in red ochre that have survived thousands of years of sun, frost and goats. The shelters are modest and fragile, often fenced, and visits usually need to be arranged locally, so treat them as a reason to explore this remote corner rather than a guaranteed sight.
