  1. 1. Cuenca Ecuador

    Cuenca is a city of domes, rivers and hats, and it gets under your skin. Its full name, Santa Ana de los Cuatro Ríos de Cuenca, refers to the four rivers that run through the broad valley at 2,550 metres, and the historic centre, founded in 1557 by the Spanish on the ruins of the Inca city of Tomebamba, is listed by UNESCO. The cathedral's blue and white domes rise over the main square, and streets of red-tiled houses with wrought-iron balconies run down to the Tomebamba river and its grassy banks.

  2. 2. Cajas gate

    Cajas looks like a place that nobody finished. The national park, west of Cuenca, is a high tableland of rock, bog and water, scattered with more than two hundred lakes left behind by glaciers, and the land sits mostly above 3,500 metres. Tussock grass and cushion plants cover the slopes, and in sheltered folds you find small woods of polylepis, the paper-bark trees that grow higher than any other tree on Earth, their twisted trunks peeling in red flakes.
