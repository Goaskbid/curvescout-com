Why this ride

406 km from Bárcabo to Bárcabo, about 6 h 45 of riding. It goes by way of Pyrénées - Mont Perdu, Cueva de los moros. and back through Pyrénées - Mont Perdu. It tops out around 1,950 m. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Pyrenees N260 Route

Pyrénées - Mont Perdu is 82 km from Barfaluy I, mostly on A-2205. You climb most of the way: 980 m, up to 1,453 m. Take a break before the climb, not halfway up.

The stops

  1. 1. Barfaluy I

    Start here.

    There is more than one reason to come to the Barfaluy ravine, but the clearest is the sudden change in the land. From Alquézar the road runs along a plateau of fields and scrub, and then the ground simply drops away into a deep, narrow gorge where the walls rise sheer on either side and sunlight reaches the floor only briefly. Barfaluy I, one of the shelters in the series catalogued along it, lies five kilometres from the village, and as with others in the Vero valley it forms part of the Levantine rock art listed by UNESCO in 1998.

    Read the complete Barfaluy I write-up.

  2. 2. Pyrénées - Mont Perdu

    82 km in. The ride passes about 6 km from it.

    Monte Perdido, the lost mountain, at 3,355 metres, is the highest limestone massif in Europe and the centrepiece of a cross-border World Heritage site shared by Spain and France. On the Spanish side, it gives its name to the Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park, with deep canyons radiating out from the summit; on the French side, it looks down on the great cirques of Gavarnie and Troumouse.

    Read the complete Pyrénées - Mont Perdu write-up.

  3. 3. Cueva de los moros.

    216 km in.

    There is a Cueva de los Moros in half the valleys of Spain. Every unexplained cave, ruin or rock shelter used to be credited to the Moors, and this one, in the hills of the western Aragonese Pyrenees between the valleys of Hecho and Ansó, is a rock shelter with ancient traces. You should approach it as an excuse to spend time in two of the most beautiful and least spoiled valleys of the Pyrenees, rather than as a monument.

    Read the complete Cueva de los moros. write-up.
