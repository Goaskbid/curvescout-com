Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Logroño

    Logroño sits on the south bank of the Ebro, in the centre of La Rioja, and for a wine lover it is as close as it comes to a capital. The city has around a hundred and fifty thousand inhabitants and a pleasant old quarter, with a cathedral, an old stone bridge and a very tasty pedestrian street called Calle del Laurel, where bar after bar serves a single speciality, such as mushrooms with prawn, or small skewers of grilled pork, along with a glass of the local red. Locals walk from bar to bar, eating one or two things in each, a ritual called the pinchos crawl.

  2. 2. Laguardia

    Laguardia stands on a ridge above the Ebro valley, a walled hill town with the long blue wall of the Sierra de Cantabria rising behind it like a stage backdrop. It is the main town of the Rioja Alavesa, the part of the Basque Country that lies south of the mountains and produces some of Spain's best-known wine, and it has about fifteen hundred permanent residents, most of whom seem to have a cellar.

  3. 3. Haro

    Haro is the capital of the Rioja Alta, a mid-sized river town with twelve thousand inhabitants and an astonishing density of wine. It sits where the Tirón river comes down to meet the Ebro, with the Sierra de Cantabria on the north side and the blue ridges of the Sierra de la Demanda far away to the south, and the soil of the surrounding slopes gives the wines their reputation for elegance rather than muscle.
