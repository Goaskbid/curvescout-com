Why this ride

From Villafranca del Cid it runs 131 km to Villafranca del Cid; allow about 2 h 16 of riding. It goes by way of molí Darrer- Abric I, Centelles- Abric I, molí Darrer- Abric II and Centelles- Abric II. The highest point is about 1,250 m.

  2. 2. molí Darrer- Abric I

    The Alt Maestrat is the high, rugged interior of Castellón province, a country of limestone plateaus, deep ravines and stone villages perched on ridges, and it is crossed by a web of dry-stone walls that farmers have been building and rebuilding for centuries. Near the villages of Benassal, Catí and Villafranca del Cid, under a rock overhang beside an old mill site, the shelter called Molí Darrer holds prehistoric paintings, part of the scattered Levantine rock art that UNESCO inscribed as one site across eastern Spain.

  3. 3. Centelles- Abric I

    Albocàsser and Catí are two quiet stone towns in the inland hills of Castellón, and between them, in a rocky ravine, the Centelles shelters hold some of the prehistoric paintings that make this corner of Spain a capital of Levantine rock art. The style is distinctive: small human figures, often archers, painted in red with long legs and dynamic poses, hunting deer, ibex and boar, sometimes in groups, sometimes in battle. The sites are part of the UNESCO listing of Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula. Nearby, the Valltorta gorge, near Tírig, holds some of the most famous examples, and its museum explains the art and the people who made it.

  4. 4. molí Darrer- Abric II

    A molí is a mill, and the one that lends its name to this shelter was presumably a water mill on one of the small streams that trickle through the hills around Benassal, in the high interior of Castellón province. Molí Darrer, the Last Mill, was probably the last one before the water ran out or the valley ended, and the shelter here, Abric II, lies about nine kilometres from the town. It carries paintings of the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain, and is linked to a neighbouring shelter at Mas d'en Badenes and to the Centelles site, which are on the same ride.

  5. 5. Centelles- Abric II

    Three or four shelters bear the name Centelles, and on a map they all collapse into one dot, which says something about how these places are catalogued: by the ravine, the farm and the order in which a surveyor stumbled across them. The Centelles of this ride lies in the upland country between Catí and Albocàsser, where the Maestrat plateau is broad, quiet and dry, and where the main event of the day is the sheep crossing the road.
