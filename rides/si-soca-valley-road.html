Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bovec

    Bovec sits at the head of the Soča valley, in a wide bowl of green meadows ringed by limestone walls that look as if they were stacked there deliberately. Kanin rises to the south, the high Julian Alps close in to the east, and the river that gave the valley its name runs cold and unbelievably turquoise just down the road. It is a small town of around sixteen hundred people, but in summer its population of kayakers, rafters, paragliders and riders makes it feel much larger.

  2. 2. Kobarid

    Kobarid is a quiet little town in a loud history. In October 1917, in what the Italians know as Caporetto, Austro-Hungarian and German troops broke through the Italian lines on the Isonzo front in the twelfth battle of the river, and the retreat that followed became a byword for catastrophe in Italy. Ernest Hemingway used it as the backdrop for A Farewell to Arms. For two and a half years before that the valley had been a front line, with thousands of men dying in the mountains above, and the town now holds one of the most thoughtful war museums in Europe, which was named European Museum of the Year in 1993.

  3. 3. Tolmin

    Tolmin sits at the meeting of two rivers, the Soča and the Tolminka, in a broad green bowl where the Julian Alps begin to soften toward the Friulian plain. It is the main town of the middle Soča valley, a place with three and a half thousand people, a handful of shops, a market and a cheerful, workaday air, and it makes a good base for riders in the region. Kobarid lies up the valley, Kanal further down, and the Italian town of Cividale del Friuli is beyond the hills to the west.
