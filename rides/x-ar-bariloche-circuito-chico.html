Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bariloche

    Bariloche stands on the southern shore of Lake Nahuel Huapi, in the Argentine Andes, and the place has the look of an alpine resort that has been picked up and set in Patagonia. Chalets of wood and stone, chocolate shops, and a civic centre built from rough stone in the 1930s give it a Central European air, a result of Swiss and German settlers who came in the late nineteenth century and the national park authority that shaped the town's style. The lake is big and cold, with long arms reaching into the mountains, and snowy peaks stand on every side.

  2. 2. Llao Llao

    Llao Llao is the best-known spot on the Circuito Chico, the short scenic loop that runs west from Bariloche along the shore of Lake Nahuel Huapi. The name comes from a Mapuche word for a type of edible fungus that grows on the southern beech trees, and it has since been taken by a famous hotel, whose pitched roofs and stone tower stand on a green headland above the water. Built in the 1930s in an Alpine style, the hotel has hosted presidents and film stars, and its setting, between two lakes and ringed by mountains, is as dramatic as the architecture is quaint.
