Why this ride

curated scenic road. Eastern Himalayas, high pass, monasteries. A one-way ride of 145 km, Tezpur to Tawang - roughly 2 h 38 in the saddle. On the way it passes Bomdila and Tawang Road via Sela Pass. The highest point is about 4,400 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Near Tezpur

    Tezpur lies on the north bank of the Brahmaputra in Assam, and it is where a ride to Tawang really starts. The town has about fifty-eight thousand people, a river that is wide enough to look like a sea, and a gentle, slightly sleepy air. Its name means city of blood in Sanskrit legend, from the story of a battle between Krishna and the demon king Banasura, who is said to have ruled here. The ruins of old temples sit at Da Parbatia, and the Agnigarh hill park looks over the river.

  2. 2. Bomdila

    Bomdila is a hill town strung along a ridge in the far west of Arunachal Pradesh, at about 2,400 metres, and it is the traditional overnight stop on the long road to Tawang. The town is home to the Monpa people, who follow Tibetan Buddhism and have lived in these valleys for centuries, and you see it in the prayer flags, the prayer wheels and the monastery that sits above the main bazaar. The hills around are covered in orchards, and apples and kiwis are among the local products, sold at roadside stalls in the right season.

  4. 4. Near Tawang

    Tawang is a town at 3,000 metres in the far west of Arunachal Pradesh, wedged between Bhutan and Tibet, and it is the destination that makes the long journey from Tezpur worthwhile. Above it, on a ridge, stands Tawang Monastery, the largest in India and one of the largest in the world, founded in the seventeenth century by Merak Lama Lodre Gyatso. It belongs to the Gelug school of Tibetan Buddhism, and the sixth Dalai Lama, Tsangyang Gyatso, is said to have been born near here. The Monpa people who live in the district are Buddhist, and the town feels more Tibetan than Indian in its prayer wheels and chanting.
