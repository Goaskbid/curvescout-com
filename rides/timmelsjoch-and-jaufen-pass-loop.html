Why this ride

A 85 km loop that starts and ends near Merano, about 1 h 22 of riding. On the way it passes Passo Giovo, Vipiteno and Timmelsjoch. The highest point is about 2,450 m. Passo Giovo and Timmelsjoch are open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Timmelsjoch High Alpine Road

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2094 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance — cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Rider note

Check the pass is open before you commit to it.

  1. 1. Merano

    Merano has a climate that surprises people. Sheltered by high mountains on three sides, it has mild winters and warm summers, and palm trees, cypresses and even a few vines grow in the town, which sits at about three hundred metres where the Passer river joins the Adige.

  2. 2. Passo Giovo

    The Jaufenpass, which Italians call Passo Giovo, is a good road that gets very little attention because the roads next to it are so famous. It crosses the ridge between the Sterzing basin and the Passeier valley at 2,094 metres, and it does so with no tolls, no crowds and no fuss. The road climbs from Vipiteno through dark spruce forest, with a clean surface and bends that keep coming, and then emerges above the tree line into open pasture and rock. At the top there is a small restaurant and a car park, and the view down the other side is long and green, with the Texel group of mountains standing in the distance.

  3. 3. Vipiteno

    Vipiteno, called Sterzing in German, is a small walled town that has been collecting travellers for a very long time. It stands on the old route over the Brenner, where the road from the Eisack valley opens out into a broad basin before climbing to the pass, and it grew rich on trade and, in the late Middle Ages, on silver mining in the surrounding hills. The result is a main street that looks like a film set, with a long, curving line of arcaded houses in pastel colours, oriel windows, wrought-iron signs and, at one end, the Zwölferturm, a tall tower from the late fifteenth century that is the town's emblem.

  4. 4. Timmelsjoch

    Timmelsjoch, at 2,474 metres, is a border pass between Austria and Italy, linking the Ötztal valley with the Passeier valley, and it is one of the great high Alpine roads. The Italians call it Passo del Rombo. It opened in 1968 as a toll road, built partly to connect the two sides of the old Tyrol, which had been divided after the First World War, and it remains a toll road today, with a charge for motorbikes collected on the Austrian side.
