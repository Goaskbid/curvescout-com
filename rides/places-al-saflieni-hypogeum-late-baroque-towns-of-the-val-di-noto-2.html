Why this ride

418 km from Fgura to Fgura, about 7 h 12 of riding. On the way it passes Megalithic Temples of Malta, Scicli, Late Baroque Towns of the Val di Noto and back through Scicli. It crosses from Malta into Italy - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum

    Beneath a quiet residential street in Paola, a few kilometres south of Valletta, there is a labyrinth carved into the rock about five thousand years ago. The Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum is a prehistoric underground sanctuary and burial complex, cut by hand with stone and antler tools into the soft limestone on three levels, with chambers, passages and niches that descend to about ten metres. It was in use from around 3300 BC to 3000 BC, and the remains of thousands of people were found in it when workmen broke into it by accident in 1902, while digging a cistern.

  2. 2. Megalithic Temples of Malta

    Before there were pyramids in Egypt, or Stonehenge on its plain, people on a small Mediterranean island were building enormous stone temples, and some of them are still standing. The group at Ġgantija, on the island of Gozo, goes back to roughly 3600 BC, which makes it among the oldest free-standing monuments in the world. The walls are made of huge limestone blocks, some weighing several tonnes, and they curve into rounded chambers like the lobes of a clover leaf. Local legend said they were built by a giantess, and the name comes from the word for giant.

  3. 3. Scicli

    In January 1693 an earthquake flattened much of south-eastern Sicily, and the towns that rose from the rubble were rebuilt in a confident, theatrical late baroque style. Scicli is one of the eight towns of the Val di Noto listed by UNESCO for that reason, and it may be the most charming. It sits at the meeting of three limestone gorges, with a rocky hill above crowned by the ruins of the old church of San Matteo, and a centre of honey-coloured churches and palaces whose balconies are held up by carved grotesque faces, horses, sirens and monsters.

  4. 4. Late Baroque Towns of the Val di Noto

    On 11 January 1693, a catastrophic earthquake struck southeastern Sicily, destroying dozens of towns and killing tens of thousands of people. What happened next is one of the great chapters in the history of town planning. Noto, which stood on a hill some kilometres away, was abandoned, and a new town was built from scratch on a gentler slope, with a grid of streets, grand stairways, churches and palaces, all in the soft, honey-coloured local limestone that glows gold and pink at sunset. Together with seven other towns rebuilt after the earthquake, including Ragusa, Modica and Scicli, Noto was listed by UNESCO in 2002 as the Late Baroque Towns of the Val di Noto.
