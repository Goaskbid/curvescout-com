Why this ride

A 248 km round trip from Morella - roughly 4 h 17 in the saddle. The stops in between: Abric de les Llibreres, Cova de l'Escoda and back through Abric de les Llibreres. The highest point is about 1,100 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cova del Llepus o Partició

    Morella is the sort of town that makes riders stop in the middle of a long day just to look. It sits at about a thousand metres in the high Els Ports country of northern Castellón, ringed by more than two kilometres of medieval walls, with a castle on a crag above and a Gothic basilica below, and the whole mass of golden stone looks as if it grew out of the rock. The Cova del Llepus, about four kilometres away, is one of the painted shelters of the district, a Levantine site protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain. The ride links it with another cave, the Escoda, and with a hill the maps call the Peñón de Santo Espíritu.

  2. 2. Abric de les Llibreres

    The Montsià range rises abruptly from the coastal plain at the southern tip of Catalonia, a long, rocky ridge of limestone that separates the Ebro delta from the inland valleys. Around its foot lie the villages of Santa Bàrbara, Masdenverge and Ulldecona, surrounded by olive groves, some of them with ancient trees hundreds of years old, their trunks twisted into extraordinary shapes. The Abric de les Llibreres is one of the prehistoric rock shelters in this area, part of the UNESCO-listed rock art of the Mediterranean Basin.

  3. 3. Cova de l'Escoda

    The Mediterranean Basin rock art does not end at the Valencian border, and the Cova de l'Escoda is a reminder that the painted shelters stretch up into Catalonia. This one lies in the hills inland from the Costa Daurada, in the dry, rugged country of the Baix Camp and the Ribera d'Ebre, with the Serra de Llaberia to the north and the Ebro valley to the south-west. The shelter belongs to the group listed by UNESCO in 1998, and its paintings are in the Levantine style. The nearest places on the map are El Perelló and Ginestar, though the largest town in the area is Mont-roig del Camp, some sixteen kilometres away.
