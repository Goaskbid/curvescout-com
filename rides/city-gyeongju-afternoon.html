  1. 1. Gyeongju

    For almost a thousand years, from 57 BC to AD 935 by the traditional count, Gyeongju was the capital of the Silla kingdom, and the city still behaves like a place that remembers it. The old centre is flat and green and quiet, with the great grass-covered burial mounds of Silla kings and queens rising among the houses and shops like a row of soft hills. Nothing in the city is allowed to climb too high, which keeps the skyline low and lets the mountains behind it stay visible.

  2. 2. Yangdong Cluster: Yangdong Village

    Yangdong is a village that was never really meant to be seen. The Wolseong Son clan and the Yeogang Yi clan settled in this fold of hills north of Gyeongju in the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, and over the next few hundred years the two families built the tiled mansions of the elite on the upper slopes and the thatched houses of everyone else below, all in careful relation to the hillside. It is one of the best preserved clan villages in Korea, and in 2010 it joined Hahoe, near Andong, on UNESCO's list of historic villages of Korea.

  3. 3. Yangdong Cluster: Donggangseowon Confucian

    Yangdong is a village that still lives like the old Korea of the Joseon dynasty. It lies in a valley north of Gyeongju, in a bowl of low wooded hills, and it is home to some of the finest surviving traditional houses in the country, hundreds of tiled-roof and thatched homes spread over the slopes. Two great clans have dominated it for centuries, the Wolseong Son and the Yeogang Yi, and their families built the large tile-roofed mansions on the high ground with the thatched houses of the tenants below. The neat layout follows the principles of feng shui: the main houses face south with a hill behind and a stream in front. UNESCO listed Yangdong along with Hahoe in 2010 as the Historic Villages of Korea.

  4. 4. Oksan-seowon

    Oksan-seowon sits in a quiet valley northwest of Gyeongju, beside a clear stream that tumbles over smooth granite slabs. It was founded in 1572 to honour Yi Eon-jeok, one of the great Neo-Confucian scholars of the Joseon dynasty, who lived in retreat nearby, and it served as a private academy where young men studied the Confucian classics, prepared for the state examinations, and performed rites in honour of the master. The buildings are arranged according to the strict logic of Confucian architecture: a lecture hall, dormitories for students, a library and, at the back, a shrine for the rites. The architecture is restrained, with wooden halls, tiled roofs and open courtyards, designed to harmonise with the landscape. Oksan is part of the UNESCO listing of the Seowon, Korean Neo-Confucian Academies, inscribed in 2019.

  5. 5. Yangdong Cluster:Oksanseowon Confucian Aca

    In the green hills east of Gyeongju, where the old Silla capital gives way to the rice fields and pine woods that lead toward the East Sea, the Joseon dynasty left a different kind of monument. Oksanseowon is a Confucian academy, a seowon, built in the sixteenth century to honour the scholar Yi Eon-jeok, known by his pen name Hoejae, who lived from 1491 to 1553. It belongs to the Yangdong cluster, part of the Historic Villages of Korea inscribed by UNESCO in 2010 together with Hahoe, and it shows how the Neo-Confucian elite wanted to live, study and be remembered.
