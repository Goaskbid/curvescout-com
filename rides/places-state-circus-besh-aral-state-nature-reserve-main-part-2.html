Why this ride

From Tashkent it runs 663 km to Tashkent; allow about 15 h of riding. The stops in between: The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve, Besh-Aral State Nature Reserve - main part and back through The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve. It tops out around 2,700 m. It crosses from Uzbekistan into Kyrgyzstan - carry ID and check the toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. State Circus

    Start here.

    The Tashkent circus looks like a landed spaceship, or a giant pale flying saucer, and for decades it was one of the city's proudest buildings. Circus was a serious art form in the Soviet Union, with its own schools, state companies and international stars, and every large city had a permanent big top made of concrete. The one in Tashkent, a round building with a huge dome, was built in the 1970s and holds nearly two thousand spectators, and the tradition of acrobats, trained animals, clowns and trapeze artists is a cherished part of the city's culture, with many families bringing their children on Sundays.

    Read the complete State Circus write-up.

  2. 2. The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve

    117 km in. The ride passes about 8 km from it.

    East of Tashkent, the land rises steeply into the western ranges of the Tian Shan, and the Chatkal range forms the border with Kyrgyzstan. The Chatkal State Biosphere Reserve, established in the Soviet era, protects a stretch of these mountains, with juniper forests, walnut and wild fruit trees, alpine meadows and rocky peaks.

    Read the complete The Chatkal State Biosphere Nature Reserve write-up.

  3. 3. Besh-Aral State Nature Reserve - main part

    331 km in. The ride passes about 19 km from it.

    Far up the Chatkal valley in western Kyrgyzstan, where the river runs between the Chatkal and Sandalash ranges of the Western Tien Shan, lies the Besh-Aral State Nature Reserve. It protects mountain steppe, juniper woodland, walnut and apple groves and rocky high country, and it is part of the transnational World Heritage Site of the Western Tien Shan, inscribed in 2016 and shared with Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan. The reserve was created in particular to protect Menzbier's marmot, a small marmot found only in this part of Central Asia.
