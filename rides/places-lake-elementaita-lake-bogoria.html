Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Lake Elementaita

    Lake Elementaita is the smallest and quietest of the three soda lakes, a shallow stretch of pale water in the Rift Valley that looks, in the right light, like a sheet of beaten silver. The name comes from a Maasai word, muteita, which means a place of dust, and the dry, windy shore bears it out. Behind the lake rises a ridge called the Sleeping Warrior, whose outline resembles a man lying on his back, and local people tell the tale of the sleeping giant with a straight face.

  2. 2. Lake Nakuru

    The first thing you notice about Lake Nakuru is the smell, and the second is the pink. The lake sits in the floor of the Great Rift Valley about 1,760 metres above sea level with no outlet, so the water is soda-rich, alkaline and rank, and in a good year it is thick with blue-green algae that lesser flamingos feed on in the hundreds of thousands. From the ridge above the shoreline they look like a pink smudge across the grey water. In other years, when the water level changes, the birds move to Lake Bogoria or Elmenteita, which is why the trio of Rift Valley soda lakes is listed together as a World Heritage site.

  3. 3. Lake Bogoria

    Lake Bogoria lies at the bottom of the Rift Valley in central Kenya, a long, narrow, alkaline lake squeezed between a steep fault wall on the west and a flat, thorny plain on the east. Its water is so salty and caustic that fish cannot live in it, but blue-green algae thrive, and the algae feed one of the great wildlife spectacles of Africa: lesser flamingos in the hundreds of thousands, which line the shore and turn the margins pink. The numbers shift with the water level, and in some years they move to other lakes. Bogoria, Nakuru and Elmenteita make up the Kenya Lake System, listed by UNESCO in 2011.
