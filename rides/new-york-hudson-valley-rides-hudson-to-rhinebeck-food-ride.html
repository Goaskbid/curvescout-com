Why this ride

Leave the city behind and spend the day on roads with texture. A 236 km loop that starts and ends near Staten Island, about 4 h 22 of riding. It goes by way of near Fair Haven, Scenic stretch near Rosedale, in Franklin Square and East Elmhurst.

  5. 5. Near East Elmhurst

    The planes give it away. East Elmhurst sits right beside LaGuardia Airport in northern Queens, and jets come in low over the rooftops every couple of minutes, their wheels down, as they line up for the runways that jut out into Flushing Bay. It is a neighbourhood of brick row houses, small gardens and churches, historically one of the main centres of Black middle-class life in Queens, and it has a strong sense of community despite the noise.

  6. 6. Near Blauvelt, on the way back

    Rockland County is the bit of New York State that feels like New Jersey and the Hudson Valley at the same time: suburban towns like Nanuet, West Nyack and Blauvelt, with shopping centres and highways, and then suddenly steep wooded ridges, cliffs above the river and old villages with church spires. Blauvelt and West Nyack sit in the middle of it, a few kilometres from the Hudson.

  8. 8. Back to Hudson to

    Staten Island is the odd one out in New York City, and that is exactly why it makes a good place to end a long ride. It is the least dense of the five boroughs, tied to the rest of the city by the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge to Brooklyn, the free Staten Island Ferry to Manhattan, and three bridges west to New Jersey. Riders returning from the Hudson Valley often arrive by the New Jersey side, crossing the Goethals or the Bayonne Bridge, whose arch was raised in the 2010s to let bigger ships pass underneath.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Food stop near Fair Haven to Scenic stretch near Rosedale

Food stop near Fair Haven → Scenic stretch near Rosedale is the logistics leg. Watch junctions, villages and speed changes, then let the roadbook do its job: move you out of the clutter without spending your best attention too early.

Scenic stretch near Rosedale to High point in Franklin Square

Scenic stretch near Rosedale → High point in Franklin Square is the reward section in New York. Look through the bends, use legal pull-offs for photos and keep a little margin for tractors, cyclists and damp shade. The best ride here is smooth, not noisy.

High point in Franklin Square to Near East Elmhurst

High point in Franklin Square → Near East Elmhurst rides best with patience. Coastal roads change character with wind and visitors; keep overtakes boring and take the photo from a proper stop.

Photo stop in North Haledon to Back to Hudson to

Photo stop in North Haledon → Back to Hudson to can flow quickly, but only if you keep village limits and junction sightlines sacred. Fast connectors are where rides get untidy when fatigue creeps in.
