Why this ride

140 km from Carlisle to Carlisle, about 2 h 39 of riding. It goes by way of Nowtler Hill 2 Roman temporary camp, Hadrian's Wall between the dismantled rail, Nowtler Hill 1 Roman temporary camp and Hadrian's Wall between Apple Garth.

The stops

  1. 1. Watchclose Roman temporary camp

    Start here.

    East of Carlisle, between the villages of Brampton and Wetheral, the countryside rolls gently towards the Pennines, with green fields, hedges and patches of woodland. Somewhere in these fields lie the faint traces of Watchclose, a Roman temporary camp, one of many marching camps built by soldiers on campaign or during the construction of Hadrian's Wall. These camps were enclosed by a ditch and bank and used for a night or a season, and today they are often visible only from the air or as slight undulations in the grass. Watchclose is part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire World Heritage site.

    Read the complete Watchclose Roman temporary camp write-up.

  2. 2. Nowtler Hill 2 Roman temporary camp

    15 km in.

    Not every Roman monument is made of stone. North of Carlisle, on low ground close to the Solway plain, the traces of a Roman temporary camp show up mainly as marks in the ground and in crops: a square or rectangle of ditch and bank, with rounded corners, where an army spent a night or two under leather tents. Nowtler Hill was one of those places. Marching armies built such camps every evening, digging the ditch and piling the earth into a rampart topped with stakes, so that even a short halt was protected.

    Read the complete Nowtler Hill 2 Roman temporary camp write-up.

  3. 3. Hadrian's Wall between the dismantled rail

    37 km in.

    The Solway Firth narrows at Bowness-on-Solway, a small village of whitewashed cottages on a low headland, and this is where Hadrian's Wall ended, or rather began, at its western end.

    Read the complete Hadrian's Wall between the dismantled rail write-up.

  4. 4. Nowtler Hill 1 Roman temporary camp

    51 km in.

    Carlisle is a border city, and it has been fought over for two thousand years. The Romans founded Luguvalium here to serve the Wall and the road north, and a settlement has stood beside the Eden ever since. The red sandstone cathedral dates from the twelfth century, and the castle beside it held Mary Queen of Scots for two months in 1568, after she fled across the Solway. In 1745 Bonnie Prince Charlie's Highland army took the city on its march into England and lost it on the way back, and the Jacobite prisoners were held in the castle. The names of the old border families, the Armstrongs, Grahams and Nixons, still fill the telephone book.

    Read the complete Nowtler Hill 1 Roman temporary camp write-up.

  5. 5. Hadrian's Wall between Apple Garth

    74 km in.

    The Solway Firth is a strange piece of sea. It is wide and shallow and has some of the biggest tidal ranges in Britain, and at low water it empties into a landscape of sand, mud and channels where the tide can return faster than a walker can run. On its northern shore, the Scottish side, the land around Eastriggs and Annan is flat salt marsh and farmland, backed by low green hills, and across the water you can see the Cumbrian fells rising bluish and far off. The Roman frontier ended on the other side, at Bowness, but the Romans watched this shore too, and the wall's western run along the English edge is the reason the area is on the heritage map.
