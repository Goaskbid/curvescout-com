Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Klaipeda

    Klaipėda is the sea gate of Lithuania, and for most of its history it belonged to somebody else. The Teutonic Knights built a castle at the mouth of the Danė river in 1252, and for seven centuries the town was known as Memel, part of Prussia and then Germany, until it was returned to Lithuania in 1923. Traces of the old name and culture remain in the half-timbered warehouses of the old town, a few of which have been carefully restored, and in a bronze statue of Ännchen von Tharau, the heroine of a seventeenth century German song, standing in the market square.

  2. 2. Curonian Spit

    Nothing prepares you for the sand. The Curonian Spit is a sliver of land nearly a hundred kilometres long, split between Lithuania and the Russian exclave of Kaliningrad, lying between the Baltic Sea and the Curonian Lagoon and never more than a few kilometres wide. Great dunes, among the highest moving sand hills in Europe, run along its length, with pine forest holding the rest in place. UNESCO recognised it as a cultural landscape in 2000, because people have fought to keep this strip alive for centuries.
