Why this ride

From Pajaroncillo it runs 374 km to Pajaroncillo; allow about 6 h 27 of riding. The stops in between: Barrio del Castillo, Abrigo de la fuente del Cabrerizo, Torre de la iglesia de San Martín and back through Abrigo de la fuente del Cabrerizo. The highest point is about 1,450 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Selva Pascuala

    Selva Pascuala is the name of a patch of pine and rock in the Serranía de Cuenca, above the village of Villar del Humo, and it is the name by which one of the best-known groups of Levantine rock paintings is known. The figures were painted in a rock shelter by people who lived by hunting and gathering, and perhaps by the first herding, some six to ten thousand years ago, although dating the pigment is hard and scholars still argue about it. The paintings show animals, including deer and wild goats, and human figures, small and lively, painted in a deep red that has survived because the shelter's overhang kept the rain off.

  2. 2. Barrio del Castillo

    Cuenca is built on a spur of rock between two gorges, and the Barrio del Castillo is its highest quarter, the old castle district at the narrow tip of the ridge. The Huécar river runs along one side and the Júcar along the other, each in a deep cleft with limestone cliffs, and the old town clings to the top between them like a ship's prow. The castle that gave the quarter its name has largely gone, though a gate and a few walls remain, and the Torre de Mangana, a clock tower that was once part of the fortress, stands nearby. The whole walled town is on the UNESCO list as an unusual example of a medieval fortress city that kept its shape.

  3. 3. Abrigo de la fuente del Cabrerizo

    Albarracín is a small walled town of rose-coloured stone, steep and narrow, built on a rocky bend of the Guadalaviar river in the Sierra de Albarracín, in the south of Teruel province. The Abrigo de la Fuente del Cabrerizo is a rock shelter about three kilometres away in the surrounding pine woods, in country of strangely red sandstone outcrops that locals call rodeno.

  4. 4. Torre de la iglesia de San Martín

    Teruel is a small provincial capital high on the Aragonese plateau, and its skyline is made of Mudéjar towers, built by Muslim craftsmen working for Christian patrons in brick and glazed ceramic tiles. The tower of San Martín, dating to the early fourteenth century, is one of the finest, a square tower straddling a street, so that traffic passes through an arch at its base. With the other Mudéjar towers of the city and the cathedral, it forms part of the World Heritage listing of Mudéjar Architecture of Aragon.
