Why this ride

451 km from Brezoi to Brezoi, about 9 h 46 of riding. The stops in between: Voineasa - Crac-Găuri, Târsa - Platoul Târsa and back through Voineasa - Crac-Găuri. It tops out around 1,600 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

The stops

  1. 1. Cozia - Lotrisor

    Start here.

    Brezoi is where two rivers meet and the mountains lean in. The Lotru comes down from the high ridges of the Parang and Lotru ranges, the Olt arrives from the north through its famous gorge, and below the confluence the valley opens just enough for a town of about seven thousand people to settle. Cozia and Lotrisor sit on either side of this junction, and the great pyramid of the Cozia massif stands over the road to the east, a dark wall of beech and spruce that glows copper in October.

    Read the complete Cozia - Lotrisor write-up.

  2. 2. Voineasa - Crac-Găuri

    104 km in.

    North of the Jiu Valley, the land rises steeply into the Șureanu and Parâng mountains, a wild region of high ridges, deep valleys, spruce forests and alpine meadows. Crac-Găuri lies up in this high country, linked by name to the old routes that crossed the mountains in Roman times, when Dacia was a province and soldiers watched the passes between the Transylvanian basin and the south. Little is visible today, and the remoteness is half the attraction: this is mountain land, where shepherds bring their flocks to high pastures in summer and the forest stretches for miles.

    Read the complete Voineasa - Crac-Găuri write-up.

  3. 3. Târsa - Platoul Târsa

    220 km in.

    Târsa sits on a high plateau in the Șureanu mountains, in the same ring of hills that sheltered the Dacian kingdom's heart. The platoul, the plateau, is open grassland and forest edge at roughly a thousand metres or higher, the sort of place where shepherds still bring flocks in summer and the wind carries the sound of bells.
