Why this ride

452 km round from Chur - plan on about 8 h of riding. The climbs: Julierpass high road, Albula Pass railway country, Flüelapass high section and Lukmanier pass road. It tops out at 2,383 m. Lenzerheide Pass, Albula Pass, Fluela Pass and Lukmanier Pass are subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. It is a high-mountain day.

The roads

Grand Tour of Switzerland

18 km from Chur fuel gate to Lenzerheide balcony road on Hauptstrasse. Mostly uphill - about 870 m, topping out near 1,470 m. Take a break before the climb, not halfway up.

Check the pass is open before you commit to it.

The stops

  1. 1. Chur fuel gate

    Start here.

    At 593 metres, in a wide bend of the Rhine valley, Chur makes a strong claim to be the oldest town in Switzerland, with traces of settlement going back thousands of years and a Roman presence from the first century BC. It has been the seat of a bishop since late antiquity, and the bishop's court, with its cathedral, sits on a terrace above the old town. Down below, the narrow streets and squares of the Altstadt, with painted houses, fountains and arcades, are a pleasant place to wander after a morning in the saddle.

    Read the complete Chur write-up.

  2. 2. Lenzerheide balcony road

    18 km in. The road climbs 882 m on the way up to it, steadily. Reckon on about 6 °C colder than the valley, and more in the wind.

    Lenzerheide is a high, wide valley south of Chur, a broad saddle of meadow, forest and small lakes at about 1,475 metres. The twin villages of Lenzerheide and Valbella have fuel, cafes and shops, and Chur, a little more than fourteen kilometres north, has everything else.

    Read the complete Lenzerheide balcony road write-up.

  3. 3. Julierpass high road

    65 km in. 2284 m, and the road stops climbing. The road climbs 809 m on the way up to it, steadily. Reckon on about 11 °C colder than the valley, and more in the wind. At this height the road is a summer one: snow gates can stay shut into June and shut again in October.

    The Julier is one of the oldest crossings in the Alps, and the summit at 2,284 metres still carries two stumps of stone columns beside the road, thought to be the remains of a Roman shrine. The Romans pushed a road over the pass in the first century, and ever since the route has linked the Oberhalbstein valley with the Engadin, carrying merchants and pilgrims between Chur and the Bergell and the passes south. A small lake sits near the top and a handful of buildings, but there is not much else, and it can be bleak and windy.

    Read the complete Julierpass high road write-up.

  4. 4. Albula Pass railway country

    99 km in. The high point of this section, 2315 m up. Reckon on about 12 °C colder than the valley, and more in the wind.

    The Albula Pass is the road, but the railway is the star. At 2,315 metres the pass links the Engadine with the Albula valley and Chur, and it is shadowed by the Rhaetian Railway's Albula line, which climbs through spiral tunnels, over viaducts and under the mountain in the Albula tunnel. UNESCO put this line and the Bernina line on the World Heritage list in 2008, and on a clear day you can watch the red trains from the road.

    Read the complete Albula Pass railway country write-up.

  5. 5. Flüelapass high section

    147 km in. Flüelapass high section tops the climb at 2383 m. Nothing on this route is higher.

    The scenery is stark: bare rock, screes, snow patches and a handful of small, dark lakes, with the Flüela Wisshorn and the Schwarzhorn rising on either side. Davos, the highest town of its size in the Alps, is ten and a half kilometres away, and Zernez about twelve.

    Read the complete Flüelapass high section write-up.

  6. 6. Davos food and weather check

    162 km in. Davos food and weather check is where this day stops to eat. You lose 823 m on the way down to it, steadily. Reckon on about 7 °C colder than the valley, and more in the wind.

    Davos sits at about 1,560 metres in a long, high valley in eastern Graubünden, and it claims to be the highest city in Europe, which depends on how you count. It has about eleven thousand people and has been a health resort since the nineteenth century, when the dry mountain air was prescribed for tuberculosis, and a sanatorium culture grew up here. Thomas Mann, who visited his wife during her treatment in 1912, based his novel The Magic Mountain on the experience, and the expressionist painter Ernst Ludwig Kirchner moved to the valley and spent his last years working in the hills.

    Read the complete Davos food and weather check write-up.

  7. 7. Landquart return gate

    205 km in. You lose 1037 m on the way down to it, steadily.

    Landquart is a railway junction that turned into a town, and a good place to understand how Graubünden ties together. The village lies on the floor of the Rhine valley, a few kilometres north of Chur, at the mouth of the Landquart river, which comes down from the Prättigau and the high passes of the east. It is the point where the Rhaetian Railway, the narrow-gauge line that climbs to Davos and St. Moritz, leaves the main line, and the red trains wait at the platform beside the big Swiss ones. At 523 metres, it is low, mild and often warm, a contrast to the high Alps that lie ahead.

    Read the complete Landquart return gate write-up.

  9. 10. Lukmanier pass road

    303 km in. Lukmanier pass road tops the climb at 1915 m. Reckon on about 9 °C colder than the valley, and more in the wind. High enough to hold ice in the shaded corners early and late in the year.

    The Lukmanier Pass is the gentlest of the great crossings between the Rhine valley and Ticino, and at 1,915 metres it is also one of the lowest, which explains why it has been used for so long. Its lack of steepness made it a trade and pilgrim route well before the Gotthard became passable, and the monastery of Disentis on the north side owed much of its influence to the traffic.

    Read the complete Lukmanier pass road write-up.

  10. 11. Andermatt base reset

    359 km in. You lose 471 m on the way down to it, steadily.

    Andermatt sits in a high, flat valley called the Urseren, at 1,444 metres, where four of the great Swiss passes meet. The Gotthard heads south to Ticino, the Oberalp runs east toward Graubünden, the Furka climbs west to the Valais, and the road north follows the Reuss down through a gorge to Lake Lucerne. People have crossed here for at least eight hundred years, ever since the Devil's Bridge was thrown across the gorge to make the Gotthard route practical, and the village has served travellers and soldiers ever since.

    Read the complete Andermatt base reset write-up.

  11. 12. Oberalppass high road

    370 km in. You top out at 2044 m here. The road climbs 600 m on the way up to it, steadily. Reckon on about 10 °C colder than the valley, and more in the wind.

    The Oberalp is the pass that links Andermatt in the Urseren valley with Disentis in the Surselva, and at 2,044 metres it is the road's high point and a historic watershed. Just above the summit, the little Lake Toma is regarded as the source of the Rhine, the river that eventually runs through Germany and the Netherlands and into the North Sea. A short walk up from the car park gets you to the lakeshore, where a plaque marks the spot.

    Read the complete Oberalppass high road write-up.

  12. 13. Disentis food stop

    391 km in. You lose 914 m on the way down to it, steadily. Reckon on about 4 °C colder than the valley, and more in the wind.

    Disentis is the monastery town of the upper Rhine valley, and the Benedictine abbey that dominates it is one of the oldest in Switzerland, founded, according to tradition, around the year 700 by a wandering monk called Sigisbert with help from a local landowner. The current buildings are mostly baroque, with a huge church and an extensive cloister, and the monks still teach at the school attached to it. The village sits at 1,130 metres where the Medelser Rhine meets the Vorderrhein, and it has the compact feel of a place that was important long before the tourist trade arrived.

    Read the complete Disentis food stop write-up.

  13. 14. Vorderrhein valley flow

    421 km in. You lose 480 m on the way down to it, steadily.

    Ilanz calls itself the first town on the Rhine, and in a river that runs a thousand kilometres to the North Sea that is a neat claim to hold. It sits in the Surselva at 650 metres, a small, practical place of about two thousand people, where the Vorderrhein valley widens and the Lugnez valley comes in from the south. Fuel, bakeries and a supermarket are all here. Laax, the ski resort village, is a few kilometres up the slope, and Flims lies beyond it.

    Read the complete Vorderrhein valley flow write-up.
