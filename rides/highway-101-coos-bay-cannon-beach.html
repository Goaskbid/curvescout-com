Why this ride

A one-way ride of 718 km, Coos Bay to Cannon Beach - roughly 9 h 51 in the saddle. On the way it passes Florence Oregon and Newport Oregon. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Coos Bay

    Coos Bay is a working harbour on the Oregon coast, and the working is visible. Stacks of timber and wood chips wait on the docks, ships load cargo, and the town's main street faces the water, with a mixture of old brick buildings and low modern shops. About sixteen thousand people live here, making it the largest city on the Oregon coast, and the bay itself is the largest natural harbour between San Francisco and the Columbia river, a fact that drove the logging and shipping industries for more than a century. A slow decline in timber has pushed the town to find other ways to earn a living.

  2. 2. Florence Oregon

    Florence sits at the mouth of the Siuslaw River, where the water meets the Pacific among tall dunes and spruce forest, and it is both a handsome old river port and the doorway to the largest expanse of coastal sand dunes in North America. The Oregon Dunes National Recreation Area runs for about eighty kilometres along the coast to the south, an extraordinary landscape of sand ridges up to sixty metres high, interspersed with forest islands, lakes and wetlands. Some dunes are used by dune buggies and others are quiet, protected zones where you can walk for hours and see no one.

  3. 3. Newport Oregon

    Newport is a working harbour first and a tourist town second, which is what makes it so likeable. The town sits on Yaquina Bay, where a long, graceful bridge swings across the water on a series of arches, one of the last great works of the engineer Conde McCullough, who designed many of the bridges along the Oregon coast and finished this one in 1936. Underneath, the bay front is crowded with fishing boats, crab pots and processing plants, and the barking of sea lions floats up from the docks, where they sprawl on the pontoons and ignore the visitors.

  4. 4. Cannon Beach

    Cannon Beach is known for a rock. Haystack Rock, a sea stack of basalt, rises 235 feet from the sand just off the shoreline, and at low tide you can walk right up to it and look into the pools at its base, where starfish, anemones and crabs cling to the stone. In spring and summer, tufted puffins nest on the upper ledges, along with gulls and cormorants, and volunteers set up scopes so that visitors can see the birds. The rock is a protected refuge, so climbing is forbidden, and giving the nesting birds a wide berth is part of the rules. It also played a starring role in the 1985 film The Goonies, which brings its own crowd each summer.
