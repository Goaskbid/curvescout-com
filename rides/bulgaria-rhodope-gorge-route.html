Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Plovdiv

    Plovdiv is one of the oldest continuously lived-in cities in Europe, and it wears its age with a kind of cheerful disorder. People have lived on these rocky hills beside the Maritsa River for some eight thousand years, and the Romans, who called the city Philippopolis, left a theatre that is still used for concerts, carved into the hillside and facing out across the rooftops. The name comes from Philip II of Macedon, who took the town in the fourth century BC. With about three hundred thousand people, it is Bulgaria's second city, and it has every kind of shop and fuel station you might need.

  2. 2. Asenovgrad

    Asenovgrad is the last proper town before the Rhodope Mountains, and it sits where the Thracian plain, flat, warm and full of vineyards, runs up against a wall of dark, forested hills. The town is large, with over fifty thousand people, and its streets, churches and markets feel Bulgarian in every detail. It was named after Tsar Ivan Asen II, the medieval ruler who rebuilt the nearby fortress, and the name of that fortress, Asen's Fortress, is what has made the place known to travellers.

  3. 3. Bachkovo

    Bachkovo Monastery stands in a narrow valley on the Chepelare river, a few kilometres into the Rhodope Mountains, and it is the second largest monastery in Bulgaria after Rila. It was founded in 1083 by Gregory Pakourianos, a Georgian-born commander in the service of the Byzantine Empire, who built it as a retreat and a fortress, and for centuries it has been a centre of Orthodox faith and Bulgarian culture. Its stone walls, wooden balconies and courtyard trees have a calm that is quite at odds with the busy road outside.

  4. 4. Shiroka Laka

    Shiroka Laka is what a Rhodope village looks like when someone has decided not to knock it down. Tall stone houses with whitewashed upper floors and overhanging wooden balconies stack up the hillside above a small river, joined by lanes of cobbles that are steep enough to test a pair of motorcycle boots. The roofs are of heavy stone slabs, the walls are thick, and the whole village has been declared an architectural reserve, so the character of the place has been preserved with unusual care.

  5. 5. Trigrad Gorge

    Trigrad Gorge is where the Rhodope Mountains show their teeth. The Trigradska river has cut a deep, narrow canyon through the limestone, with walls that rise some three hundred metres on either side, and the road runs along the bottom of it with the sound of water in your ear. Sunlight arrives late and leaves early, and the air is cool even in summer. The gorge sits close to the village of Trigrad, a small Rhodope settlement, with Devin, a spa town of about seven thousand, some sixteen kilometres away and the best bet for fuel and food.
