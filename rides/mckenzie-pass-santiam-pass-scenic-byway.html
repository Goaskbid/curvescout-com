Why this ride

A 117 km loop that starts and ends near Sisters, about 1 h 53 of riding. On the way it passes Dee Wright Observatory, McKenzie Pass, McKenzie Bridge and Santiam Pass. The highest point is about 1,600 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sisters Oregon

    The Three Sisters, a trio of volcanic peaks each over ten thousand feet, stand over the town of Sisters, and the town makes an easy base for one of the most satisfying loops in the Pacific Northwest. Sisters sits on the dry eastern side of the Cascades, where ponderosa pine and juniper replace the wet forest on the other side, and its main street is lined with wooden storefronts in an old-west style. Every July it hosts an outdoor quilt show that fills the streets with hundreds of hanging quilts and thousands of visitors, which tells you something about the town's sense of humour.

  2. 2. Dee Wright Observatory

    There is a low building of black lava rock on the summit of McKenzie Pass that looks as if it grew out of the ground, and in a sense it did, since every stone in it came from the lava fields around it. Dee Wright Observatory was built by Civilian Conservation Corps workers in the 1930s, in the style of a medieval watchtower, with arched openings and a flat roof from which you can look out over an ocean of broken black basalt. It was named for a local packer and guide who had worked on the old road.

  3. 3. McKenzie Pass

    McKenzie Pass has a split personality. On the western side, the road climbs through damp forests of Douglas fir and hemlock, past waterfalls and moss, and then, quite suddenly, you emerge into an apocalyptic field of black lava. The Cascade Range has been erupting for a long time, and the lava fields at the top of the pass, left by volcanic activity that continued until a few thousand years ago, stretch away on both sides in jagged, frozen waves, broken only by the stunted trees that have gained a foothold. At 5,325 feet, about 1,623 metres, the pass is a high, windy, exposed place, with the snowy peaks of the Three Sisters rising above the lava and Mount Washington, a spire of eroded volcanic rock, standing alone to the north.

  4. 4. McKenzie Bridge

    McKenzie Bridge is a handful of buildings along the McKenzie River on the western flank of the Oregon Cascades, and it is one of those places that remains a village because the forest around it is too large to leave room for anything else. The river here runs cold and clear, a startling blue-green over pale stones, between banks of Douglas fir, western hemlock and cedar. The air smells of wet bark and rain, and the moss on every trunk and rock gives the whole landscape a soft, cushioned look.

  5. 5. Santiam Pass

    Santiam Pass is the broad, forested saddle where US 20 crosses the crest of the Oregon Cascades, at about 4,800 feet, and it has a more complicated personality than the gentle name suggests. On the western side the road climbs from the wet valleys through dark Douglas fir and hemlock, and on the east it drops into dry ponderosa pine and the high desert. The pass is the dividing line between two climates, and you can feel the change in the air within a few miles, from damp and mossy to dry and resinous.
