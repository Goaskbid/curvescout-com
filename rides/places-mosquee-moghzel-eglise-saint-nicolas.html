Why this ride

From Midoun it runs 66 km to Midoun; allow about 1 h 08 of riding. On the way it passes Mosquée Sidi Zekri, Eglise Saint Nicolas and back through Mosquée Sidi Zekri.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mosquée Moghzel

    Djerba is a flat, low island off the south coast of Tunisia, joined to the mainland by a Roman causeway and a ferry, and its landscape of olive groves, palms and whitewashed farmhouses is dotted with small mosques that look more like fortresses than places of prayer. The Moghzel mosque is one of them. Djerba has a long Ibadi tradition, a branch of Islam that is neither Sunni nor Shia, and many of the island's villages are Berber speaking, with architecture suited to hard times: thick walls, buttresses, small domes, and a plain whitewashed look that fits the sun and the sea.

  2. 2. Mosquée Sidi Zekri

    Sidi Zekri is one of the many small religious buildings of Djerba, an island where it sometimes seems that every field has a whitewashed dome in it. The name suggests a local holy man, the sort of marabout who is buried under a domed tomb and whose tomb draws believers seeking help with illness or family troubles. Many such places are small and quiet, and are looked after by neighbours rather than by any big institution.

  3. 3. Eglise Saint Nicolas

    Houmt Souk is the island's main town, a place of whitewashed arcades, small squares and a busy fishing harbour, and the church of St Nicolas gives it a reminder that Djerba has never been exclusively one thing. The island has been home to Muslim, Jewish and Christian communities for centuries, and its location on the trade routes of the central Mediterranean brought Phoenicians, Romans, Arabs, Normans, Spaniards and Ottomans. In 1560, a Spanish and allied fleet was defeated nearby by an Ottoman force under Dragut, and the victors built a tower of the skulls of the dead beside the harbour, which stood until the nineteenth century. A monument now marks the site.
