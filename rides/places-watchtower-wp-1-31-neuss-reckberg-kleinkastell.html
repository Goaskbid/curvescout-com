Why this ride

292 km from Neuwied to Neuwied, about 4 h 09 of riding. On the way it passes Kottenforst Nord | Am Weißen Stein 2, Neuss-Reckberg | Kleinkastell and back through Kottenforst Nord | Am Weißen Stein 2. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Watchtower WP 1/31

    Start here.

    The Roman frontier in Germany was not a wall in the way that Hadrian's was in Britain. It was a line of wooden towers, small forts and a ditch and palisade, running for hundreds of kilometres from the Rhine near Neuwied to the Danube, and the stretch here, near Melsbach, is where it began. The Upper Germanic-Raetian Limes, as it is formally called, is a World Heritage Site, and the numbered watchtowers, of which this is one, were the eyes of the frontier.

    Read the complete Watchtower WP 1/31 write-up.

  2. 2. Kottenforst Nord | Am Weißen Stein 2

    83 km in.

    Bornheim is asparagus country, and if you ride past the Kottenforst in late spring you will see why. The sandy soil on the Rhine terraces just south of Cologne suits the white spears perfectly, and from roughly April to the end of June the farm stands along the roads sell them by the kilo, along with strawberries that follow soon afterwards. This is the Vorgebirge, a low ridge between the Rhine plain and the Eifel foothills, long used for market gardening and orchards, and the Weißer Stein stop sits in the woodland at its edge.

    Read the complete Kottenforst Nord | Am Weißen Stein 2 write-up.

  3. 3. Neuss-Reckberg | Kleinkastell

    150 km in.

    The Rhine was the northern frontier of the Roman Empire for centuries, and the string of forts, towers and camps along its western bank is now recognised as the Lower Germanic Limes, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2021.
