Why this ride

138 km one way from Freudenstadt - plan on about 2 h 15 of riding. It goes by way of Triberg, Titisee and Feldberg. The highest point is about 1,250 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Freudenstadt

    Freudenstadt was built to a plan. In 1599, Duke Frederick I of Württemberg laid out the town as a refuge for Protestants fleeing persecution in Austria, and the heart of it is a vast market square, one of the largest in Germany, an almost perfect square about two hundred metres on each side, with covered arcades along its edges. There was meant to be a castle in the middle, but it was never built, and the open space became the town's living room instead. The town, with about twenty-four thousand people, was almost destroyed at the end of the Second World War and rebuilt in a similar style.

  2. 2. Triberg

    Triberg is built in a steep, narrow fold of the Black Forest, a town that seems to have been poured down a gorge and set hard. Houses climb both sides of the Gutach valley, rooftops overlap, and the main street runs steeply uphill past shops full of cuckoo clocks, carved wooden bears and cherry schnapps. It is touristy, no question, but the tourists come because the place is the real thing, the heartland of the Black Forest clock trade, where families have been making timepieces in workshops since the eighteenth century.

  3. 3. Titisee

    Titisee is the lake that every German knows, and the odd thing is that it is a very small lake. It was carved by a glacier that flowed down from the Feldberg in the last ice age, and it lies in a hollow about two kilometres long, dammed by a moraine, with forested slopes rising on all sides. The water is dark and cool, the shoreline is tidy, and a legend credits the Roman emperor Titus with the name, though most scholars suspect a less glamorous origin. A hundred years ago it was a quiet mountain spot. Today it is a summer playground with a promenade full of shops selling cuckoo clocks, cherry liqueur and Black Forest ham.

  4. 4. Feldberg

    The Feldberg is the highest mountain in the Black Forest and in Germany outside the Alps, at 1,493 metres, and it behaves like a real mountain. It is a broad, bald dome of grass and heath rising above the surrounding forest, with a radio mast and an observation tower on the summit and, on its eastern flank, the dark cirque of the Feldsee, a small lake in a glacial bowl. During the last ice age, the Feldberg carried its own ice cap, and the valleys around it still show the carved, U-shaped marks. The climate is rough, with wind, fog and snow lasting well into spring.

  5. 5. Waldshut

    Waldshut is a small town on the High Rhine, with Switzerland on the opposite bank, and its character is that of a border place with a long, slightly cantankerous history. The old town, a neat street running between two gates, the Upper Gate and the Lower Gate, is painted in cheerful colours and has the feel of a medieval fortress trimmed for peacetime. Waldshut was part of Habsburg Austria for centuries, and in 1524 it became one of the first towns to join the Peasants' War, led by the preacher Balthasar Hubmaier, who was later burned in Vienna. The twin town of Tiengen, a few kilometres away, has an old castle and its own market.
