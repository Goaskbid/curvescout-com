Why this ride

A 272 km round trip from Ontinyent - roughly 4 h 41 in the saddle. On the way it passes Port de Penaguila- Abric II, Barranc d'Alpadull- Abric III, Penya de Benicadell and Barranc de l'Infern Conj. 1. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abric de la Creu

    Ontinyent and Bocairent sit close together in the southern part of Valencia province, in the upper valley of the Clariano river, surrounded by the mountains of the Serra de Mariola and the Serra Grossa. Ontinyent is a textile town with a fine old quarter on a hill above the river, crowned by the tall bell tower of the church of Santa Maria. Bocairent, a few kilometres away, is a medieval village perched on a rocky outcrop above a ravine. The Abric de la Creu, the shelter of the cross, lies in the hills between the two.

  2. 2. Port de Penaguila- Abric II

    The Port de Penàguila is a mountain pass in the interior of Alicante province, on the road between Alcoy and the Vall de Guadalest, where the hills of the Comtat rise toward the Serra d'Aitana, the highest range in the province. The village of Penàguila, below the pass, is a small, ancient place huddled beneath a castle on a rocky crest, and Benilloba, Cocentaina and Alcoy are all close. The second shelter of the Port de Penàguila is one of several painted sites in the area.

  3. 3. Barranc d'Alpadull- Abric III

    Murla sits in the Vall de Pop, a broad valley of vines, oranges and almonds where the Girona river runs down from the mountains towards the coast at El Verger. The ravine of Alpadull cuts into the hills above, and in one of its shelters, the third along, there are prehistoric paintings, part of the great scatter of rock art across Mediterranean Spain that UNESCO lists as a single site. Like most of these shelters, it is better approached as a reason to wander a beautiful valley than as a spectacle in itself.

  4. 4. Penya de Benicadell

    From almost anywhere in the southern half of Valencia province you can see it: a long, jagged ridge of grey limestone with a sharp tooth at its eastern end, standing on the skyline like a broken saw. The Benicadell, at 1,104 metres, separates the Vall d'Albaida to the north from the Comtat to the south, and the sharp summit, the Penya, is the mountain's signature. At its foot lie the villages of Beniarrés, Ràfol de Salem and Otos.

  5. 5. Barranc de l'Infern Conj. 1

    Nobody builds a gallery in a ravine called Hell without a reason, and the first group of paintings in the Barranc de l'Infern shows what the reason probably was: shade, shelter and a view of whatever walked up the valley. This is conjunto 1 in the numbering archaeologists use, one of several clusters of Levantine rock art in the Vall de Laguar, in the mountains of the Marina Alta, and it is part of the UNESCO-listed Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin.
