  1. 1. Reykjavik

    Reykjavík is a small capital of about a hundred and twenty thousand people that behaves like a bigger one. The city sits on a windy peninsula in the south-west, with the sea on three sides and a ring of mountains across the bay, and it is heated almost entirely by geothermal water, piped in from the ground. The outdoor swimming pools, filled with hot water, are the social centre of the city, and a morning soak with the neighbours is part of local life. The landmarks are the concrete tower of Hallgrímskirkja, whose design echoes the basalt columns of Iceland, and the glass facade of the Harpa concert hall by the harbour.
