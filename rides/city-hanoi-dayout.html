Why this ride

211 km from Hanoi to Hanoi, about 3 h 38 of riding. The stops in between: Bo Da Pagoda, Kiep Bac Temple, Vinh Nghiem Pagoda and Con Son Pagoda.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hanoi

    Hanoi starts the day with a roar, and the best advice is to join it calmly. This is a city of millions on the Red River, with more motorbikes than anyone has bothered to count, and the Old Quarter, with its thirty-six old guild streets named after the goods once sold there, is the loud, charming core. Hoàn Kiếm lake in the middle is where people jog, practise tai chi and sit eating ice cream at dusk, and the little Ngoc Son temple on its island is joined to the shore by a red wooden bridge.

  2. 2. Bo Da Pagoda

    Bổ Đà is a pagoda that was built from the earth up, quite literally. Its long perimeter walls are made of rammed earth, mixed with broken pottery and tiles, a traditional technique that has kept them standing for centuries, and inside lies one of the most remarkable features of any Vietnamese temple: a garden of more than a thousand stupas, the tombs of monks who lived and died here, standing in rows among the trees. The pagoda, on a hill in Việt Yên in Bắc Giang province, is also famous for its collection of wooden printing blocks carved with Buddhist texts, a treasure of Vietnamese woodblock printing.

  3. 3. Kiep Bac Temple

    Kiếp Bạc is the place where Vietnam remembers Trần Hưng Đạo, the thirteenth-century general who defeated the Mongols three times. In the 1280s, the armies of Kublai Khan's empire invaded Vietnam, and Trần Hưng Đạo, commanding the forces of the Trần dynasty, used guerrilla tactics, retreat and ambush to wear them down. The most famous victory came in 1288 on the Bạch Đằng river, when he planted iron-tipped stakes in the riverbed and lured the Mongol fleet onto them at low tide. He died in 1300, and the temple at Kiếp Bạc was built where he is said to have had his base.

  4. 4. Vinh Nghiem Pagoda

    In the countryside of Bắc Giang province, in northern Vietnam, about fifty kilometres north-east of Hanoi, the Vinh Nghiem Pagoda stands by the Thương river, surrounded by rice fields and villages. It is one of the oldest and most important Buddhist centres in Vietnam, associated with the Trúc Lâm school of Zen Buddhism, founded in the thirteenth century by King Trần Nhân Tông, who abdicated to become a monk.

  5. 5. Con Son Pagoda

    Côn Sơn lies in the pine-covered hills of Chí Linh, north-east of Hanoi, on the border of Hải Dương province, and it carries two of the great names of Vietnamese history. In the thirteenth century the Trần dynasty beat back three Mongol invasions, and its monks and commanders left a trail of temples, of which Côn Sơn, Kiếp Bạc and the sacred mountain of Yên Tử are the best known. Trúc Lâm, the Vietnamese Zen school founded by King Trần Nhân Tông, who gave up his throne to become a monk, has one of its centres here.
