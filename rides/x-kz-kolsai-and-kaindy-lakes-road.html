Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Almaty

    Almaty means place of apples, or near enough, and the apples are not just a name. Wild apple forests, the ancestors of many of the world's cultivated varieties, still grow on the slopes of the Tian Shan mountains above the city, and botanists believe the fruit began its long journey to the world's orchards somewhere in this region. For most of the twentieth century Almaty was the capital of Kazakhstan, until the government moved to Astana in 1997, and it remains the country's biggest and most relaxed city, with wide tree-lined avenues, Soviet blocks and a skyline of snow peaks to the south.

  2. 2. Saty

    Saty is a village at the end of a good road, and it looks it. Here the steppe gives way to green pastures dotted with spruce, and in summer, herders bring their horses and sheep up from the valleys and live in yurts on the hillsides. The air is clean and cold, the skies are huge, and the few buildings look small beside the mountains.

  3. 3. Kolsai Lakes

    The Kolsai Lakes are a chain of three alpine lakes strung up a valley in the Kungey Alatau, a range on the border with Kyrgyzstan. The lowest lake sits at roughly 1,800 metres, the middle at about 2,250 and the top at around 2,700, and each is smaller and colder than the last. They are often called the Pearls of the Tian Shan, which is the sort of name that sounds like marketing until you see the water, a cold, deep blue-green framed in dark spruce forest and sharp ridgelines.

  4. 4. Kaindy Lake gate

    Kaindy is a lake with a ghostly forest. When an earthquake shook the Tian Shan in January 1911, a landslide dammed a valley, and the water that backed up flooded a stand of Schrenk's spruce. The trunks of the drowned trees still stand upright in the water, bare and pale, preserved by the cold, and the lake itself is a deep, clear turquoise. It lies at around two thousand metres, in the Kolsai Lakes National Park, in the mountains of south-eastern Kazakhstan, close to the border with Kyrgyzstan.
