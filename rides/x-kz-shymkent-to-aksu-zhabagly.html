  1. 1. Shymkent

    Shymkent is the big, hot, unpretentious city of the south. With more than four hundred thousand people, it is the third largest city in Kazakhstan and, since a reform a few years ago, administered separately from the surrounding region. It lies close to the Uzbek border, in a green, irrigated plain below the western Tian Shan, and it feels different from the rest of the country: more Silk Road bazaar than steppe outpost, with a mix of Kazakh, Uzbek, Russian and other communities and a food scene to match. The name is often connected with a word for a green place, which suits an oasis.

  2. 2. Aksu-Zhabagly

    Aksu-Zhabagly is Kazakhstan's oldest nature reserve, founded in 1926, and it sits where the Western Tian Shan meets the end of the Talas Alatau. The protected area covers a wide altitude range, from steppe foothills to glaciers, and the result is a startling variety of habitats in a small space: juniper forest, meadow, scree, high snowfields and deep canyons carved by rivers with names like Aksu, meaning white water. Wildlife includes ibex, argali, brown bear, lynx and, in the high country, snow leopard, though your chances of seeing the cat are close to zero.
