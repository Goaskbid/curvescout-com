Why this ride

416 km round trip from Sciliar-Catinaccio, about 8 h 41 of riding. It runs out through Pale di San Martino San Lucano - Dolomiti, Le Colline del Prosecco di Conegliano e Va, Villa Emo and Scrovegni Chapel Church of the Eremitani and comes back by another road. It tops out around 1,800 m.

The roads

Great Dolomites Road

It is 72 km from Sciliar-Catinaccio to Pale di San Martino San Lucano - Dolomiti . Up about 740 m to 1,835 m, then 1450 m back down the far side. Take a break before the climb, not halfway up.

The stops

  1. 1. Sciliar-Catinaccio

    Start here.

    Watch the Rosengarten at sunset from the valley of Bolzano and you will understand the legend. As the sun goes down, the pale dolomite peaks glow pink, then red, then purple, a phenomenon the Ladin people call enrosadira. According to the old story, King Laurin of the dwarves had a magnificent rose garden here, and when he was captured by enemies who had spotted his roses, he cursed the garden so that no one would see it by day or by night. He forgot the twilight, and so, for a few minutes each evening, the roses bloom again on the rocks. Together with the Sciliar, or Schlern, the great flat-topped massif to the north, the Catinaccio forms one of the components of the Dolomites UNESCO World Heritage site, listed in 2009.

  2. 2. Pale di San Martino San Lucano - Dolomiti

    72 km in.

    The Val di San Lucano is a deep, shadowed gash in the southern Dolomites, and the point here lies near Rivamonte and Voltago, in the Agordino, the upper valley of the Cordevole river. The big, pale walls on either side belong to the Pale di San Martino group, one of the nine sets of mountains included in the Dolomites World Heritage listing of 2009, and their sheer rock faces rise more than a thousand metres above the narrow valley floor.

    Read the complete Pale di San Martino San Lucano - Dolomiti write-up.

  3. 3. Le Colline del Prosecco di Conegliano e Va

    143 km in.

    These are not soft, rolling wine hills. Between Conegliano and Valdobbiadene, the foothills of the Pre-Alps rise in steep hogback ridges, sharp crests of rock running east to west, with vines clinging to slopes so steep that much of the work is still done by hand. Grass-covered terraces called ciglioni hold the soil in place, and the result is a patchwork of vines, woods and small villages that UNESCO listed in 2019.

    Read the complete Le Colline del Prosecco di Conegliano e Va write-up.

  4. 4. Villa Emo

    189 km in.

    Andrea Palladio built villas for the Venetian nobility as working farms, not just as country houses, and Villa Emo at Fanzolo is perhaps the purest example of that idea. Completed around 1560 for the Emo family, it stands at the centre of a flat, fertile estate, with a long, symmetrical central block flanked by two arcaded wings, the barchesse, which housed the farm buildings, storage and dovecotes. The ramp leading up to the front door was used for threshing grain. Inside, the rooms are decorated with frescoes by Giovanni Battista Zelotti, mythological and allegorical scenes that bring colour and life to the classical architecture. The villa is part of the UNESCO listing of Vicenza and the Palladian Villas of the Veneto.

    Read the complete Villa Emo write-up.

  5. 5. Scrovegni Chapel Church of the Eremitani

    229 km in.

    Giotto painted the Scrovegni Chapel in about two years, between 1303 and 1305, and it changed how Europeans drew people. Before him faces were icons; here they are tired, tender, furious, ashamed. The chapel was commissioned by Enrico Scrovegni, whose father Reginaldo was a moneylender so notorious that Dante put him in hell. Enrico built the chapel beside the family palace, so the story goes, to ease his father's soul, and the result is a small brick box covered inside with a blue ceiling of gold stars and thirty-odd scenes from the lives of Mary and Christ, ending with a vast Last Judgment on the west wall.
