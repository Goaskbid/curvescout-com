  1. 1. Cochabamba

    Cochabamba lies in a broad, warm valley at around 2,550 metres, and the climate is what the locals brag about first: dry, sunny, and mild for most of the year, which earned it the name City of Eternal Spring. It is Bolivia's food capital, a city of nearly a million people if you count the whole urban area, spreading across the valley floor with the Tunari range rising behind it.

  2. 2. Torotoro

    Torotoro is one of those places where the road ends and the real attraction begins. The national park lies in the highlands south-east of Cochabamba, in a landscape of red cliffs, narrow canyons and cave systems carved into folded limestone and sandstone, and the village, a small, quiet place with a plaza and a handful of guesthouses, sits at its edge. There is no larger town close by, so everything you need has to come with you or be found in the village.
