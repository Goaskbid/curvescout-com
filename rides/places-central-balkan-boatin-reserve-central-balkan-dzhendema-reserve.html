Why this ride

From Anton it runs 668 km to Anton; allow about 17 h 34 of riding. On the way it passes Central Balkan - Steneto Reserve, Central Balkan - Peesh skali Reserve, Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak and Central Balkan - Dzhendema Reserve. It tops out around 1,050 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

The roads

Central Balkan - Peesh skali Reserve to Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak

Central Balkan - Peesh skali Reserve to Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak: 93 km, mostly on road 5004. A proper up-and-over: 760 m of climbing, 1,011 m at the top, 850 m down. Take a break before the climb, not halfway up.

The stops

  1. 1. Central Balkan - Boatin Reserve

    Start here.

    The Balkan range, the Stara Planina, runs across the middle of Bulgaria from the Serbian border to the Black Sea, and its central section is the wildest, highest and most heavily forested part. The Central Balkan National Park protects this area, and within it, the Boatin Reserve guards a stretch of ancient beech forest on the northern slopes, one of the components of the Ancient and Primeval Beech Forests of the Carpathians and Other Regions of Europe, a UNESCO site that stretches across many countries.

    Read the complete Central Balkan - Boatin Reserve write-up.

  2. 2. Central Balkan - Steneto Reserve

    105 km in.

    Steneto is a deep, wild valley on the northern slopes of the Balkan range in central Bulgaria, enclosed by cliffs and covered in ancient beech forest, and it is one of the strict reserves of Central Balkan National Park, part of the Primeval Beech Forests World Heritage listing. Access is restricted, and nature is left to itself: old trees fall and rot, and the forest is home to bears, wolves, wild boar and a rich variety of birds, including several species of woodpecker and owl. The Cherni Osam river flows through the valley, and its clear waters are home to trout.

    Read the complete Central Balkan - Steneto Reserve write-up.

  3. 3. Central Balkan - Peesh skali Reserve

    193 km in. The ride passes about 6 km from it.

    Peesh skali means singing rocks, after the way the wind howls and whistles through the cliffs and crags of this reserve on the slopes of the central Balkan range. It lies on the northern side of the main ridge, not far from Botev, at 2,376 metres the highest peak of the Balkan mountains, and it protects beech and spruce forests, alpine meadows and rocky slopes, part of Central Balkan National Park. Chamois live on the crags, and bears, wolves and golden eagles roam the area. Access to the reserve is restricted, but trails in the surrounding park offer spectacular walks.

    Read the complete Central Balkan - Peesh skali Reserve write-up.

  4. 4. Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak

    286 km in.

    In 1944 soldiers digging a shelter on the edge of Kazanlak broke into a small brick chamber and found themselves looking at some of the finest ancient paintings in the Balkans. The Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak, built in the fourth century BC for a ruler of the Thracians, has a narrow corridor leading to a round, beehive-shaped burial chamber, its dome covered with frescoes of a funeral feast: the dead man and his wife seated at a table, servants bringing gifts, horses and chariots racing around the top. The colours and the tenderness of the figures are extraordinary. It was listed by UNESCO in 1979.

    Read the complete Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak write-up.

  5. 5. Central Balkan - Dzhendema Reserve

    334 km in.

    Above the little town of Kalofer, the southern wall of the Balkan range rises almost vertically, cut by deep gorges with waterfalls and covered in old beech and fir forest, and up there lies the Dzhendema reserve, one of the strict nature reserves within Central Balkan National Park. The name is said to come from a Turkish word for hell, a fair description of its steep, wild terrain. Above the reserve, Botev Peak, the highest summit of the Balkan mountains at 2,376 metres, stands at the heart of the park. Bears, wolves, chamois and wild goats roam these slopes, and the old beech forests of the park are part of the transnational World Heritage site of the Ancient and Primeval Beech Forests of Europe.

    Read the complete Central Balkan - Dzhendema Reserve write-up.
