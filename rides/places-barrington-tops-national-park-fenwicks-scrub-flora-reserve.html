Why this ride

The stops in between: Mt Seaview Nature Reserve, Willi Willi NP, Fenwicks Scrub Flora Reserve and back through Willi Willi NP. The highest point is about 950 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barrington Tops National Park

    Barrington Tops is the high, cold, wet plateau at the top of the Hunter and Manning catchments in New South Wales, and it feels like a different continent from the dry coast an hour to the east. The range is a remnant of an ancient volcanic landscape, and its altitude, well over a thousand metres on the plateau, means snow gums, frost in winter and the odd dusting of snow. It is part of the Gondwana Rainforests World Heritage Area, and the forests here hold Antarctic beech, a tree that was once spread across the ancient southern supercontinent and now survives only in a few cold, damp pockets.

  2. 2. Mt Seaview Nature Reserve

    Mount Seaview is a high point on the edge of the tableland where the New South Wales Northern Tablelands begin to drop toward the Pacific, and the nature reserve there protects a patch of forest that gets more rain and mist than almost anywhere around. The land is rugged, with steep slopes and tangled gullies, and the vegetation is a blend of dry eucalypt forest on the ridges and cool rainforest in the damp folds. This is the Hastings Range, the eastern flank of the Great Dividing Range, and the forests belong to the larger Gondwana Rainforests of Australia, whose lineage reaches back to the time when the continent was part of a great southern landmass.

  3. 3. Willi Willi NP

    The coast of northern New South Wales is famous for beaches, but a few hours inland the land climbs into a different world of ridges, gorges and old rainforest. Willi Willi National Park lies in the hills behind Kempsey and Port Macquarie, in country that belongs to the Dunghutti people, and it protects a patch of that wilder landscape. The region is part of the broad belt of rainforest and wet eucalypt forest that runs up the Great Dividing Range, the remnants of an ancient forest that once covered the supercontinent of Gondwana.

  4. 4. Fenwicks Scrub Flora Reserve

    Werrikimbe National Park lies on the rim of the Great Escarpment in northern New South Wales, about a hundred kilometres inland from Port Macquarie, and it belongs to the Gondwana Rainforests of Australia, a World Heritage area that preserves remnants of the forests that covered the ancient supercontinent. The landscape is high and wet, a plateau of eucalypt forest, tree ferns, swamp and cliffs where rivers begin their descent to the coast. Fenwicks Scrub is a small patch of this vegetation, a pocket of rainforest in the folds of the hills.
