  1. 1. Berat

    Berat is called the town of a thousand windows, and for once the nickname is just. Ottoman-era white houses climb the hillside above the River Osum in tiers, their tall windows stacked like the faces of a theatre. On the north bank, the Mangalem quarter rises to a fortress, the Kala, whose walls still enclose a living neighbourhood, with families, churches and small cafes inside; on the south bank, Gorica faces it across a stone bridge. UNESCO inscribed the old town with Gjirokastër in 2008, and it is among the most attractive in the Balkans.

  2. 2. Poliçan

    Poliçan is an unglamorous town in the hills south-east of Berat, and it is the sort of place that rewards a bike rider more than a sightseer. It grew in the communist period around an industrial plant, a factory complex built for the production of ammunition and other metalwork, and the apartment blocks and wide streets still show that planned past. Today the plant is mostly a shadow of what it was, and the town of roughly ten thousand people lives on small business, remittances and the traffic of people heading for the Osum valley.

  3. 3. Çorovodë

    Çorovodë is the main town of the Skrapar region, and it sits at the upper end of one of the most photographed gorges in Albania. The Osum river leaves the mountains here through a canyon cut into pale limestone, with walls up to a hundred metres high and clear green pools below, and in spring, when the river is high, rafting groups come from Berat and Tirana to run it. The town itself is a modest place of apartment blocks and low houses, standing on a plateau above the river, with high ridges to the east and the long chain of the Tomorr massif visible to the north-west on a clear day.
