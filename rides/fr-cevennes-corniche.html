Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Florac

    Florac sits where several small rivers and several landscapes meet, at the foot of the great limestone plateaus of the Causses and the schist ridges of the Cévennes. A small town of nearly two thousand people, it is the headquarters of the Cévennes National Park, which occupies the old château, and it has a cheerful, unfussy air, with plane trees along the main street and a spring that gushes out of the rock just above town. The Tarnon river runs through, and the Tarn, a little way north, starts its journey toward the famous gorges.

  2. 2. Saint-Jean-du-Gard

    On a day in early October 1878, Robert Louis Stevenson ended his walk through the Cévennes at Saint-Jean-du-Gard, and sold his donkey. The Scottish writer, then twenty-seven, had spent twelve days crossing the mountains with a stubborn grey animal called Modestine, and he recorded the adventure in Travels with a Donkey in the Cévennes, one of the first books about hiking for pleasure. The route he followed is now a long-distance path, the GR70, the Chemin de Stevenson, and walkers with sticks and rucksacks are a common sight in the town.
