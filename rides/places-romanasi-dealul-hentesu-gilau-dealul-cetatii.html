Why this ride

A 236 km round trip from Românaşi - roughly 5 h in the saddle. On the way it passes Hurez - La Frasin 1, Bologa - Măgura Bologii, Gilău - Dealul Cetății and back through Bologa - Măgura Bologii. It tops out around 950 m. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Românași - Dealul Hențeșu

    Start here.

    Hentesu is the name of a hill near the village of Romanasi, in the Salaj hills of north-western Romania, and Dealul Hentesu is simply Hentesu Hill. It is a modest rise, but in this country of broad rounded ridges it commands good views.

    Read the complete Românași - Dealul Hențeșu write-up.

  2. 2. Hurez - La Frasin 1

    25 km in.

    This corner of Sălaj sits along the Roman frontier of Dacia, which makes it a more serious place than its gentle hills suggest. West of Zalău the Meseș mountains rise in a long wooded ridge, and through them runs a natural gate, the Meseș Pass, which the Romans fortified as part of their northern defences. The great site of Porolissum, with its fort, civil settlement and amphitheatre, stands on a ridge nearby, and the watchtowers and small works scattered across the hills belong to the same system.

    Read the complete Hurez - La Frasin 1 write-up.

  3. 3. Bologa - Măgura Bologii

    58 km in.

    Bologa is a village in the valley of the Crișul Repede, the swift river that flows west from the hills of Cluj county towards Oradea and the Hungarian plain. On the hill above it, known as Măgura Bologii, lie the remains of a castle that once guarded the valley, and in the fields nearby are the traces of a Roman fort, built to protect the north western edge of the province of Dacia. The fort, known in antiquity as Resculum, is part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire, Dacia, listed by UNESCO in 2024, a line of forts, watchtowers and walls that ran along the edge of the Roman province from the second to the third century.

    Read the complete Bologa - Măgura Bologii write-up.

  4. 4. Gilău - Dealul Cetății

    116 km in.

    Gilau sits where the flat plain of the Somesul Mic river meets the first hills of the Apuseni mountains, about fifteen kilometres west of Cluj-Napoca, in Transylvania, and the hill called Dealul Cetatii, which simply means Fortress Hill, rises almost on the edge of the town, about a kilometre away. The town has nearly eight thousand people, and from the road you can see the high, forested ridges of the Apuseni to the west.

    Read the complete Gilău - Dealul Cetății write-up.
