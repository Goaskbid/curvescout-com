Why this ride

258 km from Jabaloyas to Jabaloyas, about 4 h 27 of riding. It goes by way of Peña del Escrito II, La Hoz de Vicente and back through Peña del Escrito II. The highest point is about 1,400 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranco del Pajarejo

    Red rock, dark pines and a sky that seems higher than it should be: that is the country south of Albarracín, where the Barranco del Pajarejo cuts into the sandstone. Geologists call this stone rodeno, a coarse red sandstone that weathers into towers, overhangs and smooth shelters, and prehistoric people used those shelters as canvases. The Pinares de Rodeno protected landscape around Albarracín holds a well-known group of painted shelters with bulls, deer and human figures in white and red, and this gully is part of that wider area, quiet and seldom visited.

  2. 2. Peña del Escrito II

    The wording is a clue: Peña del Escrito means the rock that is written on, and that is an old local way of describing a shelter with painted figures. This one lies in the Serranía de Cuenca, near the small village of Villar del Humo, in a country of deep gorges, pine woods and limestone cliffs. The paintings belong to the Levantine tradition, red figures of animals and people, and the area is recognised by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin of the Iberian peninsula.

  3. 3. La Hoz de Vicente

    The Cabriel is one of the few rivers in Spain that still runs clean and fast through a deep canyon, and the Hoz de Vicente stands on its upper stretch, where Cuenca meets Valencia. The Hoces del Cabriel are a natural park of limestone gorges, pine woods and river flats, with kingfishers, otters and, on the rock faces, griffon vultures. The Hoz de Vicente shelter is one of the painted places of this wild corner, a Levantine rock art site listed within the UNESCO group of the Mediterranean Basin.
