Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Thakhèk

    Thakhek is a sleepy Mekong river town in central Laos, and it is the traditional starting point of the Thakhek Loop, one of the great motorcycle routes in Southeast Asia. The town sits on the Lao bank of the river, facing the Thai city of Nakhon Phanom across the water, a ferry ride or a short hop over the Friendship Bridge. Old French colonial shophouses line the riverfront in faded yellows and greys, a reminder of the time when this was an administrative outpost of French Indochina. In the evening the promenade fills with locals jogging, playing takraw and eating grilled fish.

  2. 2. Thakhek Loop point 2

    For years, this was a remote area of thinly populated forest and grassland, home to ethnic minority communities and wildlife. In the 2000s it was transformed by the Nam Theun 2 project, a large dam and hydroelectric scheme that flooded the central part of the plateau and created a reservoir of several hundred square kilometres, and the villagers were resettled. The water is calm and still, ringed by drowned trees and low green hills, with a bit of an eerie beauty.

  3. 3. Thakhek Loop point 3

    Ban Na Hin is the small market town where the Thakhek Loop turns toward its most famous attraction, and it sits on Route 13 at the foot of a limestone ridge. A cluster of guesthouses, noodle shops and fuel stalls line the highway, and riders stop to eat, refuel and chat with other travellers before the turn to Konglor. The town is quiet, with few buildings of any size, but it is surrounded by amazing scenery of sheer limestone cliffs rising from green fields and forests.

  4. 4. Thakhek Loop point 4

    Konglor Cave is one of the great natural wonders of Southeast Asia, a limestone tunnel about seven kilometres long through which the Nam Hin Bun river flows from one side of a mountain to the other. Visitors travel through it by long wooden boats, powered by small outboard motors and steered by local guides, sitting in near darkness with only headlamps to light the way. The cavern is vast, with ceilings that disappear into blackness, and in places formations of stalactites are lit up in colour. Midway, you stop and walk through a chamber filled with towering shapes of rock.
