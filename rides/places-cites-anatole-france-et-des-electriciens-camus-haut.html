Why this ride

A 75 km round trip from Bruay-la-Buissière - roughly 1 h 26 in the saddle. It goes by way of Monument aux morts et grilles de la cité d, Camus Haut and back through Monument aux morts et grilles de la cité d.

The stops

  1. 1. Cités Anatole France et des Electriciens

    Start here.

    Bruay-la-Buissière is a town built on coal, and the rows of brick houses that spread around it were designed to keep miners close to their pits. The Cités Anatole France and des Électriciens belong to the great mining landscape of the Nord-Pas-de-Calais, a basin of coalfields, slag heaps and company towns that UNESCO inscribed in 2012. Coal was mined here from the early eighteenth century until the last pit closed in 1990, and the memory of that work is still everywhere.

    Read the complete Cités Anatole France et des Electriciens write-up.

  2. 2. Monument aux morts et grilles de la cité d

    21 km in.

    In the mining towns of northern France, the companies built not only pits and houses but whole communities, with churches, schools, dispensaries and, after the First World War, monuments to the dead. This war memorial and the iron gates of a mining cité in Mazingarbe are small parts of that story, included in the Nord-Pas de Calais Mining Basin World Heritage site. They stand among rows of brick houses, gardens and allotments in a town shaped by coal.

    Read the complete Monument aux morts et grilles de la cité d write-up.

  3. 3. Camus Haut

    37 km in.

    Camus Haut lies on the eastern edge of the mining basin, near Annay, Pont-à-Vendin and Harnes, where the canalised Deûle carries barges north towards Lille. The label refers to one of the many mining sites and cités of the area, and while the exact spot is hard to identify from a distance, the landscape tells you what kind of place it is: brick housing estates, old pit heads, spoil heaps going green, and the wide, flat horizon of the Gohelle.

    Read the complete Camus Haut write-up.
