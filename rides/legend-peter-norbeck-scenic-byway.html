Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Custer

    Custer sits on the southern flank of the Black Hills of South Dakota, a small town in a big forest, and it makes a fine base for some of the best motorcycle roads in the Midwest. It was named for Lieutenant Colonel George Armstrong Custer, whose 1874 expedition confirmed gold in the hills, and the rush that followed was one of the sparks of the war with the Lakota, for whom these hills, the Paha Sapa, are sacred. That history is not far below the surface anywhere around here, and it is worth keeping in mind.

  2. 2. Near Custer

    The Needles Highway was laid out in the 1920s by Peter Norbeck, governor of South Dakota and later senator, who scouted much of the route on foot and on horseback, and it threads between granite spires so close together that the road squeezes through a tunnel barely wider than a car. The Iron Mountain Road, the other half of the scenic byway that bears his name, has pigtail bridges, where the road loops over itself to gain height, and tunnels aligned to frame Mount Rushmore in the distance. Together they make one of the most remarkable rides in America.

  4. 4. Peter Norbeck Scenic Byway point 4

    Iron Mountain Road is the reason many riders come to the Black Hills, and it was designed to be exactly that kind of road. When South Dakota's governor and later senator Peter Norbeck laid out the route in the 1930s, he insisted that it should be a road to enjoy, not to hurry along, and he personally scouted much of it on foot and horseback. The result is a twisting, turning ribbon of asphalt through ponderosa pine forest, with pigtail bridges, narrow tunnels and constant changes of direction.
