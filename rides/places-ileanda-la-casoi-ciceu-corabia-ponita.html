Why this ride

A 136 km round trip from Ileanda - roughly 2 h 21 in the saddle. The stops in between: Muncel - Cărămidă, Dumbrăveni - Măgura, Ilișua - Țibleș and Ciceu - Corabia-Ponița.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ileanda - La Căsoi

    Ileanda is a village on the Someș, the big river of north-western Transylvania, at a point where the valley narrows between wooded hills on its way from Dej towards Jibou. The railway runs alongside the water, the road keeps company with both, and the hills on either side rise in long slopes of beech, hornbeam and pasture. La Căsoi is a local name for a spot among those hills; you will not find a monument here, just the landscape and whatever the season is doing to it.

  2. 2. Muncel - Cărămidă

    In northern Transylvania, where the Someș river winds through low hills between Dej and Beclean, the Roman Empire once ran out of land and patience. The province of Dacia, conquered by Trajan in the early second century, had a long and complicated frontier, and in 2024 parts of it were added to the UNESCO Frontiers of the Roman Empire. Muncel–Cărămidă is one of those components, a small military site above the valley near Poiana Blenchii, part of a network of watchtowers and forts that guarded the northern edge of the province. The big fort of Porolissum, near Zalău to the west, with its reconstructed gate, gives you a sense of what the bigger garrisons looked like.

  3. 3. Dumbrăveni - Măgura

    In the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, a fortress on a hill above the Someș valley near Ciceu belonged not to the rulers of Transylvania but to the princes of Moldavia, on the far side of the Carpathians. Stephen the Great received the castle of Ciceu and its estates as compensation from the Hungarian king, and the Moldavian princes held it for decades, a curious enclave in the middle of Transylvania. The castle was later destroyed, and only ruins remain on the hill above Ciceu-Corabia, but the story gives a sense of how fluid borders and loyalties were in this part of Europe.

  4. 4. Ilișua - Țibleș

    Ilișua is a small village near Uriu in the valley of the Someș Mare, and in Roman times it was the site of a fort guarding the northern frontier of Dacia. The fort, known in antiquity as Arcobara, housed a cavalry unit, and archaeologists have uncovered its walls, gates and buildings, along with inscriptions and finds now displayed in museums in the region. Like the other sites of the Roman frontier in Romania, it is part of a nomination for UNESCO world heritage, and like most of them, it is now largely fields and grassy banks, with little to see above ground but a great deal of history beneath.

  5. 5. Ciceu - Corabia-Ponița

    Ciceu is a name that carries more history than its size suggests. The ruins of Ciceu Castle stand on a high hill in the north of Transylvania, in the border country of Bistrița-Năsăud and Cluj counties, in the hill country between Dej and Bistrița. In the fifteenth century the fortress and its estate were granted by the Hungarian crown to Stephen the Great, the ruler of Moldavia, along with the castle of Cetatea de Baltă, a rare case of a prince from beyond the Carpathians holding lands inside Transylvania. Little remains of the castle today, though the walls on the hilltop still give an idea of its position.
