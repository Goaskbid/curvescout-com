Why this ride

A 174 km round trip from Baku - roughly 3 h in the saddle. It goes by way of Jinghindagh mountain -Yazylytepe hill, Gobustan Rock Art Cultural Landscape, Boyukdash mountain and Kichikdash mountain.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Baku

    Baku is a city of wind. The name is often said to come from a Persian phrase meaning a place battered by winds, and anybody who has stood on the Caspian boulevard on a bad day will believe it. The city curves around a bay on the western shore of the sea, with a medieval walled core, Icherisheher, rising behind the seafront, and the modern glass of the Flame Towers high above, lit by moving flames in the evening that can be seen from far across the water.

  2. 2. Jinghindagh mountain -Yazylytepe hill

    Gobustan is a stony, wind-scoured landscape of low hills and mesas south of Baku, near the shores of the Caspian Sea, and on its rocks, people carved images over thousands of years: hunters with bows, boats with rowers, dancers, wild cattle, deer and goats. More than six thousand petroglyphs have been recorded here, dating back as far as the end of the last ice age, and the Gobustan Rock Art Cultural Landscape was listed by UNESCO in 2007.

  3. 3. Gobustan Rock Art Cultural Landscape

    Some places keep their secrets in plain view. Gobustan Rock Art Cultural Landscape is a low plateau of boulders and caves southwest of Baku, where over six thousand petroglyphs have been recorded, some perhaps forty thousand years old if you accept the broadest estimates, and others dating from the Middle Ages. The earliest pictures show hunters and large animals, and the later ones include boats with rows of oars, dancing figures and carts. Archaeologists believe the sea was close to the rocks in prehistoric times, and the boats fit that picture.

  4. 4. Boyukdash mountain

    Gobustan is a rocky, windswept plateau about sixty kilometres south of Baku, where outcrops of limestone and sandstone rise out of a plain of dry grass, and the Caspian Sea glints on the horizon. Boyukdash, the Big Rock, is one of the boulders that make up this open-air gallery, and its sides are covered with carvings called petroglyphs made by people who lived and hunted here over thousands of years, some of them tens of thousands. UNESCO listed the area in 2007.

  5. 5. Kichikdash mountain

    The plateau of Gobustan is a dry, stony place, grey and ochre, with boulders scattered across low hills as if a giant had emptied a bag of rubble and left. Kichikdash, whose name means Little Stone, is one of those hills, a rocky outcrop in the reserve a few kilometres from the town of Qobustan, with the Caspian Sea visible in the distance on a clear day. Its bigger neighbour is Boyukdash, Big Stone, and together they hold some of the oldest pictures in the Caucasus.
