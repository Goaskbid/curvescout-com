  1. 1. Almaty

    Almaty means place of apples, or near enough, and the apples are not just a name. Wild apple forests, the ancestors of many of the world's cultivated varieties, still grow on the slopes of the Tian Shan mountains above the city, and botanists believe the fruit began its long journey to the world's orchards somewhere in this region. For most of the twentieth century Almaty was the capital of Kazakhstan, until the government moved to Astana in 1997, and it remains the country's biggest and most relaxed city, with wide tree-lined avenues, Soviet blocks and a skyline of snow peaks to the south.

  2. 2. Tamgaly

    Tamgaly is a canyon full of drawings made three thousand years ago. In the dry Anyrakay hills, northwest of Almaty and not far from the Ili river plain, thousands of petroglyphs are scratched into dark rock, many of them from the Bronze Age, with later layers added over the centuries. The site is on the UNESCO World Heritage list, and the carvings include figures with rayed, sun-like heads, dancers, animals, riders and scenes that seem to show ritual. The name is often explained as meaning a painted or marked place.
