Why this ride

From Srinagar it runs 247 km to the finish; allow about 3 h 24 of riding. On the way it passes Sonamarg and Zoji La. The highest point is about 4,350 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Srinagar-Leh Highway

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 3528 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance — cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Rider note

Check the pass is open before you commit to it.

Srinagar-Leh Highway

3528 m come off over 75 km at around 9.4%. The road gives back what the climb took, faster than it took it. Brakes early, eyes far ahead. A descent this long asks more of concentration than of pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Srinagar

    This is the summer capital of Jammu and Kashmir, a city of canals, wooden houseboats and old mosques, where the Mughal emperors laid out gardens such as Shalimar and Nishat to take advantage of the cool air and the view across the lake. Willow, chinar and poplar trees line the roads, and in autumn the chinar leaves turn a deep orange.

  2. 2. Sonamarg

    Sonamarg means meadow of gold, and in spring and early summer, when the flowers come out on the grass, the name seems fair. It is a broad, high valley of the Sindh river, at roughly twenty-seven hundred metres, about eighty kilometres north-east of Srinagar on the road to Ladakh, surrounded by snow-streaked peaks, pine forests and glaciers. In winter it is buried in snow, and it was once a stop on the old Silk Road routes that linked Kashmir to Central Asia, with caravans of traders, pilgrims and mules passing through on their way to Leh and beyond.

  3. 3. Zoji La

    Zoji La is the door to Ladakh, and it has a reputation to match. At around 3,500 metres, it is the pass that connects the green, wet Kashmir valley with the high, dry country beyond, and the transformation on the far side is startling: one moment you are among pines and flower meadows, the next you are looking at pale, bare mountains, sand-coloured slopes and a sky of unbroken blue. The reason is simple. The Himalaya catches the monsoon on one side and leaves the other in a rain shadow, so you cross not only a pass but a climate boundary.

  4. 4. Dras

    Dras is a place of extremes, and the first thing to know is that it is cold. Sitting at about 3,200 metres in the high valley below Zoji La, it is often called the second coldest inhabited place in the world, and winter temperatures can plunge to somewhere around minus forty degrees. The snow falls heavily and stays for months, and the road from Kashmir is usually closed by the pass through the winter and early spring. In summer, though, the valley turns green, with barley fields and willows beside the river and the bare brown ranges rising on either side.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Srinagar to Sonamarg

Srinagar → Sonamarg is 65 km of getting across. Ride it tidily and keep the enthusiasm for what comes after.

Sonamarg to Zoji La

Sonamarg → Zoji La climbs about 3528 m in 21 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Zoji La to Dras

Zoji La → Dras drops about 3528 m over 37 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
