Why this ride

A 309 km round trip from Spermezeu - roughly 6 h 36 in the saddle. On the way it passes Church of the Holy Archangels, Kuziy - Trybushany and back through Church of the Holy Archangels. It crosses from Romania into Ukraine - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Romania Maramures Wooden Churches Route

It is 80 km from Church of the Holy Archangels to Kuziy - Trybushany on DJ109F. A proper up-and-over: 580 m of climbing, 954 m at the top, 660 m down. This is the bit the ride is built around.

The stops

  1. 1. Sita - Vârful Sitii

    Start here.

    On a hilltop near the village of Spermezeu, in the Someș hills of Bistrița-Năsăud county in northern Romania, a small watchtower once stood guard over the northern edge of the Roman Empire. When the emperor Trajan conquered Dacia in the early second century, the Romans built a frontier around their new province, the Dacian limes, a chain of forts, watchtowers and earthworks running along the Carpathians and through the hills. Vârful Sitii, the summit of Sita, was one of the observation points, chosen because from here soldiers could watch the valleys to the north and send signals to the forts behind them.

  2. 2. Church of the Holy Archangels

    75 km in.

    Șurdești is a small village in the hills of Maramureș, in the north of Romania, and its wooden church of the Holy Archangels Michael and Gabriel is famous for its spire. Built in the eighteenth century from oak logs, the church has a steep double roof covered in shingles and a slender tower that rises far above the trees, among the tallest wooden church towers in the region. It is one of eight wooden churches of Maramureș listed by UNESCO in 1999, each a masterpiece of local carpentry, built by village craftsmen using traditional techniques.

    Read the complete Church of the Holy Archangels write-up.

  3. 3. Kuziy - Trybushany

    154 km in.

    The river Tisza runs fast and green out of the Ukrainian Carpathians, and for many kilometres it forms the border between Romania and Ukraine. On the Ukrainian bank, the forests of Kuziy and Trybushany are among the primeval beech woods protected in the Carpathian Biosphere Reserve and listed by UNESCO as part of the Ancient and Primeval Beech Forests of the Carpathians and Other Regions of Europe. From the Romanian side, around Valea Vișeului, you look across the river at steep, wooded slopes where the trees have grown and died without interference for centuries.
