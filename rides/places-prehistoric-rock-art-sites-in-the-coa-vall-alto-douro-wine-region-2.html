Why this ride

A 340 km round trip from Castillejo de Martín Viejo - roughly 5 h 31 in the saddle. The stops in between: Ribeirinha, Alto Douro Wine Region and back through Ribeirinha. It crosses from Spain into Portugal - carry ID and check the toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Prehistoric Rock Art Sites in the Côa Vall

    Start here.

    On the Spanish side of the border with Portugal, the river Águeda cuts through granite and schist on its way to the Douro, and on the rock faces along its banks, at Siega Verde, Palaeolithic people engraved horses, aurochs, deer and goats more than ten thousand years ago. In 2010 Siega Verde was added to the Prehistoric Rock Art Sites in the Côa Valley, the Portuguese World Heritage Site just across the border, recognising that these open-air engravings form one of the largest collections of Ice Age art in the world.

    Read the complete Prehistoric Rock Art Sites in the Côa Vall write-up.

  2. 2. Ribeirinha

    61 km in.

    Ribeirinha is a quiet corner of the Coa valley, and the interest of the place is the whole valley rather than one spot. Along some seventeen kilometres of the Coa River, the schist cliffs carry thousands of engravings from the Upper Palaeolithic, drawn by people who hunted across this landscape twenty thousand years ago and more. Three main sites are open to visitors, Penascosa, Canada do Inferno and Ribeira de Piscos, and each has a different character, set in different bends of the river. The valley is a UNESCO World Heritage Site together with the open-air engravings at Siega Verde in Spain, which lie over the border in the province of Salamanca.

    Read the complete Ribeirinha write-up.

  3. 3. Alto Douro Wine Region

    170 km in.

    The Douro river has been carving a gorge through the north of Portugal for millions of years, and for about two thousand years people have been planting vines on its slopes. The terraces, held up by stone walls, climb steeply from the water and rise through schist and slate in rows, and the effect, in the right season, is of a vast green staircase. The Alto Douro was the first wine region in the world to be officially demarcated, by the Marquis of Pombal in 1756, and UNESCO listed it in 2001. Port wine, the sweet fortified red that made the region famous, is still made here.
