  1. 1. Riobamba

    At roughly 2,750 metres above sea level, Riobamba is a high-altitude city that wears its height lightly. It sits in a wide basin in the central Andes of Ecuador, surrounded by volcanoes, and on clear mornings Chimborazo, the tallest mountain in the country at about 6,263 metres, stands in the distance with its snowy summit catching the first light. Because the Earth bulges at the equator, Chimborazo's summit is the farthest point on the planet's surface from its centre, which makes it a tempting claim for the title of the world's highest mountain.

  2. 2. Chimborazo refuge gate

    Here is a fact that sounds made up and is not: the summit of Chimborazo is the farthest point on the Earth's surface from the centre of the planet. Because the Earth bulges at the equator, this 6,263-metre volcano, only a degree and a half south of the line, stands a couple of kilometres further out than Everest does. It is the highest peak in Ecuador, a broad, glaciated dome that is dormant, and the refuge gate lies on its flanks in the Chimborazo Fauna Production Reserve.
