Why this ride

From Beniatjar it runs 250 km to Beniatjar; allow about 4 h 19 of riding. The stops in between: Abric de Tollos II o de Cambriquia, Olula and back through Abric de Tollos II o de Cambriquia.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc de Carbonera- Abric II

    Between the mountain ranges of northern Alicante and the plains of Valencia lies the Vall d'Albaida, a quiet and little-visited district of small towns, vineyards and orchards, and the shelter at Barranc de Carbonera, Abric II, is on its edge. It lies about two and a half kilometres from Otos, a village of five hundred people, and close to Ráfol de Salem and Bèlgida, which are about the same size. It carries painting in the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain, and the ride links it to the Cova de la Catxupa, an even smaller site in the same area.

  2. 2. Abric de Tollos II o de Cambriquia

    Bicorp is famous for a single painting. In the Cuevas de la Araña, a short walk from the village, a figure clings to ropes on a cliff, reaching into a hole surrounded by bees: one of the oldest known depictions of honey gathering in the world. The Abric de Tollos, also called Cambriquia, is another of the painted shelters around Bicorp, in the deep gorges of the Valencian interior, one of the World Heritage rock art sites of the Spanish east.

  3. 3. Olula

    Caudete and Almansa sit in a corridor of land where Castile-La Mancha, Valencia and Murcia meet, and for centuries this was frontier country, where kingdoms and armies passed and fought. Olula is a point in the hills near Caudete, linked on the rides with rock-art shelters in the surrounding sierras, part of the extensive UNESCO listing of prehistoric art in Mediterranean Spain.
