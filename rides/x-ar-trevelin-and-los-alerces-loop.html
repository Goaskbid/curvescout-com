  1. 1. Esquel

    Esquel is a town of about twenty-eight thousand people in the northwest of Chubut province, on the dry eastern flank of the Andes, and it feels like the edge of a very big country. The mountains rise to the west, snowy and forested, and to the east the Patagonian steppe begins, an endless plain of grass and shrub where the wind never really stops. The town itself is a plain, practical place with wide streets, a few low buildings and a quiet air, yet it is the doorstep to some of the best scenery in Patagonia.

  2. 2. Trevelin

    Trevelin has a name that sounds like a joke until you learn what it means. In Welsh, tre is town and velin is mill, so the name means town of the mill, and it was given by Welsh settlers who arrived in the late nineteenth century and started growing wheat in this green valley at the foot of the Andes. They had already crossed the Patagonian steppe from the Chubut valley on the Atlantic side, and they found in the sheltered land near the Futaleufú River a place where crops would thrive. Today around six thousand people live here, and the Welsh heritage is still visible in the chapels, the family names and the tea houses.

  3. 3. Los Alerces gate

    The gate of Los Alerces National Park marks the point where the Patagonian steppe gives way to something older and wetter. The park, in the Chubut province of Argentina, protects a strip of Andean forest and a chain of deep glacial lakes, and it is named after the alerce, a giant conifer that can live for more than three thousand years. These slow-growing trees, among the oldest living things on earth, are scattered through the damp valleys, and the park was added to the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2017 in recognition of its importance.
