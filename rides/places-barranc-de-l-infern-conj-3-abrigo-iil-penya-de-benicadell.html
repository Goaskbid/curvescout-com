Why this ride

A 70 km round trip from Pego - roughly 1 h 12 in the saddle. The stops in between: Racó del Pou, Penya de Benicadell and back through Racó del Pou. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc de l'Infern Conj. 3 abrigo IIl

    Three centuries of travellers have called this ravine Hell without anyone quite agreeing why. The Barranc de l'Infern is a narrow gorge in the Vall de Laguar, in the Marina Alta of Alicante province, and it is best known today for a footpath that follows the ravine up and down a long staircase of stone steps said to number thousands, built by the Moriscos as a pack route between villages. Along the walls, in sheltered places, are several clusters of rock paintings, and this is the third group, numbered as a conjunto, with a painted shelter that belongs to the Levantine tradition.

  2. 2. Racó del Pou

    The Safor is the fertile coastal strip around Gandia, and behind it rise abrupt limestone hills, the Serra de la Safor and its neighbours, where the orange groves give way to pine, scrub and terraces of olives and almonds. Racó del Pou, a sheltered corner in these hills between Villalonga and the Vall d'Ebo, is one of the many rock shelters of the region that hold prehistoric paintings, part of the Mediterranean rock art group listed by UNESCO. Like most of them, it is modest and protected, but the walk up and the view over the hills to the sea are reason enough to come.

  3. 3. Penya de Benicadell

    From almost anywhere in the southern half of Valencia province you can see it: a long, jagged ridge of grey limestone with a sharp tooth at its eastern end, standing on the skyline like a broken saw. The Benicadell, at 1,104 metres, separates the Vall d'Albaida to the north from the Comtat to the south, and the sharp summit, the Penya, is the mountain's signature. At its foot lie the villages of Beniarrés, Ràfol de Salem and Otos.
