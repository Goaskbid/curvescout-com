Why this ride

A proper escape from New York with roads worth waking up for. Out of Englewood Cliffs and back again: 245 km, roughly 5 h 07 in the saddle. On the way it passes Scenic stretch near Kings Point, near Rumson and Bath Beach. The climb of the day is in Woodmere. The line favours views over speed.

  5. 5. Bath Beach

    Bath Beach is a small neighbourhood on the south-west shore of Brooklyn, facing Gravesend Bay, and it got its name in the nineteenth century, when it was a seaside resort for wealthy New Yorkers, modelled on the English spa town of Bath. The resort is long gone, but the bay is still there, with a promenade along the water and views of the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge, its great towers soaring over the entrance to New York Harbor.

  7. 7. Near Boonton, on the way back

    The New Jersey Highlands are the surprise of the state for anyone who only knows it from the Turnpike. North-west of Boonton the land buckles into long wooded ridges of old granite and gneiss, with lakes in every hollow, rocky streams and roads that wriggle through forest for kilometre after kilometre. Boonton, Kinnelon and Butler sit on the edge of this country, small towns of seven to ten thousand people each, and they make a natural pause on the road back from Bear Mountain.

  8. 8. Back to Bear Mountain

    Across the Hudson from upper Manhattan, the river runs below a long wall of dark rock known as the Palisades, a cliff of volcanic diabase that rises up to about five hundred feet and runs for twenty miles along the New Jersey side. Englewood Cliffs sits on top, among trees and large houses, with the George Washington Bridge nearby, and this is the southern end of a very good motorcycle road. It was saved from quarrying by a conservation movement in the early twentieth century, and the land became part of Palisades Interstate Park.

Scenic stretch near Kings Point to High point in Woodmere

This Scenic stretch near Kings Point → High point in Woodmere stretch is for rhythm and scenery. Keep your head up, let the view breathe, and resist turning every open bend into a target. Stop once properly rather than half-stopping everywhere.

Photo stop near Rumson to Bath Beach

Photo stop near Rumson → Bath Beach is the technical section. Hairpins, gradients and mixed traffic can stack up quickly; use low drama inputs, stay wide of centre lines and expect buses or riders appearing exactly where optimism says they will not.

Food stop in Metuchen to Near Boonton, on the way back

Food stop in Metuchen → Near Boonton, on the way back can flow quickly, but only if you keep village limits and junction sightlines sacred. Fast connectors are where rides get untidy when fatigue creeps in.
