Why this ride

A 32 km loop that starts and ends near Canazei, about 31 min of riding. It goes by way of Passo Sella, Selva di Val Gardena, Passo Gardena and Corvara. The highest point is about 2,250 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Great Dolomites Road

Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it. The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2240 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance.

Rider note

Check the pass is open before you commit to it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Canazei

    Canazei is a small Ladin town at the head of the Val di Fassa, and the first impression is of towering rock. The Marmolada, the highest peak in the Dolomites at 3,343 metres, rises to the south-east with its glacier, while the cliffs of the Sella group fill the northern horizon, huge walls of pale dolomite that glow pink and orange at sunrise and sunset. The Dolomites were declared a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2009, and no one who stands here is likely to wonder why.

  2. 2. Passo Sella

    Passo Sella is the pass that puts you in the middle of the Dolomites rather than at the edge of them. At 2,240 metres it connects the Val di Fassa with the Val Gardena, and it sits in a saddle between two of the great massifs of the range. To the north rises the huge, flat-topped block of the Sella group, a fortress of pale rock with vertical walls, and to the south, almost close enough to touch, are the pointed towers of the Sassolungo, called the Langkofel in German, which look like the fingers of a stone hand raised to the sky.

  3. 3. Selva di Val Gardena

    Selva is the top of the Val Gardena, a long, green valley in South Tyrol that cuts into the heart of the Dolomites, and it is the place where winter sport, woodcarving and the Ladin language all meet. The village lies below the Sella massif and the jagged spires of the Sassolungo, and its houses are strung out along the stream, with meadows climbing to the forest on either side. It is known in German as Wolkenstein, after a castle ruin up the valley, and in Ladin as Sëlva, and the three names on the signs tell you something about the place before you have left the bike.

  4. 4. Passo Gardena

    Passo Gardena, at 2,121 metres, links Val Gardena with Alta Badia, and it is one of the four passes that circle the Sella massif. It does not have the drama of the Stelvio or the fame of the Pordoi, but it has a distinctive character: a wide, open crossing of grass and scree between the Sella on one side and the jagged Cir peaks on the other, with the road threading through a landscape that feels more intimate than grand. The Italian name is Passo Gardena, the German Grödner Joch, and in Ladin it is Jëuf de Frara.

  5. 5. Corvara

    Corvara is the heart of Alta Badia, a village of tall chalets and church spire in a bowl of meadows with the Sella massif looming to the south and the Puez-Odle range to the north. It is one of the most popular ski resorts in the Dolomites and, in summer, one of the best bases for riders, because four passes meet within a short ride. It is also the start and finish of the Maratona dles Dolomites, a famous one-day cycling event, and on race weekends the whole village fills with cyclists in bright kit. Ladin is the local language, and signs are often in three languages, which gives the place a distinctive, mixed identity.

  6. 6. Passo Campolongo

    Passo Campolongo is the low, easy member of the Sella Ronda family, at 1,875 metres, and it is the one that most riders cross without much ceremony. It joins Corvara in Alta Badia with Arabba in the Livinallongo valley, and the road climbs through meadows and spruce, with views of the Sella to one side and, on a clear day, the glacier of the Marmolada in the distance. The name means long field, and it describes the pass well: a broad, gentle grassy saddle rather than a sharp notch in the rock.

  7. 7. Arabba

    Arabba is a small village in the heart of the Dolomites, and a crossroads of passes. It sits at about 1,600 metres on the upper Cordevole river, at the foot of the Pordoi, the Campolongo and the Fedaia, and its position makes it the natural halfway point of the Sella Ronda, the circuit around the huge Sella massif. The mountains here are the pale, jagged towers that make the Dolomites famous, and the whole range was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2009.

  8. 8. Passo Pordoi

    The Pordoi Pass is the highest on the Sella Ronda, at 2,239 metres, and the road from Arabba climbs it in thirty-three hairpins that are among the most photographed in the Alps. The road is wide, the surface is generally excellent and the bends are well graded, so it is one of the most enjoyable climbs in the Dolomites, a place where you can find a rhythm and let the bike dance. At the top you come out beneath the vertical cliffs of the Sella, with the Marmolada glacier shining to the south-east.
