Why this ride

372 km from Tripoli to Tripoli, about 6 h 25 of riding. On the way it passes Crac des Chevaliers and Qal’at Salah El-Di, Ouadi Qadisha and back through Crac des Chevaliers and Qal’at Salah El-Di. The highest point is about 1,300 m. It crosses from Lebanon into Syria - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Rachid Karami International Fair-Tripoli

    In the early 1960s, the Lebanese government invited the Brazilian architect Oscar Niemeyer to design an international fairground for Tripoli, the country's second city. He produced a vast, futuristic complex of sweeping concrete forms: a huge boomerang-shaped exhibition hall, a dome for experimental theatre, an open-air theatre, a helipad, a water tower and more, all set in a park on the edge of the city. Construction was almost finished when the civil war broke out in 1975, and the fair was never completed. Today the site stands as an extraordinary, melancholy monument to modernist ambition and lost hope, and it was listed by UNESCO in 2023, immediately placed on the list of heritage in danger.

  2. 2. Crac des Chevaliers and Qal’at Salah El-Di

    Crac des Chevaliers stands on a spur of the Jebel Ansariyya, overlooking the Homs Gap, the one low corridor between the Mediterranean coast and the Syrian interior, and that is why it was built here. In 1142 the Count of Tripoli gave the site to the Knights Hospitaller, who rebuilt it over the following century into the greatest castle of the Crusades, with two concentric rings of walls, a moat, a long, sloping ramp, and a great hall with Gothic vaults. At its peak it could hold a garrison of some two thousand men, with stables, storerooms and an enormous cistern.

  3. 3. Ouadi Qadisha

    Two places are tangled in this label, and it helps to separate them. The Qadisha Valley, whose name means Holy Valley, is a deep gorge in northern Lebanon beneath the Cedars of God, where Maronite Christians settled in caves and built monasteries and hermitages from the early centuries. UNESCO inscribed it in 1998, and the cliffs there fall nearly straight into a river valley that ties together prayer, farming and defence.
