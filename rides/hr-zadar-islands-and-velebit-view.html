Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Zadar

    Alfred Hitchcock is supposed to have said that Zadar has the most beautiful sunset in the world, and the people of the town have taken him at his word. The city sits on a narrow peninsula on the Dalmatian coast, with the sea on three sides and the island of Ugljan across the channel, and the sun drops into the water in a long fire of orange and pink. The best place to watch is the waterfront promenade at the end of the old town, where a pair of modern works of art, the Sea Organ and the Greeting to the Sun, turn the shoreline into an instrument and a light show.

  2. 2. Nin

    Nin is small in every sense but one: its age. This low town sits on a tiny island joined to the shore by a pair of stone bridges, in shallow lagoons north-west of Zadar, and it was once a seat of Croatian rulers and a bishopric. A walk around the old centre takes a quarter of an hour, yet it is crowded with churches, including the little pre-Romanesque church of the Holy Cross, which is often described as the smallest cathedral in the world. A statue of Bishop Grgur of Nin, the medieval champion of the Croatian language in church services, stands outside the walls, and rubbing the toe is said to bring luck.

  3. 3. Starigrad

    Starigrad lies on the Adriatic shore beneath the grey wall of the Velebit mountains, a long strip of pebble beach, pine trees and low stone houses squeezed between the sea and the rock. Locals know it as Starigrad-Paklenica, after the national park that begins at its back door, where two gorges, Velika and Mala Paklenica, cut deep into the limestone. Sheer cliffs rising hundreds of metres make it a magnet for climbers, and the sound of ropes and carabiners at the foot of the walls is part of the village soundtrack in spring.
