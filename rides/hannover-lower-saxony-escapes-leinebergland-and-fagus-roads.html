Leinebergland and Fagus Roads

Why this ride

A compact southbound ride with culture stops and clean rural corners. A 166 km loop that starts and ends near Pattensen, about 3 h 23 of riding. On the way it passes Hildesheim, Fagus Factory Alfeld sight and Bodenwerder Weser stop. Duingen Hils road is the high point. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hannover south gate

    South of Hannover the flat plain starts to fold, and the small town of Pattensen, on the Leine, marks the place where the ride leaves the city for the hills. The land here is the Calenberger Land, a fertile farming country of wheat and sugar beet, with big brick farmhouses and low ridges on the horizon. Beyond it rise the limestone ridges of the Leinebergland, the Ith and the Hils, long, forested crests with cliffs and old quarries, and they are the reason the ride exists.

  2. 2. Hildesheim fuel gate

    Hildesheim looks, from the ring road, like any prosperous middle-sized German city, with chain stores, car dealers and a ring of fuel stations, and that is exactly why it is a useful gate. Over a hundred thousand people live here, the fuel is plentiful, and the town sits in the open farmland of the Hildesheimer Börde, where black soil grows sugar beet and wheat. Filling up here before the Harz means you can take the hills without watching the needle, because once you climb towards Bad Harzburg and the Okertal the villages get small and stations get scarce.

  3. 3. Fagus Factory Alfeld sight

    The Fagus Factory is a shoe-last factory that changed the course of architecture, and it sits unassumingly beside the railway line in Alfeld, a small town on the Leine river. The buildings were designed by the young Walter Gropius, with Adolf Meyer, and built between 1911 and 1913 for Carl Benscheidt, a manufacturer who wanted a modern, light-filled factory. What they produced, with glass walls that wrap around the corners of the brick structure with no supporting pillar in the way, was one of the first buildings in the world to look genuinely modern, a forerunner of the Bauhaus that Gropius would found in 1919.

  4. 4. Duingen Hils road

    The Hils is a modest range of wooded hills in southern Lower Saxony, and it offers some of the best quiet riding in the region. The roads run along ridges and through beech forest, with gentle, flowing bends, a decent surface, and views that open suddenly over the farmland of the Leine valley. It is a landscape that never quite makes a fuss, and for that reason it is a pleasure to ride, with almost no traffic on a weekday and the smell of damp leaves in the shade.

  5. 5. Bodenwerder Weser stop

    Bodenwerder is the home town of a man who was never quite able to tell the truth, and the town has made him a hero. Baron Münchhausen, Hieronymus Carl Friedrich von Münchhausen, was born here in 1720 and lived in the family manor beside the Weser, and as an old man he entertained dinner guests with fantastical tales of riding on a cannonball, pulling himself out of a swamp by his own hair, and flying to the moon. A writer collected the stories, and the baron became a literary legend. The old manor is now the town hall and holds a museum, and a fountain shows him on his cannonball.

  6. 6. Elze return road

    Elze is the sort of small town you only really know if you have been passed through it on the way home. It sits on the Leine river south of Hannover, in the gentle, folded country that Lower Saxony people call the Leinebergland, where a river valley, a railway and a main road all squeeze past one another between low wooded ridges. With about nine and a half thousand inhabitants, it has what a rider needs after a long day: fuel, a supermarket, a bakery and the kind of cafe where the coffee comes with a small biscuit and no commentary.
