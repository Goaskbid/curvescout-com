Bolivia Sajama Route: La Paz to Sajama

Why this ride

540 km from La Paz to the finish, about 7 h 29 of riding. The highest point is about 4,600 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. La Paz

    La Paz sits in a canyon, and that is the first strange thing about it. The city is the seat of Bolivia's government, and it lies at around three thousand six hundred metres, with the poorer and larger city of El Alto perched on the rim above at over four thousand. From the rim you look down into a bowl of red-brown buildings spilling down the slopes, with the great snowy peak of Illimani, over six thousand four hundred metres, standing over the southern edge like a guardian. The air is thin, so arrive early, rest a day and drink coca tea, which locals swear by and which really does help a little.

  2. 2. Patacamaya

    Patacamaya is a market town on the altiplano, the high plain of Bolivia, at around 3,800 metres on the main road between La Paz and Oruro. It is not a pretty place, and that is part of its character: a straggle of concrete and adobe buildings along the highway, with lorries, minibuses and food stalls clustered at the junction. For motorcyclists it matters because of that junction. Here the road to Chile turns west toward the border at Tambo Quemado and the great volcano Sajama, and anyone heading for that route passes through, with a need for fuel, a meal and a rest.

  3. 3. Sajama

    Sajama rises alone from the Altiplano, a perfect volcanic cone of 6,542 metres, the highest peak in Bolivia, and the national park that surrounds it, created in 1939, was the first in the country. The mountain stands in the west, near the border with Chile, in a flat, high, windswept country of yellow grass, with twin snowy peaks of Parinacota and Pomerape, on the Chilean side, standing as neighbours. Seen from the road in the clear, thin light, it seems close enough to touch and takes hours to reach.
