Why this ride

A 95 km one-way ride from near Balestrand, about 1 h 39 of riding. On the way it passes Utsikten and Gaularfjellet. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Norwegian Scenic Route Gaularfjellet

First and second gear more often than not. Look through each corner to the next one and let the engine do the holding. The valley floor is behind you and the road starts working for its height at about 62.2%. Corners stack above one another where the ground allows nothing straighter.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Balestrand

    Balestrand is a village on the north shore of the Sognefjord, and it has one of the best settings in Norway: a wide, calm stretch of the fjord with mountains reaching up on all sides, orchards at the water's edge and a quiet, old-fashioned atmosphere. It has fewer than eight hundred inhabitants, and in the nineteenth century it became a destination for British and German tourists, who came by steamer to stay in the large wooden hotels. The Kviknes Hotel, a grand dragon-style building from 1877, is still in operation, and the English church of St Olaf, built by an Englishwoman who married a local painter, stands nearby. Vik and Hermansverk are the nearest towns of any size.

  2. 2. Utsikten

    Utsikten means the view, and at Gaularfjellet that is exactly what you get: a cantilevered platform of steel and glass pushed out over the edge of a mountain, with the valley dropping away below. The viewing point stands on the road over the Gaularfjellet, the mountain that separates the Sognefjord from the Førde district, and it looks down over a vast, folded landscape of rock, forest and river, with the fjord somewhere in the haze beyond. Nearby, the water of the Gaula comes down in several falls, and the whole slope is cut by streams. Spray from the falls drifts across the road in spring.

  3. 3. Gaularfjellet

    The summit of the Gaularfjellet road stands at about 748 metres, which is not high by Norwegian standards, but the character of the place is that of high mountains: a bare, windswept plateau of rock, lakes and heather, with no trees and few buildings. The mountain divides the Sognefjord from the valleys of the Førde district, and the road crosses it in a series of long climbs and descents. On the plateau, lakes lie in every hollow, and in the evening they hold the colour of the sky. Sheep graze on the hills in summer, and a few mountain farms survive.

  4. 4. Moskog

    Moskog is a small settlement in Sunnfjord, and it sits on the edge of Førde, the main town of the area, where the Gaularfjellet road comes to its quiet end. The countryside here is gentle compared with the high mountains behind: green farmland, birch woods and small lakes, with the Jølstravatnet lake and the Førdefjord nearby. Førde, with about eight and a half thousand people, is a modern regional centre, with fuel, shops, restaurants and a hospital, and it is only about seven kilometres away. Moskog itself is a scatter of farms and houses.
