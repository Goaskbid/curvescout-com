Serpukhov and the Oka Bend

Why this ride

Roadbook

  1. Serpukhov start

    Serpukhov is a town on the Oka that has been the southern guard post of Moscow for seven centuries. In the fourteenth century it was the seat of a small principality ruled by Prince Vladimir the Brave, a cousin and comrade of Dmitry Donskoy, who led the Serpukhov regiments at the great battle of Kulikovo in 1380. The town lay on the border between Moscow and its southern enemies, and a white-stone kremlin, built to keep the Tatars out, once stood above the river bend.

    Today the old center is a quiet quarter of churches, merchant houses and a kremlin hill where the cathedral stands among surviving walls. Just outside, the Vysotsky Monastery, founded in 1374 by St Sergius of Radonezh, the most revered saint of medieval Russia, sits behind its walls near the Nara River, a peaceful, well-kept complex that holds the Icon of the Inexhaustible Cup, a revered image that draws pilgrims. The town later grew rich on textiles, and its brick mills and old merchants' mansions recall that era.

    The riding in the district is a pleasure of its own. Roads south and west follow the Oka through bluffs, pine woods and meadow, and the river's high western bank has chalky cliffs and ravines. The Polenovo estate, home of the painter Vasily Polenov, lies on the river a short ride away, and the nearby town of Tarusa, a gentle riverside settlement, has long drawn writers and artists. The main road, the M2 from Moscow to Tula, is fast and busy, with heavy truck traffic, but the secondary roads are quieter, if patchy.

    Serpukhov, with more than a hundred thousand residents, has all the services you need, with fuel at many stations, supermarkets and cafes. It is the right place to start or end a day on the Oka. The best seasons are late May to June, with fresh green and cool nights, and September, when the meadows go golden. High summer can be hot and the roads busy on weekends with city dwellers, and spring thaw brings potholes and mud, so check the surface before pushing the pace. Deer and wild boar are common in the woods at dusk.

  2. Puschino scenic

    Pushchino is a science town, built in the 1960s on the high bank of the Oka river to house the biologists, biochemists and physicists who were moved out of Moscow to work on the problems of life. It has a neat grid of apartment blocks, tree-lined avenues and a population of twenty thousand, many of them researchers or their families. It feels unlike most Russian towns, with a campus atmosphere and a good deal of green space, and it sits among pine and birch woods that run down to the river.

    The Oka is the big river of this part of Russia, wide and slow, and it makes a large bend here, with sandy beaches and meadows on the far bank. A few kilometres away, near Danki, lies the Prioksko-Terrasny Reserve, one of the oldest nature reserves in the country, where European bison have been bred and reintroduced, and where visitors can see them in large enclosures. Pushchino also has a famous radio astronomy observatory, part of the Lebedev Institute, whose big dish is visible from the surrounding fields, a striking sight rising out of the farmland.

    The riding is easy, a gentle run along quiet roads between Serpukhov and the Oka, through villages with wooden houses, old churches and the occasional dacha. Surfaces are generally good on the main routes and patchy on the side roads, and the gradients are minimal. Traffic is light except on weekends, when people drive out from Moscow to swim, fish and walk on the riverbank. There are viewpoints over the river that are worth a stop, particularly in the evening, when the sun is low and the water turns gold.

    Pushchino has what a town of twenty thousand has: petrol stations, supermarkets, cafes and a pleasant riverside park. It is a good place to stop for a meal, with the sort of simple, decent food that serves students and researchers, and it is a more relaxed alternative to the busy road to Serpukhov. If you want to see the bison, check the opening times and the rules of the reserve in advance, since access is controlled. Late spring and early autumn are the best seasons, when the river is calm and the woods are fresh or turning.

  3. Polenovo finish

    Polenovo is an estate on the high, wooded bank of the Oka River, and it is the Russian countryside of a painter's imagination. Vasily Polenov, a landscape artist of the late nineteenth century, bought the land in the 1890s and designed a house in a fanciful, medieval style, with a tower and wide terraces, where he lived and worked among fellow painters. The estate is now a museum-reserve, with the house, a church he designed and a gallery, and the surrounding landscape, a bend of the Oka with meadows and birch groves, looks very much as it did in his paintings.

    The view from the terrace is the point. The Oka curves far below, wide and slow, with willows along its banks, and the far shore rolls away in soft blue distance, the kind of scene Russian painters tried to capture. In summer the meadows are full of wildflowers, in autumn the birches turn gold, and in winter the river freezes, with snow-covered banks. The nearby town of Tarusa, a gentle riverside settlement, became a refuge for writers and artists, and the poet Marina Tsvetaeva spent her childhood summers here.

    The ride is relaxed. From Serpukhov or Tarusa, narrow country roads wind through villages, fields and pine woods, past wooden houses with carved window frames and wells. The surface varies, with good asphalt on main roads and patchy, sometimes broken pavement on the lanes, and the ferry or bridge crossings over the Oka can mean waits. Weekends in summer bring crowds of visitors to the museum, so early morning or a weekday is best, and rain turns unpaved lanes to mud quickly.

    There are no big services at Polenovo itself, just a cafe near the entrance, so fill up in Tarusa, Serpukhov or Protvino. The best months are late May to September, with September particularly lovely, when the light is soft and the woods are turning. Winters are cold and snowy, and the estate is open year-round, but the roads are icy. Take your time, walk through the park, and watch the sun go down over the river.
