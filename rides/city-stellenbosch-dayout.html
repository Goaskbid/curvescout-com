Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Stellenbosch

    Stellenbosch is the second-oldest European settlement in South Africa, founded in 1679 by the governor Simon van der Stel, who named it after himself and his wife's family, and it has the look of a town that has been carefully looked after. Oak trees line the streets, white Cape Dutch houses with curved gables stand behind them, and the mountains rise on every side, purple at dusk. It is also a university town, home to the Afrikaans-speaking Stellenbosch University, which gives the centre a young, lively atmosphere.

  2. 2. Robben Island

    Seen from the beach at Bloubergstrand, Robben Island is a low, flat smudge on Table Bay, with Table Mountain standing up behind it from the other side. It is one of the best known and most difficult places in South Africa to think about. For several centuries the island served as a place of exile and isolation, a prison for political opponents of the Dutch and British, a leper colony and a military base. Between 1961 and 1991 it held political prisoners of the apartheid state, including Nelson Mandela, who spent eighteen of his twenty-seven years in custody here.

  4. 4. Cape Floral Region Protected Areas

    The Cape Peninsula ends in a long, wind-battered headland, and at its tip, Cape Point, the Atlantic and Indian oceans seem to argue. Here the rock plunges to the sea in great cliffs, the sky is huge, and on most days a gale tries to remove your hat. This is the southern part of Table Mountain National Park, set aside to protect the Cape Floral Region, the smallest and richest of the world's six plant kingdoms.
