Why this ride

From Sofia it runs 692 km to Sofia; allow about 13 h 47 of riding. It goes by way of \Grave of St. Ivan Rilski\ Complex, Pirin National Park, \Pchelino Convent\ Complex and back through Pirin National Park. It tops out around 1,700 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

The stops

  1. 1. Boyana Church

    Start here.

    Boyana is a village on the southern edge of Sofia, tucked against the lower slopes of Mount Vitosha, and it holds a small church that changed the history of European painting, or at least its footnotes. The church is made of three parts built over several centuries, but the middle part, a tiny two-storey chapel, was decorated in 1259 for Sebastokrator Kaloyan, a local ruler, and his wife Desislava, who are painted in the narthex as donors. The frescoes show eighty-nine scenes with more than two hundred and forty human figures, and they are astonishing for their realism. The faces have individual expressions, the saints look like real people, and they predate the Italian Renaissance by generations.

    Read the complete Boyana Church write-up.

  2. 2. \Grave of St. Ivan Rilski\ Complex

    118 km in.

    Ivan of Rila was a hermit who, in the tenth century, withdrew into the forests of the Rila mountains to live in a cave and a hollow tree, and the monastery that grew up around his grave became the spiritual heart of Bulgaria. Rila Monastery sits in a valley at about 1,150 metres, surrounded by steep forested slopes, and the Rilska river runs beside it. The buildings you see today date mostly from the nineteenth century, after a great fire in 1833 destroyed the older ones, but within the courtyard stands a medieval tower, Hrelyo's Tower, from 1335, and the main church is covered in vivid murals.

    Read the complete \Grave of St. Ivan Rilski\ Complex write-up.

  3. 3. Pirin National Park

    239 km in.

    Vihren, at 2,914 metres the highest peak of the Pirin mountains and the third highest in the Balkans, rises above the town of Bansko in south-western Bulgaria in a sharp, pale pyramid of marble. The Pirin National Park protects the high country around it, a landscape of granite and marble peaks, glacial cirques, more than a hundred lakes and ancient forests of pine and fir, and it was listed by UNESCO in 1983.

    Read the complete Pirin National Park write-up.

  4. 4. \Pchelino Convent\ Complex

    346 km in.

    The mountains of southwestern Bulgaria are among the wildest in the Balkans, and the area between Rila and Pirin holds a string of small monasteries tucked into forested valleys. During centuries of Ottoman rule these monasteries kept the Bulgarian language, the Orthodox faith and the art of icon painting alive, and the great Rila Monastery, founded in the tenth century around the hermitage of Saint Ivan of Rila, became the national symbol.

    Read the complete \Pchelino Convent\ Complex write-up.
