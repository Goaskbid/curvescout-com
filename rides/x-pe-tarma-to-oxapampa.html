Why this ride

A named mountain-road corridor with sustained elevation change and pass scenery. It links naturally to Oxapampa Ashaninka Yanesha Biosphere Reserve.

The stops

  1. 1. Tarma

    Start here.

    Tarma is known as the Pearl of the Andes, and the nickname fits a town of about fifty thousand people that sits in a sheltered, sunny valley at around 3,050 metres, framed by green hills and fields of flowers. It stands on the eastern side of the central Andes, where the high plateau drops toward the jungle, and the climate is mild and dry, with the air cool in the mornings and warm by midday. The town has a pleasant, unhurried atmosphere, with a handsome plaza, a cathedral and a lively market, and in the surrounding hills, small farms grow potatoes, maize and flowers.

    Read the complete Tarma write-up.

  2. 2. Oxapampa

    The end: 149 km.

    Oxapampa is one of the surprises of Peru, a small town of about twelve thousand people at around 1,800 metres, on the eastern slopes of the Andes, where the high plateau drops away into the Amazon basin. The wooden houses with steep roofs and flower boxes, the dairy farms and the tall, fair-haired people in the street all suggest the Alps rather than the jungle, and the explanation is historical. In the nineteenth century, colonists from Austria and Germany settled the region, beginning with the colony of Pozuzo in 1859, and their descendants still keep traditions of baking, brewing, cheese-making and costume alive.
