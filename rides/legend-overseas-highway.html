Why this ride

A island highway from Key Largo, Florida to Key West, Florida, via Islamorada. Key character: turquoise water; bridges; tropical islands. Surface: paved. Highlight: 4 ferry crossings (Islamorada, about 25 km; Marathon, about 43 km; Big Pine Key, about 53 km; Key West, about 89 km) - check timetables before you go.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Miami

    The Tamiami Trail was named for what it joined, Tampa and Miami, and it took thirteen years and an astonishing amount of dynamite to build. Begun in 1915 and opened in 1928, US 41 cuts straight west across the Everglades, one of the largest wetlands in North America, and it changed Florida by linking the Atlantic and Gulf coasts, though it also interrupted the flow of water across the Everglades in a way that scientists are still trying to repair. The road starts in downtown Miami as Southwest 8th Street, known to locals as Calle Ocho, the heart of Little Havana.

  2. 2. Key Largo

    Key Largo is the first of the Florida Keys, the one you reach after the long straight run down from Florida City, and it is also the biggest, stretching some thirty miles along the line of the old coral reef. The Overseas Highway, US 1, runs through its entire length, lined with dive shops, tackle stores, seafood shacks and motels. The town itself has about ten thousand people, and fuel, groceries and food are easy to find all along the road, which is marked by mile markers counted from Key West.

  3. 3. Islamorada

    Islamorada is a string of low islands in the Upper Keys, and the name, Spanish for purple island, is applied to a village that spreads over several of them along the Overseas Highway. The sea is on both sides, the Atlantic to the east and the shallow, glassy water of Florida Bay to the west, and from the road you can often see both within a few hundred metres. It bills itself as the sport fishing capital of the world, and the claim is not idle, with charter boats, tackle shops and marinas lining the highway and anglers heading out before dawn for sailfish, tarpon and bonefish.

  4. 4. Near Marathon

    Marathon is the middle of the Florida Keys, both geographically and in spirit, a town of about nine thousand people spread across a string of islands halfway between Key Largo and Key West. It is less polished than Islamorada and less wild than Key West, a working town of marinas, fishing boats, strip malls and seafood shacks, with the sea never more than a few hundred metres away on either side. The Overseas Highway runs straight through the middle, lined with businesses, and the mile markers, which count down from Key Largo to Key West, put Marathon at around mile 50.

  5. 5. Near Big Pine Key

    The water around Bahia Honda is the colour you think only exists in advertisements, and it is real. Bahia Honda State Park, on a small key between the Seven Mile Bridge and Big Pine Key, has some of the best beaches in the Florida Keys, with white sand, palm trees and shallow, clear water in shades of turquoise and jade. The name means deep bay in Spanish, a reference to the channel between the islands, one of the deepest in the Keys, which made it a particular challenge for the builders of the railroad.

  6. 6. Key West

    Mile Marker 0 is a small thing to stand beside, a green sign at the corner of Whitehead and Fleming streets, but after about a hundred and thirty miles of bridges and causeways over water the colour of a swimming pool, it feels like a finish line in the most literal sense. Key West is the end of US 1, and the end of the road in the continental United States, closer to Havana than to Miami's downtown skyscrapers by a surprisingly small margin.
