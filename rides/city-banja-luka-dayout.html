Why this ride

A 374 km round trip from Mejdan - Obilićevo - roughly 6 h 27 in the saddle. The stops in between: Prašuma Janj, Maculje, Ravanjska vrata and back through Maculje. The highest point is about 1,250 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Banja Luka

    Banja Luka is the largest city in the northern half of Bosnia and Herzegovina and the capital of the Republika Srpska entity, set on the Vrbas River where the mountains of central Bosnia open onto the Pannonian plain. It is a spacious, leafy place with wide boulevards, plane trees and a riverfront that is busy with walkers, anglers and café-goers, and it has long had the reputation of being one of the greenest cities in the Balkans.

  2. 2. Prašuma Janj

    Prašuma means primeval forest, and Janj is one of the few places in Europe where that word is literally true. In the hills above the Janj river, between Šipovo and Donji Vakuf in central Bosnia, a block of mixed beech, fir and spruce forest has survived without logging, its oldest trees reaching enormous heights and girths. Since 2021 it has been part of the World Heritage site of ancient beech forests of Europe, joining a chain of reserves from the Carpathians to the Pyrenees.

  3. 3. Maculje

    Central Bosnia is a country of green hills folded one behind another, and Maculje sits in the middle of it, a few kilometres from Novi Travnik in the Lašva valley. If you have not heard of it, you are in good company, because what draws people here is quiet and very old. Maculje is a necropolis of stećci, the massive carved limestone tombstones that medieval Bosnians, Croatians and Serbs raised between roughly the twelfth and the sixteenth centuries. They stand in the grass in rows, slabs shaped like chests and gabled roofs, with weathered carvings of crescent moons, spirals, hunters and dancers.

  4. 4. Ravanjska vrata

    The Kupres plateau is one of the high, open places of Bosnia and Herzegovina, more than a thousand metres up, with wide grasslands, cold winters and a sky that seems close enough to touch. Ravanjska vrata, the gates of Ravno, lies in this landscape, between Kupres and Tomislavgrad, and it is known for a group of stećci, the carved medieval tombstones that are found across Bosnia, Herzegovina and the neighbouring countries. UNESCO listed a selection of these necropolises in 2016. The stones are often decorated with spirals, crosses, hunting scenes and figures with raised hands, and their makers remain a subject of debate.
