Why this ride

406 km from Toulouse to Toulouse, about 7 h of riding. The stops in between: Episcopal City of Albi, Châteaux de Lastours, Historic Fortified City of Carcassonne and back through Châteaux de Lastours. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Toulouse

    Toulouse has two characters, and they sit on the same streets. One is the pink city, built from handmade brick that glows rose and orange in evening light, with a square, a basilica and a long river of old merchants' houses. The other is the aerospace city, home to Airbus and a museum of flight, whose engineers have shaped the modern aviation of Europe. The two live side by side, and they give the city a pleasing mix of old stone and ambitious young people.

  2. 2. Episcopal City of Albi

    Albi is built of brick, which is unusual in the south of France, and the colour changes through the day, from salmon in the morning to a deep red at dusk. The great cathedral of Sainte-Cécile dominates the old town, and from a distance it looks more like a fortress than a church, with a massive tower and walls that have no windows at the bottom. It was begun in the late thirteenth century, soon after the crusade against the Cathars had been won, and its builders wanted the faithful, and the heretics, to see where power lay. Inside, the painted vault and a huge fresco of the Last Judgement are astonishing. The whole episcopal city, with the old bridge and the bishop's palace, was listed by UNESCO in 2010.

  4. 4. Châteaux de Lastours

    Four castles perch on a narrow rock ridge above the village of Lastours, in the Montagne Noire north of Carcassonne, and they look so improbable from the viewpoint across the valley that most people stop talking when they see them. The two streams, the Orbiel and the Grésilhou, wrap around the ridge, and the castles, Cabaret, Tour Régine, Surdespine and Quertinheux, stand along its length like beads on a string.

  5. 5. Historic Fortified City of Carcassonne

    Carcassonne is the fortress that every child draws when asked for a castle: a double ring of walls with about fifty towers, pointed slate roofs, a drawbridge and a keep, rising from a hilltop above the river Aude. The medieval city, known as La Cité, is the largest walled town in Europe still standing, and much of what you see was heavily restored in the nineteenth century by the architect Eugène Viollet-le-Duc, whose steep roofs on the towers are more northern French than local. The result is spectacular if debatable, and UNESCO listed the site in 1997.
