Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Near La Paz

    La Paz fills a deep canyon carved into the edge of the Bolivian Altiplano, its houses climbing the steep walls in a jumble of red brick, and above it all, the snow-covered peak of Illimani, at 6,438 metres, stands guard to the south-east. The city centre lies at about 3,600 metres, making it the highest seat of government in the world, and the sprawling city of El Alto spreads across the plateau on the rim above, at around four thousand metres.

  2. 2. North Yungas Road point 2

    La Cumbre is the high point of the North Yungas Road, a pass at roughly 4,650 metres above sea level where the road tips over the rim of the altiplano and heads down into the cloud forest. The setting is stark: cold, bare, boulder-strewn slopes with patches of snow, a few shrines and a statue of the Virgin, and a lot of wind. On a clear day the snow peaks of the Cordillera Real line up to the north, and the contrast with the green world below is almost theatrical.

  3. 3. Near Coroico

    Coroico hangs on a green ridge in the Yungas, the steep, cloud-wrapped country where the Bolivian Andes fall away toward the Amazon basin. The town sits at about 1,700 metres, which is warm and humid after the thin air of La Paz, and the whole landscape smells of wet earth, coffee and citrus. For a rider arriving from the capital, it is the end of one of the most famous roads in the world, a place where you can finally breathe, sit in the shade and watch the clouds roll up the valley.
