Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Manchester

    Manchester invented a good part of the modern world and then spent a century being too busy to brag about it. The first passenger railway station anywhere, Liverpool Road, opened here in 1830, the Bridgewater Canal brought coal into the city, and the mills of Cottonopolis spun and wove so much cloth that the damp red-brick warehouses became the model for industrial cities from Osaka to Massachusetts. The Cooperative movement began a few miles north in Rochdale, and the splitting of the atom was first achieved at the university here. Karl Marx's friend Friedrich Engels ran a mill in the city and wrote about its slums.

  2. 2. Jodrell Bank Observatory

    You see the dish long before you arrive. The Lovell Telescope at Jodrell Bank rises out of the flat Cheshire farmland like something from a science fiction film, a white steel bowl seventy six metres across, tilting slowly to follow its targets across the sky. When it was completed in 1957 it was the largest steerable radio telescope in the world, and one of its first tasks was tracking the rocket that carried Sputnik into orbit. The observatory, founded by Bernard Lovell after the Second World War, has been at the forefront of radio astronomy ever since, and it was listed by UNESCO in 2019 for its role in the development of the science.

  4. 4. Saltaire

    Titus Salt was a Bradford wool magnate who got tired of the smoke, the sickness and the squalor of the city, and in 1851 he began building a new mill and a new town beside the river Aire, a few miles north. Saltaire, his name joined to the river's, was a model community: tidy stone terraces of different sizes for different grades of worker, a hospital, a school, almshouses, a church, a park, and a huge Italianate mill with a campanile chimney. The mill opened in 1853 and was among the largest in the world. UNESCO listed the village in 2001 as an outstanding example of Victorian industrial planning.
