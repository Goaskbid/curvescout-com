Why this ride

375 km from San José to San José, about 6 h 28 of riding. The stops in between: Grijalba-2, Batambal, Precolumbian Chiefdom Settlements with Sto and El Silencio. The highest point is about 1,800 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. San Jose

    San José is not a city people fall in love with at first sight, and it does not mind. It is a working capital in the middle of Costa Rica's Central Valley, some 1,100 metres above the sea, with nearly everything the country needs packed into a grid of traffic, shopfronts, markets and bus stops. What it does have is a climate that is almost always mild, and a ring of volcanoes standing guard on the horizon, Irazú and Poás among them, whose peaks catch the early light when the air is clear.

  2. 2. Grijalba-2

    Grijalba-2 is one of the quiet corners of southern Costa Rica where a lost civilisation left its mark in stone. The site is part of the group called the Precolumbian Chiefdom Settlements with Stone Spheres of the Diquís, a UNESCO World Heritage listing from 2014 that covers four archaeological sites in the delta of the Térraba river, close to Ciudad Cortés. The spheres themselves are the puzzle. Carved from hard rock and ground to a startlingly smooth, nearly perfect roundness, the biggest are more than two metres across and weigh many tonnes.

  3. 3. Batambal

    Batambal sits a few kilometres from Ciudad Cortés in the lowland of the Diquís delta, a patch of flat, green, humid country that you could easily ride straight through without knowing what lay under the grass. It is one of the four sites that make up the UNESCO listing for the stone spheres of southern Costa Rica, a recognition that came in 2014 for the remains of chiefdoms that flourished here long before the Spanish. At Batambal, as elsewhere, the spheres lie among the low, rounded foundations of the houses and mounds those people built.

  4. 4. Precolumbian Chiefdom Settlements with Sto

    This is the label for the whole collection, and it deserves a longer look: the Precolumbian Chiefdom Settlements with Stone Spheres of the Diquís. UNESCO placed four sites on its World Heritage list in 2014, all within the lower delta of the Térraba river in southern Costa Rica, a few kilometres from Ciudad Cortés. They are the best preserved remains of a culture that thrived here for centuries before European contact, and the reason the word chiefdom appears in the title is that archaeologists see here a society of ranked leaders, craftsmen and farmers, ruled from house-mounds.
