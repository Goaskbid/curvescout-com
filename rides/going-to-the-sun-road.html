Why this ride

It goes by way of Lake McDonald Lodge, The Loop, Logan Pass and St Mary Lake. The highest point is about 2,050 m. Logan Pass is open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Going-to-the-Sun Road

Water arrives beside the road and stays for 35 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go — slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. West Glacier

    West Glacier is a small collection of lodges, cafes and gift shops at the western entrance of Glacier National Park, a few hundred yards from the gate and the railway, and it is the starting point for Going-to-the-Sun Road. The Great Northern Railway built much of the tourism here, advertising Glacier as America's Switzerland in the early twentieth century, and constructing grand lodges in the mountains. Trains still pass through the village on the line from Chicago to Seattle, and the sound of a long freight is part of the soundtrack.

  2. 2. Lake McDonald Lodge

    The lodge is the old hotel on the lake, built in 1913 and 1914 as a Swiss-chalet-meets-hunting-lodge confection of cedar logs, with antlers on the walls and a big stone fireplace in the lobby. Great Northern Railway took it over in the 1930s as part of its drive to bring wealthy easterners west by train. The hotel faces Lake McDonald, the largest lake in Glacier National Park, about ten miles long and more than 450 feet deep, ringed by forested ridges. Its shoreline is paved with small stones in red, green and purple, polished by the water, and so clear that it looks like a mosaic.

  3. 3. The Loop

    The Loop is the one proper hairpin on the western side of Going-to-the-Sun Road, and it is the place where the road, which has been climbing steadily up the valley, finally gets tired of going straight and turns back on itself in a big, left-handed sweep. It is a pull-out and trailhead as well, so you can stop and look at the road from the outside. A trail leads from here up to Granite Park Chalet, a stone mountain hut, and a lot of walkers set out from the parking lot.

  4. 4. Logan Pass

    Logan Pass sits at 2,026 metres on the Continental Divide, and it is the top of the road. After the cliffs and the long traverse from the west, the road flattens into a little saddle ringed by peaks, with a visitor centre, a parking lot that fills before most people have finished breakfast, and a boardwalk heading off across a meadow of alpine flowers. It is the highest point reached by any paved road in the park, and it feels like it. Snow sits in the hollows well into July.

  5. 5. St Mary Lake

    St Mary Lake is the east side of the story. Where the west is damp and thick with cedar, the east is drier, more open and far windier, because the mountains of Glacier fall away abruptly onto the plains of Blackfeet country. The lake lies in a long glacial trough about nine miles long, a deep blue, with a tiny island, Wild Goose Island, sitting like a ship in the middle. It is one of the most photographed spots in the park, and with good reason: the island, the peaks behind and the sky above make an almost absurdly tidy composition.

  6. 6. St Mary

    St. Mary is a little village on the eastern edge of Glacier National Park, and here the mountains meet the prairie with a bump. To the west rise the peaks, steep and dark, with the long finger of St. Mary Lake at their foot, while to the east the land opens out into rolling grassland as far as the horizon. The village is a cluster of cafes, motels, a store and a visitor centre, and it is the eastern gateway to Going-to-the-Sun Road.
