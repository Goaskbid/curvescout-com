  1. 1. Qachas Nek

    Qacha's Nek is a border town in the far southeast of Lesotho, perched on a ridge at the top of a pass that the town takes its name from, with a sheer drop on one side and a view across a sea of ridges on the other. It is a remote place by any standard, and the border post, leads over the pass to the South African town of Matatiele, a short ride away. About twenty-five thousand people live in the district, and the town itself is a modest cluster of shops, government offices and guesthouses.

  2. 2. Sehlabathebe

    Sehlabathebe is about as far from anywhere as you can get in southern Africa, and that is its charm. The national park, in the south-east corner of Lesotho against the Drakensberg escarpment, was among the first protected areas in the country and is high, empty and rarely visited, a plateau at nearly 2,500 metres of grass, wetland and sandstone outcrops. The name is often translated as plateau of the shield, and the surrounding hills do have a wide, rounded look.
