Why this ride

A 301 km round trip from Pajaroncillo - roughly 5 h 11 in the saddle. The stops in between: Cueva de la Peña de la morería, Rillo I and back through Cueva de la Peña de la morería. The highest point is about 1,600 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abrigo de la fuente de Selva Pascuala

    Villar del Humo is a village of a few hundred people tucked into the folds of the Serranía de Cuenca, a high limestone plateau of pine woods, gorges and rocky cliffs in the east of Castilla-La Mancha, and the surrounding rock shelters hold some of the finest prehistoric paintings in Spain. They belong to what is called Levantine rock art, a style of small, lively red figures painted in shallow shelters across the east of the peninsula, which UNESCO listed in 1998 as the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin. Hunters with bows, running animals and sometimes whole scenes appear on the walls, painted thousands of years ago by people whose names and languages we will never know.

  2. 2. Cueva de la Peña de la morería

    The Sierra de Albarracín is a high, cold, empty land of pine forests, red sandstone and limestone, at well over a thousand metres, and it hides some of the most interesting prehistoric rock art in Spain. The Pinares de Rodeno, a protected landscape of pine forests and red sandstone outcrops near Albarracín, has shelters with paintings of bulls, deer and human figures, included in UNESCO's inscription of the rock art of the Mediterranean Basin. The Cueva de la Peña de la Morería is one of the sites in this region, near the villages of Bronchales and Orihuela del Tremedal, at the foot of the mountains.

  3. 3. Rillo I

    The Señorío de Molina is a high, cold, sparsely populated tableland on the boundary of Guadalajara and Aragon, and it feels like it, with big skies, wheat and sheep, wide valleys and villages of stone. Rillo, five or six kilometres from Molina de Aragón, sits at about a thousand metres on this plateau, where winters are among the harshest in Spain and snow can lie for weeks. Molina has a reputation as one of the coldest towns in the country, but a rider in June finds only a dry, bright warmth.
