Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Los Angeles / Southern California

    Joshua trees and creosote bush spread out toward a horizon cut by the San Bernardino Mountains to the south, and on a clear morning the snow on the higher ridges looks borrowed from another state. Adelanto, a town of some thirty-odd thousand, is the sensible place to sort fuel and a breakfast burrito; Victorville sits right next to it with more of everything.

  2. 2. View Park-Windsor Hills

    Up on the slopes of the Baldwin Hills, in the middle of the Los Angeles basin, View Park and Windsor Hills look out over the city in every direction: downtown's towers to the north-east, the Hollywood sign and the San Gabriels to the north, the ocean and the airport to the west. It is a leafy, quiet neighbourhood of curving streets and handsome 1920s to 1950s houses, and since the 1960s it has been one of the most affluent Black communities in the United States, sometimes called the Black Beverly Hills. Ray Charles lived here, and the area has been home to generations of doctors, judges, musicians and athletes.

  3. 3. Back to Big

    Garden Grove sits in the middle of Orange County's great flat basin, a tapestry of low-rise suburbs between Disneyland to the north and the beach towns to the south-west. The label on this stop only says Big, presumably short for Big Bear, so what I can honestly describe is the setting, which is the low, built-up plain from which the long ride into the mountains begins. The San Bernardino range stands to the north-east, and on a clear winter morning its snow-covered ridges seem to float above the haze.
