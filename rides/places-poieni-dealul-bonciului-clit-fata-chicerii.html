Why this ride

From Poieni it runs 163 km to Poieni; allow about 2 h 49 of riding. On the way it passes Buciumi - Pădurea Dosu, Frontiers of the Roman Empire - Dacia, Cliț - Fața Chicerii and back through Frontiers of the Roman Empire - Dacia. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Poieni - Dealul Bonciului

    Anyone riding west out of Cluj on the E60 meets the same sequence: a long climb to Huedin, a market town on a wide valley floor, then a long run down towards Ciucea and the gorge of the Crișul Repede, with the Apuseni Mountains closing in on the south. What I can describe is the way the road behaves.

  2. 2. Buciumi - Pădurea Dosu

    Dosu forest lies above Buciumi, in the hills where Sălaj starts rising toward the Meseș range. It is a working woodland, not a showpiece: beech and oak, a few hornbeam, tracks cut by loggers, and a floor that smells strongly of leaf mould after rain. If you stop and kill the engine, the quiet is very complete. Wild boar and roe deer use these slopes, and the ground along the verges is often turned up by the former.

  3. 3. Frontiers of the Roman Empire - Dacia

    Above the town of Zalau, where the Meses mountains form a long, wooded ridge across northern Transylvania, the Roman Empire built one of its most remote defences. The mountains separated the province of Dacia from the free tribes to the north, and the ridge was fortified with a line of watchtowers and forts, the western part of what is now called the Frontiers of the Roman Empire in Dacia. The greatest of them was Porolissum, a garrison town and customs post on the Meses Gate, the pass through the ridge, near the village of Moigrad.

  4. 4. Cliț - Fața Chicerii

    Northern Sălaj is quiet country: low wooded hills, hay meadows, plum orchards and villages strung along the Someș river and its side valleys, with wooden churches and haystacks built around poles in the fields. Cliț is a small village near Băbeni, and the point named Fața Chicerii lies in the hills above it. Names in this list follow the pattern of the Romanian archaeological record, a village and then the local name of a hillside, and in this part of Sălaj many such points belong to the line of watchtowers and small forts of the Roman frontier of Dacia, which UNESCO recognised in 2024.
