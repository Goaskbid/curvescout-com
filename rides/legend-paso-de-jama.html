Why this ride

A high desert crossing from San Pedro de Atacama, Chile to San Salvador de Jujuy, Argentina, via Jama Pass. Key character: Atacama; altiplano; salt flats. Surface: paved.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. San Salvador de Jujuy

    Jujuy, as everyone calls it, sits in a green bowl about 1,200 metres up in the far north-west of Argentina, where the wet forests of the eastern slope meet the dry high country behind. The city was founded in 1593, on the third attempt, after earlier settlements were wiped out or abandoned, and it still has the look of a provincial capital that has had to work for its place. About three hundred thousand people live here, with the river Xibi Xibi running through the middle of town and the hills of the Yungas closing in on three sides. It is the usual start for the long climb into the Andes.

  2. 2. Near Tumbaya

    Purmamarca is the place, even though the label names nearby Tumbaya: a small adobe village at the foot of the Cerro de los Siete Colores, the Hill of Seven Colours, where layers of red, purple, green, yellow and white rock are folded into a mountain like a painted cake. It sits at the mouth of a side valley off the Quebrada de Humahuaca in the far north-west of Argentina, about 2,300 metres up, and it is where the road to the Paso de Jama and Chile begins its long climb into the Altiplano.

  3. 3. Susques

    Susques is a small high-altitude town on the road to Chile, perched on the Argentine puna at about 3,600 metres, where the air is thin, the sky is deep blue and the horizon is made of bare rust-coloured hills. The Ruta 52 climbs here from Jujuy through the Quebrada de Purmamarca, over the Cuesta de Lipán, one of the highest and most dramatic mountain roads in Argentina, then crosses the great white Salinas Grandes, the salt flats, before arriving in Susques. The town marks the beginning of the real altiplano.

  4. 4. Paso de Jama point 4

    Beyond Susques the road enters one of the emptiest and highest stretches of the whole journey, the high Puna, where the plateau runs on at around four thousand metres and the horizon is a rim of volcanoes. The road climbs gently towards the Paso de Jama, which is at about 4,200 metres and is the main Argentine border crossing into the Atacama.

  5. 5. San Pedro de Atacama

    San Pedro de Atacama is a small adobe town in the driest desert on Earth, and the contradiction is the attraction. It lies at 2,400 metres on the edge of the Salar de Atacama, a vast salt flat, in an oasis fed by streams from the Andes. The streets are dusty and low, the walls are the colour of dry mud, and behind the town the Licancabur volcano, nearly 5,920 metres, stands as a perfect cone on the border with Bolivia. Everything here seems to lean towards the sky.
