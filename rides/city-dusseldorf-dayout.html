  1. 1. Dusseldorf

    Düsseldorf has two reputations that do not always sit comfortably together: fashion and beer. The Königsallee, a wide boulevard with a tree-lined canal down the middle, is lined with expensive shops, and the city is a hub for advertising, trade fairs and business. Yet the old town, the Altstadt, is crammed with traditional brewery pubs serving Altbier, a dark copper top-fermented beer poured fresh from the barrel into small glasses by waiters called Köbes. The stretch is often called the longest bar in the world, and on a Friday night it seems like it.

  2. 2. Cologne Cathedral

    Six hundred and thirty-two years. That is how long it took to finish Cologne Cathedral, from the laying of the foundation stone in 1248 to the completion of the spires in 1880, and when you stand at its foot by the main station it feels as though the building is still growing. The twin towers climb to around 157 metres, black with soot and weather, and for a few years after completion they made it the tallest building in the world.

  4. 4. Zollverein Coal Mine Industrial Complex in

    The headframe of Shaft XII at Zollverein, with its red steel lattice and double wheels, is the Eiffel Tower of the Ruhr, and it is easy to see why. Designed by the architects Fritz Schupp and Martin Kremmer and opened in 1932, the shaft and its surrounding buildings were built in a clean, functional style influenced by the Bauhaus, and it was hailed as the most beautiful coal mine in the world. At its peak, Zollverein was one of the largest and most productive mines in Europe, and the adjacent coking plant processed coal into coke for the steel industry. The mine closed in 1986, the coking plant in 1993, and UNESCO listed the complex in 2001.
