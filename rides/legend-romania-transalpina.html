Why this ride

curated scenic road. Carpathian ridge, high plateau, hairpins. From Novaci it runs 87 km to Sebeş; allow about 1 h 35 of riding. The highest point is about 2,150 m. Urdele Pass is open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Romania Transalpina

  1. 1. Novaci

    Novaci is the southern gate of the Transalpina, and for a town of fewer than three thousand people it has a good deal of mountain on its doorstep. It sits in Gorj county, on the southern edge of the Parâng range, where the Oltenian foothills fold up into the high Carpathians, and the air smells of sheep, hay and woodsmoke. Sheep farming has been the livelihood of the district for centuries, and the shepherds still drive their flocks up to the high pastures in summer, living in simple huts and making cheese.

  3. 3. Romania Transalpina point 3

    This is the top of Romania's roof road. Near here the Transalpina crosses the Parang range at Urdele pass, at about two thousand one hundred and forty metres, making it the highest paved road in the country. The landscape is almost treeless, rolling alpine grassland and bare rock, scattered with glacial lakes and patches of old snow that can last into July. On a clear day you can see ridge after ridge folding away into the haze, and on a cloudy one you can see nothing but the next hairpin.

  4. 4. Romania Transalpina point 4

    Beyond the high crest, the mountains begin to fall away to the north, and the land below is the Jiu valley, one of Romania's most industrial landscapes. For much of the twentieth century the coal mines here employed tens of thousands of men, and the towns of Petrila, Petrosani and their neighbours grew up around the pits. The miners had a fearsome reputation for solidarity and sometimes for violence, and in the early 1990s their marches on Bucharest became a national news story. Many of the mines have since closed, and the region is reinventing itself with mountain tourism and a long, complicated memory.

  5. 5. Sebeş

    Sebeş is where the Transalpina comes down to earth, a town of about twenty-seven thousand people on the Sebeş River in Transylvania, and the northern end of the great mountain road. It belongs to the world of the Transylvanian Saxons, German settlers who were invited here in the twelfth century by the Hungarian kings to defend the frontier and develop the land. They built fortified churches and walled towns, and Sebeş, which they called Mühlbach, grew into one of the seven fortified cities that gave Transylvania its German name, Siebenbürgen.
