Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Limessection between WP 4/37 and 4/39

    The Wetterau is a broad, fertile basin north of Frankfurt, and the Limes crosses it on its way from the Taunus hills towards the Main. Near Butzbach, the frontier runs through farmland and low wooded ridges, with a line of watchtowers, and the stretch between towers 4/37 and 4/39 is a quiet piece of it. The Saalburg, the reconstructed Roman fort in the Taunus, lies a short distance to the south, and it makes the best starting point for understanding what the frontier looked like.

  2. 2. Watchtowers WP 5/15 to 5/16

    Great forts are rare on the Limes; most of the frontier was watchtowers, small timber and later stone posts within sight of each other, each with a handful of men and a fire for signalling. The towers numbered 5/15 and 5/16 stood near Großkrotzenburg, at the point where the land frontier coming down from the Wetterau met the Main, and from here southward the river itself became the border as far as Miltenberg. Little of them remains to see, but the location tells the story: the Romans used water where they could and built palisades only where they had to.

  3. 3. Fort site and civil settlement of Miltenbe

    Miltenberg is one of those German towns that seem almost too pretty to be real, with half-timbered houses leaning over the cobbled streets, a market square sloping down toward the river, a castle on the hill above and the Main flowing slowly past. The Romans were here first. Two forts guarded this point on the frontier, one near the old town and another to the east, where the Limes left the river and ran overland through the Odenwald, and the sites, with their civilian settlements, are part of the UNESCO Frontiers of the Roman Empire. A museum in the town displays finds from the forts.
