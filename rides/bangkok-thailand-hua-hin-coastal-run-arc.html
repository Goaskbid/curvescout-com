Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bangkok

    Bangkok is a city you meet with all your senses at once: the smell of charcoal grills and jasmine garlands, the sound of tuk-tuks and boat engines on the river, the heat that reaches you before you step out of the hotel lobby. About five million people live in the city proper and many more in the sprawl around it, and the Chao Phraya river is the thread that still holds the old centre together. The Grand Palace, Wat Pho with its huge reclining Buddha, and the temples of the old quarter sit along its eastern bank, and ferries cross the water for a few baht.

    Read the complete Bangkok write-up.

  2. 2. Phetchaburi

    Phetchaburi is one of Thailand's old royal cities, and its skyline is defined by a hill. Phra Nakhon Khiri, known locally as Khao Wang, rises just west of the town centre, its summit crowned by a nineteenth-century palace built for King Mongkut, Rama IV, a mix of Thai, Chinese and European styles, with white chedis and a small observatory where the king, a keen astronomer, studied the stars. A cable car runs up the hill, and the monkeys that live on its slopes are notorious for grabbing anything shiny or edible, so keep your belongings close.

    Read the complete Phetchaburi write-up.

  3. 3. Hua Hin

    Thai royalty fell for the beach at Hua Hin long before anyone else, in the early twentieth century, when the new southern railway made it easy to reach from Bangkok. King Prajadhipok built a summer palace here in the 1920s and named it Klai Kangwon, which means Far from Worries. Thai kings have spent long periods here ever since, and the town has kept a slightly more refined, less frantic air than many other resorts.
