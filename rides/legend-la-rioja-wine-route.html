Why this ride

regional wine route. vineyards, hill towns, wine estates. From Logroño it runs 58 km to Nájera; allow about 1 h 03 of riding. The stops in between: Laguardia, Haro and Briones. The highest point is about 1,050 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Logroño

    Logroño sits on the south bank of the Ebro, in the centre of La Rioja, and for a wine lover it is as close as it comes to a capital. The city has around a hundred and fifty thousand inhabitants and a pleasant old quarter, with a cathedral, an old stone bridge and a very tasty pedestrian street called Calle del Laurel, where bar after bar serves a single speciality, such as mushrooms with prawn, or small skewers of grilled pork, along with a glass of the local red. Locals walk from bar to bar, eating one or two things in each, a ritual called the pinchos crawl.

  2. 2. Laguardia

    Laguardia stands on a ridge above the Ebro valley, a walled hill town with the long blue wall of the Sierra de Cantabria rising behind it like a stage backdrop. It is the main town of the Rioja Alavesa, the part of the Basque Country that lies south of the mountains and produces some of Spain's best-known wine, and it has about fifteen hundred permanent residents, most of whom seem to have a cellar.

  3. 3. Haro

    Haro is the capital of the Rioja Alta, a mid-sized river town with twelve thousand inhabitants and an astonishing density of wine. It sits where the Tirón river comes down to meet the Ebro, with the Sierra de Cantabria on the north side and the blue ridges of the Sierra de la Demanda far away to the south, and the soil of the surrounding slopes gives the wines their reputation for elegance rather than muscle.

  4. 4. Briones

    Briones sits on a hill above the Ebro, a village of fewer than nine hundred people with a ring of old walls, a big baroque church and more wine in its cellars than anyone could drink in a lifetime. It belongs to La Rioja Alta, the western and highest part of the Rioja wine region, where the river turns the landscape green and the vines run in long lines up toward the grey limestone wall of the Sierra de Cantabria. The silhouette of the village, with its church tower at the top, rises over the vineyards like a ship's prow.

  5. 5. Nájera

    Nájera has been a capital, and the town remembers it. In the eleventh century, it was the seat of the kings of Nájera-Pamplona, and the great monastery of Santa María la Real, founded in 1032 by King García Sánchez III, still stands at the foot of a red sandstone cliff. The legend says the king, chasing a dove while hunting, followed it into a cave and found a statue of the Virgin inside, and he built the monastery on the spot. The church holds the royal pantheon, with carved tombs of the kings and queens of Navarre, and a cloister of delicate stone tracery. The cliff above, the colour of rust, gives the whole town a warm tone.
