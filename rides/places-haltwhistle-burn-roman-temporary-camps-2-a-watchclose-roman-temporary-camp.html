Why this ride

A 67 km round trip from Haltwhistle - roughly 1 h 09 in the saddle. On the way it passes Nether Denton Roman fort, Watchclose Roman temporary camp and back through Nether Denton Roman fort. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Haltwhistle Burn Roman temporary camps 2 a

    Haltwhistle Burn is a small stream that cuts down toward the South Tyne just east of Haltwhistle, and the name is one of those wonderful northern English mouthfuls that seems to have been in use forever. Here, beside the burn, the Romans left a cluster of temporary camps, several overlapping enclosures that show how often armies returned to the same convenient ground. Alongside them lies a fort on the Stanegate, the military road that predates Hadrian's Wall. The camps are low earthworks in pasture, best appreciated in slanting light and with a little imagination.

  2. 2. Nether Denton Roman fort

    Before Hadrian built his wall, the Romans controlled northern Britain with a line of forts along a road called the Stanegate, which ran from Corbridge to Carlisle, and Nether Denton was one of them. The fort, on a hill above the River Irthing near Gilsland, is now marked only by faint earthworks around the small church of Nether Denton, which stands on the site. It is a quiet place, with only rooks and sheep for company.

  3. 3. Watchclose Roman temporary camp

    East of Carlisle, between the villages of Brampton and Wetheral, the countryside rolls gently towards the Pennines, with green fields, hedges and patches of woodland. Somewhere in these fields lie the faint traces of Watchclose, a Roman temporary camp, one of many marching camps built by soldiers on campaign or during the construction of Hadrian's Wall. These camps were enclosed by a ditch and bank and used for a night or a season, and today they are often visible only from the air or as slight undulations in the grass. Watchclose is part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire World Heritage site.
