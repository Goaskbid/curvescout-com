Why this ride

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Lahti

    Lahti sits on the southern shore of Lake Vesijärvi, at the end of a long ridge, and its look was shaped by ice. The ridge is the Salpausselkä, a great curved moraine pushed up at the edge of the retreating glacier about eleven thousand years ago, and it runs across southern Finland like a stone eyebrow. Lahti grew up where the railway and the ridge met, a young city by Finnish standards, officially a town only since 1905, and it has become one of the country's winter sports centres. The big ski-jumping hills dominate the skyline, with the Lahti Ski Games held every winter, and a museum tells the history.

  2. 2. Heinola

    Heinola sits on a narrow strip of land between two lakes in southern Finland, about fifty kilometres north-east of Lahti, and it is one of those towns that has quietly reinvented itself more than once. It is a lakeland place through and through, with water on both sides, and the big lake system of Päijänne and the Kymijoki watershed lies close by. The Jyrängönkoski rapids, a short walk from the centre, give the town a pleasant spot to watch the current while you drink coffee.

  3. 3. Mikkeli

    Finland's Lakeland does not rise, it spreads. Mikkeli lies in the middle of the Saimaa region, in the province of South Savo, on a landscape of long glacial ridges, forest and water that stretches in every direction. The town has about forty-six thousand people, which makes it the biggest place for a long way, and its centre sits on the shore of Lake Saimaa with a small harbour and a market square where you can buy fresh vendace, the little fish called muikku, fried crisp and eaten with mashed potato.

  4. 4. Puumala

    Puumala sits on a narrow strait in the middle of Lake Saimaa, the biggest lake in Finland, and the whole village is arranged around the point where road and water meet. Saimaa is less a single lake than an endless puzzle of channels, bays and islands, thousands of them, and the road here crosses the water on a bridge over the Puumalansalmi strait, with the glittering lake on either side and a light shimmering off the surface in summer. The endangered Saimaa ringed seal, which lives nowhere else in the world, swims in these waters, though you will be very lucky to catch a glimpse of one.
