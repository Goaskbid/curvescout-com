Why this ride

curated scenic road. volcanic mountains, laurel forest, plateau. A one-way ride of 32 km, Ribeira Brava to Porto Moniz - roughly 35 min in the saddle. The highest point is about 1,000 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ribeira Brava

    Ribeira Brava is a south-coast Madeira town that sits at the mouth of a deep valley and the foot of the old road across the island. A small bay of dark pebbles, a church with a handsome tiled tower, a seafront that locals actually use, and then the mountains rise straight behind. It has roughly six thousand people, so petrol, cash machines, bakeries and a decent lunch are all easy, which is exactly what a start point should offer.

  2. 2. Near São Vicente

    São Vicente lies on Madeira's north coast, in a narrow green valley where the mountains drop almost straight into the sea, and it is the sort of place where a road has to be persuaded into existence. The old road along the north coast, the ER101, runs between the sea cliffs and the steep slopes, in and out of tunnels carved through the rock, and over small bridges that cross waterfalls. The result is one of the most dramatic coastal roads in the Atlantic, wild, wet and green.

  4. 4. Porto Moniz

    Porto Moniz sits at the north-western corner of Madeira, where the island runs out of land and meets the Atlantic with a wall of black volcanic rock. The village is small, with just under three thousand inhabitants, and its fame rests on the natural swimming pools, a series of basins formed in solidified lava by ancient eruptions, which fill with clear seawater at high tide and make a sheltered place to swim among the rocks while the swell crashes just beyond. Fishermen still work the coast, and the village has a small fort, a handful of cafes and a lively fish restaurant tradition. The name comes from an early settler family, the Monizes, who received land here in the fifteenth century.
