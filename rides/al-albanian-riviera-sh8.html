Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Vlorë

    Vlorë is where the Adriatic and the Ionian Sea are said to meet, and that is the first thing a local will tell you, with some pride, even if the geographers are a little vague about exactly where the line falls. The town sits at the head of a wide bay, closed off to the west by the long mountainous arm of the Karaburun peninsula and the island of Sazan, and behind it the Llogara range stands up to well over two thousand metres. It is a big, busy, slightly chaotic place of nearly ninety thousand people, with a long seafront promenade of palms and cafes.

  2. 2. Himarë

    Himarë is the heart of the Albanian Riviera, a small town on the Ionian coast where the mountains stand so close to the sea that the road has to squeeze between them. The coast road, the SH8, runs for about a hundred and fifty kilometres from Vlorë to Sarandë, and it is one of the best rides in the Balkans, though it is still a road that demands attention. The town itself has a population of a few thousand, an old hilltop quarter with a Byzantine castle, and a long modern waterfront with cafes, hotels and a gravel beach.

  3. 3. Sarandë

    Sarandë sits at the bottom of Albania's long coast, in a curving bay facing the Greek island of Corfu, which on a clear evening looks close enough to swim to. It is a town of about fifteen thousand, built in tiers up a steep hillside, with a promenade running the length of the waterfront and a view that makes the end of the SH8 feel like a reward. The road you have just finished, the coastal highway from Vlorë over the Llogara Pass, is among the most dramatic in the Balkans.
