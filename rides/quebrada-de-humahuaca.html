Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. San Salvador de Jujuy

    Jujuy, as everyone calls it, sits in a green bowl about 1,200 metres up in the far north-west of Argentina, where the wet forests of the eastern slope meet the dry high country behind. The city was founded in 1593, on the third attempt, after earlier settlements were wiped out or abandoned, and it still has the look of a provincial capital that has had to work for its place. About three hundred thousand people live here, with the river Xibi Xibi running through the middle of town and the hills of the Yungas closing in on three sides. It is the usual start for the long climb into the Andes.

  2. 2. Purmamarca

    Purmamarca is the village most people photograph first in the Quebrada de Humahuaca, and the reason is the hill behind it. The Cerro de los Siete Colores rises straight from the edge of the settlement in bands of pink, rust, ochre, green and grey, and the colours change hourly: pale and chalky at noon, deep and almost wet-looking when the low sun rakes across them in the late afternoon. It is a small adobe village, about two thousand three hundred metres up in Jujuy province, in the far north-west of Argentina.

  3. 3. Tilcara

    Tilcara sits at about 2,400 metres in the Quebrada de Humahuaca, the long, colourful gorge in Jujuy province that was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2003. The town is a small place of low adobe houses and cobbled streets, with a plaza, a church with a white bell tower and a scattering of craft shops and cafes, and it is surrounded by hills striped in red, ochre, pink and green. The air is dry and sharp, the sun is strong, and the sky is a deep, dark blue at midday.

  4. 4. Humahuaca

    Humahuaca sits at almost three thousand metres in the Quebrada de Humahuaca, a long, dry, colourful valley in the far north of Argentina that UNESCO has listed as a World Heritage landscape. The valley runs for about a hundred and fifty kilometres, with the river Grande flowing between walls of red, ochre, green and violet rock, and cardón cacti standing like candelabra on the hillsides. The road through it, the Ruta 9, follows an ancient trade route used by the Incas, and before them by many peoples who carried llama caravans between the Andes and the lowlands.

  5. 5. Abra Pampa

    Abra Pampa is a high, wind-scoured town on the Puna of Jujuy, at around thirty-five hundred metres, and it marks the point where the Quebrada de Humahuaca, a long, colourful valley, finally opens onto the bare plateau. The land here is flat and brown, with low hills in the distance, and the sky seems very close. Houses of adobe stand in neat rows, and the main street holds a plaza, a church and a few shops. It is a working place, with a market, a bus terminal and a hospital, rather than a tourist stop, and that gives it a certain honest charm.
