Why this ride

Out of Salta, one way: 401 km, roughly 6 h 44 in the saddle. It goes by way of Alemania, Amphitheatre and Devils Throat. The highest point is about 3,000 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Salta

    Salta calls itself La Linda, the beautiful, and the old centre makes a decent case for it. The city lies in a green valley in the far north-west of Argentina, about twelve hundred metres above the sea, with the Andes to the west and the humid forest edge to the east. The Spanish founded it in 1582, and the colonial core still shows it, with the cathedral's pink facade, the arcaded cabildo on the main plaza and the cafés underneath the verandahs. In the evening the plaza fills with families, buskers and street food, and the empanadas salteñas, small and juicy with beef, are an essential meal.

  2. 2. Alemania

    Alemania is a small, sun-baked place at the entrance to the Quebrada de las Conchas, on the road from Salta to Cafayate, and it looks like the setting for a western. The village lies in a dry valley of the Calchaquí country, surrounded by red hills, with a river bed that is dry for much of the year and a few low houses of adobe and brick. It is quiet to the point of stillness, a place where a dog sleeping in the road is a major event, and a ghostly air hangs over the old railway station.

  3. 3. Amphitheatre

    The Amphitheatre is a huge natural bowl cut into the red rock of the Quebrada de las Conchas, a half-circle of towering sandstone with the acoustics of a concert hall. The walls are about thirty metres high, and the narrow opening lets the sound bounce around in a way that makes a single voice carry. Local musicians often play here, with charango and guitar, and a few sing for tips. It is an extraordinary experience to hear a folk song echoing around the cliffs, and even a shout comes back at you with a startling clarity.

  4. 4. Devils Throat

    The Garganta del Diablo, the Devil's Throat, is a narrow, deep cleft in the red cliffs of the Quebrada de las Conchas, and it is one of the most dramatic stops on the road between Salta and Cafayate. A short path leads from the road up into the gorge, between walls that close in overhead and cut out much of the sky. The rock is rust-coloured and streaked with other tones, and the light inside is a strange glowing orange. It is cool and shaded, a welcome contrast to the hot sun outside.

  5. 5. Cafayate

    Cafayate is a wine town at about sixteen hundred metres in the Calchaquí valleys of Salta province, in the dry north-west of Argentina, and it is the reward at the end of a long ride south. After days of high desert, salt flats and unpaved passes, you come down into a flat valley full of vines and suddenly there is shade, cold white wine and a plaza where people sit for hours. The mountains stand on both sides, brown and bare, and the light is so clear that distances seem shorter than they are.
