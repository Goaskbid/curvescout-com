Why this ride

A 381 km round trip from Cusipata - roughly 6 h 34 in the saddle. On the way it passes Segment Machaqmarka-Raqchi-Qquea, Segment Chiwuanquiri, Segment Pancca-Buena Vista and back through Segment Chiwuanquiri. The highest point is about 4,450 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Segment Cusipata-Santa Cruz de Occobamba N

    South of Cusco, the Vilcanota River runs through a broad, fertile valley flanked by high mountains, and along it ran one of the most important stretches of the Qhapaq Ñan, the Inca road system that linked an empire stretching from Ecuador to Chile. The route from Cusco to Lake Titicaca followed this valley, and sections of the original road, some paved with stone, some cut into the hillside, still survive. The segment between Cusipata and Santa Cruz de Occobamba went onto the World Heritage list in 2014 as one strand of the Qhapaq Ñan.

  2. 2. Segment Machaqmarka-Raqchi-Qquea

    The Vilcanota valley south of Cusco is the old highway of the Andes, and the Inca road ran along it towards Lake Titicaca and the southern provinces of the empire. The segment between Machaqmarka, Raqchi and Qquea is one of the sections of the Qhapaq Ñan listed by UNESCO, and it passes one of the most impressive Inca sites outside Cusco. At Raqchi, near San Pedro de Cacha, the Temple of Wiracocha, dedicated to the creator god, was an enormous building with a central wall of stone and adobe that still rises around fifteen metres high, flanked by rows of columns. Around it are the remains of storehouses, the round qollqas, lined up in rows, houses, baths and fountains, and a lava field from the nearby volcano of Kinsach'ata, which according to legend erupted when Wiracocha passed by. The village holds a festival in June, with music and dancing.

  3. 3. Segment Chiwuanquiri

    The Qhapaq Ñan, the great road system of the Inca Empire, stretched over thirty thousand kilometres across the Andes, linking Cusco with the farthest corners of the empire, from southern Colombia to central Chile. In 2014, UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site across six countries, and the listing includes hundreds of individual segments, each a fragment of the network. The Chiwuanquiri segment lies in the high province of Canas, south of Cusco, near the village of Cahuanuyo, on the high puna at around four thousand metres. Here, the old road crosses open grassland, with stone paving, retaining walls and drainage channels still visible in places, and it is a moving experience to walk on a road built more than five centuries ago.

  4. 4. Segment Pancca-Buena Vista

    A section of the old Inca road, the Qhapaq Ñan, runs through the area, part of the huge network that the Incas built to link Cusco with the far reaches of their empire, now recognised as a World Heritage site. In places the road survives as a paved or stone-edged track that crosses pasture and peat, a reminder of how far a runner or llama train once travelled.
