Why this ride

From Poiana Blenchii it runs 154 km to Poiana Blenchii; allow about 2 h 39 of riding. It goes by way of Negreni - Dealul Hoancelor, Tihău - Dealul Cucului, Zalău - Deasupra Șesurilor Tâlhăroasei and back through Tihău - Dealul Cucului. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Muncel - Dâmbul lui Golaș

    Muncel is a village in the hills north-east of Dej, and the thing to know about Dej is salt. The town, with about thirty-eight thousand people, stands at the confluence of the Somesul Mare and the Somesul Mic, the two rivers that together form the Somes, and for centuries it was one of the centres of salt production in Transylvania, with a mine at Ocna Dej whose old galleries and ponds are now used for swimming and health cures. Salt was the white gold of the Middle Ages, and the Hungarian kings, and the Romans before them, took good care to control it.

  2. 2. Negreni - Dealul Hoancelor

    A ridge called Dealul Hoancelor does not make it onto many maps, and the place is better understood from the landscape around it. This is the country of the Someș river in the north of Transylvania, between the Meseș mountains in the west and the hills of the Transylvanian plain to the east, and the valleys are broad, green and thickly farmed. The nearest places on our list are tiny villages, Lozna and Letca, and the closest town, Jibou, is about twenty kilometres away.

  3. 3. Tihău - Dealul Cucului

    Dealul Cucului means cuckoo hill, and the name fits the place: a low, wooded rise above the village of Tihău, close to the town of Jibou in the Someș valley of Sălaj county, where in spring the cuckoo's call echoes across the meadows. The point belongs to the network of archaeological sites along the Roman frontier of Dacia in the northwest of the province, near the confluence of the Almaș and Someș rivers, a strategic spot where valleys meet and roads cross. As with many of these sites, there may be little to see on the surface, but the location makes sense once you look at the map.

  4. 4. Zalău - Deasupra Șesurilor Tâlhăroasei

    The Meseș mountains rise to the west of Zalău like a long green wall, and they formed the natural western boundary of Roman Dacia in this region. Along their crest and on the slopes above the plains, the Romans built a line of watchtowers, and the site above the fields with the evocative name of Șesurile Tâlhăroasei, roughly the robbers' flats, near the villages of Treznea and Aghireș, is one of them. It is part of the Dacian frontier on the World Heritage list. Up in the forest the remains are modest, but the position, high on a slope with a view over the valley, shows how the frontier used every advantage of the terrain.
