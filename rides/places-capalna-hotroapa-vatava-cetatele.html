Why this ride

A 267 km round trip from Poiana Blenchii - roughly 5 h 24 in the saddle. On the way it passes Salva - Roata lui Todoran, Vătava - Cetățele and back through Salva - Roata lui Todoran. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Căpâlna - Hotroapă

    Start here.

    Căpâlna sits in the Someș country northwest of Dej, in a landscape of long, rounded hills that the Transylvanian plateau does so well. Orchards, hayfields and small woods climb the slopes, villages sit where streams meet, and the horizon always seems to hold another soft ridge.

    Read the complete Căpâlna - Hotroapă write-up.

  2. 2. Salva - Roata lui Todoran

    64 km in.

    Salva is a railway village. Trains for the Maramureș line split off here from the route along the Someșul Mare, and the place has a slightly busy, crossroads feel for a commune of a little over four thousand people. The site called Roata lui Todoran, Todoran's wheel, sits right by the village in the Năsăud country of Bistrița-Năsăud, a region that marked the northern edge of Roman Dacia and later formed part of a Habsburg border regiment. Few places in Transylvania have been a frontier for quite so long.

    Read the complete Salva - Roata lui Todoran write-up.

  3. 3. Vătava - Cetățele

    134 km in.

    For about a century and a half, the Roman province of Dacia was a wedge of empire pushed north of the Danube, and its eastern edge ran along the curve of the Carpathians through what is now central Romania. In 2024, UNESCO added the frontiers of the Roman Empire in Dacia to its list, a long chain of forts, watchtowers and earthworks. Vătava, in the upper Mureș valley, is one of the sites, with traces of a Roman fortification on a hill called Cetățele, the little fortresses. Not much is visible to the casual eye: earthworks, a few stones, the outline of a position chosen for its view along the valley.
