Why this ride

A 120 km round trip from Al Hufūf - roughly 2 h 04 in the saddle. On the way it passes `Ain Qinas archaeological site, Al-Asfar Lake and back through `Ain Qinas archaeological site. It strings together lakeside roads.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Qasr Ibrahim

    Hofuf is the main town of Al-Ahsa, in eastern Saudi Arabia, and Al-Ahsa is not what you picture when you think of the Arabian desert. It is a vast oasis, one of the largest in the world, with some two and a half million date palms fed by natural springs that rise from deep aquifers and flow through channels and canals. The green of the palms against the sand around it is striking, and UNESCO listed the oasis in 2018 as a cultural landscape that has been inhabited since the Neolithic era.

  2. 2. `Ain Qinas archaeological site

    Ain Qinas is a spring, one of many that bubble up through the oasis of Al-Ahsa, and it is one of the reasons for the place's long life. The water emerges warm from deep rock, at a steady temperature, and has been channelled for centuries into stone-lined pools and a network of canals that irrigate the groves. For people crossing an arid peninsula, this must have seemed like a miracle, and the springs and their surroundings are part of the oasis's World Heritage listing. The site is a pleasant, shaded corner where palm trees lean over the water and the sound of running water is constant.

  3. 3. Al-Asfar Lake

    The Al-Ahsa oasis in eastern Saudi Arabia is a green island in a very large desert. Under the sand lie huge aquifers, and springs rise to the surface to feed an area of about eighty-five square kilometres of date palms, said to be the largest oasis in the world, with perhaps two and a half million trees. It lies not far from the Gulf coast, near the city of Hofuf, and it has been settled for thousands of years, from the Bronze Age through the ancient Dilmun culture and into Islamic times. UNESCO listed it in 2018 as an evolving cultural landscape.
