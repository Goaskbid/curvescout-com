Why this ride

Out of Bishkek and back again: 664 km, roughly 10 h 43 in the saddle. It goes by way of Balykchy, Bokonbaevo, Jeti Oguz and Karakol. The highest point is about 3,300 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bishkek

    Bishkek lies at the foot of the Kyrgyz Ala-Too, the northern range of the Tian Shan, and on a clear morning the snow peaks seem close enough to touch from the city's long boulevards. The capital of Kyrgyzstan was a small fort and trading post in the nineteenth century, and was then rebuilt as a Soviet city named Frunze, after the Bolshevik commander born nearby. The legacy is a grid of broad, straight streets lined with poplars and elms, Soviet apartment blocks, wide squares, and statues, including the one of Manas, the hero of the national epic, who stands in front of the main square, Ala-Too.

  2. 2. Balykchy

    Balykchy is at the far western end of Issyk-Kul, the second-largest mountain lake in the world, and its name in Kyrgyz means fisherman. The lake, at an altitude of about 1,600 metres, is more than a hundred and eighty kilometres long and deep enough to stay ice-free in winter despite the cold, which is why its name means warm lake. The Tian Shan mountains surround it on all sides, with snow-capped peaks marching along the southern shore, and the water is a deep, saline blue that changes colour with the weather.

  3. 3. Bokonbaevo

    Bokonbaevo is a village on the south shore of Issyk-Kul, the great mountain lake of Kyrgyzstan, which lies at about 1,600 metres and is the second-largest alpine lake in the world. The name means warm lake, because the water, though cold, never freezes, even in the hard winters of the Tian Shan, and the mountains that rise behind it, the Terskey Ala-Too, carry snow all summer. On the road along the shore the lake is a sheet of deep blue on one side and the red and grey peaks on the other, and the contrast is striking.

  4. 4. Jeti Oguz

    Jeti Oguz means seven bulls, and the name belongs to a line of rust-red sandstone cliffs that rise above a pine-dotted valley on the southern side of Lake Issyk-Kul, in the Tien Shan mountains of eastern Kyrgyzstan. The cliffs are jagged and stacked side by side, and local legend says they are the petrified remains of seven bulls. A second formation, a red rock split down the middle in the shape of a heart, is known as the Broken Heart, and it is surrounded by a story of two rival suitors and a murdered khan that every guide tells slightly differently.

  5. 5. Karakol

    Karakol is the largest town on the eastern end of Lake Issyk-Kul, a place of about seventy thousand people at the foot of the Terskey Ala-Too range, and it is full of surprises. It was founded as a Russian garrison in the 1860s, and it has kept a good deal of its nineteenth-century character, with low wooden houses, painted shutters and tree-lined streets. The Holy Trinity Cathedral, a wooden Orthodox church with bright blue domes, was rebuilt in the 1990s after being used as a gymnasium in the Soviet years.
