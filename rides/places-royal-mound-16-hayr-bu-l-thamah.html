Why this ride

From Madīnat ‘Īsá it runs 912 km to Madīnat ‘Īsá; allow about 16 h 35 of riding. The stops in between: Al Zubarah Archaeological Site and back through Al Zubarah Archaeological Site. It crosses from Bahrain into Qatar - carry ID and check the toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Royal Mound 16

    Nobody knows who was buried in Royal Mound 16, and that is one of the nicest things about it. The big tombs at A'ali have been called royal for decades, because of their size and their position on the highest ground, but there is no inscription, no king's name, nothing that settles the matter. The tombs were looted long ago, probably within a few centuries of their construction, and what the robbers did not carry off was scattered by time. What remains is architecture: a vast stack of limestone, a chamber, a ramp and a silence.

  2. 2. Al Zubarah Archaeological Site

    Out on the north-west coast of Qatar, where the desert slides into a shallow turquoise sea, stand the low ruins of Al Zubarah, once one of the busiest pearling and trading towns in the Gulf. In the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the town thrived on pearls dived from the oyster beds offshore, traded on to India, Persia and beyond. Then it declined, was abandoned in the early twentieth century, and the sand covered it. Archaeologists have since uncovered streets, houses, a souk, mosques and a long town wall running in an arc around the old harbour. UNESCO inscribed the site in 2013.
