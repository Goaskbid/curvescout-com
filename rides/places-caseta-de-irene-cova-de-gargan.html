Why this ride

From Villafranca del Cid it runs 85 km to Villafranca del Cid; allow about 1 h 28 of riding. It goes by way of Barranco de Gibert II, Cova de Gargán and back through Barranco de Gibert II. The highest point is about 1,400 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Caseta de Irene

    The wind is the first thing you notice on the plateau between Culla and Villafranca del Cid. It comes in over the Maestrat from the sea or down from the mountains, depending on the day, and it has been put to work: lines of wind turbines stand along the ridges, turning slowly against a sky that seems to start at your shoulder. Caseta de Irene, a hut-sized name on a map, lies in this upland between the two villages, a little under seven kilometres from each, and the shelter belongs to the wider cluster of Gasulla and Valltorta sites that make this district special.

  2. 2. Barranco de Gibert II

    Up on the roof of the Maestrazgo, where Teruel and Castellón meet, the land rises above fourteen hundred metres into a bleak, beautiful plateau of pasture, pine and limestone. Mosqueruela, on the Aragonese side, is one of the highest villages in the area, and Vilafranca, on the Valencian side, sits in a valley below. The Barranco de Gibert cuts into the hills between them, close also to La Iglesuela del Cid, and its second shelter holds prehistoric paintings.

  3. 3. Cova de Gargán

    High in the interior of Castellón, where the coastal orange groves are long forgotten and the land climbs toward the Penyagolosa massif, the shelter known as Cova de Gargán sits in a country of limestone ledges, holm oak and dry-stone terraces. Vistabella del Maestrazgo is the nearest village, a handful of kilometres away on its high plateau, with Villahermosa del Río down in its valley to the other side and Lucena del Cid a little further south. It is quiet up here in a way that the coast never is.
