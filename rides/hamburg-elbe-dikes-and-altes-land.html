Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Blankenese view

    Blankenese hangs on a steep hillside above the Elbe, and for a city as flat as Hamburg it comes as a surprise. This was once a fishing village, separate from the city until the 1930s, and the old part still has the look of a place that grew up by itself, with narrow, winding lanes, tiny houses clinging to the slope and a network of stairways, said to number in the dozens and to contain thousands of steps, linking them. Captains and pilots lived here, and their houses have views over the river that were once a working necessity and are now a luxury.

  2. 2. Jork orchards

    The Altes Land is a strip of fruit orchards along the south bank of the Elbe, just west of Hamburg, and Jork is its heart. It is the largest continuous fruit-growing area in northern Europe, and in late April and May the blossom is so thick that the whole landscape seems to be wrapped in white and pink. In autumn, the trees are heavy with apples, and roadside stalls sell them by the sack. The scent of blossom in spring, or of fallen fruit in autumn, is as strong as the fog off the river.

  3. 3. Stade old town

    Stade has a harbour that looks like a painting. The old Hanseatic port, with a basin surrounded by tall, narrow gabled houses, a wooden crane and old warehouses, is the heart of the town, and the long, still water reflects the buildings so well that photographers return again and again. The town lies on the Schwinge, a small tributary of the Elbe, about forty kilometres west of Hamburg, in a flat landscape of marshes, orchards and dykes.
