Why this ride

A 284 km round trip from Bilbao - roughly 4 h 10 in the saddle. The stops in between: Cathedrale de Vitoria-Gasteiz, Suso Monastery and Archaeological Sites and back through Cathedrale de Vitoria-Gasteiz. It tops out around 1,200 m. The line favours views over speed.

La Rioja Wine Route

From Cathedrale de Vitoria-Gasteiz to Suso Monastery and Archaeological Sites is 78 km, mostly on N-124. It gains around 460 m and ends high, near 818 m. Take it at your own pace.

The stops

  1. 1. Cathédrale de Saint Jacques Apôtre

    Start here.

    Down in the Casco Viejo of Bilbao, where the old streets run in a tight grid beside the Nervión, the cathedral of Santiago stands at the heart of the Siete Calles, the seven original streets of the medieval town. It is a Gothic church, rebuilt after a fire in the sixteenth century, with a neo-Gothic façade and spire added later, and it sits so snugly among the tall houses that you often see it only when you are standing right in front of it.

    Read the complete Cathédrale de Saint Jacques Apôtre write-up.

  2. 2. Cathedrale de Vitoria-Gasteiz

    64 km in.

    Vitoria-Gasteiz is the capital of the Basque Country and possibly its most underrated city. It sits on a high plateau, the Llanada Alavesa, surrounded by low mountains and wide farmland, and its medieval old town climbs a small hill in the shape of an almond, with the old cathedral of Santa María at the top. This is the cathedral the label refers to, a Gothic church begun in the thirteenth century as both a temple and part of the town's defences.

    Read the complete Cathedrale de Vitoria-Gasteiz write-up.

  3. 3. Suso Monastery and Archaeological Sites

    142 km in.

    In a quiet valley in the foothills of the Sierra de la Demanda, in La Rioja, two monasteries stand one above the other, and between them they tell a story that matters to hundreds of millions of people. The upper monastery, Suso, is small and ancient, built partly into caves on the hillside where the hermit Saint Aemilian, San Millán, lived in the sixth century. The lower monastery, Yuso, is a grand complex of the sixteenth to eighteenth centuries, sometimes called the Escorial of La Rioja. Together they were listed by UNESCO in 1997, recognised as the birthplace of the written Spanish language.
