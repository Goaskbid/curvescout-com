Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Austin

    Austin sits where the flat Texas blackland prairie meets the limestone Hill Country, and that line, the Balcones Escarpment, is the reason the city is a good place to start a ride. West of downtown the land rises suddenly into oak and juniper hills cut by creeks, with the Colorado River, dammed into the long Lake Austin and Lake Travis, curling between them. The city grew here in the nineteenth century as the capital of the Republic of Texas, and the pink granite Capitol, which is taller than the one in Washington, still dominates the skyline.

  2. 2. Dripping Springs

    Dripping Springs sits on the eastern edge of the Texas Hill Country, west of Austin, where the flat blackland prairie gives way to limestone hills, live oaks and cedar. The name comes from the springs that seep from a ledge nearby, and the town proudly calls itself the gateway to the Hill Country. It is also, by its own declaration, the Wedding Capital of Texas, thanks to the number of ranches and barns in the surrounding hills that host ceremonies, so you will often see a bride or two on a weekend.

  3. 3. Fredericksburg

    Fredericksburg is a German town that happens to be in Texas. It was founded in 1846 by settlers who came from the German states under a land company led by John Meusebach, who named the town after Prince Frederick of Prussia, and the legacy of those early families still shapes everything from the architecture to the menu. The long Main Street is lined with limestone and timber buildings, many of them with steep roofs and shutters, and German bakeries, beer halls and sausage shops are still going strong.

  5. 5. Llano

    Llano sits on a bend of the Llano River in the central Texas Hill Country, where granite and limestone meet, and for a small town it has a surprising amount of stone about it. The courthouse square is built of the local pink granite, the old bridges are stone and iron, and the river itself runs clear and green over great pale boulders. The country around is the Llano Uplift, a dome of ancient Precambrian rock, some of the oldest exposed in Texas, pushed up through the younger limestone, and at nearby Enchanted Rock a huge bald granite dome rises out of the oak and juniper like the back of a buried whale.
