Why this ride

A 249 km round trip from Albocàsser - roughly 4 h 18 in the saddle. The stops in between: La fenellosa, Abrigo del Barranco Hondo and back through La fenellosa. The highest point is about 1,200 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. La Saltadora- Abric VI

    Peníscola is a rock that sticks out into the Mediterranean about twenty kilometres from here, topped by a Templar castle, and it is the one place on this stretch of coast that every visitor knows. The castle later gave refuge to Pedro de Luna, Pope Benedict XIII, who refused to give up the papacy after the Western Schism and lived out his years here, earning the nickname Papa Luna. His coat of arms and the story of his stubbornness are all over the town, which is now a busy summer resort. The shelter at La Saltadora, Abric VI, is a world away from that. It lies in the dry hills above Albocàsser, about six and a half kilometres from the village, and carries painting in the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain. The ride links it with another site, the Racó de Nando.

  2. 2. La fenellosa

    People in Aragón like to call the Matarraña their Tuscany, and for once the comparison is not entirely marketing. It is a comarca of honey-coloured stone villages on hilltops, olive groves on terraces, almond trees and vineyards, with the limestone wall of the Ports de Beseit rising behind. La Fenellosa lies in this country, between Valderrobres, Fuentespalda and Mas de Barberans across the Catalan line, in the rough hills where the farmland gives way to the mountains.

  3. 3. Abrigo del Barranco Hondo

    Castellote is one of those villages in the Teruel Maestrazgo that seems to have been built by people who wanted to be left alone. Its houses pile up beneath a great rock wall topped by the ruins of a castle associated with the Knights Templar, and the Guadalope river winds below on its way into the Santolea reservoir. The Abrigo del Barranco Hondo, a painted rock shelter in a deep ravine outside the village, is part of the extensive collection of Levantine rock art scattered through these mountains, with small figures in red ochre that have survived on the stone for millennia.
