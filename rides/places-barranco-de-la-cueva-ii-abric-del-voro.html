Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranco de la Cueva II

    This is the dry hinterland of the Costa Blanca, in the stretch between El Campello and Villajoyosa, where the sea is never far but you can easily forget it. A few kilometres inland the hills turn to scrub, rock and abandoned terraces, with barrancos that run dry for most of the year and then flood in a night after a storm. The Barranco de la Cueva is one of those ravines, and the shelter in it belongs to the scatter of painted rocks found across the mountains of Alicante.

  2. 2. Penya roja o Ulls de Canals

    This is the country of toys and herbs, an unusual pairing. Onil and Ibi, in the inland hills of Alicante, grew up as centres of toy-making, starting with tin and later plastic, and the local museum in Ibi tells how a mountain valley became a world supplier of dolls and games. On the other side, the Sierra de Mariola, a natural park rich in aromatic plants, supplies the herbs for a local liqueur and for traditional remedies. Bocairent, a handsome old town on a rocky spur, has houses built right at the edge of a gorge and a set of man-made caves carved into the cliff, the Covetes dels Moros.

  3. 3. Abric del Voro

    The Canal de Navarrés is a wide valley in the interior of Valencia province, surrounded by limestone mountains and deep gorges, and the Abric del Voro lies in the hills between Quesa and Bicorp, two small villages in the heart of some of the wildest country in the region. It is one of the many rock shelters with prehistoric paintings in the area, part of the UNESCO listing of Mediterranean rock art. The valley's most famous site is close by: the Cuevas de la Araña near Bicorp, where a painting of a figure climbing ropes to collect honey from a wild bees' nest, surrounded by bees, is one of the best-known images of prehistoric art in Spain, often cited as one of the earliest depictions of honey gathering.
