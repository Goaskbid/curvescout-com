Why this ride

349 km from Brampton to Brampton, about 5 h 11 of riding. The stops in between: Hadrian's Wall & vallum between field boun, The English Lake District and back through Hadrian's Wall & vallum between field boun. Lakes are the scenery here.

The stops

  1. 1. Hadrian's Wall & vallum from A6071 to The

    Start here.

    Do not expect a great stone wall here. Near Brampton, on the stretch running out toward the A6071, Hadrian's Wall is mostly a matter of earthworks and imagination: a line in a field, a softened ditch, the long parallel banks of the vallum to the south. In this western sector the wall was originally built of turf and timber before being rebuilt in stone, and centuries of farmers and builders carried off the dressed stones for barns and churches. What survives is listed as part of the UNESCO Frontiers of the Roman Empire, and once your eye tunes in, you start seeing the line everywhere.

  2. 2. Hadrian's Wall & vallum between field boun

    53 km in.

    Hadrian's Wall runs for about seventy-three miles across the north of England, from the Solway Firth in the west to the Tyne in the east, built on the orders of the Emperor Hadrian after his visit to Britain in 122 AD.

    Read the complete Hadrian's Wall & vallum between field boun write-up.

  3. 3. The English Lake District

    175 km in.

    Wordsworth wandered lonely as a cloud round here, and anybody who has ridden the Lake District on a wet bank holiday will understand the appeal of being lonely. The English Lake District, listed by UNESCO in 2017 as a cultural landscape, is a compact knot of mountains, lakes and valleys in Cumbria, shaped by glaciers and then by centuries of sheep farming, slate quarrying and, from the late eighteenth century, by poets, painters and tourists who came looking for the sublime.

    Read the complete The English Lake District write-up.
