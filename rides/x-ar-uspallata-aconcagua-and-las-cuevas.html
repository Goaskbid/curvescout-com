Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Uspallata

    Uspallata is a high, dry valley with a lot of history. The village sits at nearly two thousand metres in the Andes of Mendoza, in a wide basin ringed by brown, bare mountains, and it is a halfway point on the old road between Argentina and Chile. In 1817, the Army of the Andes under General José de San Martín crossed the mountains in several columns, and one of them marched through the Uspallata valley on its way to liberate Chile, a story that every schoolchild here knows.

  2. 2. Aconcagua Park

    Aconcagua is the highest mountain outside Asia, at almost seven thousand metres, and the provincial park that protects it lies a short way off the main road between Mendoza and Chile. Most riders will not climb it, but you can see enough from the valley to feel its scale. The huge, snow-streaked mass of the mountain rises from a high, stony valley, and on a clear day the summit shows above the nearer ridges, with the colour of the rock changing from red to ochre to grey as the light moves.

  3. 3. Las Cuevas

    Las Cuevas is a tiny border village at the top of the Argentine side of the Andes, at about 3,200 metres, and it stands at the mouth of the tunnel that carries traffic under the pass to Chile. It is a collection of low buildings, customs offices and a few shops huddled in a high, windswept valley, with steep slopes of loose rock rising on every side and snow lying well into summer. In winter, the village is often cut off by storms and avalanches, and the road is closed for days at a time, so it can feel like the edge of the world.
