Why this ride

Sea wind, pine forest and wide horizons. Out of Ribnitz-Damgarten and back again: 293 km, roughly 5 h 03 in the saddle. On the way it passes Ahrenshoop coast art stop, Darß forest road edge and Stralsund old town. It keeps to quiet secondary roads.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ribnitz-Damgarten fuel gate

    Ribnitz-Damgarten is really two towns that were stitched together by a river. The Recknitz, sluggish and brown as it slides toward the Saaler Bodden lagoon, was for centuries a border: Ribnitz belonged to Mecklenburg, Damgarten to Pomerania, and the two places looked across the water with different rulers and different habits. They merged only in 1950, and the bridge that joins them is still the most important thing in town. It sits on the old main road to the Baltic coast, at the neck of the Fischland-Darß-Zingst peninsula, so nearly everyone riding toward the beaches has to pass through.

  2. 2. Ahrenshoop coast art stop

    Ahrenshoop is a village where artists came first and tourists followed. In the 1890s painters from Berlin, Munich and elsewhere found this thin strip of land between the Baltic and the Saaler Bodden, drawn by the light, the sea, the reed-thatched cottages and the wide sky, and they founded an artists' colony that still defines the place. The landscape painter Paul Müller-Kaempff was among the first to settle, and the village has been a haven for creative people ever since.

  3. 3. Darß forest road edge

    The Darß forest is the wildest piece of woodland on the German Baltic coast, and the road that skirts its edge between Born and Prerow runs along the border of a landscape that has been left largely to itself. The Darß Urwald, as it is called, is a mix of beech, alder, pine and birch growing on old dune ridges and wet hollows, where fallen trunks are left to rot and the undergrowth is thick with fern. It belongs to the Western Pomerania Lagoon Area National Park, and walkers follow marked paths among trees draped in moss.

  4. 4. Stralsund old town

    Stralsund sits on a strip of land between the Baltic and a chain of shallow lagoons, facing the island of Rügen across a narrow sound, and its old town is one of the finest assemblies of brick Gothic in Europe. UNESCO listed it in 2002 together with Wismar, and the reason is plain as soon as you walk onto the Old Market: the great town hall with its ornate, almost theatrical gabled facade, and across the square the enormous church of St Nicholas, built when the town was a rich member of the Hanseatic League and wanted everyone to know it.
