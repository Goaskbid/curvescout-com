Why this ride

Out of Mesocco, one way: 172 km, roughly 3 h 17 in the saddle. Splügenpass hairpins is the high point. It tops out at 2,114 m. Splugen Pass is subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. It is a high-mountain day.

Scenic road chapters

Passo dello Spluga

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2114 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance — cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Rider note

Check the pass is open before you commit to it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mesocco food reset

    Mesocco is a valley village of about thirteen hundred people on the southern side of the San Bernardino, in the Misox, the Italian-speaking arm of Graubünden that opens toward Bellinzona and Lake Maggiore. At nearly eight hundred metres it is the last place of any size before the road climbs to the pass, and it is the right place for a breakfast or a late lunch, with a handful of simple restaurants and cafés serving polenta, cheese, local salami and chestnut dishes, and a small stretch of fuel and shops.

  2. 2. Splügenpass hairpins

    The Italian side of the Splügen is the one that makes people swear with happiness. From Campodolcino the road climbs the Valle Spluga in a rapid string of tight hairpins, stacked one above another up the valley wall, past waterfalls and a gorge that the old road builders had to blast through. At the top, at about 2,114 metres, you arrive at the border, a modest customs building and the long, cold Lago di Montespluga, held back by a dam and glinting in a bare landscape of grass and stone.

    Read the complete Splügenpass hairpins write-up.

    Planning note

    You top out at 2114 m here. The road climbs 1324 m on the way up to it, steadily. Reckon on about 10 °C colder than the valley, and more in the wind. Nothing on this route is higher. At this height the road is a summer one: snow gates can stay shut into June and shut again in October. Next is Thusis return, mostly downhill.

  3. 3. Thusis return gate

    Thusis is a gateway town, and it knows its job well. It sits where the Hinterrhein, the young Rhine, comes out of the gorge of the Viamala and into the wide, orchard-lined valley of the Domleschg, and from here roads and railway lines fan out into the Graubünden mountains: south over the Splügen and San Bernardino passes, east through the Schin gorge toward the Albula and Davos. At about seven hundred metres in altitude, it is a fairly low base for such high terrain, and that makes it a useful place to be at the start of a mountain day.

    Read the complete Thusis write-up.

    Fill up here — 57 km left and no pump marked after this. You lose 1394 m on the way down to it, steadily. Next is Back to Chur fuel.

  4. 4. Back to Chur fuel

    At 593 metres, in a wide bend of the Rhine valley, Chur makes a strong claim to be the oldest town in Switzerland, with traces of settlement going back thousands of years and a Roman presence from the first century BC. It has been the seat of a bishop since late antiquity, and the bishop's court, with its cathedral, sits on a terrace above the old town. Down below, the narrow streets and squares of the Altstadt, with painted houses, fountains and arcades, are a pleasant place to wander after a morning in the saddle.
