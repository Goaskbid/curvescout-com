Why this ride

From Derinkuyu it runs 278 km to Derinkuyu; allow about 4 h 51 of riding. It goes by way of Subterranean city of Kaymakli, Yesilöz site, Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of and Karain site. It tops out around 1,550 m.

Subterranean city of Kaymakli is 27 km from Soganli site on Derinkuyu-Yeşilhisar yolu. It stays up high the whole way, between 1,383 and 1,543 m. Take it at your own pace.

The stops

  1. 1. Soganli site

    Start here.

    Soğanlı lies in a side valley of Cappadocia that most tour buses skip, south-east of Ürgüp and Yeşilhisar, on the slopes of the volcanic uplands. The name means with onions, which tells you something about the humble character of the place. The valley sides are made of soft volcanic tuff, which the local Christians carved out in the Byzantine centuries into churches, chapels, monasteries, and houses. Many of the rock churches date from the ninth to the thirteenth century and keep fragments of frescoes: Karabaş, the Yılanlı or Snake church, the Barbara church and others, whose names reflect old stories more than documented history.

    Read the complete Soganli site write-up.

  2. 2. Subterranean city of Kaymakli

    27 km in.

    Kaymakli sits in the soft volcanic country of Cappadocia, about eleven kilometres from Derinkuyu and eighteen from Nevşehir, and from the road you would never guess what is under your wheels. The village is ordinary: flat-roofed houses, tractors, a few carpet shops. The wonder is below, a city dug downward into tuff, the pale rock that ash from ancient volcanoes pressed into something a person can cut with a hand tool and that hardens with air.

    Read the complete Subterranean city of Kaymakli write-up.

  3. 3. Yesilöz site

    84 km in.

    Cappadocia was made by volcanoes and carved by people. Millions of years ago, the volcanoes of central Anatolia, Erciyes and Hasan, covered the plateau in thick layers of ash that hardened into a soft rock called tuff, and wind and water have since eroded it into the cones, pinnacles and ridges that are known as fairy chimneys. People found the rock easy to carve, and for thousands of years they have dug homes, churches, storerooms and whole underground cities into it. The area is a World Heritage site, and it is hard to find a corner without a cave or a dovecote cut into a cliff.

    Read the complete Yesilöz site write-up.

  4. 4. Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of

    114 km in.

    Millions of years ago, two volcanoes, Erciyes and Hasan, covered central Turkey with ash, and the ash hardened into a soft, pale rock called tuff. Then rain and frost and wind went to work for a very long time. They carved the tuff into a landscape of cones, spires, mushrooms and canyons, the fairy chimneys of Cappadocia, in soft shades of cream, pink and apricot. About a thousand metres above sea level, around Goreme, the whole valley seems to have been sculpted by a mad pastry chef.

    Read the complete Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of write-up.

  5. 5. Karain site

    139 km in.

    Cappadocia is a land that was made by volcanoes and then carved by people. Millions of years ago, the eruptions of Mount Erciyes, which still stands at 3,917 metres, and other volcanoes covered the high plateau of central Anatolia with deep layers of ash, which hardened into a soft rock called tuff. Wind and water then eroded the tuff into valleys of pinnacles, cones and ridges, the fairy chimneys, and people found that the rock was easy to dig. Ever since, they have been carving houses, churches, stables and storerooms into it.

    Read the complete Karain site write-up.
