Why this ride

A 286 km round trip from Paoay - roughly 4 h 56 in the saddle. It goes by way of Historic City of Vigan, Church of La Nuestra Senora de la Asuncion, back through Church of La Nuestra Senora de la Asuncion and back through Historic City of Vigan. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Church of San Agustin

    The church at Paoay looks like it is built to withstand a siege, and in a way it is. San Agustin stands on a flat stretch of Ilocos Norte, in the northwest corner of Luzon, and its sides are propped by enormous stepped buttresses, as massive as bastions, that march along the flanks like the legs of a great animal. The Augustinian friars who built it between about 1694 and 1710 were working in earthquake country, and the buttresses were their answer. The walls are coral stone and brick, laid in thick layers and held with a mortar that, according to local lore, included sugar cane juice and egg white. The result is a heavy, dark, strangely beautiful church that belongs to the family of Baroque Churches of the Philippines, listed by UNESCO in 1993.

  2. 2. Historic City of Vigan

    Vigan survived the Pacific War almost whole, and that is the secret of its charm. Founded by the Spanish in the sixteenth century as a trading post at the mouth of the Mestizo River in Ilocos Sur, on the northwest coast of Luzon, it grew rich on Chinese silk, Ilocano goods and the galleon trade, and its merchants built houses of brick and stone with wooden upper floors and sliding capiz-shell windows. Together these make one of the best-preserved colonial towns in Southeast Asia, which UNESCO recognised in 1999.

  3. 3. Church of La Nuestra Senora de la Asuncion

    Santa Maria is the church that makes you earn the view. It stands on a hill above the town of the same name in Ilocos Sur, and reaching it means climbing an imposing stone staircase of about eighty steps, flanked by walls, which opens onto a broad, open terrace. The church was built in the eighteenth century by Augustinian friars, and it looks more like a fortress than a house of prayer, with a long, plain, buttressed brick facade and a separate bell tower standing on the hill's edge. Walls surround the whole complex, because during the colonial period a church could also be a refuge and a stronghold. It is one of the four Philippine churches in the UNESCO group of Baroque Churches.
