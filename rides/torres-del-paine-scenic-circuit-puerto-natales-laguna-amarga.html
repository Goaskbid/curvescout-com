Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Puerto Natales

    Puerto Natales sits on the edge of the Última Esperanza Sound, the Last Hope, a name given by a sixteenth-century expedition that hoped to find a passage through the channels and found only more water. The town of about twenty thousand people lies on a low shore facing west across the water to the snow-capped peaks of the Patagonian ice field, and the light on a clear evening, with the mountains turning pink, is the best the town has to offer. It was once a base for sheep ranching and meat-packing, and its character is still that of a frontier port.

  2. 2. Cerro Castillo Chile

    Cerro Castillo is a tiny settlement on the Patagonian steppe in the Magallanes region of southern Chile, close to the Argentine border, and it is the last place of any size on the road from Puerto Natales to the Torres del Paine national park. Around it the land is a vast sweep of tawny grass, scrub and low hills, broken by lakes and rivers and a long horizon with snowy peaks far off to the west. It looks empty, and it is not: guanacos graze in small herds beside the road, rheas dash through the grass, and condors circle on the thermals.

  3. 3. Laguna Amarga

    Laguna Amarga is the entrance to Torres del Paine, and the first view of the park's famous towers makes the whole long journey seem worth it. The lagoon itself is a shallow, brackish lake of milky green water on the open steppe, ringed by low hills, and behind it rise the granite spires of the Paine massif, three vertical towers of rock that seem to belong in another country. The name means bitter lake, from the salty water.
