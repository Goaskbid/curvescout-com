Why this ride

74 km one way from Gangtok - plan on about 1 h 14 of riding. On the way it passes Tsomgo Lake, Nathu La and Zuluk. The highest point is about 4,300 m. It crosses from India into China - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Sikkim Silk Route

Water arrives beside the road and stays for 23 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go — slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did.

Sikkim Silk Route

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 4310 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance. Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Gangtok

    Gangtok sits on a ridge in the Himalayan foothills of Sikkim, at about 1,650 metres, and on a clear morning you can see Kangchenjunga, the third-highest mountain in the world at 8,586 metres, floating pink above the clouds. Sikkim was an independent Buddhist kingdom until it joined India in 1975, and the culture is still a mix of Lepcha, Bhutia and Nepali traditions, with prayer flags on the rooftops, monasteries on the hills and a feeling of calm that surprises people coming from the plains. The state became the first in India to be fully organic in its farming, and the cities are noticeably clean.

  2. 2. Tsomgo Lake

    Tsomgo Lake, also called Changu, sits at about 3,750 metres in the mountains east of Gangtok, a small oval of cold water cupped in a bowl of bare slopes. It is a glacial lake, fed by melting snow, and it freezes over in winter, when the surrounding hills are white. The Sikkimese hold it sacred, and local lamas once used the colour of its water to foretell the year ahead. In spring the banks are bright with primulas and rhododendrons, and in autumn the air is sharp and clear.

  3. 3. Nathu La

    Nathu La is a mountain pass on the border between Sikkim and Tibet, at 4,310 metres, and it carries more history than its modest size suggests. It was part of an old trade route that linked Lhasa with the plains of Bengal, one of the tributaries of what people now loosely call the Silk Route, along which wool, salt, tea and silver moved by mule. The pass closed after the 1962 war between India and China and stayed shut for decades. It reopened for border trade in 2006, and today the two armies stand a few metres apart on either side of a fence.

  4. 4. Zuluk

    Zuluk is a village strung along a ridge in the eastern Himalayas, on the old Silk Route that once connected Sikkim with Tibet, and it is a very different place from the busy tourist spots of Gangtok. The village is small, with a few homestays, an army post and a prayer-flag-covered hillside, at around three thousand metres. On clear mornings, the sunrise lights up the peaks of Kanchenjunga, the third highest mountain in the world, which seems to float above the clouds.

  5. 5. Aritar

    Aritar is a small village in the east of Sikkim, close to the border with West Bengal, and its great attraction is a lake. Aritar Lake, also called Lampokhari, lies in a fold of the hills with green slopes around it, and locals take boats out on the water at weekends. The village lies on an old branch of the Silk Route that once carried wool, salt and tea between Tibet and the plains of India through the Nathu La pass. The nearest towns are Rangpo, about fifteen kilometres away, and the capital Gangtok, about seventeen, and the bigger one has the fuel stations and shops.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Gangtok to Tsomgo Lake

Gangtok → Tsomgo Lake is 20 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Tsomgo Lake to Nathu La

Tsomgo Lake → Nathu La climbs about 4310 m in 9 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Nathu La to Zuluk

Nathu La → Zuluk drops about 4310 m over 20 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Zuluk to Aritar

Zuluk → Aritar is 15 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
