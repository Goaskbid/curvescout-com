Why this ride

A 342 km round trip from Alginet - roughly 5 h 45 in the saddle. On the way it passes Barranco de las Colochas- Abrigo I, La Hoz de Vicente and back through Barranco de las Colochas- Abrigo I. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Barranc de la falaguera- Abric I → Barranco de las Colochas- Abrigo I

Barranc de la falaguera- Abric I to Barranco de las Colochas- Abrigo I: 68 km, mostly on A-7. It gains around 710 m and ends high, near 754 m. Take a break before the climb, not halfway up.

The stops

  1. 1. Barranc de la falaguera- Abric I

    Start here.

    Alginet sits in the Ribera Alta, the flat, fertile plain south of Valencia where the Júcar river spreads out among orange groves, rice fields and small towns. To the west, the plain meets the first hills of the interior, low ridges of limestone covered in pine and scrub. The Barranc de la Falaguera, the ravine of the fern, cuts into these hills near Alginet, Alfarp and Benifaió.

    Read the complete Barranc de la falaguera- Abric I write-up.

  2. 2. Barranco de las Colochas- Abrigo I

    68 km in.

    West of Valencia, the river Turia leaves the mountains through a series of deep gorges, and around the villages of Gestalgar, Sot de Chera and Chulilla, the landscape turns dramatic: limestone cliffs, pine-covered slopes and the river winding green below. In a side ravine near Gestalgar, the Barranco de las Colochas shelters a handful of painted panels that UNESCO counts among the Mediterranean rock art of Iberia. The figures are faint, but the setting is worth the effort on its own.

    Read the complete Barranco de las Colochas- Abrigo I write-up.

  3. 3. La Hoz de Vicente

    170 km in. The ride passes about 6 km from it.

    The Cabriel is one of the few rivers in Spain that still runs clean and fast through a deep canyon, and the Hoz de Vicente stands on its upper stretch, where Cuenca meets Valencia. The Hoces del Cabriel are a natural park of limestone gorges, pine woods and river flats, with kingfishers, otters and, on the rock faces, griffon vultures. The Hoz de Vicente shelter is one of the painted places of this wild corner, a Levantine rock art site listed within the UNESCO group of the Mediterranean Basin.
