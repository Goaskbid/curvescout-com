Why this ride

curated scenic road. Tatra Mountains, highland villages, forest. From Kraków it runs 99 km to Zakopane; allow about 1 h 48 of riding. The highest point is about 1,050 m. It crosses from Poland into Slovakia - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kraków

    Kraków has the kind of old town that makes you slow down on foot, and it deserves it. At the centre is the Rynek Główny, the main market square, one of the largest medieval squares in Europe, with the Cloth Hall in the middle and the two uneven towers of St Mary's Basilica at the corner. Each hour a trumpeter plays a bugle call from the taller tower, and the tune stops abruptly in mid-phrase, in memory of a watchman said to have been shot in the throat by a Tatar arrow while sounding the alarm.

  2. 2. Nowy Targ

    Nowy Targ is the main town of Podhale, the highland district at the foot of the Tatras, and it feels as if it had always been a place where mountain farmers came down to trade. About thirty-four thousand people live here, in a broad, windswept basin about six hundred metres above sea level where the Czarny and Biały Dunajec rivers meet, and the weekly market, held on Thursdays, is a long-standing tradition where highlanders in embroidered waistcoats still sell sheep, cheese, wool and wooden goods. The cheese to look for is oscypek, a smoked, spindle-shaped sheep's-milk cheese pressed in carved wooden moulds, and you will find it on stalls throughout the region.

  3. 3. Zakopane

    Zakopane has been the capital of Polish mountain life for more than a century, and it wears the role with a mixture of pride and fatigue. About twenty-eight thousand people live here, right below the Tatra range, and in peak seasons several times that many visitors pour in for skiing, hiking and a stroll along Krupówki, the crowded main street of souvenir shops, grill stands and music. Behind the bustle lies a distinct culture, that of the Górale, the highlanders, with their own dialect, music, costume and wooden architecture, and the Zakopane style of timber building, popularised in the late nineteenth century by the artist Stanisław Witkiewicz, still shapes the best old villas.

  4. 4. Near Zakopane

    Zakopane is the winter capital of Poland, and in summer it is the hiking capital too. The town lies at the foot of the Tatra Mountains, the highest range in the Carpathians, with the peak of Giewont, topped by a metal cross, looming above it. Nearly thirty thousand people live here, and many millions visit each year, so the main street, Krupówki, is crowded with souvenir stalls, grill restaurants and walkers. It is lively rather than quiet, but the mountains behind it are real.
