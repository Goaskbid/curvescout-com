Why this ride

93 km one way from Thusis - plan on about 1 h 36 of riding. The stops in between: Splugen and San Bernardino Pass. The highest point is about 2,100 m. San Bernardino Pass is open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Passo dello Spluga

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2066 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance. Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Thusis

    Thusis is a gateway town, and it knows its job well. It sits where the Hinterrhein, the young Rhine, comes out of the gorge of the Viamala and into the wide, orchard-lined valley of the Domleschg, and from here roads and railway lines fan out into the Graubünden mountains: south over the Splügen and San Bernardino passes, east through the Schin gorge toward the Albula and Davos. At about seven hundred metres in altitude, it is a fairly low base for such high terrain, and that makes it a useful place to be at the start of a mountain day.

  2. 2. Splugen

    Splügen is where the Rhine, still a young and rather modest river, runs through the high Rheinwald valley in Graubünden, and the village has been a waystation for as long as people have wanted to get over the Alps. Two passes leave from here: the Splügen itself, 2,113 metres, which drops to Italy and the Valle San Giacomo, and the San Bernardino, 2,066 metres, which heads south into Ticino. Choosing between them at the village fountain is one of the nicer decisions in the Alps.

  3. 3. San Bernardino Pass

    The San Bernardino Pass is a high, bare saddle at 2,066 metres on the Swiss side of the Alps, and it is one of the oldest crossings between the Rhine valley and Italy. Travellers, muleteers and armies have used it since Roman times, and the pass is named, according to local tradition, after the Franciscan preacher Bernardino of Siena, who is said to have crossed it in the fifteenth century. It sits in the canton of Graubünden, between the German-speaking village of Hinterrhein, where the Rhine begins, and the Italian-speaking valley of Mesolcina, which drops south toward Bellinzona.

  4. 4. Mesocco

    Mesocco is a valley village of about thirteen hundred people on the southern side of the San Bernardino, in the Misox, the Italian-speaking arm of Graubünden that opens toward Bellinzona and Lake Maggiore. At nearly eight hundred metres it is the last place of any size before the road climbs to the pass, and it is the right place for a breakfast or a late lunch, with a handful of simple restaurants and cafés serving polenta, cheese, local salami and chestnut dishes, and a small stretch of fuel and shops.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

Thusis to Splugen

Thusis → Splugen is 23 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Splugen to San Bernardino Pass

Splugen → San Bernardino Pass climbs about 2066 m in 17 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

San Bernardino Pass to Mesocco

San Bernardino Pass → Mesocco drops about 2066 m over 15 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
