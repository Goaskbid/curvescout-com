Why this ride

From Luoyang it runs 128 km to Luoyang; allow about 2 h 12 of riding. On the way it passes Site of Luoyang City from the Eastern Han, Longmen Grottoes and back through Site of Luoyang City from the Eastern Han. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. No.160 Site of Hanjia Granary

    Luoyang was the capital of thirteen dynasties, and its history runs through the ground like a seam of coal. On the edge of the modern city, the Hanjia Granary is one of the biggest ancient grain stores ever found. It was built in the Sui dynasty, in the early seventh century, and used through the Tang, and the site has hundreds of underground storage pits, each one large enough to hold hundreds of tonnes of grain. UNESCO listed it as part of the Silk Roads in 2014.

  2. 2. Site of Luoyang City from the Eastern Han

    Luoyang, in Henan province, is one of the four great ancient capitals of China, a city that served as the seat of more than a dozen dynasties over a thousand years. East of the modern city, in the fields along the Luo river, lie the remains of the capital of the Eastern Han dynasty, which ruled from Luoyang from the first to the early third century AD, and of the later Wei, Jin and Northern Wei capitals built on the same site. The site is part of the UNESCO-listed Silk Roads: the Routes Network of Chang'an-Tianshan Corridor, inscribed in 2014.

  3. 3. Longmen Grottoes

    South of Luoyang, the river Yi cuts through a narrow gap between two limestone hills, and for about a kilometre the cliffs on both sides are honeycombed with caves, niches and carvings. Over around four centuries, starting in the late fifth century under the Northern Wei dynasty and continuing through the Tang, Buddhist sculptors carved tens of thousands of statues into the rock, from tiny figures a few centimetres high to colossal Buddhas. The Longmen Grottoes were listed by UNESCO in 2000 and are among the finest examples of Chinese Buddhist art.
