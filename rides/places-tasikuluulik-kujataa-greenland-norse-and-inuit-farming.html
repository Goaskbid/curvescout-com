Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Tasikuluulik

    In the green fjords of South Greenland, roughly where the ice sheet meets sheep country, lie the ruins of a church that has outlasted the Norse people who built it. Hvalsey, known in Greenlandic as Qaqortukulooq, is the best preserved of all the Norse ruins in Greenland, its gable and walls of local stone still standing against the sky beside a quiet fjord. In 1408 a wedding was held here, and a letter written about it is the last written record of the Norse in Greenland. After that, silence, and no one is sure exactly what happened to the settlers.

  2. 2. Igaliku

    A motorcycle is an awkward guest in southern Greenland, and Igaliku is the reason to think about it carefully. There is no road network linking the settlements here; you reach them by boat, helicopter, or on foot, and the tracks between the farms are short and local. Nevertheless this is one of the great historical landscapes of the Arctic, and it deserves a place in any ride that dreams about the edge of the map.

  3. 3. Kujataa Greenland: Norse and Inuit Farming

    Kujataa is south Greenland at its gentlest, which is a relative term. The fjords here are deep, the mountains are steep, and the glacier is never far away, but in summer the valleys are green with grass and dotted with sheep. This is where Norse settlers from Iceland arrived in the tenth century, led, according to the sagas, by Erik the Red, and where Inuit hunters and Norse farmers lived within reach of each other for centuries. UNESCO listed the area in 2017 as a cultural landscape of farming at the edge of the habitable world.
