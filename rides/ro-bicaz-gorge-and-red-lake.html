Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bicaz

    Bicaz is a town of some eight thousand people at the eastern end of the Bicaz Gorge, in the Moldavian foothills of the Carpathians, and it is known for two big things, a dam and a cement works. The dam, completed in 1960 on the Bistrița river, holds back Izvorul Muntelui, the largest reservoir in Romania by volume, a long arm of water that fills the valley and reaches far into the hills. The cement works, a concrete reminder of the communist industrial plan, still dominates one side of town. It sounds grim, but the lake is beautiful and the hills around it are green.

  3. 3. Gheorgheni

    Gheorgheni is a cold, tough little town on a high basin in the heart of the Székely Land, and most riders meet it as the gateway to the Bicaz Gorge and the Red Lake. It sits at a little over eight hundred metres in the Giurgeu depression, a broad bowl ringed by the Giurgeu and Hășmaș mountains, and winters here are among the harshest in Romania, with frost pooling in the basin on still nights. The town is overwhelmingly Hungarian in language and culture, and you will see the signs in two scripts and hear Hungarian in the shops long before Romanian.
