Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Arica

    Arica is Chile's northernmost city, a port of nearly two hundred thousand people just a short drive from the Peruvian border, and its nickname, the city of eternal spring, reflects a mild, dry climate that hardly changes through the year. The city sits at the foot of El Morro, a massive headland that was the scene of a famous battle during the War of the Pacific in 1880, when Chilean forces took the fortress from Peru. After that, the city's fate was disputed for decades, until a treaty in 1929 confirmed it as Chilean.

  2. 2. Putre

    Putre is a small Aymara village high in the Andes, at about 3,500 metres above sea level, and it is the gateway to Lauca National Park and the high plateau. The village sits on a green terrace above a deep valley, with stone houses, narrow streets and a small, whitewashed church that dates from the seventeenth century. The surrounding hills are dotted with old agricultural terraces that have been farmed for centuries, and the people here, many of them Aymara, graze llamas and alpacas on the high pastures. After the dry desert of the coast, the green of the fields comes as a shock.

  3. 3. Lake Chungara

    Lake Chungará lies at about 4,500 metres on the Chilean Altiplano, close to the Bolivian border, and it is one of the highest lakes in the world. The water is deep blue, the shore is fringed with pale grass and the sky seems very close. Behind it rise the twin volcanoes of Parinacota and Pomerape, both over six thousand metres, their cones white against the dark blue, and the sight of Parinacota reflected in the still water of the lake is one of the best-known images in the Andes.
