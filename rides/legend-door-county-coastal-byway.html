Why this ride

National Scenic Byway. Lake Michigan, villages, orchards. From Sturgeon Bay it runs 60 km to the finish; allow about 1 h 05 of riding. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Door County Coastal Byway

Slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did. Water arrives beside the road and stays for 25 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sturgeon Bay

    Door County is the thumb of Wisconsin, a limestone peninsula that sticks into Lake Michigan like a hitchhiker's hand, and Sturgeon Bay is the town where you come in. It is the only real city on the peninsula, with about nine thousand people, a shipyard, a downtown that climbs a hill from the water, and a ship canal that makes the northern half of Door County, in effect, an island. The canal was cut in 1881 to connect Green Bay with Lake Michigan, saving ships the long, dangerous trip around the tip, and it is crossed by a steel bridge that lifts to let freighters through. If you are held up there, watch for the long, low ships sliding past at head height.

  2. 2. Door County Coastal Byway point 2

    Egg Harbor is a small, tidy village on the bay side of the peninsula, with a marina, a main street of low wooden buildings and the quiet air of a place that exists mostly for visitors. The name has a story attached to it that locals love to repeat: in the early nineteenth century, crews from two boats supposedly began pelting each other with eggs while waiting at the harbour, and the nickname stuck. Whether or not it is true, it is the kind of tale that makes a place memorable.

  3. 3. Door County Coastal Byway point 3

    Fish Creek is the heart of the peninsula's summer life. It sits on the edge of Green Bay at the entrance to Peninsula State Park, one of Wisconsin's best loved parks, and its lanes of wooden shops, galleries and small hotels are busy from June to October. Alexander Noble, who settled here in the 1850s, built a dock and a business around the harbour, and his name remains attached to the village's most historic house.

  4. 4. Door County Coastal Byway point 4

    Sister Bay is best known for its goats. On the roof of a Swedish restaurant on the main street, a small herd of goats grazes on real turf, a tradition that has become a roadside attraction in its own right. It is quirky and charming, and a great way to get a smile out of a cold, tired rider. The village itself, a few hundred metres of shops and cafes on the bay, has a strong Scandinavian flavour, a reminder of the Norwegian, Swedish and Icelandic immigrants who settled in this part of Wisconsin in the nineteenth century.

  5. 5. Door County Coastal Byway point 5

    Gills Rock is where Door County runs out of road, almost. It is a small fishing village at the northern tip of the peninsula, with a cluster of docks, a few weathered buildings and a view across the narrow strait to Washington Island. The strait itself, known as Death's Door, is notorious for its rough water and strong currents, and over the centuries it has swallowed many ships, from Native American canoes to nineteenth-century schooners. The French name Porte des Morts was the origin of the county's name.
