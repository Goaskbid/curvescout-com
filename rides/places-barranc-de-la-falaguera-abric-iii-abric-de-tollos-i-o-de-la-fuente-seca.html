Why this ride

A 160 km round trip from Alginet - roughly 2 h 46 in the saddle. On the way it passes Abric de la Cocina, Abric de Tollos II o de Cambriquia, Cueva del Cerro and Abric de Tollos I o de la fuente Seca.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc de la falaguera- Abric III

    Alginet, a town of about 13,000 people, and its neighbours Benifaió and Alfarp are part of a landscape of citrus groves, irrigation ditches and tiled farmhouses, where the Júcar river has laid down rich soil over thousands of years. In spring, when the orange blossom is out, the scent of azahar fills the air and can be smelled from the road with the visor up.

  2. 2. Abric de la Cocina

    The Abric de la Cocina, the kitchen shelter, is a rock overhang in the gorges of the Valencian hinterland near Dos Aguas, and on its walls prehistoric painters left scenes of archers, animals and geometric signs. It is one of the sites of the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula, inscribed by UNESCO in 1998, which covers hundreds of shelters along the eastern side of Spain with paintings showing hunting, gathering, dancing and warfare, with lively human figures in red and black. Excavations at the shelter also revealed tools and pottery, showing that people lived here over thousands of years.

  3. 3. Abric de Tollos II o de Cambriquia

    Bicorp is famous for a single painting. In the Cuevas de la Araña, a short walk from the village, a figure clings to ropes on a cliff, reaching into a hole surrounded by bees: one of the oldest known depictions of honey gathering in the world. The Abric de Tollos, also called Cambriquia, is another of the painted shelters around Bicorp, in the deep gorges of the Valencian interior, one of the World Heritage rock art sites of the Spanish east.

  4. 4. Cueva del Cerro

    Inland Valencia is a world away from the beaches on the coast. Behind the orange groves and the motorway the land rises into dry, folded mountains cut by deep gorges, where villages of a few hundred people cling to the hillsides and the pines smell of resin in the heat. Millares and Dos Aguas lie in this country, near the Júcar and Cabriel rivers, and the Cueva del Cerro is a rock shelter in the limestone, one of the many places in eastern Spain where prehistoric people left paintings on the walls.

  5. 5. Abric de Tollos I o de la fuente Seca

    This is the country of the honey-gatherer. Not far from here, in a cave near Bicorp, one of the best-known Levantine paintings shows a tiny figure climbing a rope ladder to a wild bees' nest high in a cliff, with bees swarming around him. It is thousands of years old, and it is a reminder that the artists who painted these shelters were also men and women who took honey from the rock, followed deer through the scrub and watched the weather. The Abric de Tollos belongs to the same family of shelters in the hills of inland Valencia, part of the Levantine rock art listed by UNESCO.
