Why this ride

Out of Sangarcía, one way: 493 km, roughly 10 h 07 in the saddle. On the way it passes Torrecuadradilla, near Cifuentes and Alcorcón. The highest point is about 1,150 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Photo stop in Sangarcía

    Sangarcía is a small village on the high meseta of Segovia province, in the stretch of pine and farmland that locals call the Tierra de Pinares. At roughly a thousand metres above sea level, the land is flat to gently rolling, with wide fields of cereals, stands of resin pine and big, clear skies. To the south, the Sierra de Guadarrama forms a blue wall on the horizon, often snow-capped in winter and spring.

  2. 2. Near Torrecuadradilla, on the way back

    Few places in Western Europe feel as empty as the high plateau of Guadalajara. This stop lies between Torrecuadradilla, Canredondo and Abánades, villages that once held hundreds of people and now count their year-round residents in dozens. The land is a high, rolling plateau of cereal fields, juniper and holm oak, cut by deep river valleys, with huge skies and very little traffic.

  5. 5. Back to Gredos Mountain

    Aranjuez is the town where Spain's kings went to get away from Madrid, and it has been laid out like a long, formal garden ever since. The royal palace, begun in the sixteenth century under Philip II and finished in the eighteenth, stands on the banks of the Tajo, surrounded by parks, canals, avenues of plane trees and orchards, and the whole landscape was added to the UNESCO list in 2001. The setting is exceptional: after miles of dry plain, the green of the river valley is a shock.
