Why this ride

From Alquézar it runs 353 km to Alquézar; allow about 6 h 40 of riding. It goes by way of Cueva de Revilla, Eglésia de Santa Maria de l'Assumpció and back through Cueva de Revilla. It tops out around 1,850 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Pyrenees N260 Route

Cueva de Revilla is 66 km from Cueva de la fuente de! Trucho, mostly on A-2205. It climbs 680 m to the top at 1,099 m and drops 380 m again. This is the bit the ride is built around.

The stops

  1. 1. Cueva de la fuente de! Trucho

    Start here. Cueva de la fuente de!

    The name suggests a spring and a trout, and you can hold on to the idea of water even if the shelter itself is dry. The Cueva de la Fuente de Trucho is one of the rock shelters that the archaeologists place in the Vero valley, two and a half kilometres from Alquézar, and it belongs with the painted overhangs of the Iberian Mediterranean arc that UNESCO recognised in 1998. Springs matter in this part of Aragon, which is dry for most of the year, and prehistoric people came to places like this for the same reason that modern walkers do: water, shade and a view.

    Read the complete Cueva de la fuente de! Trucho write-up.

  2. 2. Cueva de Revilla

    65 km in.

    Revilla is one of the most spectacularly placed hamlets in the Spanish Pyrenees, perched on a ledge above the Garganta de Escuaín, a deep gorge cut into the limestone of the Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park. It looks down into the canyon from a terrace of meadows, with the high peaks behind. The Cueva de Revilla is a cave in the cliffs near the hamlet, and the area between Bielsa, Plan and Boltaña is some of the wildest country in Aragón.

    Read the complete Cueva de Revilla write-up.

  3. 3. Eglésia de Santa Maria de l'Assumpció

    179 km in.

    Valleys in the high Pyrenees of Catalonia and Aragon are full of small Romanesque churches, and the area around the Alta Ribagorça is among the richest. They were built in the eleventh and twelfth centuries by craftsmen working in what is called the Lombard style, with plain stone walls, round arches and tall, slender bell towers decorated with blind arcades, and they stand in villages that often have only a few houses.
