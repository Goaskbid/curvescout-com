Why this ride

National Scenic Byway. volcanic caldera, red rock, forest. From Bernalillo it runs 95 km to Los Alamos; allow about 1 h 44 of riding. The stops in between: Jemez Pueblo and Jemez Mountain Trail. The highest point is about 2,750 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bernalillo

    Bernalillo is an old Spanish and Pueblo town on the Rio Grande just north of Albuquerque, and it has a quiet identity of its own, a mix of adobe walls, cottonwoods, cafes, a few vineyards and a long history. The Sandia Mountains stand to the east, with a ridge that turns watermelon pink at sunset, which is what the name Sandia means in Spanish. The town has about nine thousand people and holds a festival of San Lorenzo each August, with traditional dances.

  2. 2. Jemez Pueblo

    Jemez Pueblo is a living community, not a monument, and the first thing to understand about it is that the people who live here call it Walatowa, meaning this is the place in their language, Towa. The village sits at the foot of the Jemez Mountains, in a valley of red sandstone cliffs along State Route 4, and the pueblo's lands run up the canyon of the Jemez River toward the high country. The Jemez people have lived in this region for many centuries, and are the only remaining speakers of Towa, a language they share with the people of the former Pecos Pueblo.

  5. 5. Los Alamos

    Los Alamos is a town that exists because of a secret. In 1943 the US government chose this remote mesa, once the site of a boys' ranch school, for the Manhattan Project, and J. Robert Oppenheimer's team built the first atomic bombs in the pine forest at the end of a single winding road. The laboratory is still the biggest employer, which gives the town an odd, educated, slightly fenced-in feel. Street names, museums and a surprising number of PhDs in the grocery queue all hint at it.

    Read the complete Los Alamos write-up.
