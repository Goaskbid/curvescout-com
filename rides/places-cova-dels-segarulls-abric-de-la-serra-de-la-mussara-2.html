Why this ride

A 248 km round trip from Vilafranca del Penedès - roughly 4 h 14 in the saddle. On the way it passes Cova de Vallmajor, Aqueduct, Abric de la Serra de la mussara and back through Aqueduct. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Cova dels Segarulls

    Start here.

    The vineyards of the Penedès roll out in every direction from Vilafranca, and the Cova dels Segarulls lies among them, a few kilometres outside the town in the low hills toward the coastal range.

    Read the complete Cova dels Segarulls write-up.

  2. 2. Cova de Vallmajor

    29 km in.

    Inland from the Costa Daurada, behind the beach towns of El Vendrell and Calafell, the land rises into the low hills of the Baix Penedès, a landscape of vineyards, almond and olive groves, pine woods and small villages. The Cova de Vallmajor, near Albinyana and Bonastre, is a rock shelter in these hills with prehistoric paintings, part of the Levantine rock art of the Mediterranean that is recognised as a World Heritage Site. The paintings are faint and the shelter is protected, so visits are usually by arrangement.

    Read the complete Cova de Vallmajor write-up.

  3. 3. Aqueduct

    69 km in.

    The Pont del Diable, the Devil's Bridge, is not a bridge for people. It is a Roman aqueduct, a tall, two-tiered stone arcade that carried water to Tarraco, modern Tarragona, from the Francolí river, about fifteen kilometres away. It spans a small valley near the road to Constantí, two hundred metres long and about twenty-seven metres high at its tallest point, built of big dressed stones without mortar, and it has stood for roughly two thousand years. UNESCO included it in the Archaeological Ensemble of Tarraco in 2000.

    Read the complete Aqueduct write-up.

  4. 4. Abric de la Serra de la mussara

    108 km in.

    Climb out of Reus on a clear morning and the Serra de la Mussara rises ahead like a wall, a limestone escarpment that holds up the edge of the Prades plateau and drops steeply to the plain of the Camp de Tarragona. The rock shelters along this cliff line, close to the village of Vilaplana, are part of the prehistoric painted landscape of eastern Spain. The best reason to stop, though, is the view: on a good day you look straight down over hazelnut orchards and olive groves to Reus, Tarragona and the sea.

    Read the complete Abric de la Serra de la mussara write-up.
