Why this ride

A one-way ride of 101 km, Yosemite Valley to the finish - roughly 1 h 22 in the saddle. On the way it passes Olmsted Point, Tenaya Lake, Tuolumne Meadows and Tioga Pass. The highest point is about 3,050 m. Tioga Pass is open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Tioga Road

For 19 km this is Tioga Road, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it. Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast.

Tioga Road

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 3031 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance — cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Yosemite Valley

    Yosemite Valley is a mile-wide trench cut into the granite of the Sierra Nevada, with walls that rise three thousand feet or more from the flat, meadow-covered floor, and it is as astonishing in person as in any photograph. El Capitan, a sheer, blank face, stands on the north side, Half Dome, a sliced dome of granite, towers at the head, and waterfalls drop from the rim, most dramatically Yosemite Falls, which falls nearly twenty-five hundred feet in three stages. The Merced River winds through the meadows below.

    Read the complete Yosemite Valley write-up.

  2. 2. Olmsted Point

    Olmsted Point is where you finally see the back of Half Dome, and it is a shock. From the valley floor, Half Dome is a sheer-faced icon; from here, several miles away, it is a smooth, rounded profile, set at the far end of the long granite trench of Tenaya Canyon. The overlook is on the Tioga Road, a wide, flat shelf of pale glacier-polished granite, and in front of you the rock rolls off in great bare slabs toward the canyon, with giant boulders sitting about on it like dropped marbles.

  3. 3. Tenaya Lake

    Tenaya Lake lies right beside the Tioga Road at about 2,500 metres, a long, bright, glacier-carved basin ringed by polished granite domes, and it is one of the few places in Yosemite where you can park, walk thirty steps and be sitting on a beach with a mountain view. The water is startlingly clear and cold, and on a still morning the domes and pines are reflected in it so perfectly that it is hard to tell which way up the picture goes. A fine sandy beach sits at the eastern end and a smaller one at the west, and in summer people swim, if briefly.

  4. 4. Tuolumne Meadows

    Tuolumne Meadows is the largest subalpine meadow in the Sierra Nevada, a wide, grassy bowl at about 2,600 metres along the Tuolumne River, ringed by granite domes and peaks. In the early morning the grass is silver with frost even in summer, and mist hangs on the river, and the sun comes up over Lembert Dome and the long, bare ridge of Cathedral Peak. It is a favourite with climbers, walkers and photographers, and the car parks and campgrounds are busy from July to September.

  5. 5. Tioga Pass

    Tioga Pass is the highest highway pass in California, at 3,031 metres, and it is the eastern gate to Yosemite. A small park sign, a ranger kiosk and a stony meadow mark the top, with snowbanks lingering into July and the peaks of the Sierra crest rising to either side. It feels like the edge of something, and it is: on the east side the road drops about 3,000 feet in a dozen miles to the high desert of Mono Lake and the Great Basin, a change of climate and scenery as abrupt as any in North America.

    Read the complete Tioga Pass write-up.

  6. 6. Lee Vining

    Lee Vining is a tiny town on the eastern side of the Sierra Nevada, at the foot of Tioga Pass and the edge of Mono Lake, and it is the eastern gateway to Yosemite. At nearly seven thousand feet, with a few hundred residents, it has a main street along Highway 395 of cafes, motels, gas stations and a store, a mountain town with a desert view. The air is dry and sharp, and the light, bright and clear, is the kind that makes the mountains look cut out of paper.

Tuolumne Meadows to Tioga Pass

Tuolumne Meadows → Tioga Pass climbs about 3031 m in 12 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Tioga Pass to Lee Vining

Tioga Pass → Lee Vining drops about 3031 m over 16 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
