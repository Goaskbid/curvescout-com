Why this ride

From Wassertrüdingen it runs 218 km to Wassertrüdingen; allow about 3 h 46 of riding. The stops in between: Fort site and civil setllement of Weissenb, Fort site and civil settlement of Pfünz, Watchtowers WP 15/27 to 15/47 and back through Fort site and civil settlement of Pfünz. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Watchtower WP 13/40

    The Raetian Limes, the eastern part of the Roman frontier in Germany, is different from the Upper Germanic section, because here the Romans built a wall. The remains of the stone wall, which stretches across Bavaria for about a hundred and sixty kilometres, are known locally as the Devil's Wall, the Teufelsmauer, because people long believed that no human could have made it. Watchtower WP 13/40, near the village of Arberg in Middle Franconia, stands along this section, and its foundations can be traced in a field or at the edge of a wood.

  2. 2. Fort site and civil setllement of Weissenb

    Weißenburg in Bayern is the best Roman stop in this part of Franconia, and the fort here, known in antiquity as Biriciana, was a serious place. It stood on the Upper German-Raetian Limes, which UNESCO listed in 2005, and it held a cavalry unit at its height. Today you can walk the reconstructed gate and walls and then visit the Roman baths, which are among the best preserved in southern Germany and sit under a modern roof beside the old town. In 1979, builders found a hoard of bronze and silver objects buried in the fort's grounds, the Weißenburg treasure, which includes ceremonial armour, and it is now one of the key finds of Roman Bavaria.

  3. 3. Fort site and civil settlement of Pfünz

    Above the village of Pfünz, on a spur of white Jura limestone looking down into the Altmühl valley, the Romans built a fort they called Vetoniana. Today a reconstructed gate and a stretch of wall stand on the hilltop, and from up there you understand the choice at once: the valley floor curls below you, the river meanders slowly, and anyone moving along it would have been seen hours before they arrived.

  4. 4. Watchtowers WP 15/27 to 15/47

    Up in the hills above the Altmühl valley, west of Neustadt an der Donau and the Danube, the old Raetian frontier crossed some of its prettiest country. The numbers 15/27 to 15/47 cover a long stretch of the line, and because that line follows the ridges and plateau edges, the landscape is a mixture of fields, juniper-dotted limestone slopes and sheltered woods. The Altmühl itself is a slow river that has cut a deep, winding valley through Jurassic limestone, and the whole district is a nature park, known for fossils from the quarries at Solnhofen.
