Why this ride

A 257 km round trip from Cuenca - roughly 4 h 26 in the saddle. It goes by way of La Peña del Castellar, Abric del Rincón del Tío Escribano and back through La Peña del Castellar. The highest point is about 1,350 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Historic Walled Town of Cuenca

    The postcard view of Cuenca is the Casas Colgadas, the hanging houses, wooden balconies thrust out from the rim of the Huécar gorge, one of which houses a museum of Spanish abstract art. The less photographed truth is that the old town is really an exercise in vertical living. Every building here exists because someone found a way to build on a ledge too small for it, and the result, recognised by UNESCO in 1996, is a medieval city that looks balanced on a blade.

  2. 2. La Peña del Castellar

    Villar del Humo is a tiny village in the Serranía of Cuenca, the high, forested mountain country east of the city of Cuenca. Around it, sandstone outcrops rise from pine forests, and on their faces prehistoric painters left images of animals and people. The Peña del Escrito and La Peña del Castellar are among the most important shelters here, and they are part of the World Heritage Site of Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin. The paintings include deer, horses and human figures, some in the dynamic Levantine style and others more schematic.

  3. 3. Abric del Rincón del Tío Escribano

    High in the interior of Valencia province, where the coastal orange groves give way to limestone plateaus and pine forest, the village of Titaguas sits on the road towards the Rincón de Ademuz. Close by lies the Abric del Rincón del Tío Escribano, a rock shelter with prehistoric paintings, one of the hundreds of sites that make up the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula, listed by UNESCO in 1998.
