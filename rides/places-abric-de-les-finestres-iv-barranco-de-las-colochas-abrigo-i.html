Why this ride

A 342 km round trip from Ontinyent - roughly 8 h in the saddle. The stops in between: Abric del Charco de la madera, Barranco de las Colochas- Abrigo I and back through Abric del Charco de la madera. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Abric del Charco de la madera → Barranco de las Colochas- Abrigo I

The 100 km between Abric del Charco de la madera and Barranco de las Colochas- Abrigo I, mostly on CV-425. Up about 960 m to 890 m, then 740 m back down the far side. Fill the tank before the climb.

The stops

  1. 1. Abric de les finestres IV

    Start here.

    Bocairent is a village built on a rock, its old houses clinging to a limestone spur above a ravine in the Serra de Mariola, in the south of the province of Valencia. Across the ravine, in the cliff face, a row of small square openings looks out like windows: these are the Covetes dels Moros, a series of artificial caves cut into the rock, connected by internal passages, whose purpose is still debated. They may have been granaries, refuges or even a monastery, and their name means the little caves of the Moors.

    Read the complete Abric de les finestres IV write-up.

  2. 2. Abric del Charco de la madera

    69 km in.

    The sign says Abric del Charco de la Madera, and the name suggests a pool, a charco, in a rocky gully near the village of Bicorp, in the highlands of the Valencian interior. This is the Canal de Navarrés and the Valle de Ayora-Cofrentes, a country of limestone ranges, river canyons and poor farming land, a long way from the Valencian beaches. The shelter holds paintings in the Levantine style, part of the UNESCO-listed group. The second stop on this ride, Penya Escrita, is a different site altogether, in the mountains of the Marina Alta far to the east, so treat the two as separate worlds.

    Read the complete Abric del Charco de la madera write-up.

  3. 3. Barranco de las Colochas- Abrigo I

    169 km in.

    West of Valencia, the river Turia leaves the mountains through a series of deep gorges, and around the villages of Gestalgar, Sot de Chera and Chulilla, the landscape turns dramatic: limestone cliffs, pine-covered slopes and the river winding green below. In a side ravine near Gestalgar, the Barranco de las Colochas shelters a handful of painted panels that UNESCO counts among the Mediterranean rock art of Iberia. The figures are faint, but the setting is worth the effort on its own.
