Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Phnom Penh

    Phnom Penh sits where three great rivers meet, the Mekong, the Tonle Sap and the Bassac, and the confluence is known in Khmer as Chaktomuk, the four faces. The city is Cambodia's capital and by far its biggest, with about one and a half million people, and it has an energy that arrives in stages: first the heat, then the noise, then the smell of charcoal smoke, frangipani and river mud. The riverfront promenade at dusk, with families on the pavement, food carts and the golden roofs of the Royal Palace, is one of the great evenings of Southeast Asia.

  2. 2. Cambodian Memorial Sites: From centres of

    West of Phnom Penh, the flat rice plain begins to roll into low hills, and the land of Kampong Speu opens out in a scatter of sugar palms, small farms and monasteries. This is the province of palm sugar, tapped from the toddy palms by climbers who go up the trunks twice a day, and the roadsides are lined with stalls selling blocks of it, along with fresh coconuts and fruit. The region is quiet and rural, with a way of life that has hardly changed in decades, and the sense of a different Cambodia from the capital's crowds.
