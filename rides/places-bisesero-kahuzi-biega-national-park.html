Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bisesero

    Bisesero is a place where the story is carried by the hills themselves. In April 1994, in the first weeks of the genocide against the Tutsi in Rwanda, thousands of people from the surrounding area fled to the steep, wooded ridges of Bisesero, above Lake Kivu, and held out for weeks against armed attackers with sticks, stones and whatever they could find. They were eventually overwhelmed, and tens of thousands were killed in the area, in one of the worst massacres of the genocide. Today a memorial site stands on the hillside, with mass graves and a small museum, and survivors and guides are on hand to explain what happened.

  2. 2. Cyamudongo Natural Forest

    In the far southwest corner of Rwanda, the land drops toward Lake Kivu and the Ruzizi river, and the green hills here are mostly tea, banana and small farms. Cyamudongo is the exception: a patch of natural forest, a few hundred hectares at most, standing as an island of tall trees in the middle of cultivation. It is separated from the great Nyungwe forest by a belt of farmland, yet it still shelters a small population of chimpanzees, along with other primates and a remarkable range of birds and butterflies.

  3. 3. Kahuzi-Biega National Park

    Kahuzi-Biega is the place in the world to meet an eastern lowland gorilla, the largest of all the great apes. The park lies in the east of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, in South Kivu province, west of Bukavu and Lake Kivu, and it covers a mix of lowland rainforest and the high forested slopes of two extinct volcanoes, Kahuzi at 3,308 metres and Biega at 2,790. Bamboo forest grows on the upper slopes, and the whole mass rises abruptly from the Albertine Rift, one of the richest areas for wildlife in Africa.
