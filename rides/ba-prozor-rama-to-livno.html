Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Jablanica

    Jablanica lies on the Neretva, a river of an astonishing blue-green colour, in a narrow canyon between the mountains of Herzegovina, and it is one of the most memorable stops on the road between Mostar and Sarajevo. The town of about six thousand people sits beside a long reservoir, formed by a dam in the 1950s, and its main road, the M17, runs along the water, with cliffs and tunnels on one side and the lake on the other.

  2. 2. Prozor

    Prozor, often written Prozor-Rama, is the old centre of the Rama valley, a green pocket of northern Herzegovina that opens onto one of the most striking pieces of water in the country. The town stands near the head of Ramsko Jezero, a reservoir created in the 1960s when the Rama river was dammed. Below the dam wall the valley has lost its old villages, and as you ride along the shore you pass a monastery that stands on a little island in the lake, Šćit, the Franciscan house that is the spiritual heart of the Rama region.

  3. 3. Kupres

    Kupres is one of those places where you notice the sky first. The town sits on a very high karst plain in western Bosnia, among the highest open farmland in the country, at roughly 1,100 metres. The plain is broad and almost treeless, ringed by gently rounded hills, and the wind moves across it unhindered. Locals say the winters are fierce, and the snow can lie for months. In summer the same plain is a bright carpet of grass and wildflowers, with cattle and sheep grazing and hay stacked in small cones.

  4. 4. Livno

    Livno sits on the edge of a huge karst plain, the Livanjsko polje, the largest of its kind in the Dinaric mountains, ringed by bare limestone ridges. In winter the plain floods and becomes a shallow lake, in spring it turns green, and in summer it is a patchwork of hay meadows with the dark mass of Cincar mountain rising behind. The town is about twelve thousand people and has an unhurried, provincial air, with a small old quarter, a mosque and Catholic and Orthodox churches standing within a short walk of each other.
