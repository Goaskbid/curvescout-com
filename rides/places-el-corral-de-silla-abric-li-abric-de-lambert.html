Why this ride

From Tuéjar it runs 323 km to Tuéjar; allow about 5 h 34 of riding. It goes by way of Barranco de las Colochas- Abrigo I, Balma de la fabriqueta, La Lonja de la Seda de Valencia and Abric de Lambert. The highest point is about 950 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. El Corral de Silla- Abric lI

    A small group of villages in the Valencian interior goes by the name Los Serranos, and Tuéjar, Chelva and Alpuente are part of it. It is a country of mountain ridges, pine forest and river gorges, where the Turia and its tributaries have cut deep valleys, and where, because of its remoteness, many farms and hamlets have been abandoned. El Corral de Silla II is one of a series of painted shelters in the area, three kilometres from Tuéjar and about six from Chelva, and as with all the shelters on these routes it forms part of the Mediterranean rock art protected by UNESCO since 1998.

  2. 2. Barranco de las Colochas- Abrigo I

    West of Valencia, the river Turia leaves the mountains through a series of deep gorges, and around the villages of Gestalgar, Sot de Chera and Chulilla, the landscape turns dramatic: limestone cliffs, pine-covered slopes and the river winding green below. In a side ravine near Gestalgar, the Barranco de las Colochas shelters a handful of painted panels that UNESCO counts among the Mediterranean rock art of Iberia. The figures are faint, but the setting is worth the effort on its own.

  3. 3. Balma de la fabriqueta

    The Serra Calderona is the green ridge that rises north-west of Valencia, separating the city's plain from the valleys of the interior. It is a natural park of pine forest, cork oak and rocky hills, close enough to the city for weekend walkers and riders, yet surprisingly wild once you get into it. The rock shelter of Balma de la Fabriqueta lies near the village of Olocau on its southern side, one of the painted shelters that make up the Levantine rock art inscribed by UNESCO. Access to many of these sites is restricted or guided, so check with the local tourist office before trying to visit.

  4. 4. La Lonja de la Seda de Valencia

    In the heart of Valencia's old town, a Gothic building with crenellated walls and gargoyles looks more like a castle than a market, and that was rather the point. The Lonja de la Seda, the silk exchange, was built in the late fifteenth and early sixteenth centuries when Valencia was one of the richest cities in the Mediterranean, and it was meant to show the power of its merchants. Its great hall, the Sala de Contratación, is one of the masterpieces of late Gothic architecture, with slender columns that twist like palm trunks up to a ribbed vault. It was listed by UNESCO in 1996.

  5. 5. Abric de Lambert

    Cullera is not where most people would look for prehistory. It is a busy beach town at the mouth of the Júcar, with high-rise apartments along the sand, a lighthouse on the point and rice fields stretching inland toward the Albufera lagoon. But the town has a mountain at its back, a limestone outcrop crowned by a castle and a sanctuary, and in that rock there are shelters, including the one known as the Abric de Lambert.
