Why this ride

A 90 km one-way ride from near Dublin, about 1 h 33 of riding. On the way it passes Sally Gap, Glenmacnass Waterfall and Glendalough. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Wicklow Mountains Military Road

The ground tilts up and stays tilted for 53 km. 495 m of it, taken steadily rather than in one wall. A steady rhythm suits this better than bursts. The height arrives whether you hurry or not.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Dublin

    Dublin is a city you leave by the back door if you are a rider, and the back door is the one that points toward the Wicklow hills. Beginning somewhere in the middle of the city, with over a million people in the area, you spend the first half hour in ordinary urban work: buses, bus lanes, cyclists, taxis, the Liffey in the distance, and traffic lights that never seem to line up. Ireland's capital is compact, friendly and slow to cross at rush hour, so leave before eight or after nine.

  2. 2. Sally Gap

    Sally Gap is a bleak crossroads in the middle of the Wicklow Mountains, about 495 metres above sea level, where the road from Dublin meets the one running across the hills from Glendalough to Roundwood. There is nothing there but a signpost, a sheep or two on the tarmac and a view of huge, rolling bog under a sky that seems to change every ten minutes. It is the sort of place that feels at the edge of the world, although the centre of Dublin is only about forty minutes away.

  3. 3. Glenmacnass Waterfall

    The road over the Wicklow Mountains was built by soldiers, and it shows. After the 1798 rebellion the British army cut a military road straight through the upland bog, so that troops could reach rebels who had been hiding in the glens, and the route from Laragh up to Sally Gap and over to Dublin still follows that logic: no hairpins, just long lines that climb where the ground allows. Glenmacnass is one of the points where the road leans over a drop, and the waterfall is the reason people pull in.

  4. 4. Glendalough

    Glendalough, the valley of two lakes, is a hollow gouged out by a glacier in the Wicklow Mountains, with a pair of dark, still lakes between steep, wooded slopes, and at its foot a ruined monastic city that is one of the most important in Ireland. The place feels sheltered and still, and the stone ruins look as if they have grown from the hillside. It is only about an hour from Dublin, so it is also one of the busiest places in the country on a fine summer weekend.

  5. 5. Laragh

    Laragh is a crossroads village in the middle of County Wicklow, and it is where the Military Road finishes its long run across the bog and drops into the green valleys. There are few buildings: a handful of pubs and cafes, a hostel, a church and a gift shop. It sits at the meeting of several glens, and the nearest larger towns are Rathdrum and Rathnew, ten to fifteen kilometres away, so this is not the place to count on fuel. Fill the tank before you come up from the city.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Dublin to Sally Gap

Dublin → Sally Gap climbs about 495 m in 30 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Sally Gap to Glenmacnass Waterfall

Sally Gap → Glenmacnass Waterfall drops about 495 m over 12 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Glenmacnass Waterfall to Glendalough

Glenmacnass Waterfall → Glendalough is 6 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Glendalough to Laragh

Glendalough → Laragh is 3 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
