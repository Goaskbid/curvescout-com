Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Budapest

    Budapest is two cities that were joined in 1873: hilly, historic Buda on the west bank of the Danube, and flat, lively Pest on the east. The river between them is wide and powerful, crossed by a string of bridges, of which the Chain Bridge, opened in 1849, is the oldest and most graceful. From the Buda side, the castle hill rises above the water with its palace and the spire of Matthias Church, and across the river the neo-Gothic Parliament building stretches along the bank, lit gold at night. It is one of the best-looking capitals in Europe, and it knows it.

  2. 2. Szentendre

    Szentendre is the artists' town of the Danube Bend, a short ride north of Budapest, with narrow cobbled streets, baroque churches and a mass of little galleries and cafés. It has been a popular day out for people from the capital for generations, and the old centre fills with visitors on any warm weekend. But the town is small enough, and the houses colourful enough, that it keeps its charm, and the riverside promenade on the Danube is a pleasant place to rest.

  3. 3. Visegrad

    The Danube makes a sharp turn at Visegrád, squeezing between two ranges of hills in a narrow gorge before swinging south toward Budapest, and the town sits at the point of the bend, below a fortress that watches the whole thing. The citadel stands on a steep hill about three hundred and fifty metres above the river, and its walls look out over the water, the town and the forested slopes across the way. It is one of the best views in Hungary, and it has been used for strategic purposes for more than a thousand years.

  4. 4. Esztergom

    Esztergom was Hungary's capital before Budapest was anything, and the Hungarians still talk about it with a sort of quiet pride. Stephen, the first Christian king, was born here around the year 975, and he was crowned in 1000 or 1001, so in a sense the nation begins on this bluff above the Danube. Kings lived in the castle on the hill until the thirteenth century, when a Mongol invasion pushed them south, and the town became the seat of the Catholic primate of Hungary, which it remains.
