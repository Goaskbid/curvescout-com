Why this ride

From Kowloon it runs 442 km to Kowloon; allow about 7 h 37 of riding. On the way it passes Zone 2, Historic Centre of Macao, Zone 1 and Yinglong Lou. It crosses Hong Kong SAR China, Macao SAR China and China - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hong Kong

    Hong Kong looks like a city built by someone who ran out of flat land and decided to go up. The skyscrapers of Central and Kowloon crowd the shoreline of Victoria Harbour, with green mountains rising immediately behind, and the effect, especially at night, with the towers lit in colour and the ferries crossing the black water, is startling. Kowloon, on the mainland side, is the older, grittier half, with dense neon-lit streets, street markets and the old Star Ferry terminal, and more than two million people live in its small area.

  2. 2. Zone 2

    Macau is a small piece of land with an outsized past, and the old peninsula is where it is most visible. Portuguese traders settled here in the sixteenth century, and the city stayed under Portuguese administration until 1999. Its centre still has the feel of a Mediterranean town transplanted to the coast of China, with pastel colonial houses, wave-patterned pavements and the Largo do Senado, a square of cream and yellow buildings with a fountain in the middle.

  3. 3. Historic Centre of Macao

    The southern end of the Macau peninsula is where the city began. Here the Barra hill rises above the harbour, and at its foot stands the A-Ma Temple, a collection of small shrines and courtyards climbing the rock among boulders carved with red inscriptions. It is dedicated to the goddess of seafarers, and legend says that the name of Macau itself comes from it, since the first Portuguese sailors heard the name A-Ma Gau, the bay of A-Ma, and used it for the whole place. Fishermen's boats still bob in the harbour in front.

  4. 4. Zone 1

    For four hundred years Macau was a trading post, handed back only in 1999, and the legacy shows in the streets: pastel-coloured colonial buildings, black and white wave-patterned paving in Senado Square, and small Catholic churches next to Taoist temples. Its historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the most famous fragment, the carved stone façade of the Ruins of St Paul's, stands at the top of a flight of steps, all that is left of a church that burned in 1835.
