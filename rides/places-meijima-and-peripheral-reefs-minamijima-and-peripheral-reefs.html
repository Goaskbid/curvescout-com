  1. 1. Meijima and peripheral reefs

    Meijima is one of those names that sounds like a joke until you learn the story: the small islands of the Ogasawara chain were given family names, a sort of island clan strung out across the Pacific. Meijima, roughly translated, is the niece. It lies far south of Tokyo, around a thousand kilometres out in the open Pacific, and there is no road to it, no bridge, and nothing resembling a petrol station.

  2. 2. Some parts of Hahajima and peripheral reef

    The Ogasawara Islands lie about a thousand kilometres south of Tokyo, in the Pacific, and getting there takes a full day on a ferry, which is a good start to understanding why they have remained so special. The Bonin Islands, as they are also known, have never been connected to a continent, and as a result the plants and animals that reached them evolved in isolation, giving rise to a wealth of species found nowhere else. UNESCO listed the islands in 2011, and they are often called the Galapagos of the East.

  3. 3. Minamijima and peripheral reefs

    A thousand kilometres south of Tokyo, in the Pacific Ocean, the Ogasawara Islands rise from the sea, a scattered chain of volcanic islands that have never been connected to a continent. Minamijima, a small uninhabited island just off Chichijima, is one of their jewels: a ring of jagged limestone around a sheltered cove, the Ogiura, with white sand, turquoise water and a sinkhole beach that looks like something from a dream. The island and its reefs are part of the Ogasawara Islands World Heritage site, listed by UNESCO in 2011.
