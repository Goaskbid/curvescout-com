Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Chengguan

    Chengguan is the old core of Huixian, a county-level city in the north of Henan that sits right where the North China Plain stops being flat. To the south and east the land runs out level, a grid of wheat and maize fields and straight poplar-lined roads; to the north-west, close enough to fill the skyline on a clear day, the Taihang Mountains rise in tiers of red and buff rock. For anyone riding to Guoliang, this is the last proper town before the climb, and it behaves like one: traffic, scooters, food stalls, shops, mechanics and filling stations, with the provincial city of Xinxiang about thirty kilometres away for anything bigger.

  2. 2. Guoliang Tunnel Road point 2

    Up on the high shelf of the Taihang, above the cliffs that make the area famous, sits Guoliang, a village of stone houses built from the same reddish sandstone that forms the walls around it. For centuries the only practical link to the valley below was a stairway cut into the cliff, and the village lived the way remote mountain places do: terraced plots, a few animals, water carried by hand. Today it is part of the Wanxianshan scenic area, and on a summer weekend there are more visitors than residents, but walk a few lanes back from the main square and you still find low doorways, stone roofs and persimmon trees.

  3. 3. Guoliang Tunnel Road point 3

    The Guoliang tunnel is one of those roads that is famous because of how it was made rather than how fast you can ride it. In the 1970s, with no road to the outside world and no help coming, villagers from Guoliang decided to cut one themselves, through the cliff face rather than around it. They sold animals to buy hammers, chisels and explosives and worked for about five years, mostly by hand, until a gallery roughly 1.2 kilometres long ran along the inside of the rock wall, wide enough for a small vehicle and lit by a row of rough openings that look straight out over the gorge.
