Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Historic Town of Banská Štiavnica and the

    Banská Štiavnica is a town built into a volcano. A great crater, the caldera of an extinct volcano, surrounds the place, and the streets climb steeply up its inner slopes, so that the whole centre is a tumble of pastel houses, churches and castles on a hillside. Silver and gold were mined here from the Middle Ages, and in the eighteenth century the town was one of the richest in the Habsburg Empire. In 1762 the mining academy was founded, one of the first technical colleges in the world, and its graduates carried mining knowledge to many countries. UNESCO listed the town in 1993, along with its technical monuments.

  2. 2. Old Village of Hollókő and its Surrounding

    Hollókő is a village of about three hundred people in the Cserhát hills of northern Hungary, thirty-odd kilometres south of the Slovak border, and it is one of the few places in Europe where a whole way of life has been kept, not by a museum but by the people still living in the houses. It was one of the first places inscribed by UNESCO, in 1987, as a living example of a traditional village of the Palóc, a Hungarian ethnic group of this region.

  3. 3. Andrássy Avenue and the Underground

    Andrássy Avenue is Budapest at its most confident. It was laid out in the 1870s as a grand boulevard from the city centre to the City Park, lined with neo-Renaissance mansions, embassies and the State Opera House, completed in 1884, and it runs straight and wide under rows of plane trees. UNESCO listed it in 2002, together with the first underground railway on the European continent, the Millennium Underground, which opened in 1896, a few years before the Paris metro, and runs just beneath the avenue.
