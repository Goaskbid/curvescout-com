Why this ride

812 km from Fagerstrand to Bjørkelangen, about 17 h 04 of riding. On the way it passes in Røyken, Sandvika, Scenic stretch near Vik and near Grua. near Kirkebygda is the high point. Long stretches are under trees.

Scenic road chapters

Lysefjord and Suleskar fuel gate → Lysefjord and Suleskar high point

Slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did. Water arrives beside the road and stays for 17 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go.

  3. 3. Sandvika

    Sandvika is the centre of Bærum, the wealthy municipality on Oslo's western edge, and it is a curious mix of shopping mall, fjord town and transport hub. Its heart is the mouth of the Sandvikselva, a short river that tumbles down from the forest and meets the fjord among marinas, footbridges and new apartment blocks, and its biggest building is one of the largest shopping centres in Norway.

  6. 6. Near Aursmoen, on the way back

    East of Oslo, towards the Swedish border, the land flattens into a quiet country of spruce forest, bogs, small farms and lakes, and Aurskog-Høland is one of its largest municipalities. Aursmoen and Bjørkelangen are its main villages, each with a few thousand people, a church, a supermarket and a petrol station, and between them lie long roads through the woods where you may not meet another vehicle for minutes at a time.

  8. 8. Back to Lysefjord

    The Oslofjord narrows here, and the town to know is Drøbak, about ten kilometres away on the eastern shore. It is a long way from the real Lysefjord in Rogaland, but it has its own claim to a place in history.

  9. 9. Fuel stop near Bjørkelangen

    Bjørkelangen is the main village of Aurskog-Høland, east of Oslo, on the shore of a long lake of the same name, in a landscape of forests, lakes and farmland stretching towards the Swedish border. It is a quiet, unpretentious place, with shops, a church and a few cafés, and it makes a practical start for rides into the forests and lakes of the region.

    Read the complete Fuel stop near Bjørkelangen write-up.

  10. 10. Near Stange, on the way back

    Mjøsa is the reason to stop here. Norway's largest lake runs for more than a hundred kilometres from Lillehammer down to Minnesund, and on this stretch of its eastern shore, south of Stange and around the farming village of Tangen, the water lies wide and calm below gentle fields of grain. Across the lake you can see Skreia and the low wooded ridge of Toten, and on a still evening the far shore doubles itself in the surface.

    Read the complete Near Stange, on the way back write-up.

  11. 11. Food stop near Reinsvoll

    West of Lake Mjøsa, Norway's largest lake, the land rises into the gentle hills of Toten, some of the best farmland in the country. Reinsvoll is a small community here, set among fields, farmsteads and patches of spruce forest, with long views over the lake and the hills beyond. It is a quiet place, where you are more likely to meet a tractor than a tourist.

  12. 12. Photo stop near Lampeland

    Lampeland is a small village in Flesberg, in the lower part of Numedal, the long valley that runs north-west from Kongsberg into the mountains of southern Norway. The river Numedalslågen flows through the valley, wide and calm in places, rushing over rapids in others, and the road follows it, with forested slopes rising on both sides and farms on the valley floor.

  13. 13. Near Nesoddtangen

    Out on the Oslofjord, between the Nesodden peninsula and the western shore near Slemmestad, the water is wide, island-studded and busy with sails on summer weekends. This point sits in that stretch of fjord country, a few kilometres from Nesoddtangen, where wooded shores, smooth granite skerries and summer cabins line the water, and the towers of Oslo are visible to the north. The ferries that cross the fjord, and the many small boats, give the area a maritime feel.

  16. 16. Back to Sognefjellet

    Sognefjellet itself is a long way from here, up in the mountains between Lom and Skjolden, and this finish point is not on it. It is calm farm country, a patchwork of fields cut into dark spruce, with small lakes and red barns, and it is where a long mountain ride can wind down gently.
