Why this ride

64 km one way from Covilhã - plan on about 1 h 05 of riding. The stops in between: Torre and Manteigas. The highest point is about 2,000 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Serra da Estrela Road

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 1993 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance — cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Covilha

    Covilhã clings to the steep eastern flank of the Serra da Estrela, so much so that its streets seem to be climbing the mountain one stair at a time. It is Portugal's highest-lying city of any size, a place where the air changes noticeably as you ride up from the plains of the Beira Baixa. The town is the starting point for the road up to the Torre, the highest point in mainland Portugal at around 1,993 metres, and a good place to prepare.

  2. 2. Torre

    The highest point of mainland Portugal is a disappointment for about thirty seconds and then a joy. Torre is a bare, rounded granite summit in the Serra da Estrela at 1,993 metres, and it is seven metres short of two thousand. Some years ago a stone tower was built on top to make up the difference, so the monument can claim the round number even if the mountain cannot. Around it the plateau is a landscape of boulders, thin grass, small lakes and wind, and in clear weather the view is vast, running across the plains of the Beira to the Spanish border.

  3. 3. Manteigas

    Manteigas lies at the bottom of a valley that was cut by a glacier. The Zêzere river runs along the floor of a great U-shaped trough, with granite walls on either side rising to the plateau of the Serra da Estrela, and it is one of the clearest examples of glacial scenery in the Iberian Peninsula. The town clings to the lower slopes, a tight cluster of stone houses with slate roofs, steep narrow lanes and the smell of woodsmoke in winter. At under a thousand metres, it is a gateway rather than a summit, and it is the best base from which to explore Portugal's highest mountains.

  4. 4. Gouveia

    Gouveia is where the Serra da Estrela stops being a map feature and turns into somewhere people live. It is an old granite town on the western flank of the mountains, with solid stone houses, a mid-sized main square and a long history of sheep, wool and cheese. At the end of a day crossing Portugal's highest mainland range, it makes a good place to get off the bike, because the climate here is gentler than up on the plateau and the evenings are mild.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Covilha to Torre

Covilha → Torre climbs about 1993 m in 13 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Torre to Manteigas

Torre → Manteigas drops about 1993 m over 14 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Manteigas to Gouveia

Manteigas → Gouveia is 14 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
