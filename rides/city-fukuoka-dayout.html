Why this ride

324 km from Fukuoka to Fukuoka, about 5 h 54 of riding. The stops in between: Nakatsu-miya, Okitsu-miya Yohaisho, Onga river Pumping Station and Mietsu Naval Dock. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fukuoka

    Fukuoka is the big city of Kyushu, home to about one and a half million people, and it feels closer to Seoul and Shanghai than to Tokyo in the way it turns its face to the sea. The harbour of Hakata has been a gateway to the continent for two thousand years, and a gold seal found nearby, dated to the first century AD, is said to record a gift from the Chinese emperor. In 1274 and 1281 the Mongols landed here with huge fleets, and the stone wall built to resist them survives in places.

  2. 2. Nakatsu-miya

    Munakata Taisha is not one shrine but three, spread across the sea off northern Kyushu, and Nakatsu-miya is the middle one. The boat leaves from the little harbour of Kōnominato and takes roughly a quarter of an hour, crossing water that was once the main sea lane between Japan and the Korean peninsula. Shrines were built at three points along that lane to protect sailors, and the middle one honours the goddess Tagitsuhime.

  3. 3. Okitsu-miya Yohaisho

    Some holy places are meant to be seen from a distance. Off the coast of Munakata, in Fukuoka Prefecture on the island of Kyushu, the island of Okinoshima rises from the Genkai Sea about sixty kilometres offshore. It is a sacred island where rituals were performed from the fourth to the ninth century, and tens of thousands of offerings, including mirrors, jewels and gold rings, were left on its rocks. Ordinary visitors are not allowed to land, so on the island of Oshima, closer to the mainland, stands the Okitsu-miya Yohaisho, a hall from which worshippers can pray towards Okinoshima across the sea.

  4. 4. Onga river Pumping Station

    Steel needs water, a great deal of it, and in the early twentieth century the new Imperial Steel Works at Yawata in northern Kyushu needed more than the local streams could provide. The answer was this pumping station on the Onga river at Nakama, built in 1910, which drew water from the river and sent it through pipelines to the steel works. Remarkably, it is still in operation more than a century later. The red brick building with its tall windows is part of the UNESCO listing of Sites of Japan's Meiji Industrial Revolution, alongside shipyards, coal mines and iron works that tell the story of Japan's dramatic transformation into an industrial power.

  5. 5. Mietsu Naval Dock

    The Mietsu Naval Dock lies on the Saga Plain, where the Chikugo river flows into the Ariake Sea. It is a place that easily gets overlooked: a modest dry dock and a few stone structures among reeds and creeks. Yet UNESCO listed it in 2015 among the sites of Japan's Meiji industrial revolution, and the reason is the Saga domain's ambition. In the 1850s, under its daimyo Nabeshima Naomasa, Saga set about learning Western shipbuilding, cannon casting and steam technology faster than almost any other domain in Japan, and Mietsu was where the navy was trained and its ships were built and repaired.
