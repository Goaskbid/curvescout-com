  1. 1. Kelowna

    Kelowna spreads along the eastern shore of Okanagan Lake, a long, narrow lake in a dry valley of southern British Columbia, and the sun is its great asset. The Okanagan is Canada's fruit and wine country, with orchards of apples, cherries, peaches and apricots, and vineyards stretching along the hillsides above the water. The name comes from a Syilx word for grizzly bear, and the Syilx people have lived here for thousands of years.

  2. 2. Penticton

    Penticton is pinched between two lakes, Okanagan to the north and Skaha to the south, joined by a short river channel that becomes a slow parade of inflatable tubes on hot summer days. The city of nearly forty thousand people has beaches at both ends, a downtown with good cafes and a long lakeside promenade, and it serves as the main town of the South Okanagan, a dry valley of orchards, vineyards and sagebrush hills that feels more like California than the rest of Canada.

  3. 3. Osoyoos

    The hottest summers in Canada happen in this little corner of British Columbia, where the Okanagan Valley reaches the US border. Osoyoos sits on a narrow neck of land almost cutting Osoyoos Lake in two, and the lake is often described as the warmest in the country. Around it, the hills are covered in sagebrush, antelope-brush and bunchgrass, home to rattlesnakes, scorpions and other creatures you would not expect in Canada. The area is often called Canada's pocket desert, though scientists prefer to describe it as shrub-steppe.

  4. 4. Vernon

    Kalamalka Lake is the reason to slow down when you reach Vernon. Just south of the city it lies in a long trough between dry hills, and on a sunny day its water shifts from deep blue to bright turquoise and jade green, colours caused by calcium carbonate crystals in the water catching the light. Locals call it the lake of many colours, and from the viewpoint on Highway 97 above its northern end it looks almost tropical, which is a strange thought in interior British Columbia.
