Why this ride

245 km from Tivissa to Tivissa, about 4 h 13 of riding. It goes by way of Abric de les Llibreres, Calçaes del matá and back through Abric de les Llibreres. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cova del Racó d'en Perdigó

    Joan Miró painted his farm. The great Catalan artist's family owned a farmhouse at Mont-roig del Camp, a few kilometres from the coast of Tarragona, and he came here every year from his youth, and in 1921 and 1922 produced there the large painting called The Farm, which captured the landscape of rocks, carob trees, chickens and tools with an obsessive tenderness. The village, sixteen kilometres from the Cova del Racó d'en Perdigó, is a reminder that this corner of Catalonia has been seen by artists as well as by hunters.

  2. 2. Abric de les Llibreres

    The Montsià range rises abruptly from the coastal plain at the southern tip of Catalonia, a long, rocky ridge of limestone that separates the Ebro delta from the inland valleys. Around its foot lie the villages of Santa Bàrbara, Masdenverge and Ulldecona, surrounded by olive groves, some of them with ancient trees hundreds of years old, their trunks twisted into extraordinary shapes. The Abric de les Llibreres is one of the prehistoric rock shelters in this area, part of the UNESCO-listed rock art of the Mediterranean Basin.

  3. 3. Calçaes del matá

    Ares del Maestrat sits on a crag so steep that the village seems glued to the rock, and below it, in the Gasulla gorge, the Cingle de la Mola Remigia holds some of the most famous Levantine paintings in Spain, scenes of archers, hunters and even an execution. Calçaes del Matà is a shelter in the same broad landscape near Albocàsser, and with the Gasulla and Valltorta sites it belongs to the World Heritage Site of Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin. The shelters here are reached on foot, and guided visits can be booked through the local museums.
