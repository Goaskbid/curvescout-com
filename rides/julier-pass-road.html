Why this ride

A 66 km one-way ride from near Tiefencastel, about 1 h 09 of riding. It goes by way of Savognin and Julier Pass. The highest point is about 2,450 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Grand Tour of Switzerland

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2284 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance. Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Tiefencastel

    Tiefencastel sits at the bottom of a deep, narrow fold in the Graubünden mountains, at the point where the Albula river meets the Julia, and its name, the deep castle, is accurate in a geographical sense. The village is small, with a little baroque church on a rock above the river, and its importance is mostly as a junction: roads and railways from Chur, Davos, the Engadine and the Surselva meet here. For a motorcyclist, it is the start of the Julier Pass, one of the oldest crossings of the Alps, and the entry to a landscape that rises steadily from woodland to bare rock.

  2. 2. Savognin

    Savognin is the main village of the Surses, or Oberhalbstein, the high valley that climbs from Tiefencastel toward the Julier Pass. It lies at about 1,200 metres, in a broad green basin with forest on either side, and it has the feel of a place that has lived off the road for a long time. The people speak Romansh, the old Latin-derived language of the Graubünden, and the road signs and village names have a gentle, rolling quality that sets them apart from German-speaking Switzerland.

  3. 3. Julier Pass

    The Julier is the pass that stays open. At 2,284 metres, it carries the main road between Chur and the Upper Engadin, and the cantonal snow crews work to keep it passable through most of the winter, which makes it unusual among Swiss crossings of this height. That reliability is why it has been a trade road since Roman times, when merchants carried goods north and south over the same saddle, and why it was recorded in travellers' accounts for centuries.

  4. 4. Silvaplana

    Silvaplana is where the Upper Engadine opens out into water and wind. The village sits on the edge of a lake of the same name, at around 1,800 metres, between the larger resort of St. Moritz and the little village of Sils, and above it the high peaks of the Bernina and Albula ranges hold glaciers in their cirques. The light here is famously clear, an effect of altitude and dry air that has drawn painters and thinkers for generations.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Tiefencastel to Savognin

Tiefencastel → Savognin is 9 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Savognin to Julier Pass

Savognin → Julier Pass climbs about 2284 m in 21 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Julier Pass to Silvaplana

Julier Pass → Silvaplana drops about 2284 m over 7 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
