Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Gqeberha

    Gqeberha is the name that Port Elizabeth took in 2021, an isiXhosa word usually linked to the Gqeberha River, the stream that runs into Algoa Bay. The change still divides opinion, and you will hear both names on the street, but the city that carries them is the same big, breezy, workmanlike place on the Eastern Cape coast. It forms the heart of the Nelson Mandela Bay metropolitan area, with a car industry that has employed generations, a port, and a waterfront of long pale beaches that seem to go on forever.

  2. 2. back through Kareedouw

    Kareedouw is a farm town in the folds of the Eastern Cape, and its quiet is the first thing a rider notices after the long road. It lies on Route 62, the scenic alternative to the N2 coastal highway, in a valley between the Tsitsikamma and Kouga mountains, about forty kilometres inland from the coast near Humansdorp. The surrounding land is a patchwork of orchards, with apples and pears grown in the long valley called the Langkloof, and in spring the white and pink blossom turns the slopes into a soft cloud. In late summer and autumn the fruit is picked, and trucks carry it down to the packhouses.

  3. 3. back through Oudtshoorn

    Oudtshoorn owes its elegant sandstone buildings to the feathers of a very large and bad-tempered bird. In the years before the First World War, ostrich feathers were fashionable in Europe and America, and farmers in the Little Karoo made fortunes supplying them. Merchants built grand houses known as feather palaces, with fine stonework and ornate interiors, and some of them still stand. The boom collapsed around 1914, when fashion changed and the war disrupted trade, but ostrich farming continues, now for meat, leather and tourism.
