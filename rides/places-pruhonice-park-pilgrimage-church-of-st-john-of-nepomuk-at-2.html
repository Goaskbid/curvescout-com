Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Průhonice Park

    Průhonice is a village on the south-eastern edge of Prague, close enough to the capital that you can hear the city's hum, and yet its park feels like a separate country. The park was shaped from the 1880s by Count Arnošt Emanuel Silva-Tarouca, a passionate plantsman who worked with the landscape designer Adolf Vaněk to create a naturalistic landscape of lakes, meadows and woodland. He collected trees and shrubs from across the temperate world, and the park is especially known for its rhododendrons, which bloom in a riot of colour around May.

  2. 2. Kutná Hora: Historical Town Centre with th

    Silver made Kutná Hora. In the late thirteenth century rich veins were found in the hills here, and within a few decades this small town east of Prague had become one of the richest places in medieval Europe. The Bohemian kings minted their silver groschen here, in the building known as the Italian Court, and it was money from these mines that helped finance the reign of Wenceslas II and later Charles IV. The result is a town that has a cathedral's worth of ambition packed into a small hillside setting.

  3. 3. Pilgrimage Church of St John of Nepomuk at

    On a low wooded rise called Zelená hora, Green Mountain, just outside Žďár nad Sázavou, stands one of the strangest and most beautiful churches in Central Europe. The architect was Jan Blažej Santini-Aichel, a Prague builder of Italian descent who loved to mix Gothic forms with Baroque drama, and here he built a pilgrimage church to St John of Nepomuk in the 1720s, shortly before the saint was even canonised. Everything in it plays on the number five: a star-shaped ground plan, five gates, five altars, five-pointed geometry repeated in the cloister around it. The reason is the legend that five stars appeared over the Vltava when John's body was thrown into the river in Prague, and the saint's tongue, said to have been found uncorrupted, is the other motif you will spot once you know to look for it. UNESCO listed it in 1994.
