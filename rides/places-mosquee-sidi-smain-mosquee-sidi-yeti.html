Why this ride

78 km from Houmt El Souk to Houmt El Souk, about 1 h 21 of riding. It goes by way of Zone Temlel, Mosquée Sidi Yeti and back through Zone Temlel. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mosquée Sidi Smain

    Close to the coast road on Djerba's north side, a few kilometres from Houmt El Souk, the mosque of Sidi Smain is one of many small religious buildings on the island, and like most of them it is better enjoyed as part of the landscape than as an isolated monument. Djerba is a flat island of olive groves, palm gardens and low white houses, and the mosques here come in two types: the large village ones with thick buttresses, and the small neighbourhood ones that look a bit like lime-washed cubes with a dome on top.

  2. 2. Zone Temlel

    Midoun is the second town of Djerba, and Temlel lies just beside it, a rural zone of walled plots, olive trees, palms and whitewashed houses in the east of the island. It is the kind of place that a guidebook would skip, and yet it is exactly what the island's World Heritage listing is about. In 2023 UNESCO recognised Djerba as an outstanding example of how a community lived on an island with no rivers, by dividing the land into small family holdings, each with its well, its cistern, its olive press or oven, and its orchard, grouped in neighbourhoods called houmas, around a mosque and a market.

  3. 3. Mosquée Sidi Yeti

    Djerba is the sort of island where you can ride for an hour and never see anything higher than a date palm. The mosque of Sidi Yati sits on the flat interior, a short run from Erriadh, which is the nearest proper town, with Midoun and the capital Houmt Souk a little further out. Everything here is low, white and thick-walled, and the mosques follow the same rule: buttressed, squat, almost fortress-like, with small windows and none of the soaring minarets you might expect from the mainland.
