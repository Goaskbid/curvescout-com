Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Yuso Monastery and Monastic Kitchen Garden

    What does a monastery eat? That is a question that visitors rarely ask, but at Yuso, in the valley of San Millán de la Cogolla, the answer lies in the kitchen garden, the huerta, that stretches beside the walls. The monastery, rebuilt from the sixteenth century, is a large, plain-faced building of stone and brick, run today by Augustinian Recollects, and it was listed as a World Heritage site by UNESCO in 1997 together with the older monastery of Suso above. The garden is a reminder that monasteries were also farms, and that the monks' discipline included hoeing and watering as much as chanting.

  2. 2. Archaeological Site of Atapuerca

    Atapuerca does not look like much from the road: low, scrubby hills, a railway cutting, oaks and gorse. But the limestone caves here have yielded some of the most important remains in the history of humanity. In the Gran Dolina, archaeologists found bones of Homo antecessor, around 850,000 years old, among the oldest human fossils in Western Europe, and in the Sima de los Huesos, a deep pit, they found the remains of at least twenty-eight individuals about 430,000 years old. UNESCO listed the site in 2000.

  3. 3. Cathedrale de Vitoria-Gasteiz

    Vitoria-Gasteiz is the capital of the Basque Country and possibly its most underrated city. It sits on a high plateau, the Llanada Alavesa, surrounded by low mountains and wide farmland, and its medieval old town climbs a small hill in the shape of an almond, with the old cathedral of Santa María at the top. This is the cathedral the label refers to, a Gothic church begun in the thirteenth century as both a temple and part of the town's defences.
