Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Changchuan Tomb No. 1

    Changchuan Tomb No. 1 is among the painted tombs of the ancient Koguryo kingdom, a state that ruled northern Korea and much of Manchuria from the first centuries BC until 668. The tomb itself lies on the Chinese bank, in a valley of fields and low hills where the Koguryo kings had one of their capitals.

  2. 2. Guonei City

    Guonei was the first capital of Koguryo on the Yalu, the city to which the court moved in the first years of the Common Era, and it grew into a significant centre. The name means something like the inner kingdom, and the city lay on a flat terrace by the river, protected by mountains on three sides. Its stone walls, built to enclose the palace, the markets and the houses, were several kilometres long, and traces of them can still be seen in the streets of modern Ji'an, which has been built over the old town.

  3. 3. Wunu Mountain City

    Wunu Mountain City is a ruined fortress on a flat-topped crag in Liaoning province, in the green hills of northeastern China, and it was probably the first capital of the Goguryeo kingdom. Before that kingdom grew into a power that rivalled Chinese dynasties, its founders chose a mountain stronghold above a river for safety. Cliffs shelter the plateau on almost every side, and the builders closed the few gaps with stone walls. Inside, there are traces of buildings, a spring, a soldier's quarters and a platform that may have served as a lookout. It is part of the Capital Cities and Tombs of the Ancient Koguryo Kingdom, listed by UNESCO in 2004, which takes in sites around Huanren and Ji'an.
