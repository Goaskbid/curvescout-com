Why this ride

128 km from La Paz to Coroico, about 1 h 44 of riding. On the way it passes La Cumbre and Yolosa. The highest point is about 4,650 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

North Yungas Road

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 4650 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance. Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Rider note

Check the pass is open before you commit to it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. La Paz

    La Paz sits in a canyon, and that is the first strange thing about it. The city is the seat of Bolivia's government, and it lies at around three thousand six hundred metres, with the poorer and larger city of El Alto perched on the rim above at over four thousand. From the rim you look down into a bowl of red-brown buildings spilling down the slopes, with the great snowy peak of Illimani, over six thousand four hundred metres, standing over the southern edge like a guardian. The air is thin, so arrive early, rest a day and drink coca tea, which locals swear by and which really does help a little.

  2. 2. La Cumbre

    La Cumbre is the top of the world's most famous bad road. At about 4,650 metres, on a windswept pass in the Cordillera Real a short ride from La Paz, the tarmac gives way to the old North Yungas Road, which drops from this bare, cold, grey landscape to the steamy green jungle valleys about three and a half thousand metres below. The air is thin enough that a short walk from the bike leaves you breathless, and small roadside shrines hold the offerings that drivers leave for a safe descent.

  3. 3. Yolosa

    Yolosa is the bottom of the Death Road, and the first place where you notice that you have stopped being cold. After hours of descent from the freezing pass, your jacket suddenly feels absurd. The air is warm and wet, the vegetation is thick and loud with insects, and the village sits at the junction where the old road meets the new highway, at about 1,200 metres in the lower Yungas. It is a small, dusty and unpretentious place, and it exists mainly as a crossroads.

  4. 4. Coroico

    Coroico hangs on a green ridge in the Yungas, the steep, cloud-wrapped country where the Bolivian Andes fall away toward the Amazon basin. The town sits at about 1,700 metres, which is warm and humid after the thin air of La Paz, and the whole landscape smells of wet earth, coffee and citrus. For a rider arriving from the capital, it is the end of one of the most famous roads in the world, a place where you can finally breathe, sit in the shade and watch the clouds roll up the valley.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

La Paz to La Cumbre

La Paz → La Cumbre climbs about 4650 m in 24 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

La Cumbre to Yolosa

La Cumbre → Yolosa drops about 4650 m over 44 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Yolosa to Coroico

Yolosa → Coroico is 5 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
