Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mount Robson

    Mount Robson is the highest peak in the Canadian Rockies, at 3,954 metres, and the way it appears from the Yellowhead Highway is something you remember. The mountain rises about three thousand metres above the valley floor in a series of huge, banded cliffs and glaciers, and it is often wrapped in cloud, which makes the moment when it clears all the more dramatic. The Yellowhead Highway, Highway 16, runs right beneath it, and a visitor centre and viewpoint give an unobstructed look on a clear day.

  2. 2. McBride

    McBride sits in the Robson Valley, a broad, quiet trench between the Rocky Mountains and the Cariboo Mountains, with the Fraser River flowing through it on its way north before turning west and south. The village has a population of just a few hundred, and the feel of a place where everybody knows everybody. The valley is unusual because it holds an inland temperate rainforest, one of the few in the world, where western redcedar and hemlock grow in the damp, cool conditions created by mountains on both sides.

  3. 3. Prince George

    Prince George sits where the Nechako River meets the Fraser, in the middle of British Columbia, and it is the biggest city for hundreds of kilometres in any direction. It is a working town of about sixty-five thousand, built on forestry, with sawmills and pulp mills that sometimes give the air a distinct smell, and it has the practical, straightforward character of a place that supplies a vast northern region. After the long run from Jasper along the Yellowhead Highway, it is a place to take a deep breath and appreciate the services.
