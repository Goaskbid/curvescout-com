Why this ride

A one-way ride of 432 km, Bad Doberan to Ribnitz-Damgarten - roughly 8 h 22 in the saddle. It goes by way of Kühlungsborn sea road, Rerik coast view, Satow inland bends and Bad Doberan minster. The coast is the point of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bad Doberan minster

    Bad Doberan has one of the finest brick Gothic churches in Europe, and it was built by monks. The minster, the Doberaner Münster, was the church of a Cistercian abbey founded in the twelfth century, and the present building, finished in the fourteenth century, is a tall, plain, pale-red brick basilica whose clear lines and soaring interior are the essence of the Baltic Gothic style. The abbey was dissolved at the Reformation, but the church survived, and the dukes of Mecklenburg were buried inside. Its stained glass, carved altars and a huge cross from the Middle Ages are still there.

  2. 2. Kühlungsborn sea road

    Kühlungsborn is the largest seaside resort on the German Baltic coast, and from the top of the sea road you see why. A promenade runs for several kilometres along the beach, backed by rows of white villas in the old seaside style, with turrets, balconies and carved woodwork, many of them built in the decades before the First World War, when this was a fashionable resort for the Mecklenburg court and for visitors from Berlin. The beach is wide, pale and fine-grained, and the sea beyond is calm and shallow.

  3. 3. Rerik coast view

    Rerik lies on the Salzhaff, a sheltered lagoon on the Mecklenburg Baltic coast, separated from the open sea by a long narrow peninsula called Wustrow, and the viewpoint looks across a landscape of water, reeds and low hills. It is a place of birds: in spring and autumn migrating geese, cranes and waders fill the lagoon, and the quiet shore draws birdwatchers with binoculars. The air smells of salt, seaweed and grass, and the light is wide and pale.

  4. 4. Satow inland bends

    Satow is a village on the long, low plateau behind the Mecklenburg Baltic coast, a few kilometres inland from the sea and well off the usual tourist track. The town of Satow-Oberhagen has about six thousand people, and the countryside around it is a mix of cornfields, small woods and the avenues of old lime and chestnut trees that line so many roads in Mecklenburg. Following them is half the pleasure of riding here.

  6. 6. Ribnitz-Damgarten fuel gate

    Ribnitz-Damgarten is really two towns that were stitched together by a river. The Recknitz, sluggish and brown as it slides toward the Saaler Bodden lagoon, was for centuries a border: Ribnitz belonged to Mecklenburg, Damgarten to Pomerania, and the two places looked across the water with different rulers and different habits. They merged only in 1950, and the bridge that joins them is still the most important thing in town. It sits on the old main road to the Baltic coast, at the neck of the Fischland-Darß-Zingst peninsula, so nearly everyone riding toward the beaches has to pass through.

  7. 7. Ahrenshoop coast art stop

    Ahrenshoop is a village where artists came first and tourists followed. In the 1890s painters from Berlin, Munich and elsewhere found this thin strip of land between the Baltic and the Saaler Bodden, drawn by the light, the sea, the reed-thatched cottages and the wide sky, and they founded an artists' colony that still defines the place. The landscape painter Paul Müller-Kaempff was among the first to settle, and the village has been a haven for creative people ever since.

  8. 8. Darß forest road edge

    The Darß forest is the wildest piece of woodland on the German Baltic coast, and the road that skirts its edge between Born and Prerow runs along the border of a landscape that has been left largely to itself. The Darß Urwald, as it is called, is a mix of beech, alder, pine and birch growing on old dune ridges and wet hollows, where fallen trunks are left to rot and the undergrowth is thick with fern. It belongs to the Western Pomerania Lagoon Area National Park, and walkers follow marked paths among trees draped in moss.

  9. 9. Stralsund old town

    Stralsund sits on a strip of land between the Baltic and a chain of shallow lagoons, facing the island of Rügen across a narrow sound, and its old town is one of the finest assemblies of brick Gothic in Europe. UNESCO listed it in 2002 together with Wismar, and the reason is plain as soon as you walk onto the Old Market: the great town hall with its ornate, almost theatrical gabled facade, and across the square the enormous church of St Nicholas, built when the town was a rich member of the Hanseatic League and wanted everyone to know it.
