Why this ride

541 km from Torredonjimeno to Torredonjimeno, about 9 h 42 of riding. The stops in between: Baeza, Abrigo del.melgar, Cañada de la Cruz and back through Abrigo del.melgar. It tops out around 1,400 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Cueva del Encajero

    Start here.

    Olives, and then more olives. Riding west out of Jaén towards Torredonjimeno you cross one of the most single-minded agricultural landscapes in Europe, a rolling grid of grey-green trees running over every hill to the horizon, broken only by white farmhouses and the occasional oil mill. Somewhere among these low hills near Villardompardo, the Cueva del Encajero is a small rock shelter with traces of prehistoric painting, one of the many scattered sites that make up the World Heritage listing of rock art in Spain's Mediterranean basin.

    Read the complete Cueva del Encajero write-up.

  2. 2. Baeza

    70 km in.

    Baeza is a town of honey-coloured stone set on a ridge above a sea of olive trees. From its edge, the view drops over the Guadalquivir valley to the dark line of the Sierra Mágina, and in every direction the hills are covered with orderly rows of olives, millions of them, the source of more olive oil than almost anywhere on earth. The town's Renaissance palaces, churches and squares, together with those of neighbouring Úbeda, make up a World Heritage site.

    Read the complete Baeza write-up.

  3. 3. Abrigo del.melgar

    125 km in.

    Quesada is a white town at the foot of the Sierra de Cazorla, in the province of Jaén, surrounded by an ocean of olive trees. The groves stretch to the horizon in every direction, a landscape that makes Jaén the world's largest producer of olive oil. Behind the town, the mountains rise steeply, with pine forest and limestone cliffs, and the Guadalquivir river has its source not far away. The Abrigo del Melgar lies in the hills just outside the town.

    Read the complete Abrigo del.melgar write-up.

  4. 4. Cañada de la Cruz

    270 km in.

    The Cañada de la Cruz is a high valley or drovers' pasture in the Segura mountains; cañada is the word for the traditional livestock routes and grazing grounds used by shepherds moving their flocks between summer and winter pastures, and this part of Spain has a long history of transhumance.

    Read the complete Cañada de la Cruz write-up.
