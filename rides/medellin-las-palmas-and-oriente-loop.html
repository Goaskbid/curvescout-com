Why this ride

A 112 km loop that starts and ends near Medellín, about 1 h 48 of riding. It goes by way of Alto de Las Palmas, El Retiro and Guatape. The highest point is about 2,550 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Medellin Las Palmas and Oriente Loop

Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it. The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2550 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Medellin

    Medellín is a city in a bowl. It fills the Aburrá Valley in the Colombian Andes, with houses climbing the steep sides in tight brick and tin-roofed rows, and the mild climate at around 1,500 metres has earned it the nickname of the city of eternal spring. For decades the world knew it for the drug trade and its violence, but over the last twenty years it has changed itself with a good metro, cable cars that link the poorest hillside districts to the centre, library parks and outdoor escalators in the former no-go neighbourhood of Comuna 13. The sculptor and painter Fernando Botero, born here in 1932, gave the city a collection of his plump bronze figures in a plaza in the centre.

  2. 2. Alto de Las Palmas

    Medellín lies in a narrow valley at about 1,500 metres, and the road out of it to the east climbs sharply to the Alto de Las Palmas, 2,550 metres above sea level, in a long series of curves above the city. At the top, a viewpoint looks back over the whole Aburrá Valley, with the city's towers laid out in a ribbon far below and the cloud often curling over the ridges. It is one of the classic views of the city, and on a clear evening, when the lights come on, it is hard to leave.

  3. 3. El Retiro

    El Retiro is the sort of Antioquian mountain town where people from Medellín come to eat lunch and then pretend to consider moving. It lies on the plateau east of the city, in cool, green country at over two thousand metres, with a tidy square, a church, a handful of craft shops and a mood of unhurried prosperity. The surrounding hills are a patchwork of farms, flower nurseries and weekend homes, and the climate is a relief after the heat of the valley, though it can be damp and misty.

  4. 4. Guatape

    Guatapé is a lakeside town in the hills east of Medellín, and its fame rests on two things: the colour of its streets and a giant rock. The houses are decorated along the lower walls with painted reliefs known as zócalos, showing scenes of family life, animals, flowers and local trades, and every street is a gallery in bright blues, reds, greens and yellows. The effect is playful and the town takes it seriously, with each building competing to look better than its neighbours.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Medellin to Alto de Las Palmas

Medellin → Alto de Las Palmas climbs about 2550 m in 14 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Alto de Las Palmas to El Retiro

Alto de Las Palmas → El Retiro drops about 2550 m over 15 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

El Retiro to Guatape

El Retiro → Guatape is 53 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Guatape to Medellin

Guatape → Medellin is 58 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
