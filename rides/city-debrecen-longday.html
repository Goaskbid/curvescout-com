Why this ride

229 km from Debrecen to Debrecen, about 3 h 49 of riding. It goes by way of Tokaj Wine Region Historic Cultural Landsc, Tokaj Wine Region, Oremus Cellars and Tolcsva Wine Museum Cellars.

The stops

  1. 1. Debrecen

    Start here.

    Debrecen is the capital of the Great Hungarian Plain, a city of two hundred thousand people where the horizon is perfectly flat and the sky does most of the work. For centuries it was the stronghold of Hungarian Calvinism, so much so that it was nicknamed the Calvinist Rome, and its yellow Great Church, the largest Protestant church in the country, dominates the main square. It was here, in 1849, that Lajos Kossuth declared Hungary's independence from the Habsburgs, and for a short time Debrecen served as the capital of the revolution.

    Read the complete Debrecen write-up.

  2. 2. Tokaj Wine Region Historic Cultural Landsc

    94 km in.

    Some wine regions are famous for a grape. Tokaj is famous for rot. Here in north-eastern Hungary, where the volcanic Zemplén hills meet the Great Plain and the Bodrog flows into the Tisza, autumn mists from the rivers encourage noble rot on the grapes, shrivelling them and concentrating their sugar. The berries, picked one by one, become aszú, and the sweet wine made from them has been celebrated for centuries. A line attributed to Louis XIV calls it the wine of kings and the king of wines.

    Read the complete Tokaj Wine Region Historic Cultural Landsc write-up.

  3. 3. Tokaj Wine Region

    Same place as the previous stop.

    Tokaj is the place where the world's first classified wine region was drawn on a map, in 1737, long before Burgundy or Bordeaux got round to the idea. The wine country of Tokaj-Hegyalja lies in the far north-east of Hungary, where the Zemplén hills, old volcanoes worn down into rounded cones, fall away to the plain at the meeting of the Tisza and Bodrog rivers. Tarcal and Tokaj sit at the foot of the slope, and the vines climb the south-facing hills above them, in a patchwork of terraces, stone walls and small plots.

    Read the complete Tokaj Wine Region write-up.

  4. 4. Oremus Cellars

    115 km in.

    Tolcsva is a quiet village in the Tokaj wine region of northeastern Hungary, on the slopes of the Zemplén hills above the Bodrog river. The Oremus cellars here are among the most famous in the region: a network of tunnels cut into the volcanic rock over centuries, their walls black with the noble mould that thrives in the damp, steady cold. The estate has been owned since the 1990s by the family behind Vega Sicilia, one of Spain's most celebrated wineries, and it has become a showcase for the revival of Tokaji after the communist decades.

    Read the complete Oremus Cellars write-up.

  5. 5. Tolcsva Wine Museum Cellars

    Same place as the previous stop.

    Under the village of Tolcsva, in the foothills of the Zemplén mountains, there are wine cellars cut into volcanic rock. The Tokaj region in the north-east corner of Hungary is one of the oldest wine landscapes in Europe, and UNESCO listed it in 2002 as the Tokaj Wine Region Historic Cultural Landscape. The soil is volcanic, the hills are covered in vines, and the villages are full of cellars that run deep into the soft tuff. The temperature underground stays cool and constant, which is exactly what sweet wine needs.
