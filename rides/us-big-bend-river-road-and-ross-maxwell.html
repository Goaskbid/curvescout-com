  1. 1. Alpine

    Alpine lies in the high desert of West Texas, at roughly 4,500 feet, in a basin between the Davis Mountains to the north and the long, empty country that runs south toward Big Bend. It is a railroad and ranching town of just under six thousand that has quietly become the logical base for anyone heading into the park, and also a college town, because Sul Ross State University sits on a hill above the main street. The result is better coffee than you would expect at the end of a long highway.

  2. 2. Presidio

    Presidio shares its river and most of its life with the Mexican town across the bridge. The Rio Grande runs along the southern edge, Ojinaga stands on the opposite bank, and the border crossing between them is the busiest thing in the area. West Texas is huge, and Presidio is a rare green strip in it, where the Rio Conchos joins the Rio Grande. This confluence, La Junta de los Rios, has been farmed for centuries, and the Spanish gave the place its name, which simply means fortress, after the military post that once stood here.
