  1. 1. Quito

    Quito sits almost on the equator and almost two miles up, and the two facts together explain a lot. At about 2,850 metres the air is thin, the sun is fierce, and the days are mild whatever the calendar says, so the city lives in a permanent early spring. It stretches for kilometres along a narrow Andean valley, with Pichincha volcano rising on the western side and, on clear mornings, the snow cone of Cotopaxi and other peaks visible to the south and east.

  2. 2. Otavalo

    Saturday is market day and the whole town changes shape. Otavalo, a town of some thirty thousand people in the northern Ecuadorian Andes, is home to the Otavalo people, an indigenous Kichwa community famous for its weaving, and the Plaza de Ponchos fills with stalls of textiles, hats, hand-knitted jumpers, beads and paintings. The men wear their hair in a long braid and the women dress in embroidered blouses, blue wrap skirts and strings of golden beads, and many of the merchants speak Kichwa among themselves.

  3. 3. Ibarra

    Ibarra is called the White City, and on a clear morning with the Imbabura volcano standing behind its low roofs you can see why. It lies in a wide northern Andean valley at around 2,200 metres, the capital of Imbabura province and a city of about a hundred and thirty thousand. Its colonial centre was founded in the early seventeenth century and rebuilt after a severe earthquake in 1868, which is why the streets run in neat lines and the squares feel calm and open.
