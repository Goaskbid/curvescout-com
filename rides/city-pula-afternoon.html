Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Pula

    Pula's amphitheatre is the first thing you see and the last thing you forget. Built in the first century AD, in the reign of Vespasian, it stands on the edge of the harbour with its outer wall almost entirely intact, three storeys of arches rising above the water, and it is one of the six largest Roman arenas still standing. Gladiators once fought here before a crowd of thousands, and today the arena hosts concerts and a film festival, with the bay glittering through the arches behind the stage.

  2. 2. Episcopal Complex of the Euphrasian Basili

    Poreč sits on a little limestone peninsula on the western coast of Istria, and the old town still follows the grid the Romans laid down, with the Decumanus running straight down the middle. The Euphrasian Basilica is tucked a few lanes from the main street, behind a quiet atrium, and it is easy to walk past if you are looking for something grand from the outside. Inside is the reason it earned its UNESCO listing: a sixth-century church, built under Bishop Euphrasius, that kept its Byzantine mosaics through a millennium and a half of earthquakes, Venetian rule and tourism.
