Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Roseburg

    Roseburg is the main town of the Umpqua valley in southern Oregon, with around twenty-two thousand people, an old timber economy and a position right where Interstate 5 crosses the South Umpqua River. For many years the city was proud to call itself the Timber Capital of the Nation, and mills, logging trucks and sawdust were everyday companions. The industry has shrunk, but the town still carries a rugged working feel, with a pleasant old downtown, murals, and several breweries.

  5. 5. Gold Hill

    Gold Hill lives up to its name in a modest way. A gold strike in the early 1850s brought miners into this stretch of the Rogue River valley in southern Oregon, and the town grew where the river cuts through a narrow stretch of rock, with a small downtown of brick storefronts and a bridge across the water. Today it is a quiet place of about twelve hundred people, with a diner or two, a hardware store and the feel of a place that lets the interstate pass on the other side of the hill. Locals fish for salmon and steelhead in the Rogue, and rafting companies run the stretches of river below.
