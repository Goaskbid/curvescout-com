Why this ride

From Bicorp it runs 97 km to Bicorp; allow about 1 h 40 of riding. The stops in between: Cueva del Cerro, Barranc de la falaguera- Abric I and back through Cueva del Cerro. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abric del Zuro

    In July 2012, a huge fire swept through the hills around Cortes de Pallás, not far from Bicorp, and burned tens of thousands of hectares of pine forest, one of the worst wildfires in the history of the Valencian Community. Fire is a fact of life in these dry Mediterranean mountains, and the landscape tells the story: the pines that regrow quickly from seed, the scrub that returns within a few years, and the gaunt grey trunks that still stand on some of the slopes. It is a sobering reminder that the beauty of this country is fragile.

  2. 2. Cueva del Cerro

    Inland Valencia is a world away from the beaches on the coast. Behind the orange groves and the motorway the land rises into dry, folded mountains cut by deep gorges, where villages of a few hundred people cling to the hillsides and the pines smell of resin in the heat. Millares and Dos Aguas lie in this country, near the Júcar and Cabriel rivers, and the Cueva del Cerro is a rock shelter in the limestone, one of the many places in eastern Spain where prehistoric people left paintings on the walls.

  3. 3. Barranc de la falaguera- Abric I

    Alginet sits in the Ribera Alta, the flat, fertile plain south of Valencia where the Júcar river spreads out among orange groves, rice fields and small towns. To the west, the plain meets the first hills of the interior, low ridges of limestone covered in pine and scrub. The Barranc de la Falaguera, the ravine of the fern, cuts into these hills near Alginet, Alfarp and Benifaió.
