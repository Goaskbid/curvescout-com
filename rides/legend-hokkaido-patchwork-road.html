Why this ride

  1. 1. Asahikawa

    It has around three hundred and fifty thousand people, a long, cold winter and a reputation for ramen, furniture and sake. The city sits at the confluence of several rivers, and its wide streets and big sky have a frontier feel that suits the island.

  2. 2. Hokkaido Patchwork Road point 2

    Around Biei the land does something you rarely see in Japan: it opens up. Instead of tight valleys and forested slopes, there are long, soft hills covered in fields, each a different colour depending on the crop, wheat, potatoes, buckwheat, beans, sugar beet, so that from any rise the landscape looks like a quilt laid over a sleeping body. That is the Patchwork Road, a loose network of farm lanes north and west of Biei town, with Asahikawa a little over twenty kilometres away and the Tokachi volcanoes standing to the south-east.

  3. 3. Hokkaido Patchwork Road point 3

    A little west of Biei, the hills lean towards the Asahikawa basin and the views stretch further. From the tops of the farm roads here you can look back across the patchwork to the Tokachi range, a line of volcanic peaks, the most active of them Tokachi-dake, which often trails a thin plume of steam. In June and July the slopes still carry streaks of snow, and in the evening light the whole range turns pink while the fields below go dark.

  4. 4. Shimo-furano

    Furano sits in a long, flat valley in the middle of Hokkaido, with the Tokachi range rising to the east and the Yūbari mountains to the west, and the town calls itself the island's navel. It even holds a summer festival in honour of the claim, with residents painting faces on their bellies and dancing through the streets. That sense of humour suits the place: Furano is a friendly, relaxed farming town that has become one of Hokkaido's best-loved destinations without losing its everyday character.
