Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sturgeon Bay

    Door County is the thumb of Wisconsin, a limestone peninsula that sticks into Lake Michigan like a hitchhiker's hand, and Sturgeon Bay is the town where you come in. It is the only real city on the peninsula, with about nine thousand people, a shipyard, a downtown that climbs a hill from the water, and a ship canal that makes the northern half of Door County, in effect, an island. The canal was cut in 1881 to connect Green Bay with Lake Michigan, saving ships the long, dangerous trip around the tip, and it is crossed by a steel bridge that lifts to let freighters through. If you are held up there, watch for the long, low ships sliding past at head height.

  2. 2. Egg Harbor

    Egg Harbor is the first village of Door County that makes you stop, and it has a name with a story to match. The legend says that in the 1860s a group of boaters returning from a trip to Green Bay got into a fight over a basket of eggs, throwing them at one another until the boat was thoroughly and unmistakably scrambled. Whether or not that is true, the name stuck, and it suits a place that tends to be more playful than solemn.

  3. 3. Fish Creek

    Fish Creek feels like the postcard version of Door County, and it has the good sense to wear it lightly. The village sits on the shore of Green Bay at the edge of Peninsula State Park, its main street lined with old frame buildings, galleries, and cafes, while the harbour is dotted with boats. It was settled in the mid-nineteenth century by Alexander Noble, a pioneer who built a pier and a mill, and the town grew up around the needs of lake traffic and lumbering.

  4. 4. Sister Bay

    Sister Bay is the northern anchor of Door County's bay shore, and it wears its Scandinavian heritage with a certain amount of humour. Norwegians, Swedes, and Danes settled this part of Wisconsin in the nineteenth century, drawn by the fishing and the timber, and you can still see their influence in the names, the food, and the architecture. The most famous example is a restaurant with a sod roof on which goats graze all summer, a sight that makes most riders pull over and take a picture before they quite know why.

  5. 5. Gills Rock

    Gills Rock sits at the end of the line on Door County's northern tip, a tiny fishing village of a few houses, a maritime museum, and a harbour full of working boats. The road that has carried you up the peninsula finally runs out of room here, with Green Bay on one side and the strait beyond. That strait has a name that sells itself: Porte des Morts, or Death's Door, a stretch of water between the peninsula and Washington Island where shifting currents, shoals and sudden storms have wrecked a long list of ships. Whether the French or the Potawatomi named it is debated, but the reputation is earned.
