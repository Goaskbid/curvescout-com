Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. San Pedro de Atacama

    San Pedro de Atacama is a small adobe town in the driest desert on Earth, and the contradiction is the attraction. It lies at 2,400 metres on the edge of the Salar de Atacama, a vast salt flat, in an oasis fed by streams from the Andes. The streets are dusty and low, the walls are the colour of dry mud, and behind the town the Licancabur volcano, nearly 5,920 metres, stands as a perfect cone on the border with Bolivia. Everything here seems to lean towards the sky.

  2. 2. Socaire

    Socaire is a small oasis village in the Atacama Desert, standing at about 3,200 metres above sea level on the road from San Pedro de Atacama to the high plateau. It is one of the older settlements of the Atacameño people, and the evidence is in the green terraces of the surrounding fields, which are irrigated by channels carrying meltwater from the mountains and still grow maize, alfalfa and other crops. After the dust and rock of the salt flats below, this small green patch comes as a surprise, with poplars, fruit trees and a little white church standing out in the dry landscape.

  3. 3. Miscanti Lagoons

    Laguna Miscanti and its smaller neighbour Laguna Miñiques lie at about 4,100 metres in the Chilean Altiplano, among the high volcanoes of the Atacama, and they are some of the most beautiful lakes in northern Chile. The water is an intense deep blue, set against pale shores and dark, bare hills, with the cone of Cerro Miscanti rising to more than five thousand metres behind. The lakes are part of the Los Flamencos National Reserve, and their position, high on the plateau with nothing between them and the sky, gives them a stark, strange beauty that is hard to forget.
