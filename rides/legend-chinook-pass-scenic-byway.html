Why this ride

All-American Road. Mount Rainier, high pass, forest. From Enumclaw it runs 116 km to Tieton; allow about 2 h 07 of riding. The highest point is about 1,650 m. Chinook Pass is subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Enumclaw

    Enumclaw sits on the plateau east of Seattle where the suburbs thin out into horse paddocks, hay fields and dairy farms, with Mount Rainier standing on the horizon on any clear day. The mountain, at about 4,390 metres, is an active volcano and the highest in the Cascade range, covered in more glacier ice than any other peak in the lower forty-eight states. It looks very close from here, and it is, which is a large part of why so many riders begin the day in this town.

    Here the forest is thick and old, with Douglas fir, western red cedar and hemlock towering over the road, and the river below runs milky grey with silt ground off the Emmons Glacier, the largest glacier by area in the contiguous United States.

  3. 3. Chinook Pass Scenic Byway point 3

    Chinook Pass sits at 1,656 metres on the crest of the Cascades, and it is one of the loveliest spots on any road in Washington. Just below the summit on the west side, Tipsoo Lake lies in a little basin of meadow and subalpine fir, and on a calm morning the lake holds a perfect upside-down reflection of Mount Rainier. In August the meadows around it are thick with lupine, paintbrush and heather, and the air smells of warm grass and resin.

  4. 4. Near Tieton

    Coming down out of the Cascades on Highway 410, the forest thins, the air dries out and suddenly you are in orchard country. The contrast with the wet green mountains a few hours behind you is startling. This is the rain shadow of the Cascades, and without irrigation it would be sagebrush.
