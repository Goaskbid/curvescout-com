Ruta 40 Argentina North to South: La Quiaca to Cachi

Why this ride

A one-way ride of 884 km, La Quiaca to Cachí - roughly 12 h 09 in the saddle. The highest point is about 3,900 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. La Quiaca

    La Quiaca is the last town in Argentina, and at about 3,400 metres above sea level it feels like it. The Puna plateau here is high, cold and windswept, with a sky of hard blue and a landscape of tawny grass and bare hills, and the border with Bolivia runs along a dry riverbed on the edge of town. A bridge crosses to Villazón, a bustling Bolivian town just two kilometres away. Argentine National Route 40, the long road that runs the length of the country along the Andes for more than five thousand kilometres, ends here, and a sign at the border marks the northern terminus.

  2. 2. Humahuaca

    Humahuaca sits at almost three thousand metres in the Quebrada de Humahuaca, a long, dry, colourful valley in the far north of Argentina that UNESCO has listed as a World Heritage landscape. The valley runs for about a hundred and fifty kilometres, with the river Grande flowing between walls of red, ochre, green and violet rock, and cardón cacti standing like candelabra on the hillsides. The road through it, the Ruta 9, follows an ancient trade route used by the Incas, and before them by many peoples who carried llama caravans between the Andes and the lowlands.

  3. 3. Cachi

    Cachí sits at around 2,300 metres in the Calchaquí valleys of Salta province, and everything about it seems to have been made quiet by altitude and dryness. White adobe houses line narrow streets, the plaza is shaded by trees, and the church has a roof and doors made from the wood of the cardón, a tall columnar cactus that grows on the slopes around. The backdrop is the Nevado de Cachi, a snow-capped peak in the range that stands above 6,000 metres.
