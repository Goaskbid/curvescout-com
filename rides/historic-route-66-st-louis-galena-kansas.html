Historic Route 66: St Louis to Galena Kansas

Why this ride

From St. Louis it runs 1097 km to Galena; allow about 15 h 03 of riding. It goes by way of Cuba Missouri and Springfield Missouri. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. St Louis

    St. Louis is a river city with a very large arch in it. The Gateway Arch, designed by Eero Saarinen and finished in 1965, rises 630 feet on the west bank of the Mississippi, a catenary curve of stainless steel that is the tallest monument in the United States and one of the most recognisable. It marks the city's historic role as the gateway to the West, the starting point for settlers, trappers and explorers, including Lewis and Clark, who set out up the Missouri in 1804 from a camp not far north. At its feet, a museum tells the story of westward expansion, and a tram carries visitors to the top.

  2. 2. Cuba Missouri

    Cuba, Missouri, is a small town of about three and a half thousand people in the Ozark foothills, and it has turned itself into the Mural City of Route 66. More than a dozen big paintings cover the walls of old buildings, showing the history of the town, the railroad, the highway and the people, and the effect is cheerful and slightly surreal, like a gallery laid out along a main street. The town is said to take its name from the island of Cuba.

  3. 3. Springfield Missouri

    Springfield, Missouri, calls itself the birthplace of Route 66, and the claim has a document behind it. On 30 April 1926 officials from Springfield sent a telegram proposing that the new highway from Chicago to Los Angeles be numbered 66, and the idea was accepted. The city of about a hundred and sixty-seven thousand grew up around the railroads and the highway, and many of the old motels and diners from the golden age still stand along St. Louis Street and College Street, with their neon signs and mid-century lines.

  4. 4. Galena Kansas

    Galena, Kansas, is the first or last town on the shortest stretch of Route 66 in any state, just thirteen miles in the south-east corner of Kansas, and the town makes the most of it. The name is Latin for lead ore, and the place was founded in the 1870s when miners struck rich lead and zinc deposits in the Tri-State District, which spread across Kansas, Missouri and Oklahoma. The boom made it a rowdy town of thousands, and the legacy remains in the huge piles of mine waste, known as chat, which still stand on the horizon, and in the contaminated ground that has taken decades to clean up.
