Why this ride

A one-way ride of 335 km, Coffs Harbour to Armidale - roughly 4 h 31 in the saddle. The stops in between: Bellingen, Dorrigo and Ebor Falls. The highest point is about 1,150 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Waterfall Way

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 60 km this is Waterfall Way, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Coffs Harbour

    Coffs Harbour is where the Waterfall Way meets the sea, and the town has the easygoing, banana-and-surf character you expect from the mid-north coast of New South Wales. The Big Banana, opened in 1964 and often credited as Australia's first big roadside novelty, still stands beside the highway as a reminder of the plantations that once covered the hills. These days blueberries have mostly replaced bananas, but the sunny slopes are still there.

  2. 2. Bellingen

    Bellingen is a town that took a wrong turn somewhere and ended up far better off. It sits on the Bellinger River in the hills behind Coffs Harbour on the New South Wales north coast, a small place of timber verandahs, bookshops, galleries and cafes, with a reputation for artists, potters, organic growers and people who moved up from Sydney and never went back. On a sunny morning the main street has a laid-back air that would be dull in a more pretentious place and is simply pleasant here.

  3. 3. Dorrigo

    Dorrigo is a quiet town of about a thousand people on a high plateau in northern New South Wales, a dairy and potato district that feels cool and green compared with the coast an hour below. The plateau is the edge of the old volcanic land that rose after the great Gondwana breakup, and it still catches the rain from the Pacific. The result is a rainforest that has survived as an island of tall, dripping trees, now protected in Dorrigo National Park and listed with other east-coast reserves as the Gondwana Rainforests of Australia. A short drive from the town you can walk a boardwalk through the canopy and look down on ferns and strangler figs.

  4. 4. Ebor Falls

    Cold air is the first thing you notice at Ebor, because you have climbed up from the subtropical coast onto the New England tableland and the climate has changed with you. The falls are on the Guy Fawkes River, a short walk from the Waterfall Way, and they drop in two stages over dark basalt into a gorge thick with tree ferns, ancient-looking eucalypts and mist. After rain, the noise carries up through the forest, and the sun can throw a faint rainbow across the spray.

  5. 5. Armidale

    Armidale sits on the New England tableland at close to a thousand metres, which means it feels cooler than anywhere else for hundreds of kilometres around. It is a handsome city of about twenty-four thousand people, with two cathedrals, wide streets lined with deciduous trees that turn gold and red in autumn, and a university that gives it a lively student feel. After the long climb from the coast on the Waterfall Way, it is a welcome place to stop, and it is a proper city, with every service a rider could want.
