  1. 1. Câmpeni

    Câmpeni lies in the Apuseni Mountains of western Transylvania, in a high, wooded valley cut by the river Arieș. This is Țara Moților, the land of the Moți, a highland people known for their woodcraft, their wooden buckets and troughs, and a long history of rugged independence. The town is small, about eight thousand people, but it is the capital of the area, and it carries more history than its size suggests.

  2. 2. Scărişoara

    Up in the Arieș valley, in the heart of the Apuseni, sits a scattered Moți commune that lends its name to one of the strangest places in Romania: a cave full of ice. The Scărișoara ice cave lies a few kilometres up a side road from Gârda de Sus, at around eleven hundred metres in the karst plateau above the valley, and it holds one of the largest underground glaciers in Europe. You descend a long metal stairway into a great shaft, and at the bottom there is a block of ice tens of metres thick, layered like a cake, some of it thousands of years old. Even in August the air down there is close to freezing.

  3. 3. Arieşeni

    Arieșeni is where the Arieș river begins its long run east across Transylvania, and where the road out of the valley starts to climb toward the Vârtop pass and the border between Alba and Bihor. The village spreads along the upper valley at around nine hundred metres, among spruce forests and high meadows, and in winter it is one of the small ski resorts of the Apuseni, with a few lifts and plenty of guesthouses. In summer it is quiet, green and cool, the kind of place where the smell of woodsmoke and cut hay hangs over the road in the evening.

  4. 4. Beiuş

    Beiuș is a modest market town on the western side of the Apuseni, sitting in a wide basin on the Crișul Negru with the mountains forming a curved wall to the east. It is the first place of any size you reach after coming down off the Vârtop pass and through Ștei, and it feels like a different world from the high Moți villages: flatter, warmer and more open, with orchards, maize fields and long straight village streets. The town has around ten thousand people and the slightly sleepy charm of a place that has been an administrative centre for a long time without ever becoming big.
