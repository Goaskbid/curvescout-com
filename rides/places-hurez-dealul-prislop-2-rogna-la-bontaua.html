Why this ride

163 km from Cizer to Cizer, about 2 h 49 of riding. The stops in between: Zalău - La Nord de Pârâul Măgurii, Tihău - Grădiște, Rogna - La Bontauă and back through Tihău - Grădiște. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hurez - Dealul Prislop 2

    Before telegraphs, there were bonfires. A Roman frontier needed a way to send a message along a ridge faster than a rider could gallop, and the answer was a chain of watchtowers, each within sight of the next, where a guard could light a fire at night or raise smoke by day to warn of an attack. Trajan's Column shows such towers, with a torch at the top and a ladder. On the Meses hills, in what is now north-western Romania, the line was dense, and each hilltop had to be chosen with a careful eye for what could be seen from where.

  2. 2. Zalău - La Nord de Pârâul Măgurii

    Zalău is the county seat of Sălaj, a city of around sixty thousand people sitting in a shallow valley where the Meseș hills fold down toward the Transylvanian plain. The stop sits just north of the Măgurii stream, on the edge of the town, which means you arrive through suburbs and allotments rather than through any grand gateway. That suits the county. Sălaj is not a place of famous passes; it is a place of rolling ridges, orchards, hay meadows and small roads that wander from one village to the next without much of a plan.

  3. 3. Tihău - Grădiște

    The Someș river makes its way through the hills of north-western Romania between Cluj and Baia Mare, and near the small town of Jibou, in Sălaj county, it passes through a narrow valley guarded on its slopes by the remains of a Roman fort. The site, known locally as Grădiște, which simply means old fort, lies above the village of Tihău, close to Surduc and Gâlgău Almașului.

  4. 4. Rogna - La Bontauă

    Rogna is a scatter of farms in the hills of northern Sălaj, close to the Someș valley and the border with Maramureș, and La Bontauă is the name the locals give to a piece of high ground where a Roman frontier site has been recorded. The northern edge of Roman Dacia ran through these hills, and the Romans placed watchtowers and small forts on high points to keep an eye on the river valleys and the free Dacians to the north. Today there is little to see apart from the shape of the land, but the view over the rolling hills makes it easy to imagine a sentry looking out across the valley.
