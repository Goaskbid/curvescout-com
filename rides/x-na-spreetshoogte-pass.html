  1. 1. Windhoek

    Windhoek sits at about 1,700 metres in the middle of Namibia, in a bowl of hills that gives it a cooler, drier climate than most of the country. It is the capital and by far the largest city, with a population approaching three hundred thousand, and a mix of German colonial buildings, modern shopping centres and sprawling townships. The Christuskirche, a Lutheran church with a pale stone facade, stands on a hill in the middle of the town and has become the city's best-known landmark.

  2. 2. Spreetshoogte Pass

    Spreetshoogte is the pass that makes you grip the bars a little tighter, and then grin inside your helmet. It drops the Khomas Hochland highlands down to the edge of the Namib desert in one dramatic fall, and it is among the steepest in Namibia, with gradients that reach something like one in seven in places. The gravel road is cut into the face of the escarpment, and from the top the view is immense: a plain of red sand and pale grass, flat and empty to the horizon, with the dark lines of the Naukluft mountains running along the south.

  3. 3. Solitaire

    Solitaire is not so much a town as a fuel pump, a cafe and a graveyard of rusting cars in the middle of the Namibian desert, and it is one of the most reliable landmarks on the gravel road between Walvis Bay and Sesriem. The name suits it: there is nothing else for a long way in any direction, only stony plains, camelthorn trees and the far wall of the escarpment. The settlement sits on the edge of the Namib-Naukluft Park, on the C14, where the plateau of central Namibia drops toward the Atlantic.
