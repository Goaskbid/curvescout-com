Why this ride

154 km from Rugat to Rugat, about 2 h 39 of riding. It goes by way of Port de Confrides- Abric I, Barranc de l'Infern Conj. 2, Cova de la Catxupa- Abric I and back through Barranc de l'Infern Conj. 2. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc de les Coves- Abrigo III

    The Benicadell range is the thing that dominates every view here, a pale limestone ridge that rises above 1,100 metres between the Vall d'Albaida and the Comtat. The shelter marked here lies at the base of it, among villages that most Valencians have heard of but few have visited: Ráfol de Salem, Castelló de Rugat and Beniarrés, all within four kilometres of one another. This is quiet country of orchards, olive terraces and little hamlets with a church and a bar.

  2. 2. Port de Confrides- Abric I

    Inland from Benidorm, barely half an hour from the high-rise towers and the beach crowds, the mountains of Alicante rise steeply and the whole mood changes. The Port de Confrides is the saddle on the CV-70 between the Guadalest valley and the upland basin of Confrides and Benasau, with the long limestone wall of the Sierra de Aitana to the south. Aitana, at 1,558 metres, is the highest peak in the province, and on a clear winter morning you can see the sea glittering behind you as you climb.

  3. 3. Barranc de l'Infern Conj. 2

    Local walkers call it the cathedral of hiking, and once you have seen the Barranc de l'Infern you understand why. This deep limestone gorge, between the Vall de Laguar and the Vall d'Ebo in the mountains behind Dénia, is crossed by an old Moorish mule path that descends and climbs again by thousands of stone steps, carved and built into the rock walls over centuries. The route, a classic day walk, is spectacular and exhausting, with the gorge narrowing at its deepest point to a passage between towering walls. The gorge also holds prehistoric rock art in its shelters, and the sites grouped as Conjunt 2 are among them, part of the wider family of Mediterranean rock art protected by UNESCO.

  4. 4. Cova de la Catxupa- Abric I

    The Marina Alta is the northern Costa Blanca at its most mixed: villas on every slope, a ribbon of coast running from Moraira to Jávea, and just behind it, farm terraces, dry stone walls and small rock shelters where prehistoric people left their mark. The Cova de la Catxupa, near Teulada and Benitachell, is one of them, a shelter in the hills a few kilometres from the sea, listed with the other Mediterranean rock art of the Iberian Peninsula.
