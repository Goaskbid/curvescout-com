  1. 1. Walvis Bay

    Flamingos, both greater and lesser, gather here in their thousands, along with pelicans, terns and waders, and the pink line of birds against the grey water is a sight that makes even a hurried rider slow down. The Benguela current, cold and rich, brings fish and fog, and the town is often wrapped in mist while the desert behind is sunny.

  2. 2. Kuiseb Pass

    The Kuiseb River is the line that holds back the dunes. Flowing only after rare rains, it winds across the gravel plains of western Namibia toward Walvis Bay, and for most of its length it forms a natural barrier, with the great sand sea of the Namib to the south and the stony desert to the north. Where the road crosses it, in the Kuiseb canyon, the land drops into a gorge of dark rock, with dry riverbed, acacia and ana trees far below the plain, and the pass is a short, steep ride down and up the other side.

  3. 3. Solitaire

    Solitaire is not so much a town as a fuel pump, a cafe and a graveyard of rusting cars in the middle of the Namibian desert, and it is one of the most reliable landmarks on the gravel road between Walvis Bay and Sesriem. The name suits it: there is nothing else for a long way in any direction, only stony plains, camelthorn trees and the far wall of the escarpment. The settlement sits on the edge of the Namib-Naukluft Park, on the C14, where the plateau of central Namibia drops toward the Atlantic.
