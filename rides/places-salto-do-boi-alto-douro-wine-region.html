Why this ride

From Vila Nova de Foz Côa it runs 416 km to Vila Nova de Foz Côa; allow about 7 h 10 of riding. It goes by way of Prehistoric Rock Art Sites in the Côa Vall, Alto Douro Wine Region and back through Prehistoric Rock Art Sites in the Côa Vall. It crosses from Portugal into Spain - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Salto do Boi

    What I can describe is the landscape, which is the Alto Douro, one of the most worked and most beautiful wine regions in the world. The hillsides here are carved into narrow stone-walled terraces, the socalcos, built by hand over centuries, and covered with vines that turn from green to gold and red in October.

  2. 2. Prehistoric Rock Art Sites in the Côa Vall

    On the Spanish side of the border with Portugal, the river Águeda cuts through granite and schist on its way to the Douro, and on the rock faces along its banks, at Siega Verde, Palaeolithic people engraved horses, aurochs, deer and goats more than ten thousand years ago. In 2010 Siega Verde was added to the Prehistoric Rock Art Sites in the Côa Valley, the Portuguese World Heritage Site just across the border, recognising that these open-air engravings form one of the largest collections of Ice Age art in the world.

  3. 3. Alto Douro Wine Region

    The Douro river has been carving a gorge through the north of Portugal for millions of years, and for about two thousand years people have been planting vines on its slopes. The terraces, held up by stone walls, climb steeply from the water and rise through schist and slate in rows, and the effect, in the right season, is of a vast green staircase. The Alto Douro was the first wine region in the world to be officially demarcated, by the Marquis of Pombal in 1756, and UNESCO listed it in 2001. Port wine, the sweet fortified red that made the region famous, is still made here.
