Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Lindau

    Lindau is an island that behaves like a postcard, and the amusing thing is that it knows it. The old town sits on a small island in the eastern end of Lake Constance, the Bodensee, linked to the mainland by a causeway and a railway embankment, and the harbour at its south side is guarded by two monuments: a tall lighthouse and a stone lion of Bavaria on a pillar. The lighthouse was built in the nineteenth century, and from its top you see the whole lake, with the Austrian and Swiss Alps behind. About twenty-four thousand people live in the municipality, and most of them are on the mainland.

  2. 2. Oberstaufen

    Oberstaufen is a spa town with a taste for fasting. Its fame comes from the Schroth cure, a regime of dry days, wet days, wraps and very little food that visitors have been putting up with here for generations, and the place wears its wellness reputation with a certain Bavarian shrug. You see it in the clean flower boxes, the Kneipp-style water basins in the park, and the clinic-like hotels. It is also a proper Allgäu town, and a pleasant one, with about seven thousand people, cow bells in the background, and a brewing tradition that makes the fasting look like a very optional part of life.

  4. 4. Garmisch-Partenkirchen

    Garmisch-Partenkirchen lies in a wide Alpine valley in Bavaria, close to the Austrian border, with the Wetterstein range rising behind it and the Zugspitze, at 2,962 metres the highest mountain in Germany, standing at the end of the valley. The town is really two, Garmisch and Partenkirchen, joined together for the 1936 Winter Olympics, and each still has its own character. Partenkirchen, the older part, has a Roman history, as it stood on the road across the Alps, and its main street, with painted houses, is the more charming.

  6. 6. Near Rottach-Egern

    At the southern end of the Tegernsee, Rottach-Egern sits where the lake meets the mountains, and it has long been one of the most comfortable addresses in Upper Bavaria. Painted houses, carved balconies and well-kept gardens line the shore, and in summer the lake is busy with sailors and swimmers. The Wallberg rises steeply behind the village, and on a clear day its summit looks close enough to touch.

  7. 7. Reit im Winkl

    Reit im Winkl is the snow-sure village at the end of the Chiemgau Alps, a pocket of Bavaria wedged into the corner where Germany meets Austria. At about 700 metres, it sits in a high, green bowl among steep peaks, with the Kaisergebirge, the jagged limestone range of the Tyrolean Kaiser mountains, standing across the border to the south. The snow here is dependable in winter, and the place has produced a number of Olympic skiers.

  8. 8. Berchtesgaden

    Berchtesgaden sits in the far southeast corner of Germany, in a pocket of Bavaria that pokes into Austria, and the mountains here do not bother to be modest. The Watzmann, at 2,713 metres Germany's second highest mountain, stands above the valley in a long ridge, with the pale wall of the Hochkalter opposite. The town is the end of the German Alpine Road, which runs from Lake Constance, and it feels like the end of the line, with the peaks closing in on all sides. For centuries the local economy rested on salt, and the salt mine, worked since the sixteenth century, is open to visitors who put on miners' clothes and slide down the wooden chutes.
