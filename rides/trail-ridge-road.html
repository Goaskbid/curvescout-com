Why this ride

A 84 km one-way ride from near Estes Park, about 1 h 26 of riding. The stops in between: Many Parks Curve, Rock Cut, Alpine Visitor Center and Milner Pass. The highest point is about 3,600 m. Alpine Visitor Center and Milner Pass are open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Trail Ridge Road

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 3279 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance. Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Estes Park

    Elk wander through the streets of Estes Park in autumn as if they had a lease on the place. During the rut, in late September and October, bulls bugle on the lawns and golf course, and visitors line up with cameras at a respectful distance, while the town itself, sitting at around 7,500 feet in a mountain bowl where the Big Thompson and Fall rivers meet, carries on with its cafes, fudge shops and outdoor stores. It is the eastern gateway to Rocky Mountain National Park and the starting point for Trail Ridge Road.

  2. 2. Many Parks Curve

    Many Parks Curve is a hairpin on Trail Ridge Road with a viewpoint so good that the engineers who built the road clearly wanted you to stop. From the overlook, at roughly 9,600 feet, the land opens up below like a book: the broad, flat valleys, which early settlers called parks, lie in a row with forested ridges between them, and beyond them the Continental Divide rises in a long line of snow-streaked summits. The word park in this part of Colorado means a high, open grassy basin, and from here you see several at once.

    Read the complete Many Parks Curve write-up.

  3. 3. Rock Cut

    Rock Cut is a place where the road runs into a cliff and the cliff loses. The engineers blasted a deep trench through a ridge of ancient Precambrian rock here, high on Trail Ridge Road at more than twelve thousand feet, and the trench left steep walls on either side, with the highway squeezed between them. You stop at the car park nearby, climb a short paved path and step out onto the alpine tundra, with the world dropping away on all sides and the wind pulling at your jacket.

  4. 4. Alpine Visitor Center

    The Alpine Visitor Center sits at about 11,800 feet, near the high point of Trail Ridge Road, and it feels like a base camp on the roof of the park. The building is a stone-and-timber lodge with a cafeteria, a gift shop and a view out across the alpine tundra to the peaks of the Never Summer Mountains and the Continental Divide. In clear weather the air is brilliantly sharp, and in poor weather the building is a welcome shelter, with the windows rattling in the wind and the smell of hot coffee and soup inside.

  5. 5. Milner Pass

    Milner Pass is where Trail Ridge Road crosses the Continental Divide, at 3,279 metres, and it does it so quietly that you could miss it. There is a modest sign, a small parking area, and a little tarn called Poudre Lake lying right beside the road. Rain that lands on the east side of the sign heads for the Cache la Poudre and eventually the Platte; rain on the west side ends up in the Colorado. Standing there in a riding jacket, still ringing from the climb, it feels like a very large idea for a very small place.

  6. 6. Grand Lake

    Grand Lake is the largest natural lake in Colorado, and the little town on its shore is the western doorway to Rocky Mountain National Park. At around eight thousand seven hundred feet, it is a village of boardwalks and wooden storefronts, with canoe rentals, ice cream shops and souvenir stalls strung along the water, and it is surrounded by dark forest and high peaks. The lake is cold and deep, and the surface is often dotted with sailing boats in summer.
