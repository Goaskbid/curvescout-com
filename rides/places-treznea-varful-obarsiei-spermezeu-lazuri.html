Why this ride

A 249 km round trip from Meseşenii de Jos - roughly 4 h 18 in the saddle. It goes by way of Negreni - Poiana la Arbore, Spermezeu - Lazuri and back through Negreni - Poiana la Arbore. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Treznea-Vârful Obârșiei

    Treznea is a small village that appears in Romanian history books for a bad reason. In August 1940, the Second Vienna Award, imposed by Germany and Italy, handed northern Transylvania to Hungary, and in the weeks that followed, Hungarian troops entered the area. On 9 September, in Treznea and the neighbouring village of Ip, dozens of Romanian civilians were killed in reprisals, and a memorial in Treznea recalls them. The territory returned to Romania in 1944, but the memory of those years still shapes local life.

  2. 2. Negreni - Poiana la Arbore

    Somewhere in the hills of Sălaj county, between the villages of Lozna, Rus and Letca, a meadow called Poiana la Arbore, the clearing by the tree, marks one of the small Roman sites on the north-western frontier of Dacia. The name alone tells you what to expect: a glade, a ridge, perhaps a lone tree, and the faint traces of a watch post that once looked out over the Someş valley. The Frontiers of the Roman Empire listing in 2024 included many such places, and their value lies in the chain they form rather than in any single ruin.

  3. 3. Spermezeu - Lazuri

    Spermezeu is a village in the hilly country of western Bistriţa-Năsăud county, in northern Transylvania, where low ridges covered with orchards, hayfields and beech woods roll between the Someş valley and the Ţibleş mountains. The site called Spermezeu - Lazuri belongs to the series of places along the northern edge of the Roman province of Dacia, part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire listed in Romania in 2024. Here the Romans watched the border with watchtowers and small fortifications on hilltops, and though little remains above ground, the commanding views explain why these spots were chosen.
