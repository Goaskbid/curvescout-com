Why this ride

From Klisura it runs 685 km to Klisura; allow about 16 h 33 of riding. The stops in between: Central Balkan - Stara Reka Reserve, Central Balkan - Kozya stena Reserve, Central Balkan - Severen Dzhendem Reserve and Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak. It tops out around 1,050 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

The stops

  1. 1. Central Balkan - Tsarichina Reserve

    Start here.

    The Balkan range, called Stara Planina in Bulgarian, runs across the whole of the country like a long, wooded wall, and it gave its name to the entire Balkan peninsula. The Tsarichina reserve lies in its central part, within the Central Balkan National Park, in a deep valley that holds some of the oldest and best preserved forests in Bulgaria. The beech and fir trees are huge, and in places have never been cut, so the forest has the tall, silent, shadowy character of ancient woodland. It belongs to the beech forest sites that UNESCO linked together across Europe.

    Read the complete Central Balkan - Tsarichina Reserve write-up.

  2. 2. Central Balkan - Stara Reka Reserve

    96 km in.

    Just above Karlovo, the Stara Reka tumbles out of the Balkan mountains through a steep gorge of beech and rock, and climbing alongside it you leave the town's lowland heat behind within minutes. The reserve that bears the river's name protects the upper part of that valley and forms part of the Central Balkan National Park, one of the largest protected areas in this part of Europe. Its ancient beech forests are included in the UNESCO listing that ties together primeval beech woods across the continent.

    Read the complete Central Balkan - Stara Reka Reserve write-up.

  3. 3. Central Balkan - Kozya stena Reserve

    171 km in.

    High on the main ridge of the Balkan range, the Stara Planina, that runs across Bulgaria from west to east, the Kozya Stena reserve protects a wild landscape of limestone cliffs, beech forests and alpine meadows. Its name means goat wall, after the chamois that live on its crags. The reserve is part of Central Balkan National Park, one of the largest protected areas in Europe, and since 2017 its oldest beech stands have been inscribed alongside the primeval beech forests of the Carpathians and the rest of Europe. Wolves, bears and wild boar roam the forests, golden eagles soar over the ridges, and the meadows burst with wildflowers in early summer.

    Read the complete Central Balkan - Kozya stena Reserve write-up.

  4. 4. Central Balkan - Severen Dzhendem Reserve

    228 km in.

    Bulgaria's Balkan range gave the whole peninsula its name, and the central stretch is where it looks most like a proper mountain wall. The Severen Dzhendem reserve lies in the Central Balkan National Park, the high ridge that runs east to west above the Rose Valley. To the south the land drops towards Kalofer and Karlovo, and to the north it falls away into the foothills and the towns around Apriltsi and Troyan. The tallest peak in the range, Botev, rises to a little over 2,300 metres not far from here, and the high ground is grassy ridge, cliff and old beech and fir forest.

    Read the complete Central Balkan - Severen Dzhendem Reserve write-up.

  5. 5. Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak

    342 km in.

    In 1944 soldiers digging a shelter on the edge of Kazanlak broke into a small brick chamber and found themselves looking at some of the finest ancient paintings in the Balkans. The Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak, built in the fourth century BC for a ruler of the Thracians, has a narrow corridor leading to a round, beehive-shaped burial chamber, its dome covered with frescoes of a funeral feast: the dead man and his wife seated at a table, servants bringing gifts, horses and chariots racing around the top. The colours and the tenderness of the figures are extraordinary. It was listed by UNESCO in 1979.
