  1. 1. Paris

    Paris is a good place to start a ride and a bad place to ride in. The Périphérique, the ring road, carries more than a million vehicles a day, scooters thread through the lanes with great confidence, and cobbles, tram rails and painted crossings are slippery in the wet. The city also operates a low-emission zone, so check the rules and get the right sticker for your bike before you arrive. If you can, leave the bike at the hotel, ride the metro and save the machine for the morning when you head out.

  2. 2. Palace and Park of Fontainebleau

    Fontainebleau is the palace French kings actually lived in, as opposed to the one they built to impress, and for that reason it feels more human than Versailles. Almost every monarch from the Middle Ages to Napoleon III added a wing, a staircase or a garden, so the place is a jumble of styles, held together by its forest and its long ponds. Napoleon called it the true home of kings, and in April 1814 he said goodbye to his Old Guard in the courtyard that is still known as the Cour des Adieux, at the foot of the horseshoe staircase.

  4. 4. Provins

    Provins rises out of the wheat fields of the Brie like something from a film set, and the odd thing is that it is almost entirely real. In the twelfth and thirteenth centuries this was one of the four great fair towns of the counts of Champagne, where merchants from Flanders, Italy and beyond met to trade cloth, spices and money. When the fairs declined, Provins simply stopped growing, and so its ramparts, its gates and its medieval upper town survived the centuries more or less intact. UNESCO listed it in 2001 as a rare surviving example of a medieval fair town.
