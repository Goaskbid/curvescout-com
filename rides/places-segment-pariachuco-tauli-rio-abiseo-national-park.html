Why this ride

A 357 km round trip from Conchucos - roughly 6 h 09 in the saddle. The stops in between: Segment Escalerilla/ Casa Blanca - Cerro H, Río Abiseo National Park and back through Segment Escalerilla/ Casa Blanca - Cerro H. The highest point is about 3,850 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Segment Pariachuco - Tauli

    Some roads you can still feel. At this part of the northern Peruvian Andes, the old Inca road survives as a worn stone staircase and a retaining wall, the surface polished by centuries of llamas and sandals, with drainage channels still carrying water off the path. It belongs to the Qhapaq Nan, the Inca road system that was listed by UNESCO in 2014, and this segment, which the label ties to two places called Pariachuco and Tauli, shows what the engineering was like at its best. Where a slope was steep, the builders cut steps; where a cliff was in the way, they cut a ledge; where a stream crossed, they set stepping stones or built a bridge of stone slabs.

  2. 2. Segment Escalerilla/ Casa Blanca - Cerro H

    Huamachuco is a big market town in the highlands of La Libertad, at about 3,200 metres, and it has been an important place for much longer than the Incas. On the hills above the town, the ruins of Marcahuamachuco spread across a long ridge, with huge stone walls and galleries that were raised during the first millennium AD, in a culture that flourished at the same time as the Wari empire and ruled the northern highlands. A big part of the stonework still stands, including a long building called the Castillo, with three storeys of stone galleries. The Incas arrived much later, in the fifteenth century, and ran their road system through the area, and parts of the Qhapaq Ñan around Huamachuco are among those listed by UNESCO in 2014.

  3. 3. Río Abiseo National Park

    Río Abiseo National Park covers a large block of the eastern Andes in the San Martín region of Peru, a tangle of steep cloud forest and high grassland where the mountains fall away toward the Amazon basin. It was set up in 1983 and is a World Heritage site for both its nature and its archaeology. Much of it has never been properly explored, and the interior is reachable only on foot after days of travel, with permits from the authorities.
