Why this ride

From Neustadt an der Donau it runs 73 km to Neustadt an der Donau; allow about 1 h 35 of riding. It goes by way of Watchtowers WP 15/11 to 15/26 and fortlets, Watchtowers WP 15/1 to 15/7 and back through Watchtowers WP 15/11 to 15/26 and fortlets.

The stops

  1. 1. Fort site and civil settlement of Pfoerrin

    Start here.

    Between Ingolstadt and Kelheim, the Danube flows through a broad, flat valley in Bavaria, with hop gardens, fields of maize and asparagus beds on its banks. Pförring is a small market town on the north bank, with Neustadt an der Donau and Vohburg on the other side. In Roman times, this was the frontier: the Raetian Limes ended at the Danube just downstream, and the Romans built a fort here, known as Celeusum, with a civil settlement outside its walls.

    Read the complete Fort site and civil settlement of Pfoerrin write-up.

  2. 2. Watchtowers WP 15/11 to 15/26 and fortlets

    13 km in.

    The Altmühl valley in Bavaria is one of Germany's best kept riding secrets, a winding river valley cut deep into Jurassic limestone, lined with juniper heaths, rock towers and castles. Near where the valley meets the Danube, the Roman frontier, the Raetian Limes, ran across the land, and the stretch of watchtowers and small forts numbered WP 15/11 to 15/26 lies in the wooded hills here, between Riedenburg, Dietfurt and the Danube town of Vohburg.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 15/11 to 15/26 and fortlets write-up.

  3. 3. Watchtowers WP 15/1 to 15/7

    38 km in.

    In Bavaria, the Roman frontier changed character. Instead of the wooden palisade used in the Upper German section, the Raetian Limes was eventually built as a stone wall, known locally as the Devil's Wall, because medieval people could not imagine anyone but the devil building something so long. The stretch between Watchtowers 15/1 and 15/7 crosses the hills around the Altmühl valley, near Kipfenberg and Kinding, and the line of the wall can still be traced in places as a low ridge in the forest. It is part of the Upper German-Raetian Limes World Heritage site.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 15/1 to 15/7 write-up.
