  1. 1. La Falaise de Bandiagara

    The Bandiagara Escarpment runs for about a hundred and fifty kilometres across central Mali, a sandstone wall that drops some five hundred metres to a sandy plain. From the top it looks like the edge of the world, flat and dry and hazy in every direction. Along the base and up the face, the Dogon people have built villages of mud-brick houses and granaries with pointed thatch roofs, many of them tucked into cracks in the cliff itself.

  2. 2. Cliff of Bandiagara

    A sandstone wall some 150 kilometres long and in places 500 metres high runs across the Sahel of central Mali, and clinging to its face are houses, granaries and shrines that have been there for a thousand years. The Cliff of Bandiagara is the home of the Dogon, a people famous for their masks, their dances and their cosmology, which is among the most elaborate in Africa. The first inhabitants of the cliff were the Tellem, small people who built granaries in caves high up on the rock, and the Dogon who arrived from about the fifteenth century built their villages below, in the cracks and at the foot, linked by paths, stone steps and ladders. The area was inscribed as a World Heritage site in 1989.

  3. 3. Village de Songo

    Songo stands on the edge of the Bandiagara escarpment in central Mali, the long sandstone cliff that is the heartland of the Dogon people. From the top, the land drops several hundred metres to a sandy plain, and houses of mud and stone cling to the rock face, stacked like swallows' nests, with granaries topped by pointed thatched roofs. The area is a World Heritage site, valued for both its landscape and its living culture.
