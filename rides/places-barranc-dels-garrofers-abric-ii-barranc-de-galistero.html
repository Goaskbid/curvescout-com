Why this ride

68 km from Planes to Planes, about 1 h 10 of riding. It goes by way of Penyó de les Carrasques- Abric II, Barranc de Galistero and back through Penyó de les Carrasques- Abric II. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc dels Garrofers- Abric II

    The name Garrofers means carob trees, and the Barranc dels Garrofers is the ravine where they grow. The carob, with its dark, leathery leaves and long brown pods, is a tree of the dry Mediterranean, and it was once a staple of the poorest farms, feeding mules, goats and, in hard years, people. The ravine lies about three and a half kilometres from Planes, a village of some eight hundred people in the Comtat, the green interior country of northern Alicante, between the mountains of the Serra de Mariola and the Vall de Gallinera.

  2. 2. Penyó de les Carrasques- Abric II

    Cocentaina lies in the green hollow of the Comtat, a valley in the Alicante hills, with its palace and castle watching the Serpis river from the edge of town. Above and to the east rise limestone crags, among them the Penyó de les Carrasques, named for the carrasca, the holm oak that clings to dry slopes here. A rock shelter in the cliff, Abric II, preserves prehistoric paintings, part of the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin listed by UNESCO in 1998. As elsewhere in this tradition, the figures are small and fragile, and access can be restricted.

  3. 3. Barranc de Galistero

    Castell de Castells is a village of about four hundred people at the heart of the Marina Alta's high valleys, and it is one of the most important places for rock art in the Valencian country. The Barranc de Galistero is a ravine nearby, and the shelter in it belongs to the rock art of the Mediterranean Basin that UNESCO inscribed in 1998. The village is also near the Pla de Petracos, a famous rock shelter with remarkable paintings that attracts specialists from around the world, so a day in the area can include several sites. The painted shelters are fragile and often have limited access, so check before you go.
