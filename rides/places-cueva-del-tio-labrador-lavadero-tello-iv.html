Why this ride

From Velez Rubio it runs 147 km to Velez Rubio; allow about 2 h 32 of riding. On the way it passes Lavadero Tello V, Molinos II, Lavadero Tello IV and back through Molinos II. The highest point is about 1,200 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cueva del Tio Labrador

    Vélez-Rubio is a larger and busier place than its neighbour Vélez-Blanco, with seven thousand people, a lively main street, and a church that is a surprise in a town this size. The Church of the Encarnación, begun in the eighteenth century, has twin towers and a massive Baroque façade, usually described as the largest Baroque church in Almería, and its bulk dominates the roofs. It is a good place to start before riding up toward the mountains, because the fuel stations here are the nearest reliable ones for a long way.

  2. 2. Lavadero Tello V

    Los Vélez, in the north of Almería, is wool and almond country, and its history is one of shepherds, noblemen and Moriscos. For centuries flocks of sheep moved between the winter grazing of the lowlands and the summer pastures of the high sierras along old drove roads, and the area had a thriving wool trade. The shelter here, one of a group around Vélez-Blanco, carries prehistoric paintings, belonging to the Levantine art tradition that UNESCO recognises.

  3. 3. Molinos II

    Vélez-Blanco is a white town under a limestone wall, and the castle above it is one of the oddest in Spain. The Marquises of Los Vélez built it in the early sixteenth century, in a hurry to look both fortified and Italian, so the outside has round towers and the inside had a marble Renaissance courtyard that was later sold, piece by piece, and ended up in New York's Metropolitan Museum. Below the castle the town clings to the slope, with narrow lanes and a church, and the Sierra María-Los Vélez rises behind, a natural park of pine, juniper and bare crags. At about a thousand metres, it is cold in winter and delicious in summer.

  4. 4. Lavadero Tello IV

    Vélez-Blanco has a castle that has been in two countries. Built in the early sixteenth century by the first Marquis of los Vélez, it was a Renaissance palace of white marble inside a fortified shell, and in 1904 the owner sold the marble patio, which was shipped in crates to Paris and then to New York, where it is now in the Metropolitan Museum. The castle's empty courtyard remains in the village. Lavadero Tello is sixteen kilometres away, in the Sierra María-Los Vélez natural park, where red-and-gold hills, almond groves and pale limestone peaks rise above the plains of Almería and Granada.
