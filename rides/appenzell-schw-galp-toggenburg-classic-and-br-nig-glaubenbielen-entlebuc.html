Why this ride

A one-way ride of 383 km, Rapperswil to Luzern - roughly 7 h 32 in the saddle. The stops in between: Appenzell old town, Toggenburg flow road and Walensee line. The climbs: Schwägalp Säntis road, Glaubenbielen high road and Brünigpass sweepers. It tops out at 1,611 m. Brunig Pass is subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. It is a high-mountain day.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Rapperswil lake bridge

    The wooden bridge at Rapperswil is the kind of crossing that makes you slow down on purpose. It stretches across the upper end of Lake Zurich, a footbridge of oak planks on piles, rebuilt in the early 2000s on the line of a medieval predecessor, and it joins the old town of Rapperswil on the north shore to the village of Hurden on the south. Pilgrims on the Way of St James have walked across it for centuries, and the modern version makes no attempt to be anything but simple and beautiful.

  2. 2. Appenzell old town

    Appenzell is a town that has kept hold of itself, and that is the first thing you notice when you ride in. Houses are painted in bright colours, with decorated gables and flower boxes, and the main street feels more like a village than a town of five and a half thousand. The canton it governs, Appenzell Innerrhoden, is the smallest in Switzerland by population, and it clings to traditions that others have dropped.

  3. 3. Schwägalp Säntis road

    The Säntis is the high point of the Alpstein, 2,502 metres of limestone that rises almost out of nowhere above the rolling hills of eastern Switzerland. The road that gets you to its foot is the Schwägalp pass, which climbs from Urnäsch in Appenzell Ausserrhoden to a high saddle at 1,278 metres and then drops toward the Toggenburg. At the saddle, a cable car carries people up the last steep stretch to the summit in a few minutes, and a famous weather station sits at the top.

    Read the complete Schwägalp Säntis road write-up.

  4. 4. Toggenburg flow road

    Toggenburg is a long valley in eastern Switzerland, carved by the young Thur river, and it is a landscape of soft green hills with steep stone walls above. The road here runs from Wildhaus, high up at the head of the valley, toward Gams and the Rhine plain, and it has a flowing quality that has earned it a name among riders: long, open bends, gentle gradients and hardly any need to touch the brakes.

  5. 5. Walensee photo line

    The Walensee is one of the most dramatic lakes in Switzerland, and one of the least visited. It lies in a trench between the Churfirsten range to the north and the Glarus Alps to the south, a long narrow lake of intense green, with cliffs that fall almost vertically into the water. The photograph is the Churfirsten wall reflected in the surface on a still morning, seven jagged peaks mirrored in the lake.

    Read the complete Walensee photo line write-up.

  6. 6. Back to Rapperswil

    Rapperswil sits at the eastern end of Lake Zürich, where a long wooden footbridge crosses the water to Hurden on the far shore, and it calls itself the Rose Town for the thousands of rose bushes that bloom in the gardens beside its castle. The old town climbs a small hill capped by a medieval castle with three towers, and its lanes are a mix of arcaded houses, small squares and lakeside terraces, with a view over the lake to the mountains. The lake here is about four hundred metres above sea level, and the air feels mild and soft.

  7. 7. Lucerne lake exit

    Lucerne sits where the lake narrows into the Reuss, with the Alps stacked up behind it, and at 436 metres it is one of the most visited cities in Switzerland. The best way to see the old town is on foot, so find a car park and leave the bike.

  8. 8. Entlebuch food stop

    Entlebuch gave the world a dog. The Entlebucher Sennenhund, a small, sturdy, tricolour herding breed, was developed in these valleys to drive cattle on the steep pastures, and the village is proud of it. The surrounding region is a UNESCO biosphere reserve, recognised in 2001, a landscape of moors, wooded ridges and green pasture, where the Kleine Emme river runs down from the Alps toward Lucerne.

  9. 9. Glaubenbielen high road

    Glaubenbielen is a pass for people who like their mountains empty. It crosses a green saddle at 1,611 metres between Giswil in Obwalden and the Entlebuch valley in Lucerne, and the road is narrow, steep and often quiet, with long stretches where you will meet nothing but cows and the occasional cyclist. The scenery is gentle but big, with moorland, scattered spruce and an open sky.

  10. 10. Brünigpass sweepers

    Brünig is the easy pass, and it is more fun than that makes it sound. At 1,008 metres it is one of the lowest of the main Alpine crossings in central Switzerland, joining Lungern in Obwalden with the shore of Lake Brienz and the Haslital, and it is open all year. The road is fast and well made, with long sweepers that let you lean the bike and keep the revs up, a kind of riding that is rare in the high mountains.

  11. 11. Giswil fuel gate

    Giswil sits at the bottom of the Brünig road, and so it is a place where riders gather. The village, with about three and a half thousand people, lies in the valley of the Sarner Aa, at 485 metres, south of Lake Sarnen, between wooded hills and steep slopes. It is a quiet farming community in the canton of Obwalden, the heart of central Switzerland, and the main road runs through it on the way up to Lungern and the Brünig pass.

  12. 12. Zug lake return

    Zug is a small city with a big lake and a reputation for money. The lake, the Zugersee, lies in a bowl of low hills at the northern edge of the Alps, a calm, blue sheet of water with the Rigi rising across on the south side and the Pilatus further off. The old town sits on the northeastern shore, with a medieval clock tower, pastel houses and a promenade where boats bob in the harbour.
