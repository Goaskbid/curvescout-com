Why this ride

326 km from Venice to Venice, about 5 h 37 of riding. It goes by way of Villa Thiene, Villa Valmarana Zen, Le Colline del Prosecco di Conegliano e Va and back through Le Colline del Prosecco di Conegliano e Va. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Venice

    You cannot ride a motorbike in Venice, and that is the best thing about it. The city is built on over a hundred small islands in a shallow lagoon, linked by about four hundred bridges, and there are no roads, only canals and narrow lanes. Cars and bikes stop at Piazzale Roma or at the parking islands, and everything else moves on foot or by water. So the ride begins on the mainland, at Mestre or Tronchetto, with the bike left in a guarded garage and a vaporetto to carry you in.

  2. 2. Villa Thiene

    Quinto Vicentino is a quiet farming town on the flat plain just north-east of Vicenza, and its town hall happens to be a villa by Andrea Palladio. Villa Thiene was commissioned in the sixteenth century by the brothers Marcantonio and Adriano Thiene, members of one of the most powerful families in Vicenza, and Palladio drew up a grand design for it with courtyards and wings that would have spread across the fields. Only part of that plan was ever built. What stands today is a solid, rather austere block, and the council offices are inside it, which gives the place a pleasingly everyday feeling.

  3. 3. Villa Valmarana Zen

    Not every Palladian villa sits on a hill with a view. Villa Valmarana at Lisiera lies on the flat Vicentine plain east of the city, among fields of maize and soy, irrigation ditches and poplar rows, and it was built for a family whose wealth came from the land itself. Palladio designed it in the middle of the sixteenth century, and it belongs to the family of villas around Vicenza that UNESCO lists together with his town palaces. The house follows his idea of a working farm dressed as a Roman temple: a central block with a portico, flanked by long service wings, the barchesse, that once held grain, tools and animals.

  4. 4. Le Colline del Prosecco di Conegliano e Va

    These are not soft, rolling wine hills. Between Conegliano and Valdobbiadene, the foothills of the Pre-Alps rise in steep hogback ridges, sharp crests of rock running east to west, with vines clinging to slopes so steep that much of the work is still done by hand. Grass-covered terraces called ciglioni hold the soil in place, and the result is a patchwork of vines, woods and small villages that UNESCO listed in 2019.
