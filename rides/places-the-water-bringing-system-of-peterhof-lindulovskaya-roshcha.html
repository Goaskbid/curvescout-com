Why this ride

A 394 km round trip from Ol’gino - roughly 5 h 40 in the saddle. The stops in between: Zelenogorsky, The E.I.Lopukhina's, Koltushskoe Highway and Lindulovskaya Roshcha. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

From The Water-bringing system of Peterhof to Zelenogorsky is 38 km, mostly on КАД. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 0 and 70 m. Watch for sand and spray near the water.

The stops

  1. 1. The Water-bringing system of Peterhof

    Start here.

    The fountains of Peterhof run without a single pump, and that is the cleverest thing about them. When Peter the Great planned his gardens in the 1710s and 1720s, engineers led by Vasily Tuvolkov worked out that the water of the Ropsha hills, about twenty kilometres to the south, stood high enough above the Gulf of Finland to feed the fountains by gravity alone. They dug canals, built ponds and laid pipes, so that the water flows from the heights to the Upper Garden and down to the Great Cascade, where it shoots high into the air. The system, still essentially the same, is part of the UNESCO listing for Saint Petersburg.

    Read the complete The Water-bringing system of Peterhof write-up.

  2. 2. Zelenogorsky

    38 km in. The ride passes about 6 km from it.

    The northern shore of the Gulf of Finland, west of Saint Petersburg, was for centuries the summer playground of the capital, and Zelenogorsk, which the Finns called Terijoki, was the best known of its resort towns.

    Read the complete Zelenogorsky write-up.

  3. 3. The E.I.Lopukhina's

    78 km in.

    North of Saint Petersburg's centre, the city thins out into a mix of suburbs, dachas, pine woods and the long marshy shores of the Karelian Isthmus.

    Read the complete The E.I.Lopukhina's write-up.

  4. 4. Koltushskoe Highway

    115 km in.

    East of Saint Petersburg, the Koltushskoe highway runs through suburban sprawl and countryside toward the village of Koltushi, and it belongs to a memorial landscape that ties the whole region together: the Green Belt of Glory, a chain of monuments marking the defensive line around Leningrad during the siege of 1941 to 1944, when nearly nine hundred days of blockade cost the lives of hundreds of thousands of civilians. Monuments, steles and memorials stand along the roads where the front lines and supply routes ran, including the Road of Life across Lake Ladoga.

    Read the complete Koltushskoe Highway write-up.

  5. 5. Lindulovskaya Roshcha

    201 km in.

    Out on the Karelian Isthmus, in a landscape of pine, birch and sandy soil, stands a grove of larches that looks entirely out of place. The Lindulovskaya Grove was planted in 1738 by an admiral, Fyodor Soimonov, who wanted to grow shipbuilding timber for the Russian navy, and he chose Siberian larch, a durable and rot-resistant wood. Some of the trees are nearly three centuries old and very tall, and the grove is protected as a natural monument, the oldest planted larch forest in Russia. Walking among the trunks, with their orange-grey bark, and the soft light filtering through the needles, is surprisingly moving.

    Read the complete Lindulovskaya Roshcha write-up.
