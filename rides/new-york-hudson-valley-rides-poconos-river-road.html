Why this ride

A rider’s loop: clean exit, memorable middle, calm return. A 249 km loop that starts and ends near Fordham, about 4 h 32 of riding. It goes by way of Highlands, Scenic stretch near Eltingville, Sayreville and near Pompton Lakes.

  2. 2. Highlands

    High ground is rare on the Jersey Shore, which is what makes Highlands special. The little town sits where the Shrewsbury River meets Sandy Hook Bay, at the foot of a steep wooded bluff, the Navesink Highlands, which rises abruptly from the water in one of the few real hills along the Atlantic coast south of New England. Down below are marinas, clam bars and a bridge over to the barrier beach; up above are old houses and the twin brownstone towers of the Navesink lighthouse.

  4. 4. Sayreville, on the way back

    Sayreville was built on clay. For more than a century the pits along the Raritan river supplied brick works that sent millions of bricks up the river and across the bay to build New York, and the town's lakes and ponds are mostly old clay pits filled with water. Later it became a gunpowder and chemical town, and later still the hometown of Jon Bon Jovi, which is the fact most visitors already know.

  7. 7. Near East Rutherford

    The Hackensack Meadowlands were once written off as a wasteland of marsh, dumps and smouldering garbage, the place where gangsters supposedly disposed of their problems. Today they are something unexpected: a recovering wetland, full of herons, egrets and ospreys, wrapped around a stadium that holds more than eighty thousand people. This finish lies at the edge of that landscape, near East Rutherford, Carlstadt and Moonachie.

  8. 8. back to Poconos River Road

    Fordham is the Bronx at its most characteristic, a dense, cheerful neighbourhood of apartment buildings, shops and street life around Fordham Road and the Grand Concourse. Fordham University was founded here in 1841, when the area was still countryside, and has been run by Jesuits since 1846, and the campus, with its Gothic stone halls and tree-lined lawns, is a surprising pocket of calm in a very busy borough. Edgar Allan Poe lived in a small cottage nearby in the late 1840s and wrote some of his last poems there, and the cottage still stands in a little park.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Scenic stretch near Eltingville to Sayreville, on the way back

Scenic stretch near Eltingville → Sayreville, on the way back is the technical section. Hairpins, gradients and mixed traffic can stack up quickly; use low drama inputs, stay wide of centre lines and expect buses or riders appearing exactly where optimism says they will not.

Food stop near Pompton Lakes to High point in Fair Lawn

Food stop near Pompton Lakes → High point in Fair Lawn is the logistics leg. Watch junctions, villages and speed changes, then let the roadbook do its job: move you out of the clutter without spending your best attention too early.

High point in Fair Lawn to Near East Rutherford

This High point in Fair Lawn → Near East Rutherford stretch is for rhythm and scenery. Keep your head up, let the view breathe, and resist turning every open bend into a target. Stop once properly rather than half-stopping everywhere.
