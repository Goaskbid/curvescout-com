Why this ride

165 km from Ares del Maestre to Ares del Maestre, about 2 h 51 of riding. It goes by way of Calçaes del matá, Abric del mas dels Ous, Valmayor V and back through Abric del mas dels Ous. The highest point is about 1,100 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cingle de la mola Remigia- Abric I

    A mola, in Catalan, is a flat-topped mass of rock, and the one that gives this site its name is a block of Cretaceous limestone that has been sliced out of the plateau by the Gasulla ravine, leaving a cingle, a long band of cliff, along its flank. If you like geology, this is a place for you: the layers are clear, the cliffs are weathered into pockets and overhangs, and the shelters that hold the paintings are simply the places where the rock fell away in the right shape. Benassal is a little over five kilometres to the east.

  2. 2. Calçaes del matá

    Ares del Maestrat sits on a crag so steep that the village seems glued to the rock, and below it, in the Gasulla gorge, the Cingle de la Mola Remigia holds some of the most famous Levantine paintings in Spain, scenes of archers, hunters and even an execution. Calçaes del Matà is a shelter in the same broad landscape near Albocàsser, and with the Gasulla and Valltorta sites it belongs to the World Heritage Site of Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin. The shelters here are reached on foot, and guided visits can be booked through the local museums.

  3. 3. Abric del mas dels Ous

    Xert is the kind of village you pass through on the way to somewhere else and then wonder why you did not stop. It lies in the Baix Maestrat in northern Castellón, below a ridge of bare limestone, with the old village climbing the slope and a newer part down by the road. The shelter called the Abric del Mas dels Ous, the shelter of the farmhouse of the eggs, is out in the dry hills nearby, between Xert, Canet lo Roig and Rosell.

  4. 4. Valmayor V

    The Matarraña, in the far east of Teruel province, is often called the Tuscany of Aragon, and for once the comparison holds. Stone villages crown hilltops above olive groves and vineyards, rivers run clear through limestone gorges, and the light in the evening is honey-coloured. Valmayor is a ravine in the hills here, between Valderrobres, Fuentespalda and the Catalan border, and its shelters hold prehistoric paintings, part of the UNESCO-listed Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin. As elsewhere, they are protected, and visits are best arranged through local tourist offices.
