  1. 1. Windhoek

    Windhoek sits at about 1,700 metres in the middle of Namibia, in a bowl of hills that gives it a cooler, drier climate than most of the country. It is the capital and by far the largest city, with a population approaching three hundred thousand, and a mix of German colonial buildings, modern shopping centres and sprawling townships. The Christuskirche, a Lutheran church with a pale stone facade, stands on a hill in the middle of the town and has become the city's best-known landmark.

  2. 2. Waterberg Namibia

    The Waterberg is a wall of red sandstone that rises about two hundred metres out of the flat bushveld of central Namibia, running for roughly fifty kilometres. Its flat top, fed by springs that seep from the rock, supports woodland and wildlife unlike anything on the plains below, and the escarpment, in rust and gold, glows at sunrise and sunset. The plateau is a national park, home to rare species such as sable and roan antelope, white and black rhinos, and a colony of Cape vultures that nests on the cliffs.
