Why this ride

From Bardon Mill it runs 141 km to Bardon Mill; allow about 2 h 18 of riding. It goes by way of Hadrian's Wall and vallum between the trac, Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 10, Roman fort and back through Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 10. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Housesteads fort

    Start here.

    Of all the forts on Hadrian's Wall, Housesteads is the one that most people picture. It stands on the edge of the Whin Sill, high above the moors, with long grey walls running along the ridge on either side and an enormous view in every direction. The Romans called it Vercovicium, and from the road it takes about ten minutes to walk up the hill from the car park and museum to the gate, with the wind in your face.

    Read the complete Housesteads fort write-up.

  2. 2. Hadrian's Wall and vallum between the trac

    20 km in.

    Just north of Hexham, the A road climbs out of the Tyne valley and meets the Roman frontier on a high, windy shoulder of Northumberland. Here, near Acomb, Wall and the crossing of the North Tyne at Chollerford, the line of Hadrian's Wall and its vallum runs across open farmland, sometimes visible as a low bank, sometimes followed by the road itself. Like every stretch of the frontier, it belongs to the UNESCO Frontiers of the Roman Empire, and this middle section is where the wall starts to feel serious, heading west into the wild central crags.

    Read the complete Hadrian's Wall and vallum between the trac write-up.

  3. 3. Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 10

    39 km in.

    Heddon-on-the-Wall is aptly named. Just west of Newcastle, on a ridge above the Tyne, the village sits right on the line of Hadrian's Wall, and at its eastern edge is the longest surviving stretch of the broad wall still visible in the east, a section of thick, solid masonry about a hundred metres long, complete with the foundations of a medieval kiln built into it. Wall mile 10 is the stretch around here, counting westward from Wallsend, and it is part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire World Heritage site. South of the wall, the vallum, a huge ditch flanked by earth mounds, can still be traced across the fields.

    Read the complete Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 10 write-up.

  4. 4. Roman fort

    70 km in.

    At the mouth of the Tyne, on a rise above the river, stands a reconstructed Roman gateway that looks surprisingly new. It belongs to Arbeia, the Roman fort at South Shields, which supplied the soldiers along Hadrian's Wall and was built, strategically, to guard the entrance to the river. Ships unloaded grain and other goods here, and the stores were carried along the Wall's line, so this was the logistical heart of the frontier. Reconstructed buildings, including the gatehouse, a barrack and a commander's house, show how it looked, and the museum holds finds from the site.

    Read the complete Roman fort write-up.
