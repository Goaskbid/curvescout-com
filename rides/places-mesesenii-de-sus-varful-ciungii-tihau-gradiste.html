Why this ride

A 72 km round trip from Aghireș - roughly 1 h 14 in the saddle. The stops in between: Brusturi - Certiae / LaTâlhăroasei Ruine, Tihău - Grădiște and back through Brusturi - Certiae / LaTâlhăroasei Ruine. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Meseșenii de Sus - Vârful Ciungii

    Some summits have a name that is hardly more than a shrug, and Vârful Ciungii is one of them. Vârf means summit or peak, and ciung is the Romanian word for a stunted or crooked thing, a word used for gnarled trees and bent hills. It stands above the village of Mesesenii de Sus, on the ridge of the Meseș Mountains, a low, long range of wooded hills in north-western Transylvania that forms a barrier between the Transylvanian plateau and the plains of the Pannonian basin to the west. It is not a spectacular range, with its highest points just under a thousand metres, but it has an importance out of proportion to its height.

  2. 2. Brusturi - Certiae / LaTâlhăroasei Ruine

    Zalău is the county town of Sălaj, a modern small city of about sixty thousand people that grew fast in the communist period, and the countryside around it is far more interesting than the blocks of flats. Brusturi, a few kilometres off, sits in the hills that lead up toward the Meseş mountains, and the name on our list points to a Roman fort called Certiae, one of the line of military posts that kept watch on the northern edge of Dacia, along with a ruin the locals link with an old legend of robbers.

  3. 3. Tihău - Grădiște

    The Someș river makes its way through the hills of north-western Romania between Cluj and Baia Mare, and near the small town of Jibou, in Sălaj county, it passes through a narrow valley guarded on its slopes by the remains of a Roman fort. The site, known locally as Grădiște, which simply means old fort, lies above the village of Tihău, close to Surduc and Gâlgău Almașului.
