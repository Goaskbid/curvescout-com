Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Moss Beach

    Moss Beach clings to a low bluff above the Pacific, a scatter of houses, cypress trees and fields on the San Mateo coast between Montara and Half Moon Bay. It is a small, quiet place, often wrapped in fog, where the sound of the surf is constant and the air smells of salt, seaweed and eucalyptus. For many riders, it is a first taste of the wild coast just south of San Francisco.

  2. 2. Near Daly City

    South of San Francisco's city limits, the land runs out abruptly. Daly City's houses, the ones with the colourful matching boxes that inspired the song Little Boxes, cover the hills right up to the edge of high, crumbling sea cliffs, and below them the Pacific pounds a long empty beach.

  4. 4. Scenic stretch near East Palo Alto

    East Palo Alto sits on the flat edge of San Francisco Bay, right where the Dumbarton Bridge launches its long, low span towards Fremont. To the west lies the wealth of Palo Alto and Menlo Park, home of Silicon Valley's giants; to the east, the salt ponds and marshes of the bay. It is a dense, busy corridor, cut by Highway 101, and for riders it is mostly a place to pass through, refuel and plan the escape to the hills.

  5. 5. Back to Mount Hamilton

    It is not especially high, but because it stands alone above the surrounding plains, the view from the top is among the widest in the world, taking in the Sierra Nevada on a very clear day and, some say, more of the earth's surface than any peak apart from Kilimanjaro. The claim is a local legend, and nobody quite tests it.
