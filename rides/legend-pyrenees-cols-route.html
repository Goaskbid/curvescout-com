Why this ride

curated scenic road. Pyrenees, hairpins, cycling history. A one-way ride of 122 km, Lourdes to Saint-Girons - roughly 2 h 13 in the saddle. On the way it passes Campan, Saint-Lary-Soulan and Bagnères-de-Luchon. The highest point is about 2,100 m. Col du Tourmalet, Col d Aspin and Col de Peyresourde are subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. The line favours views over speed.

  1. 1. Lourdes

    Lourdes is a town built around a miracle, and everything in it, from the souvenir shops to the shape of the streets, follows from that. In February 1858, a fourteen-year-old miller's daughter named Bernadette Soubirous reported seeing a lady in a grotto by the river Gave de Pau, an apparition she saw eighteen times in that year. The Church eventually accepted it, and the grotto became one of the most visited Catholic shrines in the world. Some three million pilgrims come each year, including many sick and disabled people, and the vast sanctuary has basilicas, processions and a candlelit procession in the evening that is moving whatever your beliefs.

  4. 4. Near Bagnères-de-Luchon

    Luchon is a spa town built by the Belle Époque on the back of hot sulphurous water, and it still has the grand, slightly faded air of a place that once hosted the fashionable world. Its thermal springs were used by the Romans and revived in the eighteenth century, when an intendant named d'Étigny laid out the long, tree-lined avenue, the Allées d'Étigny, that still forms the town's backbone. Today the avenue is lined with cafés, shops and old hotels with wrought-iron balconies, and the baths still operate, though they are mostly for visitors rather than kings.

  6. 6. Saint-Girons

    Saint-Girons is the market town of the Couserans, a green, little-visited corner of the French Pyrenees in the Ariège, and it has the quiet self-sufficiency of a place that has always been on the way to somewhere else. The Salat river runs through the middle, with old houses along its banks and a bridge from which you can see the mountains rising to the south. On Saturday mornings the market fills the main square and the surrounding streets with cheese, honey, cured meat and vegetables, and it is a good place to stock up for a picnic. About seven thousand people live here, so there is fuel, there are supermarkets and the other services of a real town.
