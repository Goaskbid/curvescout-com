Why this ride

  1. 1. Near Woodstock

    The Kancamagus Highway crosses the White Mountains of New Hampshire for some fifty-five kilometres between Lincoln and Conway, and for all that distance there is not a single gas station, restaurant or shop. That is what makes it special: one of the most beautiful roads in New England, through unbroken forest, along rivers and over a mountain pass, with nothing commercial to interrupt it. This stop lies near its western end, close to Lincoln and North Woodstock, where the road leaves the valley and begins to climb.

  3. 3. White Mountain Trail point 3

    Crawford Notch is a mountain pass with a bit of drama in its past. The road, Route 302, runs through a deep, narrow gap in the Presidential Range, hemmed in by steep rock walls and forested slopes, with a stream running beside it and waterfalls tumbling down the cliffs. It is the sort of place where you look up a lot, and where the weather feels close and serious. The notch has been a route through the mountains since the early nineteenth century, when a turnpike was cut through to link the Saco valley to the north country.

  4. 4. North Conway

    North Conway is the busy heart of the Mount Washington Valley, a small town of about two thousand people that fills at weekends with skiers, hikers, leaf-peepers and shoppers. The reason is the setting: the valley is a broad, flat basin ringed by the high, rocky peaks of the White Mountains, with the Saco River winding slowly through meadows, and the great granite cliffs of Cathedral Ledge and White Horse Ledge rising straight above the town. Mount Washington, the highest summit in the northeastern United States at 1,917 metres, lies to the northwest, and is famous for having some of the worst weather on earth.
