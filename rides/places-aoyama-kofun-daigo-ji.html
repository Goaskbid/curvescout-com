Why this ride

From Kashihara it runs 150 km to Kashihara; allow about 2 h 35 of riding. The stops in between: Gangô-ji, Daigo-ji and back through Gangô-ji. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Aoyama Kofun

    Aoyama Kofun is a modest tomb among many, one of the Furuichi mounds in southeastern Osaka that together make up a landscape of burials from the fifth century and thereabouts. The ride calls it by a nearer town, Kashihara, but the ground is the Kawachi plain, below the Kongo and Katsuragi hills, and the character is residential: low houses, small shops, canals of traffic on the main roads and quiet lanes that circle the mounds.

  2. 2. Gangô-ji

    Nara lives in the shadow of its grand temples, Todai-ji and Kofuku-ji, but Gango-ji is the one with the best sense of age. It started life in Asuka, in the late sixth century, as Asuka-dera, the first true Buddhist temple in Japan, founded by the powerful Soga clan. When the capital moved to Heijo-kyo in the early eighth century, the monks followed, and the temple was rebuilt on a new site in what is now the Naramachi district, with some of the original roof tiles carried across the plain and reused. You can still pick out the older, darker tiles on the roof of the main hall, a faint line of continuity across thirteen centuries.

  3. 3. Daigo-ji

    The oldest building in Kyoto does not stand in the city centre but in the southeastern hills, at the temple of Daigo-ji: a five storey pagoda completed in 951, which has survived fires, earthquakes and wars that destroyed almost everything else of its age in the city. The temple was founded in the ninth century on Mount Daigo, and its grounds spread from the foot of the mountain to the summit, linked by a steep path, with halls, gardens and sub temples along the way. It is part of the Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto World Heritage Site.
