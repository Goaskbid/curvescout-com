Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Turkistan

    Turkistan is the spiritual centre of Kazakhstan, a city of nearly a hundred thousand people in the hot, flat south, built around one great building. The Mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi, a twelfth-century Sufi poet and teacher whose ideas helped spread Islam among the Turkic nomads, rises from the middle of town with a huge blue-glazed dome and a portal covered in tilework. Timur ordered it built in the late fourteenth century, and it was never entirely finished, which gives its rough brick back a curious, unfinished honesty. It is a UNESCO site, and it is a pilgrimage destination for Kazakhs who consider three visits equal to one trip to Mecca.

  2. 2. Sauran

    Sauran is a city that has mostly turned into mounds, but what mounds. On the steppe northwest of Turkistan, not far from Kentau, the ruins of a medieval walled town rise from the plain, with long lines of mud-brick fortifications, bastions and gates still tracing a shape that can be read from a distance. For centuries this was one of the important towns on the northern branch of the Silk Road, a market and a fortress on the edge of the nomad world, and it changed hands between powers that wanted its walls.

  3. 3. Otrar

    Otrar is where a single diplomatic disaster helped change the history of Asia. In 1218, the governor of this city, an official of the Khwarezmian empire, seized a trade caravan sent by Genghis Khan and executed its merchants, and when the Mongol ruler's envoys protested, one of them was killed too. The Mongols responded with a campaign that destroyed Khwarezm and, in time, much of Central Asia. Otrar was besieged and sacked, and its governor reportedly met a grim end.
