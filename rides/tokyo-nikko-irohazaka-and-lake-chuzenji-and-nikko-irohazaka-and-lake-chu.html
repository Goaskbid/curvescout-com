Why this ride

390 km from Utsunomiya to Nikkō, about 8 h 25 of riding. On the way it passes Nikko shrine stop and Lake Chuzenji water view. The climbs: Irohazaka hairpins and Irohazaka ascent. It tops out at about 1,350 m. It is a high-mountain day.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Utsunomiya fuel gate

    Say gyoza in Japan and most people will say Utsunomiya. The capital of Tochigi has made the dumpling its civic emblem, with dozens of specialist shops, statues of dumplings in the station area and a popular rivalry with Hamamatsu over which city eats more of them. It is a cheerful, big-city sort of town, home to four hundred and fifty thousand people, and for riders it is the gateway to Nikkō, thirty-odd kilometres to the north-west.

  2. 2. Nikko shrine stop

    The shrines at Nikko are among the most lavish in Japan, and they are there because of a single man. Tokugawa Ieyasu, who unified the country and founded the shogunate that ruled for over two and a half centuries, died in 1616 and was enshrined here the following year, in a mountain setting selected for its sacred character. His grandson Iemitsu rebuilt the complex on a grand scale in the 1630s, and the result is Toshogu, a collection of buildings covered in gold leaf, carved animals, flowers and painted beams, set among very tall cedars.

  3. 3. Irohazaka hairpins

    The Irohazaka is the most famous road in Nikko, and one of the best known in Japan. It climbs from the town at about 640 metres to Lake Chuzenji at roughly 1,270 metres, in a series of forty-eight hairpin bends, each marked with a sign bearing one of the characters of the old Japanese alphabet, called iroha, which has forty-eight letters. The road is split into two one-way sections, with the original, built in the 1950s, carrying traffic down and a second, with twenty-eight bends, carrying traffic up, so you never meet oncoming vehicles on the corners.

  4. 4. Lake Chuzenji water view

    Lake Chuzenji lies at about 1,270 metres in the mountains above Nikko, and it was born in violence. Some twenty thousand years ago, an eruption of Mount Nantai, the cone that now rises above the northern shore, sent lava across the Daiya river and dammed the valley, and the water that built up behind it became the lake. The result is a deep, cold, clear body of water, shaped like a crescent, with wooded slopes plunging into it and no real beach.

  6. 6. Irohazaka ascent

    The Irohazaka climbs from Nikkō, at about six hundred metres, to Lake Chūzenji at roughly twelve hundred and seventy, and each bend is labelled with one of the forty-eight characters of the old Japanese iroha syllable poem, so you can tick them off in order like a very steep alphabet. The road is actually two roads, a one-way uphill route built in 1954 and a one-way downhill route added in 1965, so you meet the bends in one direction only and never face oncoming traffic on them.

  7. 7. Lake Chuzenji

    Lake Chuzenji lies at about 1,270 metres in the mountains above Nikko, and it was made by a volcano. Some twenty thousand years ago, Mount Nantai, a steep cone that now rises to nearly 2,500 metres on the north shore, erupted and its lava dammed the Daiya River, forming this long, clear, deep lake. The water is cold, even in summer, and it is stocked with trout and salmon, and the lake is surrounded by forest. Seen at dawn, with mist rising from the surface, it is a calm and lovely sight.

  8. 8. Kegon Falls

    Kegon Falls drops ninety-seven metres from the edge of Lake Chuzenji into a rocky gorge, and it is counted among the three great waterfalls of Japan. The lake's outflow, the Daiya river, simply runs to the lip of an ancient lava flow and falls straight down, creating a white column in a dark basin of volcanic rock. Around the main fall, smaller streams seep from the cliff face, and in winter some of them freeze into long icicles, a magnificent sight if you can bear the cold.

  9. 9. Nikko food

    Imaichi is the quiet partner of Nikko, a working town of sixty thousand people on the plain where the mountain roads meet the Kanto lowlands, and a very good place to eat after the climb to Chuzenji. Some of the trunks are as thick as a small car, and the shade under them is deep and green even at noon.
