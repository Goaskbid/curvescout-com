Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Laboe memorial view

    The Laboe naval memorial is the tallest thing on this coast by a long way, a slim brick tower, seventy-two metres high, that rises from a bluff at the mouth of Kiel Fjord and can be seen from far out at sea. It was built between 1927 and 1936 to commemorate German sailors who died in the First World War, and later its meaning was widened to remember seafarers of all nations who lost their lives at sea, a gesture that still stirs debate. You can climb the tower, or take a lift, for a view over the whole fjord, where ferries, warships and sailing yachts cross the water in a constant parade.

  2. 2. Schönberg coast road

    East of the Kiel Fjord the shore settles into the long, open sweep of the Probstei, and the coast road gives you the Baltic in small glimpses between dike and field. The most famous landmark of the area stands at the fjord's mouth: the naval memorial at Laboe, a tall, narrow brick tower, around eighty-five metres high, shaped like the stern of a ship, that can be seen from far across the water. It was built in the 1930s as a monument to German sailors and was reopened after the war as a memorial to seafarers of all nations, and a lift takes visitors to the top for a view over the fjord and the sea. Beside it lies a Second World War submarine, which can be visited.

  3. 3. Hohwacht bay stop

    Hohwacht Bay is a wide curve of the Baltic coast in eastern Holstein, with a long, sandy beach, low dunes and shallow, gentle water that warms quickly in summer. The land here is flat and green, with the sea on one side and the rolling hills of Holstein Switzerland on the other, and the light has that clear, northern quality that painters love.

  4. 4. Lütjenburg old-road reset

    The land around Lütjenburg is what geologists call young moraine, which means the last ice sheet only left about fifteen thousand years ago, and the ground still looks freshly rumpled. Instead of the flat plains of the west or the old, rounded hills of the south, this corner of Holstein is a patchwork of small hills, hollows and ponds, with farmland cut into fields by hedges and lines of trees. The old roads here follow the grain of the land, dipping into valleys, crossing little streams and climbing again, with none of the dead-straight grid of the modern bypasses.

  5. 5. Plön lake sweep

    A circuit of the Great Plön Lake is one of the nicest easy rides in Schleswig-Holstein, and the best part of it is that the lake never really leaves you. At times the water is a bright sheet ahead through the trees, at others a dark gleam behind a field, and now and then the road comes down to the shore itself, at a harbour, a landing stage or a beach. The lake is the largest in the state, wide and island-dotted, and the glacial hills around it give the road a pleasant rise and fall.

  6. 6. Back to Laboe

    By the time the road brings you back to Laboe, the day has usually done its work, and the town greets you with the particular tiredness of a seaside place at dusk. Fishing boats bump gently in the harbour, the promenade lights flicker on, and somewhere a ship's horn sounds out on the fjord. It is a pleasant place to end a ride, small enough that you can see everything in a few minutes.

  7. 7. Lütjenburg fuel gate

    Lütjenburg is a small, tidy market town on the Baltic side of Schleswig-Holstein, a few kilometres from the sea in a rolling country of fields, lakes and low hills. People here call the region Holstein Switzerland, a joke that is half serious, because by the flat standards of northern Germany the landscape counts as dramatic, with hills of a hundred metres or so and lakes in the hollows. About five and a half thousand people live in the town, and it has the feel of a place that has been a local centre for a long time.

  8. 8. Hohwacht bay view

    Hohwacht Bay is a wide, shallow curve of the Baltic between the Bay of Kiel and the Fehmarn Belt, and the view from the shore on a clear day opens a long, gentle arc of pale sand and low green land. The beach at Hohwacht is a broad one, backed by low dunes and a wooded cliff, and the water in front of it is so shallow that you can wade out a long way before it reaches your waist. That makes it good for families, and for anyone who wants a cool dip without the effort of swimming.

  9. 9. Heiligenhafen harbour

    The first thing you notice in Heiligenhafen is the smell, a mix of diesel, salt and smoked fish that tells you the harbour is still working. A small fleet of cutters ties up along the quay, and in the morning you may see men sorting the catch of herring, cod and flatfish before it goes to the fish shops and the stalls. The harbour sits at the end of a peninsula that pushes out into the Baltic, and the town spreads around it, with a seafront promenade, a long pier and a marina full of sailing boats.

  10. 10. Fehmarn bridge view

    Fehmarn is joined to the mainland by one of the most recognisable bridges in Germany, a steel arch about a kilometre long that spans the Fehmarnsund and carries both road and rail. Opened in 1963, it has an elegant, slightly improbable shape, and locals call it the coat hanger. The bridge was built as part of the so-called Bird Flight Line, a transport route linking Hamburg with Copenhagen via the island and a ferry to Denmark, and it has been the main way on and off Fehmarn ever since.

  11. 11. Eutin return lake

    The ride back toward Eutin at the end of the day has its own light. The sun is low, the colours deepen, and the lakes of Holstein Switzerland turn from blue to a deep pewter or a burnished gold, depending on the sky. The road comes in from the north through beech and farmland, past Kasseedorf and the small lakes, and the town appears with its church tower and palace roofs above the trees. On a still evening the water is like glass, and the only sound is the tick of the engine cooling.
