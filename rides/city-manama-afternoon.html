Why this ride

A 215 km round trip from Manama - roughly 3 h 54 in the saddle. On the way it passes Madinat Hamad 2 Burial Mound Field, Madinat Hamad 3 Burial Mound Field, Al Zubarah Archaeological Site and back through Madinat Hamad 3 Burial Mound Field. It crosses from Bahrain into Qatar - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Manama

    An afternoon in Manama begins with a lesson in geography that the map sometimes hides: the Al Zubarah of the ride's name is not in Bahrain. It is a ruined pearling and trading town on the north-west coast of Qatar, a short sea crossing away but with no road between the two countries, so no rider will get there from here. What links them is a family. In 1783 the Al Khalifa, who had built up Zubarah, moved across the water and took Bahrain, and their descendants still rule. So the afternoon in Manama is best spent seeing where that move led.

  2. 2. Madinat Hamad 2 Burial Mound Field

    Flat, pale and apparently empty, the central plain of Bahrain hides one of the oldest cemeteries on earth. Some four thousand years ago the island of Dilmun, as it was known to the Sumerians, buried its dead under thousands upon thousands of low stone mounds, and in places like Madinat Hamad and A'ali the mounds still stand in dense, grey fields. At one time there may have been several hundred thousand of them, which would have made Dilmun a famous necropolis in its day, and many have since been flattened by roads, housing and quarrying.

  3. 3. Madinat Hamad 3 Burial Mound Field

    Bahrain was once covered with burial mounds. Over thousands of years, between roughly 2200 BC and the early centuries AD, the people of Dilmun, an ancient civilisation that controlled trade in the Gulf between Mesopotamia and the Indus valley, built tens of thousands of tombs across the northern part of the island. Many have been lost to modern development, but some fields survive, and in 2019 a selection of them, including the Madinat Hamad mound fields, was inscribed as the UNESCO World Heritage Site of the Dilmun Burial Mounds.

  4. 4. Al Zubarah Archaeological Site

    Out on the north-west coast of Qatar, where the desert slides into a shallow turquoise sea, stand the low ruins of Al Zubarah, once one of the busiest pearling and trading towns in the Gulf. In the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the town thrived on pearls dived from the oyster beds offshore, traded on to India, Persia and beyond. Then it declined, was abandoned in the early twentieth century, and the sand covered it. Archaeologists have since uncovered streets, houses, a souk, mosques and a long town wall running in an arc around the old harbour. UNESCO inscribed the site in 2013.
