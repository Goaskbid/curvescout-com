Why this ride

249 km from Hanoi to Hanoi, about 4 h 18 of riding. It goes by way of Con Son Pagoda, Thanh Mai Pagoda, King Chu Cavern and Nham Duong Pagoda.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hanoi

    Hanoi starts the day with a roar, and the best advice is to join it calmly. This is a city of millions on the Red River, with more motorbikes than anyone has bothered to count, and the Old Quarter, with its thirty-six old guild streets named after the goods once sold there, is the loud, charming core. Hoàn Kiếm lake in the middle is where people jog, practise tai chi and sit eating ice cream at dusk, and the little Ngoc Son temple on its island is joined to the shore by a red wooden bridge.

  2. 2. Con Son Pagoda

    Côn Sơn lies in the pine-covered hills of Chí Linh, north-east of Hanoi, on the border of Hải Dương province, and it carries two of the great names of Vietnamese history. In the thirteenth century the Trần dynasty beat back three Mongol invasions, and its monks and commanders left a trail of temples, of which Côn Sơn, Kiếp Bạc and the sacred mountain of Yên Tử are the best known. Trúc Lâm, the Vietnamese Zen school founded by King Trần Nhân Tông, who gave up his throne to become a monk, has one of its centres here.

  3. 3. Thanh Mai Pagoda

    In the late thirteenth century, a Vietnamese king who had twice helped drive back Mongol invasions gave up his throne, put on a monk's robe and founded a Zen school of his own. Trần Nhân Tông's Trúc Lâm school, the Bamboo Grove sect, became the most distinctly Vietnamese form of Buddhism, and the temples connected with it are scattered across the hills of the north-east, from Yên Tử mountain to Côn Sơn and Kiếp Bạc. Thanh Mai pagoda, on a forested slope in the Chí Linh area, is one of these places, associated with the early patriarchs of the school, and it belongs to the Yên Tử, Vĩnh Nghiêm, Côn Sơn and Kiếp Bạc complex that UNESCO listed in 2025.

  4. 4. King Chu Cavern

    In the delta country east of Hanoi the land is mostly flat, a patchwork of rice paddies and ponds, so when limestone hills rise abruptly out of it they feel like a different world. Kinh Môn, in Hải Dương province, is one of those places, a small range of karst hills pitted with caves, and the Kinh Chủ cave, which I have placed under the name on the map, is one of them. Caves in northern Vietnam are often turned into shrines, with Buddha statues and incense in the dim light, and visitors come both for the religion and for the cool air inside.

  5. 5. Nham Duong Pagoda

    Out in the hills of Chí Linh, in Hai Duong province east of Hanoi, the land crumples into low forested ridges after the flat Red River delta, and this is where Nham Duong sits among the trees. The pagoda belongs to a cluster of temples tied to the Trần dynasty and its home-grown school of Zen, the Trúc Lâm tradition, whose three patriarchs taught in these hills during the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries. Thanh Mai, the other pagoda on the same ride, is part of that story too.
