Valles Calchaquies: Cachi to Cafayate

Why this ride

A one-way ride of 284 km, Cachí to Cafayate - roughly 3 h 54 in the saddle. The highest point is about 3,600 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cachi

    Cachí sits at around 2,300 metres in the Calchaquí valleys of Salta province, and everything about it seems to have been made quiet by altitude and dryness. White adobe houses line narrow streets, the plaza is shaded by trees, and the church has a roof and doors made from the wood of the cardón, a tall columnar cactus that grows on the slopes around. The backdrop is the Nevado de Cachi, a snow-capped peak in the range that stands above 6,000 metres.

  2. 2. Molinos

    Molinos sits at the bottom of a dry, wide valley in the province of Salta, about two thousand metres above the sea, with the Calchaquí mountains standing around it in bands of rust and ochre. The river that made the valley is mostly a braid of stones for most of the year, and the green is confined to a thin ribbon of poplars, vineyards and walled fields along it. Cachí is the nearest proper settlement to the north, roughly forty kilometres off, and Cafayate lies a long day's gentle riding to the south.

  3. 3. Cafayate

    Cafayate is a wine town at about sixteen hundred metres in the Calchaquí valleys of Salta province, in the dry north-west of Argentina, and it is the reward at the end of a long ride south. After days of high desert, salt flats and unpaved passes, you come down into a flat valley full of vines and suddenly there is shade, cold white wine and a plaza where people sit for hours. The mountains stand on both sides, brown and bare, and the light is so clear that distances seem shorter than they are.
