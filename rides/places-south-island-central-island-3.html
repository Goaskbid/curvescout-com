Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. South Island

    South Island is the southern of Lake Turkana's three island parks, a low, bare, volcanic outcrop in the south of the lake, near the settlement of Loiyangalani. The lake wraps around it, usually the colour of old jade, and when the wind is up the waves can be steep and brown. Birds nest on the island and crocodiles patrol the water. The land is stark. It is not a place people have lived in, and the interest lies in the feeling of looking out on a lake as large as a small sea, with nothing but desert on every side.

  2. 2. Lake Turkana National Parks

    Lake Turkana is known as the Jade Sea, and when the light is right the name is obvious: a long, narrow expanse of green-blue water lying across the desert, with black volcanic hills on one side and pale sand on the other. It is the largest permanent desert lake in the world and the biggest alkaline lake. The Omo River in Ethiopia supplies most of its water, and what flows in has nowhere to leave except by evaporation, so the lake is salty. Upstream dams on the Omo have raised concerns about how the water level will behave in the future, a worry that fishing families here follow closely.

  3. 3. Central Island

    Central Island is a volcanic cone in the middle of Lake Turkana, a small rough dot of black rock and red earth rising out of green water, and there is no road to it. Reaching it takes a boat from the western shore, which is a matter of local arrangement and good weather, because the lake can turn violent in minutes when the wind picks up. The island holds three crater lakes, and the largest is a vivid, quiet pool where Nile crocodiles breed in big numbers. It is among the strangest places in Kenya, steaming and barren, with cormorants nesting on ledges and the smell of sulphur on warm days.
