Why this ride

An 84 km round trip from Kronstadt - roughly 1 h 20 in the saddle. The stops in between: Northern battery No. 6, The Village of Olgino and back through Northern battery No. 6. The coast is the point of it.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

14 km from The Coastal fort “Shanetz” to Northern battery No. 6, mostly on КАД. An easy profile, never above 12 m. Best in the early light, before the traffic.

The stops

  1. 1. The Coastal fort “Shanetz”

    Start here.

    Shanetz sounds like a word for a small redoubt, and that is roughly what these coastal forts were: low, squat batteries put on islets and spits to cover the fairways into the Gulf of Finland. This one lies in the western approaches of Kotlin, on the shoals north of the island, and it belongs to the great ring of defences that earned Kronstadt its nickname as the key to Petersburg. The forts earned their keep during the Crimean War, when Allied ships came up the Baltic, took a look at the defences and decided not to risk an attack.

    Read the complete The Coastal fort “Shanetz” write-up.

  2. 2. Northern battery No. 6

    14 km in.

    Kronstadt's guns were never only on the island. Peter the Great founded the fortress on Kotlin Island in 1703 to shut the mouth of the Neva, and over the next two centuries the defences spread across the shallows of the gulf, onto artificial islands and onto the northern shore, where numbered batteries covered the channel from the Karelian side. Northern Battery No. 6 belongs to that system, a low earthwork-and-masonry position on the coast near Gorskaya and Aleksandrovskaya, the old Gulf resort belt west of Lisy Nos.

    Read the complete Northern battery No. 6 write-up.

  3. 3. The Village of Olgino

    31 km in.

    On the northern shore of the Gulf of Finland, just beyond the city's edge, Olgino is a place of pines, sand and low wooden houses, a seaside suburb that grew up a century and more ago as a retreat for Saint Petersburg families who wanted fresh air and the beach without travelling far. It is part of the World Heritage Site that wraps the historic city and its surroundings, which includes not only palaces but villages, parks and landscapes like this one.
