Why this ride

It goes by way of Ogasawara Islands, Ototojima and peripheral reefs, Minamijima and peripheral reefs and back through Ototojima and peripheral reefs. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Nakodojima and peripheral reefs

    No road leads to Nakodojima, and for a good reason: it lies in the Pacific about a thousand kilometres south of Tokyo, in the Mukojima group of the Ogasawara or Bonin Islands, and it is a small uninhabited rock surrounded by reef. The name is a joke the old mariners would have enjoyed. In this group of islands, Mukojima means bridegroom island, Nakodo means matchmaker, and the nearby Yomejima means bride, so the three islets together make a wedding party. Access to the Mukojima group is restricted, and visitors need a permit or must go with a licensed tour.

  2. 2. Ogasawara Islands

    About a thousand kilometres south of Tokyo, in the Pacific, a scatter of small volcanic islands sits on the road to nowhere. These are the Ogasawara Islands, also called the Bonin Islands, and they have never been joined to any continent. That isolation is the whole point. Snails, plants and insects have evolved here in ways found nowhere else, which is why UNESCO listed the islands in 2011 and why some people call them the Galapagos of the East.

  3. 3. Ototojima and peripheral reefs

    Let us be honest from the start: you will not ride a motorcycle on Ototojima. This small, uninhabited island lies in the Ogasawara archipelago, about a thousand kilometres south of Tokyo in the western Pacific, and it has no roads, no people and strict rules about who can land. What it does have is a remarkable natural world. The Ogasawara Islands, also known as the Bonin Islands, have never been connected to a continent, and their plants and animals evolved in isolation, producing many species found nowhere else. The archipelago was listed by UNESCO in 2011, and Ototojima is one of its most precious parts.

  4. 4. Minamijima and peripheral reefs

    A thousand kilometres south of Tokyo, in the Pacific Ocean, the Ogasawara Islands rise from the sea, a scattered chain of volcanic islands that have never been connected to a continent. Minamijima, a small uninhabited island just off Chichijima, is one of their jewels: a ring of jagged limestone around a sheltered cove, the Ogiura, with white sand, turquoise water and a sinkhole beach that looks like something from a dream. The island and its reefs are part of the Ogasawara Islands World Heritage site, listed by UNESCO in 2011.
