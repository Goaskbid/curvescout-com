Why this ride

From Junnar it runs 392 km to Junnar; allow about 6 h 46 of riding. It goes by way of Lohagad, Raigad, Rajgad and back through Raigad. The highest point is about 800 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Shivneri Fort

    Shivneri is a hill fort above the town of Junnar in the Sahyadri range, and its fame comes from a single event: Shivaji, the founder of the Maratha kingdom, was born here in 1630. The fort stands on a steep, flat-topped hill of black basalt, one of the many Deccan trap formations that give western India its distinctive stepped skyline. Seven gateways, each with strong walls and sharp turns, lead up the slope, and inside the fortifications are cisterns, a small mosque, a temple to the goddess Shivai after whom Shivaji is said to have been named, and a cluster of old buildings, including one said to mark the room of his birth.

  2. 2. Lohagad

    In the monsoon, Lohagad disappears into the clouds and reappears, drenched and green, with waterfalls pouring off every ledge. This hill fort stands on a ridge of the Western Ghats a short distance from Lonavla, between Mumbai and Pune, and its walls and gateways crown a basalt plateau a few hundred metres above the surrounding valleys. Its name means iron fort, and it is one of the forts associated with the Maratha ruler Chhatrapati Shivaji, which in 2025 were grouped together on the UNESCO list as the Maratha Military Landscapes.

  3. 3. Raigad

    Raigad is a flat-topped mountain in the Sahyadri, the Western Ghats of Maharashtra, with cliffs falling away on almost every side, and it was chosen by Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj as the capital of the Maratha kingdom. In 1674 he was crowned here, a ceremony that announced the Marathas as a sovereign power in the face of the Mughal empire, and the fort became the political heart of the young state. Today the summit holds the ruins of the palace, the market street with its rows of stone shop plinths, water tanks cut into the rock, and Shivaji's samadhi, which draws a steady stream of visitors who come as pilgrims rather than tourists.

  4. 4. Rajgad

    In the folded hills of the Sahyadri, south-west of Pune, a great flat-topped mountain rises above the valleys with sheer cliffs on all sides and walls running along its crest. This is Rajgad, the king's fort, and for about twenty-five years in the seventeenth century it was the capital of Shivaji, the Maratha warrior king who carved out an independent kingdom against the Mughal Empire and the sultanates of the Deccan. It is one of the most revered places in Maharashtra, and climbing it is something of a pilgrimage.
