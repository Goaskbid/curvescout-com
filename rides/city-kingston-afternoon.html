Why this ride

A 70 km round trip from Kingston - roughly 1 h 12 in the saddle. On the way it passes Blue and John Crow Mountains and back through Blue and John Crow Mountains.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kingston

    Kingston lies in a bowl between the Blue Mountains and a long sandbar called the Palisadoes, which curls out to shelter one of the finest natural harbours in the Caribbean. About a million people live in the city and its surrounds, and the first thing to remember is that Jamaica drives on the left, so if you arrive from Europe you are fine and if you arrive from anywhere else, take the first roundabout very carefully. Traffic in town is quick, honking is a language, and minibuses stop wherever somebody waves.

  2. 2. Blue and John Crow Mountains

    The Blue and John Crow Mountains rise behind Kingston to a ridge that is often wrapped in cloud, and the highest point, Blue Mountain Peak, stands at 2,256 metres, which makes it the tallest in Jamaica by a distance. The range was listed by UNESCO in 2015 for both its wildlife and its history, and the history is the unusual part. These slopes were a refuge for the Maroons, descendants of enslaved people who escaped the plantations and held the hills against British soldiers for generations, finally winning a treaty in the eighteenth century. Their communities still exist, and their tradition of independence is part of the landscape.
