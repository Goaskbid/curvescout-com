Why this ride

Switzerland's signed scenic driving route: 1,643 km linking twelve UNESCO sites, five alpine passes and twenty-two lakes. It is a marked touring route rather than a single road, so you meet it in pieces - the piece here follows the Seestrasse along the eastern shore of Lake Zurich through Küsnacht, Meilen and Stäfa before turning inland.

Scenic road chapters

Grand Tour of Switzerland

For 23 km this is Grand Tour of Switzerland, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it — expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Zürich

    Zurich is the polite, prosperous kind of city that keeps its history discreet. It lies at the northern end of Lake Zurich, where the Limmat river runs out of the lake and through the old town, and it began as a Roman customs post called Turicum on the hill now known as the Lindenhof. By the sixteenth century it was a centre of the Reformation under Huldrych Zwingli, who preached at the Grossmunster, the twin-towered church that still dominates the skyline. Across the river, the Fraumunster holds stained glass windows by Marc Chagall, and the light inside on a bright morning is worth a short detour.

    Read the complete Zurich write-up.

  2. 2. Near Embrach

    North of Zurich, in the quiet countryside between the airport and the Rhine, the Töss river runs its last few kilometres through a wooded valley before joining the big river at Tössegg. Around Freienstein and Rorbas the valley is narrow and steep-sided, with mixed forest of beech and spruce, and the ruins of Freienstein Castle sit on a wooded spur above the village. It is a surprisingly wild corner for a place so close to Switzerland's largest city, and it is where many Zurich riders go for a short afternoon loop.

  3. 3. Near Diessenhofen

    A covered wooden bridge, a medieval town gate and a river the colour of bottle glass: Diessenhofen is the kind of small Swiss town that makes you wonder why you are in such a hurry. It sits on the south bank of the Rhine in the canton of Thurgau, between Stein am Rhein and Schaffhausen, and the wooden bridge across to Gailingen in Germany is one of the loveliest border crossings anywhere, a long, dim timber gallery where your footsteps or your exhaust echo off the beams. The old town behind it is compact, with painted facades, oriel windows and a clock tower over the main street.

    Read the complete Near Diessenhofen write-up.

  4. 4. Konstanz

    Konstanz sits right where the Rhine leaves Lake Constance, and so close to Switzerland that Kreuzlingen's streets seem to flow into its own. The border runs through the built-up area with only a fence in places, and because the Germans built the old centre on the northern bank and the Swiss on the south, the two towns are practically one. This closeness is the first thing to know. The second is that the old city was never bombed in the Second World War, spared partly because of its position next to a neutral country, and as a result the medieval streets and merchants' houses survive intact.

    Read the complete Konstanz write-up.

  5. 5. Appenzell

    Every year on the last Sunday in April, the people of Appenzell Innerrhoden gather in the main square, men and women, many with a ceremonial sword or a bayonet at the hip, and vote with raised hands. The Landsgemeinde is one of the last open-air assemblies of its kind in Switzerland, and it takes place in the middle of this small town, which has been the capital of the half-canton for centuries. Women only gained a vote at the cantonal level here in 1990, after a federal court ruling, which tells you something about how conservative the valley is, and also how proud.

    Read the complete Appenzell write-up.

  6. 6. Vaduz

    Liechtenstein is a country you can cross in half an hour, a narrow strip of about twenty-five kilometres between the Rhine and the Austrian border, and Vaduz is its small, tidy capital with just over five thousand people. It sits under a steep hill crowned by the castle of Vaduz, a medieval fortress that is the home of the princely family, and its wide valley is closed in by the high wall of the Rätikon mountains. It is a country of banks, postage stamps, dental products and orderly lawns.

  7. 7. Chur

    At 593 metres, in a wide bend of the Rhine valley, Chur makes a strong claim to be the oldest town in Switzerland, with traces of settlement going back thousands of years and a Roman presence from the first century BC. It has been the seat of a bishop since late antiquity, and the bishop's court, with its cathedral, sits on a terrace above the old town. Down below, the narrow streets and squares of the Altstadt, with painted houses, fountains and arcades, are a pleasant place to wander after a morning in the saddle.

  8. 8. Near Davos

    Flüela tops out at 2,383 metres, a wide, bare saddle between the Schwarzhorn and the Weisshorn, and the first thing you notice when you get there is the silence. The road from Davos has climbed through the Flüela valley, first through pine and larch, then out onto open pasture, and now you are in a stony world of scree, snowfields and two small dark lakes beside the road. The old hospice at the top, a solid stone building, has served travellers for generations and still serves coffee, soup and cake to riders who arrive cold.

  9. 9. Near Samedan

    The Upper Engadine is the high valley where the Inn begins its long journey to the Danube, and around Samedan and Celerina it is at its most open: a flat valley floor at more than 1,700 metres, broad meadows, the river meandering between larch woods, and mountains on both sides rising to glaciers. The light here is famous. Painters such as Giovanni Segantini came for it in the nineteenth century, and the dry, clear air gives the valley more sunshine than almost anywhere in Switzerland. In autumn the larches turn gold, and the whole valley glows.

    Read the complete Near Samedan write-up.

  10. 10. Near Poschiavo

    The Bernina Pass, at 2,328 metres, crosses the high Alps between the Engadine and the Valposchiavo in south-eastern Switzerland, and it is a place of water and ice. On the broad, rocky summit lie two lakes, Lago Bianco and Lago Nero, separated by a low ridge that is also a great continental watershed: water from one side flows to the Inn and the Danube and eventually to the Black Sea, while water from the other flows to the Adda, the Po and the Adriatic.

    Read the complete Near Poschiavo write-up.

  11. 11. Near Teglio

    Teglio gave the Valtellina its name, so the locals say, and it also gave Italy one of its most satisfying dishes. Pizzoccheri, short, flat ribbons of buckwheat pasta cooked with potatoes and cabbage, then layered with melted Valtellina Casera cheese and garlic butter, were born in this hilltop village, and there is an academy here dedicated to defending the recipe. After a morning on the passes, a plate of it will fuel you for the rest of the day, or put you to sleep, depending on your constitution.

    Read the complete Near Teglio write-up.

  12. 12. Piazzatorre

    High up the Val Brembana, north of Bergamo, the valley narrows and the villages get smaller until you reach Piazzatorre, a little resort of a few hundred people on a sunny slope surrounded by spruce forest and meadows. In winter it has a modest ski area; in summer it is a place for walkers and families from the plains escaping the heat of Bergamo and Milan. For riders, it sits near the start of one of the finest climbs in the Bergamo Alps: the road over the Passo San Marco, which leaves the valley at Mezzoldo and climbs toward the Valtellina.

    Read the complete Piazzatorre write-up.

  13. 13. Lugano

    Lugano belongs to Switzerland by history and to Italy by temperament. It sits on the northern shore of a lake of the same name, in the canton of Ticino, with mountains, Monte San Salvatore and Monte Brè, rising straight from the water on either side. Italian is the language, the coffee is Italian, and the pace of life slows by a visible notch compared with Zurich. Ticino joined the Swiss Confederation only at the start of the nineteenth century, after centuries as subject territory of the Swiss cantons, and the town still has the atmosphere of a place with its own story.

    Read the complete Lugano write-up.

It is about as far from the bustle of the Gotthard as you can get while staying within a few kilometres of it.

  15. 15. Near Engelberg

    At 2,224 metres, the summit of the Susten Pass sits on the border between the cantons of Bern and Uri, under the glaciated peaks of the Sustenhorn and the Gwächtenhorn. Engelberg, the name on the label, lies on the other side of the Titlis massif, ten kilometres away as the crow flies but a long way round by road, so think of this as the pass itself, the highest point of one of the most beautiful mountain roads in the Alps.

  16. 16. Near Meiringen

    Between Innertkirchen and the Grimsel Pass, the Haslital is a narrow, steep valley of granite and forest, and this is where the road starts climbing in earnest. The landscape is raw and powerful, shaped by glaciers that filled the valley during the ice ages and left behind smooth, rounded slabs of granite that gleam in the sun.

  17. 17. Interlaken

    Interlaken means between the lakes, which is exactly where it sits: a flat strip of land on the Aare, wedged between Lake Thun and Lake Brienz, with the Bernese Oberland piled up behind. On a clear day the Jungfrau fills the end of the main avenue, and visitors line up to take pictures of that view across the green lawn of the Höhematte, where paragliders land.

  18. 18. Near Leukerbad

    The road up to Leukerbad feels like it is going nowhere, and then it arrives at a wall. From Leuk in the Rhône valley, it climbs north through the Dala gorge, past the village of Inden, in a series of hairpins and tunnels cut into the rock, and finally opens into a high basin at about 1,400 metres, ringed by sheer limestone cliffs that rise more than a thousand metres above the village. The wall of rock is the Gemmi, and the only way over it, unless you take the cable car, is a footpath carved into the face.

  19. 19. Sitten

    Two hills rise from the floor of the Rhône valley at Sion, and on top of each stands a medieval landmark: the fortified church of Valère on one, the ruined castle of Tourbillon on the other. They give the capital of the Valais one of the most striking skylines in Switzerland, and around them the old town, with its narrow lanes, squares and cafés, has a relaxed, almost southern feel. Sitten is the German name; most locals speak French and call it Sion.

  20. 20. Near Aigle

    Here the Rhône valley is flat, straight and wide, a broad plain between two walls of mountains, and the river runs canalised between high dykes toward Lake Geneva, a few kilometres downstream. On the east side, Aigle sits at the foot of vineyards that climb the slopes in terraces, with its castle, a turreted white fortress surrounded by vines, standing at the edge of town. On the west side, the villages of Vionnaz and Collombey sit at the foot of the Chablais Alps, and the Dents du Midi, a jagged ridge of peaks more than three thousand metres high, dominate the view up the valley.

  21. 21. Near Bussigny

    The Gros-de-Vaud is the breadbasket of western Switzerland, a rolling plateau of wheat and rapeseed between Lausanne and the foot of the Jura, and the Venoge river cuts through it in a winding green valley. Here, around Penthalaz and Cossonay, the river runs in a deep wooded ravine below the hilltop town of Cossonay, an old medieval market town with a church tower that is visible for miles. The landscape is gentle and pastoral, with farms, orchards, small woods and villages of stone houses with big barns.

  22. 22. Erlach

    Erlach is a one-street town with a castle on top and a lake at the bottom, and that is most of its charm. It sits at the south-western tip of Lake Biel, where the Jura comes down to the water on the far shore, and its old town climbs a short, steep cobbled lane from the harbour up to the castle, a solid medieval fortress that now houses a school. The houses along the lane are of yellowish Jura limestone, with arcades at street level and red-tiled roofs, and from the top you look out over the lake and the vineyards on the slopes of the Jura opposite.

  23. 23. Biel/Bienne

    Biel is the only city in Switzerland where you can order a coffee in either German or French and nobody blinks, because it is officially bilingual: street signs come in pairs, schools teach in both languages, and conversations switch mid-sentence. Bienne in French, Biel in German, it is a working city of about fifty thousand people at the north-east end of Lake Biel, and it has a confidence and a grittiness that sets it apart from the polished cities of the Swiss plateau.

  24. 24. Basel

    Basel is the city where Switzerland, France and Germany meet, and you can stand at the point where the three borders converge, on the Rhine in the harbour district, and see all three countries at once. The Rhine is the heart of the city: it sweeps through in a broad curve, dividing the old town of Grossbasel on the south bank from Kleinbasel on the north, and on summer evenings thousands of people float downstream in the current, holding their clothes and phones in waterproof fish-shaped bags called Wickelfisch.

  25. 25. Near Lenzburg

    Schloss Lenzburg crowns a rounded hill above the town, so prominent that you can see it from miles away on the motorway, and it has been watching over this part of the Aargau since the eleventh century. The castle has passed through the hands of counts, the Habsburgs, the city of Bern and private owners, and today it is a museum, with gardens and views over the Seetal and the Jura.
