Why this ride

From Zaragoza it runs 452 km to Zaragoza; allow about 7 h 48 of riding. The stops in between: Iglesia de Santa María, Forau del Cocho and back through Iglesia de Santa María. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Zaragoza

    Zaragoza is a big, grown-up city on the Ebro, halfway between Madrid and Barcelona, and it has a no-nonsense character that suits a motorcyclist. It is the capital of Aragón, with some six hundred and seventy thousand people, and it sits in a wide, dry basin where the Ebro has laid down a flat plain bordered by low hills. The wind here is famous. The cierzo blows hard from the north-west, sometimes for days, and can push a bike sideways on an open bridge, so check the forecast and keep a firm grip on the bars when crossing the river.

  2. 2. Iglesia de Santa María

    Somewhere in the folds of the Sierra de Algairén, south-west of Zaragoza, a bell tower pokes up above a cluster of ochre roofs, and that is your Iglesia de Santa María. Half the villages of Aragon have a church of that name, and this one does the job the others do: it marks the middle of a small place where the shop, the bar and the plaza all orbit the same square of stone. The nearest neighbours on the map are Cosuenda, Almonacid de la Sierra and Alpartir, each a few hundred souls, each about nine kilometres off.

  3. 3. Forau del Cocho

    South of Huesca the land opens out. The Pyrenees are behind you, a snowy line on a clear day, and ahead stretches the gently rolling cereal country that eventually turns into the dry steppes of Los Monegros. Forau del Cocho lies in this transitional landscape, between Torres de Barbués, Almuniente and the outskirts of Huesca itself, a place where the only relief is the occasional low ridge or gully cut into soft rock.
