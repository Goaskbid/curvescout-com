Why this ride

From Bárcabo it runs 326 km to Bárcabo; allow about 5 h 37 of riding. It goes by way of Abrigo de Los Gitanos, Hayedos de Navarra - Aztaparreta and back through Abrigo de Los Gitanos. The highest point is about 1,450 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mascún III

    The Sierra de Guara is among the great limestone landscapes of Spain, and the Mascún gorge, cut deep into its southern flank, is one of the best known. The river has worn a long, narrow canyon, with walls hundreds of metres high, and at its head stands the little village of Rodellar, which has become a pilgrimage place for climbers from across Europe. Mascún III lies about fifteen kilometres from Alquézar and farther still from any big town, and it is one of a series of painted shelters in the canyon. They carry figures in the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain. Another ride connects it to the beech woods of the Hayedos de Navarra, which tells you how far these routes travel.

  2. 2. Abrigo de Los Gitanos

    The road north-west out of Huesca toward Pamplona runs along the foot of a dramatic wall of rock. To the north are the Pre-Pyrenean sierras, and studded along them are some of Aragón's most striking sights: the Castle of Loarre, a superbly preserved Romanesque fortress on a spur, and the Mallos de Riglos, huge red conglomerate towers rising straight out of the hillside. The rock shelter known as the Abrigo de los Gitanos lies in this country, between Ayerbe, Loarre and Luesia.

  3. 3. Hayedos de Navarra - Aztaparreta

    Aztaparreta is a beech forest that has been left alone. High in the Pyrenees of Navarre, at the head of the Roncal valley, in the karst massif of Larra-Belagua on the border with France, this forest has never been logged, and its trees, some of them centuries old, grow, die and decay naturally. It is one of the components of the Ancient and Primeval Beech Forests of the Carpathians and Other Regions of Europe, a World Heritage Site that brings together beech forests from across the continent, from the Carpathians to the Pyrenees. Walking through it is like entering a cathedral, with tall grey trunks, a green canopy high above, and a thick carpet of moss and fallen leaves.
