Why this ride

A 72 km round trip from Velez Rubio - roughly 1 h 14 in the saddle. On the way it passes Yedra, Izquierda E. de Santonje and back through Yedra. The highest point is about 1,250 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Las Covaticas II

    A covatica is a little cave, and the diminutive is charming, because the shelters of Las Covaticas are small, shallow hollows in the rock rather than great vaulted caverns. They lie in the high, dry country of north Almería, far into the hills beyond Vélez-Blanco, where the land climbs toward the Sierra de María and the horizon is made of bare ridges and pale fields of cereal. It is a very different Spain from the beach resorts of the south coast, and the people who live here are used to long winters and big skies.

  2. 2. Yedra

    In the souvenir shops of Almería there is a little stick figure with outstretched arms and a rainbow arching over his head, and it comes from here. This is the Indalo, a prehistoric painting found in the Cueva de los Letreros near Vélez-Blanco, and it has become the emblem of the whole province, painted on houses and doors and carved on bracelets. Nobody knows quite what it meant, though some say it was a charm against storms. Yedra is a nearby shelter, a short drive from the village, and belongs to the same group of rock art sites that UNESCO lists as part of the Mediterranean Basin tradition.

  3. 3. Izquierda E. de Santonje

    The Indalo is everywhere in Almería: a little stick figure holding an arc over its head, painted on houses, sold as a souvenir and used as the symbol of the province. It comes from a rock shelter near Vélez-Blanco, the Cueva de los Letreros, where it was painted in prehistoric times, and the area around the village is rich in rock art, part of the World Heritage rock art of the Mediterranean. The shelters around Santonje, near the villages of María and Vélez-Blanco, are among them.
