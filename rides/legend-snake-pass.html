  2. 2. Near Glossop

    The summit of the Snake Pass, at a little over 500 metres, is a bleak and beautiful place: a broad saddle of peat moorland between the high plateaus of Kinder Scout to the south and Bleaklow to the north, with heather, cotton grass and dark, eroded peat hags stretching away on both sides. The A57 crosses here on its way between Glossop and Sheffield, and the Pennine Way, Britain's oldest long-distance footpath, crosses the road at the top. On a clear day the view east down the valley of the Snake is wide and wild; on a bad day you can barely see the road.

  3. 3. Near Hope Valley

    Below the Snake Pass, on the eastern side, the A57 crosses the Ladybower Reservoir on a long viaduct, and in a few seconds you go from bare moorland to a scene that looks almost Alpine: dark water fingering into wooded valleys, steep slopes of conifers, and the stone walls of the dams further up the valley. Ladybower and its neighbours, Derwent and Howden, form a chain of reservoirs in the Upper Derwent Valley that supply water to the cities of the East Midlands, and on a calm morning their surfaces reflect the hills perfectly.
