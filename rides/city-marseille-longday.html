Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Marseille

    Marseille is France's oldest city, founded around 600 BC by Greek sailors from Phocaea, and it has never entirely lost its Mediterranean temper. The Vieux-Port, the old harbour, is the heart: a long rectangle of water lined with fishing boats and yachts, a market where fishermen sell the morning's catch, and the quays where restaurants serve bouillabaisse, the fish stew that is the city's pride. The district of Les Grands Carmes lies a little to the north, near the old port and the Joliette docks, where warehouses have been turned into offices and flats.

  2. 2. Arles

    Arles is where the Rhône splits in two and the Camargue begins, and for more than two thousand years it has been a city of power, art and sun. The Romans made it one of the great cities of Gaul, building an amphitheatre that still hosts events, a theatre, baths and an underground gallery, the cryptoportiques, and the Roman and Romanesque monuments of Arles are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Romanesque church of Saint-Trophime, with its carved portal and cloister, is one of the finest in Provence.

  4. 4. Historic Centre of Avignon: Papal Palace

    For most of the fourteenth century, the popes lived in Avignon rather than Rome. In 1309, Pope Clement V, a Frenchman, settled here to escape the turmoil of Italian politics, and his successors built a vast palace, the Palais des Papes, the largest Gothic palace in Europe, with massive walls, towers and halls, more fortress than residence. Seven popes reigned here until 1377, when the papacy returned to Rome, and then rival popes continued to rule from Avignon during the Great Schism. The palace, the cathedral, the ramparts and the famous bridge, the Pont Saint-Bénézet, were listed by UNESCO in 1995.
