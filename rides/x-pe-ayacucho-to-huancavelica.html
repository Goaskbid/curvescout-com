  1. 1. Ayacucho

    Ayacucho is one of the great colonial cities of the Andes, a town of about 140,000 people in a high, dry valley at around 2,750 metres, and it has a reputation for churches, crafts and Holy Week processions. The Quechua name is often translated as corner of the dead, and the history of the region is, in some respects, rooted in conflict. In 1824, the Battle of Ayacucho, fought on a high plain nearby, ended Spanish rule in South America, and the region later suffered badly during the internal conflict of the 1980s and 1990s. Today, the city has recovered its calm, and the old centre is a pleasure to walk around.

  2. 2. Huancavelica

    Huancavelica is a high, cold city of about forty thousand people, standing at nearly 3,700 metres in the central Andes, and its fortune was built on a metal that is now regarded as a poison. Under the Spanish, the nearby mine at Santa Bárbara produced mercury, which was used to extract silver from the ore of Potosí, and for centuries it was one of the most important mines in the colonial world. The conditions for the miners, many of them forced labourers, were appalling. Today the city is quiet, with a handsome plaza, a cathedral and a collection of colonial churches built in the time of that wealth.
