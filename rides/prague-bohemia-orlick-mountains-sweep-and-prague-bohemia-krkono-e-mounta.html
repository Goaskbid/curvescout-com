Why this ride

From Všenory it runs 812 km to Kouřim; allow about 17 h 04 of riding. The stops in between: Rudná, Scenic stretch near Slaný and near Roudnice nad Labem. The climbs: near Řevnice and near Votice. Much of it runs through woodland.

  7. 7. Food stop near Kouřim

    Long before Prague dominated Bohemia, Kouřim was one of its centres of power. On a promontory above the Kouřimka stream lie the earthworks of Stará Kouřim, a vast early medieval Slavic hillfort that archaeologists consider one of the most important in the country. The later town, founded in the thirteenth century, still has stretches of its walls, a Gothic gate and the sturdy church of St Stephen, which together give this small place a strong sense of age.

  8. 8. Back to Orlické Mountains

    Two rivers meet a short ride south of Prague, and this point sits right beside the meeting. The Vltava, the great river of Bohemia, swings through the villages of Vrané and Davle on its way north to the capital, and at Davle it takes in the Sázava, which has come all the way from the Highlands to the east. The label calls this a return to the Orlické Mountains, but the Orlické range is far away on the Polish border; here you are in the green gorge country just south of the city, in the middle of what Czechs think of as their riverside weekend.

  10. 10. Near Jablonné v Podještědí, on the way back

    The long ridge of Ještěd rises to the east, and on its summit stands one of the most unusual buildings in Central Europe: a hyperboloid tower that combines a television transmitter and a hotel, designed by the architect Karel Hubáček and completed in 1973, its silver cone visible for miles around. This stop is on the western side of the ridge, near the small town of Jablonné v Podještědí, in a landscape of hills, forests, ponds and villages on the way back from the Krkonoše.

  11. 11. Food stop near Benešov nad Ploučnicí

    Few small towns can boast two Renaissance châteaux, but Benešov nad Ploučnicí has both, an upper and a lower castle standing side by side above the Ploučnice river. They were built in the sixteenth century by the Wartenberg and Salhausen families, and their gables, painted rooms and courtyards make a surprising sight in a town of a few thousand people. Guided tours run in season.

  12. 12. Photo stop near Kožlany

    Kožlany is a small town on the high, rolling plateau north of Plzeň, and if the name rings a faint bell it is because Edvard Beneš was born here in 1884. The man who would become Czechoslovakia's second president, and who led the government in exile during the Second World War, grew up in a farming family in this quiet corner of western Bohemia. A memorial in the town marks the connection.

  13. 13. Near Třebotov

    Just beyond Prague's south-western edge, the landscape changes abruptly. The city's apartment blocks give way to wooded hills and limestone cliffs, and the Berounka river winds through a valley lined with summer cottages, villages and the occasional factory. You start and finish on the plateau above the valley, near Třebotov and Černošice, a pleasant, practical place to start and finish rides without fighting through the centre of the capital.

  14. 14. Scenic stretch near Sedlčany

    Sedlčany sits in the Central Bohemian uplands south of Prague, in a landscape of low hills, forests, fields and fishponds. The town itself is modest, with a market square, a Gothic church and a scattering of shops and cafés, but the countryside around it is the real attraction.

  15. 15. High point near Votice

    The hills around Votice belong to the Central Bohemian uplands, a rumpled country of fields, fishponds and small forests between Prague and Tábor. Votice itself is a modest market town with a square, a church and a few thousand inhabitants, a quiet place where the main event of the day might be the arrival of the bus. It has a bakery, shops and fuel, which is all a rider really needs from it.

  16. 16. Back to Krkonoše Mountain

    The title of this stop is a mountain range far to the north, but you are in the rolling farmland of central Bohemia, east of Prague, around the small town of Kouřim. The Krkonoše, the Giant Mountains on the Polish border, lie more than a hundred kilometres away, and this is the quiet, gentle country you cross on the way home. The land is a mixture of fields, orchards and woodland, with long views across the plain and the occasional church spire.
