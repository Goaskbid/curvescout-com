Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kolsai Lakes

    Kolsai Lakes are three alpine lakes strung out in a valley of the Kungey Alatau range, in the far south-east of Kazakhstan, close to the Kyrgyz border. The first lies at around 1,800 metres, and the second, reached by a longer walk, at about 2,250, with a third higher still in the mountains. The water is a deep, cold turquoise, held between steep slopes of dark spruce, and the reflections of the forest and the sky in the still surface make a memorable sight. The lakes were formed by landslides that blocked the valley, long ago.

  2. 2. Saty

    Saty is a village at the end of a good road, and it looks it. Here the steppe gives way to green pastures dotted with spruce, and in summer, herders bring their horses and sheep up from the valleys and live in yurts on the hillsides. The air is clean and cold, the skies are huge, and the few buildings look small beside the mountains.

  3. 3. Charyn Canyon

    The Charyn river has spent roughly twelve million years cutting a canyon through the red plain of south-eastern Kazakhstan, and the result is one of the great surprises of Central Asia. The gorge runs for about a hundred and fifty kilometres, reaching depths of around three hundred metres, and the most visited stretch is the Valley of Castles, where wind and water have carved the red sandstone into towers, arches and ramparts that seem to have been built by giants. At sunset, the whole thing glows deep orange and the shadows run purple.
