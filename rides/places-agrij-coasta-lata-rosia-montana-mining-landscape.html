Why this ride

From Stârciu it runs 401 km to Stârciu; allow about 7 h 26 of riding. The stops in between: Gilău - La Castel, Roșia Montană Mining Landscape and back through Gilău - La Castel. It tops out around 1,150 m. It keeps close to the shore for long stretches.

The roads

Agrij - Coasta Lată → Gilău - La Castel

The 82 km between Agrij - Coasta Lată and Gilău - La Castel, mostly on DN1F. Over the top at 527 m - roughly 300 m up and 420 m down. Water alongside for much of it - pick one good pull-out and stop.

The stops

  1. 1. Agrij - Coasta Lată

    Start here.

    The Meseș mountains are a modest ridge by Carpathian standards, but for the Romans they were a natural wall, and they built their frontier along it. The north-western edge of Dacia ran along these hills, with forts, watchtowers and walls guarding the passes from the plains of the Barbaricum beyond. Agrij - Coasta Lată, near the villages of Stârciu, Buciumi and Meseșenii de Jos, is one of the watchtower sites named in the World Heritage inscription of 2024. Nearby, at Buciumi, are the remains of a fort, and a little further north, at Moigrad, lies Porolissum, the largest Roman site in the region, with a reconstructed gate.

  2. 2. Gilău - La Castel

    82 km in.

    Gilău is a market village on the Someșul Mic, the first real settlement west of Cluj-Napoca on the road toward the Apuseni mountains, and the place name La Castel says it plainly: there was a fortification here, and there still is one, layered through the centuries. Under the later castle grounds, archaeologists found a Roman auxiliary fort guarding the valley route into Dacia from the west, and the medieval and early modern castle that followed sits on the same strategic ground. Rome, Hungarian nobles and later owners all made the same calculation about this spot.

    Read the complete Gilău - La Castel write-up.

  3. 3. Roșia Montană Mining Landscape

    201 km in.

    Gold has been mined at Roșia Montană, in the Apuseni mountains of western Romania, for over two thousand years. The Romans, who called the place Alburnus Maior, dug an extensive network of galleries into the mountains to extract the gold, and some of these galleries survive, along with wax tablets found in the mines, recording contracts and daily life. In 2021, after years of controversy, the mining landscape of Roșia Montană was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
