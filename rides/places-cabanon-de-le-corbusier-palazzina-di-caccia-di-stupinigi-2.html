Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cabanon de Le Corbusier

    The cabin is smaller than you expect. Le Corbusier built his Cabanon in 1951 on a rocky shore at Roquebrune-Cap-Martin, a single room of about three and a half metres square, panelled in pine and furnished with fitted beds, a table and a few shelves. It was a retreat, a place where one of the most famous architects of the century could work and swim, and he spent a part of every summer here for years. He died in 1965 swimming in the bay just below, at the age of seventy-seven.

  2. 2. Vineyard Landscape of Piedmont: Langhe-Roe

    Barolo the village has a few hundred inhabitants and a castle; Barolo the wine has a worldwide reputation built on the Nebbiolo grape and on the peculiar soils and slopes of these hills south of Alba. Around the village, the Langhe rise in steep ridges striped with vineyards, each hill crowned with a village, a castle or a church tower: La Morra, Monforte d'Alba, Serralunga, Castiglione Falletto, Novello. In 2014 UNESCO listed the vineyard landscapes of the Langhe, Roero and Monferrato for the way centuries of winemaking have shaped every slope, every row, every cellar.

  3. 3. Palazzina di Caccia di Stupinigi

    On the southern edge of Turin, at the end of a long straight avenue lined with trees, a bronze stag stands on the dome of a palace that looks like a star dropped on the plain. The Palazzina di Caccia di Stupinigi was built from 1729 by the architect Filippo Juvarra for King Vittorio Amedeo II of Savoy as a hunting lodge, though the word lodge hardly does justice to its grand halls, frescoed ceilings and sprawling wings. It is part of the World Heritage Site of the Residences of the Royal House of Savoy, inscribed in 1997.
