Why this ride

From Fujikawaguchiko it runs 369 km to Fujikawaguchiko; allow about 5 h 33 of riding. It goes by way of Shiraito no Taki waterfalls, Fuji Sengen-jinja Shrine, Fujisan Hongu Sengen Taisha Shrine and Mihonomatsubara pine tree grove. It tops out around 1,050 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

The roads

Lake Kawaguchiko → Shiraito no Taki waterfalls

38 km from Lake Kawaguchiko to Shiraito no Taki waterfalls on 国道139号. Mostly downhill - about 480 m down, from a high point of 980 m. Best in the early light, before the traffic.

The stops

  1. 1. Lake Kawaguchiko

    Start here.

    You can ride around Kawaguchiko in a morning and spend the next three days waiting for Mount Fuji to come out of the cloud. That is the local joke, and it holds a grain of truth: the volcano, at 3,776 metres, makes its own weather, and the summit often hides until the cold, clear hours just after dawn, especially in winter. Lake Kawaguchiko is the most visited of the Fuji Five Lakes, sitting at about 830 metres on the north side of the mountain, in the town of Fujikawaguchiko, and the classic view is from the northern shore with the cone reflected in still water.

    Read the complete Lake Kawaguchiko write-up.

  2. 2. Shiraito no Taki waterfalls

    38 km in.

    The water that falls at Shiraito has spent a long time underground. Rain and snowmelt seep into the porous volcanic rock of Mount Fuji, travel through the layers for years, and emerge from a cliff face southwest of the mountain as hundreds of thin streams, pouring over a curved wall about twenty metres high and some one hundred and fifty metres wide. The name means white threads, and the effect is just that: a curtain of silvery strands falling into a clear pool, with the Fuji-ko pilgrims once performing purification rituals here. The falls are part of the Fujisan World Heritage Site, and the neighbouring Otodome falls, a single powerful cascade, roar a short walk away.

    Read the complete Shiraito no Taki waterfalls write-up.

  3. 3. Fuji Sengen-jinja Shrine

    87 km in.

    On the eastern side of Mount Fuji, in the village of Subashiri, a shrine has stood at the start of the pilgrims' path for more than a thousand years. The Fuji Sengen shrine, often called Higashiguchi Hongū, the eastern main shrine, marks the beginning of the Subashiri trail to the summit, and pilgrims traditionally prayed here before starting their climb. The shrine is set in a forest of tall cedars and old trees, with a red gate, wooden halls and a quiet, spiritual atmosphere, and it is one of the components of the Fujisan UNESCO site, recognised as part of the mountain's cultural and religious landscape.

    Read the complete Fuji Sengen-jinja Shrine write-up.

  4. 4. Fujisan Hongu Sengen Taisha Shrine

    143 km in.

    Mount Fuji has been worshipped as a sacred mountain for as long as people have lived beneath it, and the Fujisan Hongū Sengen Taisha in Fujinomiya is the head shrine of that worship, the principal sanctuary of over a thousand Sengen shrines across Japan. Its main hall, rebuilt in the early seventeenth century under the patronage of Tokugawa Ieyasu, has an unusual two-storey design, painted in vermilion and set among tall trees. The shrine is dedicated to Konohanasakuya-hime, a goddess associated with the mountain, with blossoms and with fire.

    Read the complete Fujisan Hongu Sengen Taisha Shrine write-up.

  5. 5. Mihonomatsubara pine tree grove

    185 km in.

    Miho is a thin hook of land curling out into Suruga Bay, and Mihonomatsubara is the pine grove that covers it: tens of thousands of dark pines behind a long beach of grey, pebbly sand. People come for the view. On a clear day Mount Fuji sits on the other side of the bay, framed by the trees and the curve of the shore, and that image has been painted, printed and photographed for centuries. It is why the grove was included in the UNESCO listing for Fuji in 2013, as an artistic setting for the mountain rather than as a piece of nature in itself.
