Why this ride

curated scenic road. alpine lakes, larch forest, mountain villages. A one-way ride of 45 km, Silvaplana to Zernez - roughly 49 min in the saddle. The highest point is about 2,500 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Scenic road chapters

Julier Pass Road

Slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did. Water arrives beside the road and stays for 6 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go.

  1. 1. Near Silvaplana

    Silvaplana is where the Upper Engadine opens out into water and wind. The village sits on the edge of a lake of the same name, at around 1,800 metres, between the larger resort of St. Moritz and the little village of Sils, and above it the high peaks of the Bernina and Albula ranges hold glaciers in their cirques. The light here is famously clear, an effect of altitude and dry air that has drawn painters and thinkers for generations.

  4. 4. St. Moritz

    St. Moritz is not subtle, and the world's most famous winter resort does not intend to be. It sits at about 1,800 metres in the Upper Engadine, on a sunlit shelf above a lake, with ranges of white peaks on every side and a record of sunny days that the tourist board advertises with some pride. Its mineral springs were known in the Bronze Age, and in the Middle Ages pilgrims came to drink the water. Winter tourism, legend says, started in 1864 when the hotelier Johannes Badrutt bet English summer guests that they would enjoy the Alps in the snow. The town has hosted the Winter Olympics twice, in 1928 and 1948, and the Cresta Run, an ice toboggan track, still runs every winter.

  5. 5. Zernez

    Zernez is a small village in the Lower Engadine, at about fifteen hundred metres, and the gateway to Switzerland's only national park. The Inn river runs through the valley, a cold green stream fed by glaciers, and the surrounding mountains are dark with larch and pine. The village has an unusual look for the Alps, with white houses decorated with sgraffito, patterns scratched in plaster, and small windows set deep into thick walls, typical of Engadine architecture. Most of the older buildings date from after 1872, when a fire destroyed much of the village, and the rebuilt streets are wider than most in the valley.
