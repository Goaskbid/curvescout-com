Why this ride

From Famorca it runs 68 km to Famorca; allow about 1 h 10 of riding. It goes by way of Barraac de la Palla, Penya Escrita and back through Barraac de la Palla. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Racó de Gorgorí- Abric III

    From the ridge tops above Castell de Castells you can have the sea and the tower blocks of Benidorm in view on a clear day, and the contrast is almost comic: a village of a few hundred people, goats and almond terraces, and not much more than twenty kilometres away a skyline of glass. The Racó de Gorgorí lies in the hills above the valley, and from the coast a good road climbs to it by way of Callosa d'en Sarrià and the Guadalest valley. A rider who does this in a morning will have made one of the most abrupt changes of scene in Spain.

  2. 2. Barraac de la Palla

    Tormos and Sagra are tiny villages at the foot of the mountains in the north of Alicante province, at the point where the plain of the Marina Alta meets the hills. They are surrounded by orange groves and orchards, with the mountains of the Vall de Laguar and the Serra del Migdia rising steeply behind. The Barranc de la Palla, the ravine of the straw, cuts into the hills near Tormos, close also to Orba and the town of Pego.

  3. 3. Penya Escrita

    In the folded limestone hills behind the Costa Blanca, far enough inland that the resorts feel like another country, a rock shelter called Penya Escrita holds prehistoric paintings. The name means something like the written rock, which is what the people of Tàrbena and Bolulla called it long before archaeologists arrived. The figures belong to the rock art of the Mediterranean Basin of the Iberian Peninsula, a UNESCO site made up of hundreds of shelters scattered from Catalonia down to Andalusia, painted by hunter-gatherers and early farmers thousands of years ago. They are small, faded and easy to miss, and they are best appreciated with patience rather than expectation.
