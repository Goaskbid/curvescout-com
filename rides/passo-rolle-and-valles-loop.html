Why this ride

58 km one way from Siror - plan on about 59 min of riding. On the way it passes Falcade, Passo Valles and Passo Rolle. The highest point is about 2,050 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

For 19 km this is Passo Rolle and Valles Loop, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it — expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast.

Passo Rolle and Valles Loop

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2032 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance. Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. San Martino di Castrozza

    San Martino di Castrozza lies in a green basin in the Trentino Dolomites, at about 1,450 metres, with the Pale di San Martino rising behind it in a wall of pale, jagged rock. The Pale group is one of the most dramatic in the Dolomites, a high limestone plateau with towers and spires that glow pink at sunrise and sunset, the enrosadira that people travel far to see. The town grew up as a mountain resort in the nineteenth century, and today it is a base for walkers, climbers and skiers, with hotels, cafes and a small church dating from the earlier hospice that once stood here for pilgrims and travellers.

  2. 2. Falcade

    Falcade is a ski village at the head of the Biois valley, in the Veneto side of the Dolomites, and it has the slightly sleepy, practical feel of a place that is much busier in January than in June. The valley is narrow and green, with steep forested slopes and, rising behind, the pale walls of the Pale di San Martino and the Marmolada group beyond. Villages along it, such as Canale d'Agordo, a few kilometres down, are small and traditional, with wooden balconies, stone fountains and churches with onion-domed or pointed towers. Canale d'Agordo is also the birthplace of Albino Luciani, who became Pope John Paul I in 1978 and died after only thirty-three days.

  3. 3. Passo Valles

    Passo Valles is the quiet pass of the Dolomites, a ridge crossing at just over two thousand metres between Falcade and the high forests of Paneveggio, and it has none of the crowds of its famous neighbours. There are no cable cars, no big souvenir stalls and few buses, just a road that climbs steadily through spruce forest, emerges into open pasture and tops out in a landscape of grass and stone. A small refuge or bar is sometimes open near the top, but services are minimal, so bring what you need.

  4. 4. Passo Rolle

    Passo Rolle is one of the great classics of the Dolomites, at 1,989 metres, and the reason is not just the road but what it looks at. As you reach the top, the Pale di San Martino rear up on the southern side in a wall of pale, jagged rock, with the Cimon della Pala and the rounded Cima Vezzana lighting up in the sun. The pass connects the Fiemme valley around Predazzo with the Primiero valley and San Martino di Castrozza, and has been a crossing for centuries, used by traders and shepherds long before the road was paved.

  5. 5. Predazzo

    Predazzo is the largest village in the Val di Fiemme, and it has a surprisingly special place in the world of geology. The valley lies in Trentino, between the pale limestone Dolomites and the darker volcanic rock of the Latemar and Monzoni areas, and Predazzo sits on the line where these meet. Geologists have studied the rocks here since the early nineteenth century, and there is a small museum, the Museo Geologico delle Dolomiti, that explains how ancient seas, volcanoes and later uplift built the mountains. With about four thousand people, the village is large enough for fuel, supermarkets and several hotels.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

San Martino di Castrozza to Falcade

San Martino di Castrozza → Falcade is 15 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Falcade to Passo Valles

Falcade → Passo Valles climbs about 2032 m in 9 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Passo Valles to Passo Rolle

Passo Valles → Passo Rolle is 5 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Passo Rolle to Predazzo

Passo Rolle → Predazzo drops about 1989 m over 16 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
