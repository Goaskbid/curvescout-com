  1. 1. Swellendam

    Swellendam is the third-oldest town in South Africa, and it wears its age with an easy dignity. The Dutch East India Company established a magistracy here in 1743, and the settlement grew up under the Langeberg mountains, which stand along the north side of the town like a blue wall. In 1795, discontented local farmers declared a short-lived republic, with their own flag and officials, which lasted only a few months before the British arrived. The old Drostdy, the magistrate's residence and offices, is now a museum, its thatched roof and white gables intact.

  2. 2. Tradouw Pass

    The Tradouw Pass is a short, pretty road, and it has a good claim to being the best-surfaced mountain road in the Cape. It slices through the Langeberg mountains for a few kilometres between Swellendam and the Little Karoo, following the Tradouw river as it cuts a narrow gorge through red and orange rock. The name is said to come from Khoi words meaning pass of the women, though the exact meaning is debated, and the road was engineered in the 1870s by Thomas Bain, son of the more famous Andrew Bain, who built so many of the great Cape passes.

  3. 3. Barrydale

    The R62 through Barrydale is a road of long distances and small surprises. The town is a modest place, sitting in the Little Karoo between the Langeberg and the Swartberg ranges, a few tidy streets, a main road, and a population of a few thousand. It was founded as a farming settlement, and its economy still depends on fruit, olives, sheep and now, increasingly, on travellers on Route 62 who stop for a meal and some air.
