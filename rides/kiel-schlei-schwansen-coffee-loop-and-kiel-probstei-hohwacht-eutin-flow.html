Why this ride

From Gettorf it runs 231 km to Wendtorf; allow about 4 h 15 of riding. The stops in between: Eckernförde bay, Schwansen villages, Kappeln Schlei stop and Gettorf road.

The roads

Hohwacht bay → Lütjenburg reset

Lütjenburg reset is 8 km from Hohwacht bay. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 13 and 37 m. Watch for sand and spray near the water.

Lütjenburg reset → Eutin lake

Eutin lake is 23 km from Lütjenburg reset. Fairly level - 29 to 37 m end to end. Water alongside for much of it - pick one good pull-out and stop.

The stops

  1. 1. Gettorf road

    Start here.

    Gettorf is a small town on the Schwansen peninsula, a few kilometres inland from the Baltic, with about six thousand inhabitants and all the basics a rider needs: fuel, a bakery, a supermarket and a handful of cafes. Everything here is gentle, rolling and green, and the sea is never far away.

    Read the complete Gettorf road write-up.

  2. 2. Eckernförde bay

    14 km in.

    Eckernförde Bay is a long, narrow arm of the Baltic that cuts into the coast of Schleswig-Holstein between the Danish Wohld and the Schwansen peninsula, and the little port of the same name sits at its head. The water is shallow, the beaches are wide and sandy, and the whole place has the relaxed air of a north German seaside town, with a promenade, a harbour full of boats and a smell of smoked fish. The altitude here is a modest eight metres, and the horizon is all sea and sky.

    Read the complete Eckernförde bay write-up.

  3. 3. Schwansen villages

    34 km in.

    Schwansen is the little peninsula that sticks out into the Baltic between the Schlei inlet and Eckernförde Bay, and it is one of the quieter corners of Schleswig-Holstein. The land is low, rolling and green, at only a few tens of metres above the sea, and the villages of Damp, Waabs and Rieseby are separated by fields, small woods and hedgerows. It was once part of the old Danish duchy of Schleswig, and the mix of Danish and German history shows in the names and the architecture.

    Read the complete Schwansen villages write-up.

  4. 4. Kappeln Schlei stop

    49 km in.

    Kappeln is a small Baltic port on the Schlei, the long narrow inlet that cuts forty kilometres into Schleswig-Holstein, and its harbour has the busy, salty feel of a working fishing town. Wooden boats line the quays, nets hang to dry, and gulls wheel above the fish stalls. The town sits at the point where the Schlei narrows, so a bridge crosses it, and from the quay you can watch it lift to let tall-masted sailing ships through.

    Read the complete Kappeln Schlei stop write-up.

  6. 6. Probstei villages

    133 km in.

    The name comes from the provost of the convent at Preetz, who once held the land and drew rent from its farmers. The villages are strung along the lanes at the edge of the fields, with brick farmhouses, village churches and long avenues of trees.

    Read the complete Probstei villages write-up.

  7. 7. Hohwacht bay

    161 km in.

    Hohwacht Bay is a wide curve of the Baltic coast in eastern Holstein, with a long, sandy beach, low dunes and shallow, gentle water that warms quickly in summer. The land here is flat and green, with the sea on one side and the rolling hills of Holstein Switzerland on the other, and the light has that clear, northern quality that painters love.

    Read the complete Hohwacht bay stop write-up.

  8. 8. Lütjenburg reset

    169 km in.

    Lütjenburg is a small, tidy market town on the Baltic side of Schleswig-Holstein, a few kilometres from the sea in a rolling country of fields, lakes and low hills. People here call the region Holstein Switzerland, a joke that is half serious, because by the flat standards of northern Germany the landscape counts as dramatic, with hills of a hundred metres or so and lakes in the hollows. About five and a half thousand people live in the town, and it has the feel of a place that has been a local centre for a long time.

    Read the complete Lütjenburg fuel gate write-up.

  9. 9. Eutin lake

    191 km in.

    Eutin is a town with a lake, a castle and a composer. The lake, the Großer Eutiner See, is a broad stretch of water at the heart of the Holstein Switzerland region, with wooded shores and a slender island, and the castle stands at its edge, a moated palace of the old prince-bishops of Lübeck, surrounded by a landscape park. The composer is Carl Maria von Weber, who was born in the town in 1786, and the summer opera festival here is held in his honour on an open-air stage by the lake.

    Read the complete Eutin lake write-up.
