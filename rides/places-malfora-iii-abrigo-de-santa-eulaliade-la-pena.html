Why this ride

From Alquézar it runs 104 km to Alquézar; allow about 1 h 48 of riding. It goes by way of Mascún I, Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña and back through Mascún I. The highest point is about 1,050 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Malforá III

    Malforá III is the third in a trio of shelters that share a name, and the neighbouring village of Alquézar, four kilometres away, is the base for all of them. The shelters lie among the dry terraces and oak scrub on the northern side of the valley, and they are part of the Levantine rock art of the Spanish Mediterranean, protected by UNESCO since 1998. If you have already seen the first two, you may wonder why the early painters kept returning to the same patch of rock. The answer is probably the position: sheltered from the north wind, facing the midday sun and looking over a wide stretch of country.

  2. 2. Mascún I

    The Mascún gorge is one of the great pieces of rock in the Sierra de Guara, a canyon with walls that rise several hundred metres, and it is the reason the village of Rodellar has become a pilgrimage site for climbers. Mascún I is a small shelter on the high ground above, in a district of cliffs, pines and thin soil, and it belongs to the Mediterranean-arc rock art that UNESCO listed in 1998. I can tell you that you will not have to share the view with a crowd. This is one of the emptiest corners of the Pyrenean foothills.

  3. 3. Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña

    Just north of Huesca, where the flat cereal land of the Hoya suddenly rears up into the first limestone walls of the Pre-Pyrenees, lies the little village of Nueno and, above it, the rock shelter named after Santa Eulalia de la Peña. This is the southern edge of the Sierra de Guara country, a landscape of gorges, conglomerate cliffs and scrub where griffon vultures ride the thermals and the light comes hard off pale stone.
