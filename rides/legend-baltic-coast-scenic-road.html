Why this ride

curated scenic road. Baltic coast, Hanseatic towns, beaches. From Lübeck it runs 185 km to Stralsund; allow about 3 h 22 of riding. It goes by way of Timmendorfer Strand, Wismar, Ostseebad Kühlungsborn and Warnemünde. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Scenic road chapters

Baltic Coast Scenic Road

Slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did. Water arrives beside the road and stays for 16 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go.

  2. 2. Timmendorfer Strand

    Lübeck Bay curves round in a long, shallow arc, and Timmendorfer Strand sits halfway along it, which is why the whole stretch from Travemünde to Neustadt in Holstein feels like one continuous holiday. The beach is wide and pale, the water is shallow enough that children paddle out a long way, and in July the sand is dotted with Strandkörbe, those hooded wicker beach chairs that Germans treat as portable living rooms. Behind the dunes, the promenade is lined with big hotels, ice cream stalls and a pier, and in the evening it fills with people in sandals pretending not to look at their phones.

  3. 3. Wismar

    Wismar is a red brick town on the Baltic, on a sheltered bay in Mecklenburg, and its story is that of the Hanseatic League, the medieval trading network of North German cities that dominated commerce in the Baltic and the North Sea. The town grew rich on beer, grain and the herring trade, and put its wealth into huge Gothic churches in dark brick, with towers that serve as landmarks at sea. Together with Stralsund it was listed by UNESCO in 2002 for the quality of its Hanseatic architecture, and the market square, one of the largest in northern Germany, is the heart of the place, with gabled houses of many centuries and a curious twelve-sided fountain house, the Wasserkunst, built in the Dutch Renaissance style in the early seventeenth century.

  5. 5. Warnemünde

    Warnemünde is where Rostock goes to the sea. The old fishing village at the mouth of the Warnow river was swallowed by the city long ago, but it keeps a stubborn independence, with a narrow canal called the Alter Strom lined with low fishermen's houses, rows of small fishing boats tied up at the quay, and stalls selling fish sandwiches straight off the cutters. The smell of smoked fish and diesel is the first thing you notice, and the second is the wind.

  6. 6. Stralsund

    Stralsund's skyline tells you at once that this was a rich merchant town. Red-brick Gothic gables, pointed spires and the enormous bulk of the Marienkirche, whose tower rises more than a hundred metres, crowd the old town island, which is surrounded by water and linked to the mainland by causeways. In the Middle Ages, Stralsund was one of the leading members of the Hanseatic League, the trading alliance that dominated the Baltic, and together with Wismar its old town is a World Heritage site. In the harbour, the three-masted Gorch Fock I, a sail training ship, is moored, and the Ozeaneum, a striking modern museum of sea life, stands beside it.
