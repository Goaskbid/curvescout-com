Why this ride

On the way it passes Ensemble mixte de Nuku Hiva, Ensemble mixte de Ua Pou and back through Ensemble mixte de Nuku Hiva. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Aire marine côtière de Ua Huka

    Ua Huka is the dry, rugged island of the Marquesas, where the wind blows steadily, the hills are bare and brown, and herds of wild horses graze on the plateau. The island has about six hundred people, living in three villages, Vaipaee, Hane and Hokatu, and its coastline is lined with cliffs, sea caves, and small offshore islets that are important nesting sites for seabirds. The marine area around it is a protected stretch of water, rich in fish, rays and sometimes dolphins.

  2. 2. Ensemble mixte de Nuku Hiva

    The Marquesas Islands lie some fourteen hundred kilometres north-east of Tahiti, and they are the most remote inhabited islands in French Polynesia. Nuku Hiva, the largest, is a jagged tropical mountain of valleys, ridges and sheer green walls that fall into the sea. Its people carved stone and wood with great skill, and the island still has ceremonial platforms, called paepae, and giant stone figures, tiki, hidden in the forest. The Marquesas are being proposed for recognition as a mixed cultural and natural site, which is where the name of this point comes from.

  3. 3. Ensemble mixte de Ua Pou

    Ua Pou looks like nothing else in the Pacific. Basalt spires, narrow as organ pipes, rise above the island in a crown of grey rock, some of them over a thousand metres high, and clouds often catch on them and turn the whole skyline into a stage set. This is the Marquesas, one of the most remote island groups on earth, about fifteen hundred kilometres northeast of Tahiti, and the first sight of them from the sea is a good argument for taking the long way round.
