Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Tolvkarlevej and Højager

    Hillerød is the town where the Danish kings built their most ambitious castle, and the roads around it carry some of the best light in northern Zealand. Frederiksborg Castle stands on three small islands in a lake, a red-brick and sandstone Renaissance palace with towers, spires and a copper roof turned green. King Christian IV, who rebuilt it in the early seventeenth century, was a builder of grand projects and a lover of show, and he made Frederiksborg a stage for royal life. Today it houses the Museum of National History, and its gardens in the baroque style stretch away in terraces.

  2. 2. Jægersborg Dyrehave and Jægersborg Hegn

    Danish kings loved to hunt, and in the late seventeenth century they laid out forests north of Copenhagen in a geometric pattern of straight paths radiating from central points, designed for par force hunting, in which riders and hounds chased a stag until it was exhausted. Jægersborg Dyrehave, the deer park, and Jægersborg Hegn are included in UNESCO's inscription of the par force hunting landscape in North Zealand, together with forests further north. The star-shaped network of rides is still visible, with numbered stone posts at the intersections that helped the hunters find their way.

  3. 3. Roskilde Cathedral

    Roskilde Cathedral is where Denmark keeps its kings. Since the fifteenth century, almost every Danish monarch has been buried here, and the chapels and crypts hold around forty kings and queens, from medieval stone tombs to ornate baroque sarcophagi and modern designs. The cathedral itself, begun in the late twelfth century under Bishop Absalon, the warrior-churchman who also founded Copenhagen, was one of the first big brick Gothic churches in Scandinavia, and it set the style for church building across the region. UNESCO listed it in 1995. Inside, look for the clock with the figure of St George slaying the dragon, which strikes the hour, and the pillar where royal visitors have marked their height.
