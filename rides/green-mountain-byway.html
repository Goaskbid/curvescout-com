Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Waterbury Vermont

    Waterbury is a small Vermont town at the junction of the Winooski river and the road north to Stowe, a place with brick storefronts, a church spire, and an unusually good number of places to eat for a village of under two thousand. It is probably best known to the rest of the world for Ben and Jerry's ice cream, which has a factory and a visitor tour just outside town, and for the Cold Hollow cider mill, where doughnuts come hot from the fryer in autumn. Neither is a bad reason to start a ride.

  2. 2. Stowe

    Stowe sits in a bowl of the Green Mountains, with the long ridge of Mount Mansfield rising behind it, and its white church steeple is perhaps the most photographed in Vermont. Mansfield, at 4,393 feet the highest point in the state, has a profile that resembles a human face, with a forehead, nose, lips and chin along the ridgeline, and the summit is reached by a toll road in summer and by ski lifts in winter. The town has about four thousand residents, a lively main street of shops and restaurants, and a long tradition of skiing that goes back to the 1930s.

  3. 3. Smugglers Notch

    Smugglers Notch is the sort of road that makes a motorcyclist grin and a camper-van driver clench. Vermont Route 108 climbs through a narrow gap between Mount Mansfield, the highest peak in the state, and Spruce Peak, and at the top, at about 660 metres, it squeezes between enormous boulders that have tumbled off the cliffs and been left where they landed. The tarmac is tight, with hairpins and blind corners, no shoulder to speak of, and in some places barely a car and a half wide. Large vehicles and trailers are banned, which is the highway department's way of admitting that physics is going to win.

  4. 4. Jeffersonville

    Smugglers' Notch gets its name honestly. When President Jefferson's Embargo Act of 1807 closed trade with Canada, Vermonters hauled goods north through this narrow gap in the Green Mountains rather than go without, and a century later the same pass served bootleggers during Prohibition. Jeffersonville sits on the Lamoille River at the northern foot of that pass, a small village of white clapboard houses, a general store feel, and a covered bridge or two within easy riding distance. Mount Mansfield, Vermont's highest peak at 4,393 feet, looms behind it, and the village has long attracted painters and potters who came for the light and stayed for the quiet.

Waterbury Vermont to Stowe

Waterbury Vermont → Stowe is 19 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Stowe to Smugglers Notch

Stowe → Smugglers Notch climbs about 660 m in 16 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Smugglers Notch to Jeffersonville

Smugglers Notch → Jeffersonville drops about 660 m over 13 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
