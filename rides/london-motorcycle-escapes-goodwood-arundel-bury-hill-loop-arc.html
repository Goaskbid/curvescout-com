Why this ride

A 886 km loop that starts and ends near London, about 16 h 07 of riding. It goes by way of Arundel, Wheatley, Whittington and Bury.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. London

    London is the one place on the map where the best advice is to leave the bike at the edge. The centre around Charing Cross and Trafalgar Square is a maze of one-way streets, bus lanes, taxis and tourists, and parking is limited and expensive. The city also has a congestion charge and a low-emission zone, whose rules for motorcycles have changed over the years, so check the current conditions for your own machine before you ride in. For a start and finish, a quiet side street or a secure garage on the outskirts is a better plan.

    Read the complete London write-up.

  2. 2. Arundel

    Come over the downs from the north and Arundel appears all at once: a castle of grey towers and battlements on a ridge above the River Arun, a French Gothic cathedral beside it, and a steep little town tumbling down the slope to the bridge. It looks almost too much like a film set, and it has served as one more than once.

    Read the complete Arundel write-up.

  3. 3. Wheatley

    Wheatley sits on a hill just east of Oxford, where the A40 crosses the M40, and for most riders it is a quick pit stop: fuel, a sandwich, back on the road. The old village, though, deserves a minute. Its stone cottages climb a steep main street, and at the top stands a small octagonal stone lock-up, built in the nineteenth century to hold drunks and petty criminals overnight, a reminder of rougher days on the coaching road to London.

    Read the complete Wheatley write-up.

  4. 4. Whittington

    Three spires rise from the flat land south of the Trent, and they belong to Lichfield Cathedral, the only medieval cathedral in England to have kept all three. Whittington is a village a few miles east of it, with a church, a green, old pubs and a barracks that has housed soldiers since the 1880s. The Staffordshire Regiment's museum is there, with a reconstructed First World War trench that schoolchildren walk through in awed silence.

    Read the complete Whittington write-up.

  5. 5. Bury

    Bury market is one of the most famous in the north of England, and its fame rests largely on a sausage. Black pudding, made from pig's blood, oatmeal and fat, has been sold here for generations, and the stalls in the market hall still serve it hot, sliced and with mustard, to queues of locals and visitors. It is the obvious food stop, and anyone who wrinkles their nose should try it before deciding.

    Read the complete Bury write-up.

  6. 6. Etwall

    Etwall is a quiet village in the south of Derbyshire, between Derby and the River Dove, with a church, almshouses and old brick houses. It sits on the edge of a district full of history, and a short ride in any direction brings something worth stopping for.

    Read the complete Etwall write-up.

  7. 7. Hardingstone

    Edward I, grieving for his queen, Eleanor of Castile, erected a stone cross at every place where her funeral procession stopped on its way from Lincoln to London in 1290. Twelve were built; three survive, and one of them stands by the roadside at Hardingstone, on the southern edge of Northampton. It is a slender Gothic monument of weathered stone, with statues of the queen looking out over the traffic, and it is more than seven centuries old.

    Read the complete Hardingstone write-up.

  8. 8. London

    Return visit. Read the place description above.

    Read the complete London write-up.
