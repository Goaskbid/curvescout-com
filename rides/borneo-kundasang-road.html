Why this ride

From Kota Kinabalu it runs 147 km to Ranau; allow about 2 h 02 of riding. It goes by way of Tamparuli and Kundasang. The highest point is about 1,150 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Borneo Kundasang Road

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 55 km this is Borneo Kundasang Road, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kota Kinabalu

    Kota Kinabalu sits on the north-west tip of Borneo, with the South China Sea on one side and the jungle-covered Crocker Range on the other, and on a clear morning the peak of Mount Kinabalu, at 4,095 metres, floats above the clouds to the east. The city, the capital of Sabah, has about four hundred and fifty thousand people, a waterfront lined with seafood restaurants, a lively Filipino market selling dried fish and handicrafts, and a string of islands just offshore that make up the Tunku Abdul Rahman Marine Park. The sunsets over the sea are famously good.

  2. 2. Tamparuli

    Tamparuli is a small town on the road from Kota Kinabalu toward Mount Kinabalu, about thirty kilometres from the coast. It is the last place of any size before the road leaves the plains and begins to climb into the Crocker Range, and it behaves like one: a busy main street, a market, lines of shophouses, and a lot of hungry traffic going both ways. The town is known across Sabah for its suspension bridge over the river, immortalised in a song that every Malaysian of a certain age seems to know. It is a modest thing, but it has an honest charm.

  3. 3. Kundasang

    Kundasang is a highland town below the huge shoulder of Mount Kinabalu, which at 4,095 metres is the highest mountain in Borneo and one of the most striking in Southeast Asia. The town sits well above a thousand metres, and the climate is a surprise to anyone who has come up from the coast: cool, damp, often wrapped in cloud, with farms of cabbage, carrots and tomatoes on slopes so steep it seems unreasonable. When the mist clears, the peak appears above the town in a long ridge of granite, and for a few minutes everyone stops to look. The mountain and the park around it are a World Heritage Site, recognised for its extraordinary plant life, including a great number of orchids and pitcher plants.

  4. 4. Kinabalu Park

    Mount Kinabalu stands 4,095 metres above the north of Borneo, and it dominates the landscape from every angle, a huge granite dome that appears and disappears in cloud. The park at its foot, Kinabalu Park, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the richest places for plants on earth, with thousands of species, including orchids, rhododendrons and pitcher plants. The park headquarters sits at about 1,500 metres, so the air is cool and often damp, and the forest around is moss-covered and dripping. The nearest towns are Ranau, about eighteen kilometres east, and Kota Belud, further to the west, so fuel and supplies should be taken there.
