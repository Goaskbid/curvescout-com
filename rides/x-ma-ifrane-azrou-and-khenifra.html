Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ifrane

    Ifrane looks wrong in the best possible way, like a Swiss resort that has been teleported to the Middle Atlas. The French built it in the 1930s as a mountain station for their officials, and the pitched roofs, red tiles, neat lawns and tidy villas still give it an Alpine flavour, quite unlike anywhere else in Morocco. At around 1,650 metres the air is cool even in summer, and in winter the town is regularly under snow, which is why Moroccans sometimes call it the Switzerland of Africa. Ifrane also claims a record cold temperature for the continent, set in the middle of the last century.

  2. 2. Azrou

    The name Azrou means rock in the Berber language, and the town takes it from a large outcrop that rises above the centre. It lies at about 1,250 metres in the Middle Atlas, a market town where the roads from Fez, Meknes and Midelt converge, and it has an easy-going character, with a bustling souk, craft shops, and a scatter of cafés on the main street. The people are mostly Amazigh, and Azrou is the place in the region where you hear the language spoken everywhere.

  3. 3. Khenifra

    Khénifra has the look of a working town that has seen a few battles, which it has. It lies on the Oum Er-Rbia river, Morocco's second-longest, at the south-western edge of the Middle Atlas, and its old quarter of red-brown buildings still bears the stamp of the Zayane Berbers, who held the area. Their leader, Moha ou Hammou Zayani, is remembered for resisting French forces in the early twentieth century and for the victory at El Herri in 1914, where his fighters inflicted a heavy defeat on a French column. His ruined kasbah stands by the river, and a statue in town commemorates him.
