Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. City of Graz - Historic Centre and Schloss

    Graz has the best-preserved old town in Austria outside Salzburg and Vienna, and it is much less crowded than either. The historic centre, a UNESCO site since 1999, sits on the river Mur beneath the Schlossberg, a rocky hill with a clock tower on top, and the roofs of the old town are a sea of red tile punctuated by domes and spires. Add a modern touch, the glass and steel Murinsel, an artificial island in the river, and the Kunsthaus, a blue bubble nicknamed the friendly alien, and the city has a mix of old and new that works.

  2. 2. Semmering Railway

    Carl von Ghega's railway over the Semmering was a bold idea in the 1840s, when many engineers doubted that a conventional locomotive could haul a train over a mountain pass. Built between 1848 and 1854, the line climbs from Gloggnitz to the Semmering summit through fourteen tunnels and across sixteen viaducts, some with two tiers of stone arches, curving around the flanks of the mountains on ledges cut into the rock. It became the first mountain railway inscribed on the UNESCO list, in 1998, and it still carries trains between Vienna and the south, with the new Semmering Base Tunnel under construction beneath it to take over the heavy traffic.

  3. 3. Baden bei Wien

    The Romans already knew about the hot sulphur springs at Baden, just south of Vienna, but it was in the early nineteenth century that the town became the summer playground of the Habsburg court. Emperor Franz I spent his summers here, and the aristocracy and the wealthy bourgeoisie followed, building elegant villas, parks and bathhouses in the Biedermeier style. Beethoven came to take the waters too, staying many times and working on his Ninth Symphony in a house that is now a museum. In 2021, Baden was listed by UNESCO as one of the Great Spa Towns of Europe, alongside Bath, Baden-Baden, Karlovy Vary and others.
