  1. 1. Lauro Muller

    Lauro Müller is a coal-mining town at the foot of the Serra do Rio do Rastro, one of the most famous roads in Brazil, and it sits in a quiet, green valley under a wall of cliffs. The town of ten thousand has a modest, working look, with a railway that once carried coal to the coast and the faded colour of an industry that has declined. The name honours Lauro Müller, a Brazilian engineer and politician born in this region in the nineteenth century, who served as governor of the state and later as a government minister.

  2. 2. Bom Jardim da Serra

    Bom Jardim da Serra sits on the high plateau of Santa Catarina, a cold, windswept, rolling grassland dotted with araucaria pines and apple orchards, at the very top of the Serra Geral escarpment. It is a small place, and the larger towns are close: Lauro Muller, about ten thousand people, lies at the foot of the escarpment, and Sao Joaquim, sixteen thousand, is up the plateau and known as one of the coldest towns in Brazil, where snow falls in some winters. Fuel and services are in those towns, so plan accordingly.
