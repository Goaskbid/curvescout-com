Why this ride

From Tuéjar it runs 270 km to Tuéjar; allow about 4 h 39 of riding. On the way it passes La Peña del Castellar, Barrio Tiradores and back through La Peña del Castellar. The highest point is about 1,350 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the

    Tuéjar, Chelva and Alpuente are small towns in the Serranía, the rugged highland of inland Valencia, where the rivers cut deep valleys between pine-clad ridges. It is a corner of the province that most visitors to the coast never see. The name on the stop refers to the UNESCO listing of 1998 for the rock art of the Mediterranean basin on the Iberian peninsula, and the paintings of Barranco de las Colochas, the ravine nearby, belong to it. The ravine and the surrounding woods are worth the trip on their own.

  2. 2. La Peña del Castellar

    Villar del Humo is a tiny village in the Serranía of Cuenca, the high, forested mountain country east of the city of Cuenca. Around it, sandstone outcrops rise from pine forests, and on their faces prehistoric painters left images of animals and people. The Peña del Escrito and La Peña del Castellar are among the most important shelters here, and they are part of the World Heritage Site of Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin. The paintings include deer, horses and human figures, some in the dynamic Levantine style and others more schematic.

  3. 3. Barrio Tiradores

    Tiradores is one of Cuenca's old quarters, a slice of the hillside town where the steep lanes climb between tall, narrow houses and the sound is mostly footsteps and swifts. It is not a sight in the sense of a single monument. It is a neighbourhood, and that is why it is worth the effort: Cuenca's famous gorge-edge silhouette is made of ordinary houses like these, stuck onto the rock with extraordinary stubbornness, and the people who live here still carry their shopping up the hill.
