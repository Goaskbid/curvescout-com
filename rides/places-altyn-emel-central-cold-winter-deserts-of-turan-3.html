Why this ride

It goes by way of Altyn-Emel West, Cold Winter Deserts of Turan and back through Altyn-Emel West. The highest point is about 1,250 m. Dry, open country - carry water.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Altyn-Emel Central

    The central part of Altyn-Emel, in south-eastern Kazakhstan, is a landscape of space more than anything else. The national park lies between the Ili River and the Dzungarian Alatau, a couple of hundred kilometres east of Almaty, and its name is often translated as golden saddle. The land is steppe and semi-desert in broad swathes of dusty yellow, broken here and there by dry gullies, clumps of saxaul and the strange coloured hills of the Aktau mountains, where layers of white, pink and orange clay look as though they were painted on. This is the geology of an old sea bed, tilted and eroded.

  2. 2. Altyn-Emel West

    Altyn-Emel means golden saddle, and when the evening sun hits the ridge it is easy to see why. This national park, in the south-east of Kazakhstan, sits in a bend of the Ili river, in the dry country between the Dzungarian Alatau and the Chu-Ili mountains, and it is a place of enormous quiet. Colourful badlands, white chalk hills, a field of wild flowers in spring and an expanse of dry steppe fall away to the horizon. The park's most famous feature is the Singing Dune, a sandy hill a hundred and fifty metres high and several kilometres long that hums or booms when the sand slides in dry weather, a sound that has been compared to an organ or a distant aeroplane.

  3. 3. Cold Winter Deserts of Turan

    Turan is the name of the great lowland that stretches across Central Asia, from the Caspian to the foothills of the Tian Shan, and its cold winter deserts are one of the continent's lesser-known biomes.
