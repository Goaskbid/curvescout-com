Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Pittsburgh

    Pittsburgh makes sense once you see where it sits. The city grew at the meeting of the Allegheny and Monongahela rivers, which merge to form the Ohio, and the point where they meet is now a park with a fountain at the tip. Steep hills rise from the water on every side, and the streets climb them in flights of stairs, so that the old neighbourhoods look stacked on one another. More than four hundred bridges cross the rivers and ravines, and it is the sort of city where you cross a bridge without noticing.

  2. 2. Fallingwater

    Frank Lloyd Wright built the house over the stream, not beside it. Fallingwater stands in the Laurel Highlands of southwestern Pennsylvania, at Mill Run, where Bear Run tumbles over a series of rock ledges in a hemlock and rhododendron forest. Edgar Kaufmann, a Pittsburgh department store owner, hired Wright in 1935 to design a weekend house, and expected a view of the waterfall. Wright put the house right on top of it, with concrete terraces cantilevered over the rock, and it was finished in 1937.
