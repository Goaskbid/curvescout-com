Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Quinta and Molino de Presa

    Lima begins in a desert, and an old country house in the Rimac district is as good a place as any to start understanding it. The Quinta and mill of Presa, a colonial estate of the eighteenth century, stands in the oldest part of the city, close to the river that gave the capital its name. The Rimac, a thin stream when it reaches the sea, brings snowmelt from the high Andes, and the whole city depends on it. Lima was founded by Francisco Pizarro in 1535 on the banks of this river, and for three centuries it was the capital of Spanish South America, so colonial houses, courtyards and balconies fill the old quarter.

  2. 2. Segment Quebrada Verde - Pachacamac

    Lima is one of the driest capitals on earth, and yet just south of it, in the hills above the Lurín valley, the desert turns green for a few months every year. The Lomas de Quebrada Verde are fog oases, where the winter mist called garúa, rolling in from the cold Pacific between roughly June and October, condenses on the hillsides and brings to life a brief explosion of grass, flowers, cacti and shrubs, attracting birds, butterflies and the people of Lima, who come here to walk. The segment between Quebrada Verde and Pachacamac is part of the Qhapaq Ñan, the Inca road system, which linked the coast with the highlands.

  3. 3. Segment Molle - Huarangal

    The names tell a story about the land. Molle is the Peruvian pepper tree, with its weeping branches and pink berries, and a huarangal is a grove of huarango, the tough, deep-rooted desert tree that once covered much of the Peruvian coast. Between these two places, in the lower hills east of Lima toward the Lurín valley, runs a short stretch of the Qhapaq Ñan, the Inca road network. Here the road descended from the highlands toward the sanctuary of Pachacamac on the coast, one of the most important religious centres of the ancient Andes, where an oracle drew pilgrims from far and wide. The segment is part of the UNESCO listing of the Andean Road System.
