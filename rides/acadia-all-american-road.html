Why this ride

From Bar Harbor it runs 23 km to Bar Harbor; allow about 22 min of riding. It goes by way of Sieur de Monts, Sand Beach, Otter Point and Jordan Pond. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bar Harbor

    Bar Harbor is the door to Mount Desert Island, the large granite island off the coast of Maine where Acadia National Park spreads over mountains, ponds and rocky shore. The town sits on Frenchman Bay, with the humped shapes of the Porcupine Islands offshore, and it has been a summer destination since the 1800s, when wealthy families from Boston and Philadelphia built huge shingle mansions they called cottages. A great fire in 1947 burned a lot of them, but the town rebuilt, and its streets are now a mix of old inns, gift shops, and lobster restaurants.

  2. 2. Sieur de Monts

    Sieur de Monts is the quiet doorway to the Acadia you came for, a shaded corner of Mount Desert Island with a spring, a nature centre and a small botanical garden. The name comes from Pierre Dugua, Sieur de Monts, the French nobleman who led the expedition that landed on these coasts in 1604 with Samuel de Champlain. The spring was developed by George Dorr, one of the park's founders, who spent decades assembling land and gifting it to the nation. Acadia became the first national park east of the Mississippi in 1916, under the name Lafayette, and took its current name in 1929.

  3. 3. Sand Beach

    Sand Beach is the only real sandy beach on Mount Desert Island, and it is a small one, a curve of pale sand tucked between granite cliffs, about 290 metres long. The sand is made largely of crushed shells and the skeletons of sea urchins and other creatures, ground fine by the waves, rather than the quartz you would find on a beach farther south. The water is cold even in August, rarely warmer than the mid-teens in Celsius, which discourages most swimmers beyond the brave and the foolish. Behind the beach, the peaks of Champlain and Huguenot Head rise in a long ridge of pink granite and spruce.

  4. 4. Otter Point

    Pink granite meets the Atlantic at Otter Point, and the sea has not yet won. The cliffs here, known as Otter Cliffs, rise about a hundred feet straight out of the water, among the highest headlands on the Atlantic coast between Maine and Rio de Janeiro, and the Park Loop Road runs along their top through a thin screen of spruce. Below, the waves work at the base of the rock with a constant, hollow thud, and the spray hangs in the air on a windy day.

  5. 5. Jordan Pond

    Jordan Pond is one of the loveliest bodies of water in Maine, a deep, clear lake cut by glaciers and ringed with spruce and granite. At the far end two rounded hills called the Bubbles rise like a pair of bumps on the skyline, and the water is so clear that you can see boulders far below the surface. It is a place where rides slow down, because the setting asks for it and because the car park beside the lake is usually busy.

  6. 6. Cadillac Mountain

    At 466 metres, Cadillac Mountain is the highest point on the US Atlantic coast north of Brazil, and its bare granite summit is the first place in the country to see the sunrise for part of the year, a distinction that has turned a quiet hill into a pilgrimage. The summit is a bald dome of pink rock, scoured by glaciers and polished by wind, and the view from it takes in the whole of Mount Desert Island, the islands of Frenchman Bay, and the blue sweep of the Gulf of Maine. On a clear day, from the right spot, you feel like you can see the curve of the earth.
