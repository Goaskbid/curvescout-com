Why this ride

A 641 km one-way ride from near Bonney Lake, about 12 h of riding. The climbs: near Tanglewilde-Thompson Place, Scenic stretch near Seabeck and near Sequim. It tops out at about 1,035 m. The line favours views over speed.

    Start here.

  2. 2. High point near Tanglewilde-Thompson Place

    54 km in. You top out here; the road stops climbing.

  3. 3. Scenic stretch near Seabeck

    164 km in. This is where the road stops climbing.

  4. 4. Photo stop near Sequim

    290 km in. The high point of this section.

    Sequim sits on the north side of the Olympic Peninsula, on a broad plain between the Olympic Mountains and the Strait of Juan de Fuca. It is famous for its climate: lying in the rain shadow of the Olympics, it receives far less rain than Seattle or the rainforests on the west side of the peninsula. That dry, sunny microclimate made it a farming centre, and today it is known for its lavender fields, which bloom in July and fill the air with fragrance.

    Read the complete Photo stop near Sequim write-up.

  5. 5. Leavenworth

    605 km in. The ride passes about 7 km from it.

    Beyond the map there is only forest. No bike can go there, and it is part of the appeal of the region that the surrounding tarmac skirts it.
