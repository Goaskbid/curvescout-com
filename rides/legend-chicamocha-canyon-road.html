Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bucaramanga

    Bucaramanga is a big, hilly, surprisingly green city in the northern Andes, and for riders it is the doorway to one of the great canyons of South America. It lies at around a thousand metres on a plateau in the department of Santander, with some 570,000 people in the city proper and many more in the neighbouring towns of Floridablanca and Girón. People call it the city of parks, and the bulk of its trees and gardens do, in fairness, make the heat easier to bear.

  3. 3. San Gil

    San Gil calls itself the adventure capital of Colombia, and it earns the title with rivers, caves and cliffs in every direction. The town sits in the Santander hills above the Fonce river, near its meeting with the Suárez, and the rapids on both are among the best in the country for rafting. Paragliders launch from the ridges above the canyon, and cavers explore the limestone passages nearby. After a morning on the road, the shops offering tours look more tempting than the usual souvenir stalls.

  4. 4. Barichara

    Barichara is the kind of town that makes people use superlatives, and for once they are mostly justified. Founded in the early eighteenth century on a ledge above the Suárez river canyon in Santander, it has kept its colonial streets almost intact: whitewashed houses with red-tile roofs, wooden doors and balconies, and pavements of pale stone that glow in the evening light. The name is thought to mean a place of rest in the language of the old Guane people, and the town lives up to it. With about four thousand people, it is small enough to cross on foot in a few minutes.
