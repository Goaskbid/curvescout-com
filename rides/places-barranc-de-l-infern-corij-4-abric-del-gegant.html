Why this ride

From Pego it runs 148 km to Pego; allow about 2 h 33 of riding. The stops in between: Cueva de la Clau, Penya de Benicadell, Abric del Gegant and back through Penya de Benicadell.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc de l'Infern Corij. 4

    The farmer's calendar explains this country better than any history book. In late winter the almond trees of the Marina Alta bloom white and pink, in spring the carob pods begin to swell, and in early summer the loquats and cherries ripen on the lower slopes. In August the muscat grapes are laid out to dry in the sun, and by autumn the oranges are turning and the olives are ready. The Barranc de l'Infern, where this fourth group of painted shelters lies, sits in the middle of that cycle, in the Vall de Laguar and its neighbouring valleys of Alicante province.

  2. 2. Cueva de la Clau

    Behind Gandia, the orange groves climb into the folded limestone hills of La Safor, and among the tiny villages there, Palma de Gandía and Ador sit almost touching each other at the foot of the slopes. Cueva de la Clau is one of the many rock shelters in the Valencian hills that form part of the UNESCO-listed Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula, a scattered collection of hundreds of sites where prehistoric people painted hunters, animals, dancers and abstract signs on limestone overhangs.

  3. 3. Penya de Benicadell

    From almost anywhere in the southern half of Valencia province you can see it: a long, jagged ridge of grey limestone with a sharp tooth at its eastern end, standing on the skyline like a broken saw. The Benicadell, at 1,104 metres, separates the Vall d'Albaida to the north from the Comtat to the south, and the sharp summit, the Penya, is the mountain's signature. At its foot lie the villages of Beniarrés, Ràfol de Salem and Otos.

  4. 4. Abric del Gegant

    Ontinyent is the kind of Valencian town that does not bother selling itself to tourists, which is part of its charm. It grew rich on textiles, and the old quarter of La Vila, perched above the gorge of the Clariano river, still has a fine bell tower that is among the tallest in the region. The Abric del Gegant, a rock shelter recorded among the prehistoric painted sites of eastern Spain, sits in the hills just outside town towards Bocairent, where the valley narrows and the Serra de Mariola begins to rise.
