Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Site of Kulan

    Nobody lives at Kulan now, except sheep and the odd herder, but in the early Middle Ages it was a town of the Silk Road, a stop on the route that ran from China across the Tian Shan foothills into Central Asia. The plain it stands on lies in the south of Kazakhstan, in the Zhambyl region, between the snowy ridges of the Kyrgyz Alatau, which rise to the south, and the dry steppe stretching north to Lake Balkhash and beyond. Here the Chu river valley and the Taraz road made a natural highway for caravans, and the settlement of Kulan, in the early medieval centuries, was a market and a staging post, with the mud-brick walls, towers and a citadel typical of the region. It was listed in 2014, together with Kostobe and many other sites, as part of the Silk Roads network's Tian-Shan corridor.

  2. 2. Site of Ornek

    Ornek is a place where the Silk Road becomes tangible in the grass. It lies in the foothills of the Talas Alatau, at the western end of the Tian Shan, in southern Kazakhstan near the border with Kyrgyzstan, and it was a settlement of the Turkic peoples who dominated these steppes between roughly the eighth and twelfth centuries. Its earthen walls, the outline of its citadel and the lines of houses are visible as low mounds, and scattered around are balbals, carved stone figures with faces, raised by the Turks to honour the dead. Ornek, like nearby Kulan and Kostobe, was inscribed as part of the Silk Roads corridor running from Chang'an to the Tian Shan.

  3. 3. Site of Kostobe

    The ruined town of Kostobe lies in the broad valley of the Talas river, a few kilometres from Sarykemer and about sixteen from Taraz, in the south of Kazakhstan. It belongs to the string of settlements along the Silk Road that UNESCO recognised in 2014 as the Chang'an-Tianshan corridor, and its low mounds and eroded walls recall a time when caravans passed through here between China, Central Asia and the Middle East. Details of the excavations are the business of archaeologists, but the general picture is clear: a walled medieval trading town on fertile land, close to a river and a major route.
