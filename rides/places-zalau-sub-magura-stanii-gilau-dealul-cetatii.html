Why this ride

From Zalău it runs 212 km to Zalău; allow about 3 h 39 of riding. It goes by way of Hurez - Dealul Prislop 5, Bologa - Măgura Bologii, Gilău - Dealul Cetății and back through Bologa - Măgura Bologii. The highest point is about 950 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Zalău - Sub Măgura Stânii

    Sub Magura Stanii means below the sheepfold hill, and the stop sits on the lower slopes, where the hill gives way to farmland and the first houses. This is the part of the landscape where people live and work: small plots of potatoes and maize, plum orchards, vegetable patches behind the houses, and a few cows grazing on the verge. The label offers no monument, so the best description is a day in the country, in a place where life goes on at the speed it always has.

  2. 2. Hurez - Dealul Prislop 5

    Some frontiers look dramatic on the map and modest on the ground, and the Roman watchtower site on Dealul Prislop near Hurez is a good example. Here, in the hills of Sălaj county in northwestern Romania, archaeologists have identified a series of small towers on high ground along the western frontier of Roman Dacia, which ran along the Meseș mountains and the hills around them. The towers were part of a chain of observation posts linked by signals, controlling movement across the frontier, and they are now included in the UNESCO listing of the Dacian frontier. Little is visible except low mounds and the shape of the land, but the views from the ridges explain why the Romans chose these spots.

  3. 3. Bologa - Măgura Bologii

    Bologa is a village in the valley of the Crișul Repede, the swift river that flows west from the hills of Cluj county towards Oradea and the Hungarian plain. On the hill above it, known as Măgura Bologii, lie the remains of a castle that once guarded the valley, and in the fields nearby are the traces of a Roman fort, built to protect the north western edge of the province of Dacia. The fort, known in antiquity as Resculum, is part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire, Dacia, listed by UNESCO in 2024, a line of forts, watchtowers and walls that ran along the edge of the Roman province from the second to the third century.

  4. 4. Gilău - Dealul Cetății

    Gilau sits where the flat plain of the Somesul Mic river meets the first hills of the Apuseni mountains, about fifteen kilometres west of Cluj-Napoca, in Transylvania, and the hill called Dealul Cetatii, which simply means Fortress Hill, rises almost on the edge of the town, about a kilometre away. The town has nearly eight thousand people, and from the road you can see the high, forested ridges of the Apuseni to the west.
