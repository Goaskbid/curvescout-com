Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Porto

    Porto is not a place you pass through quietly. It spills down the north bank of the Douro in a jumble of tiled facades, steep alleys and church towers, with the river at the bottom and the double-decker Dom Luís I bridge arching overhead. That iron bridge, finished in the 1880s, was designed by a pupil of Gustave Eiffel, and it carries a metro line on the upper deck and traffic on the lower. Across the river are the port wine lodges of Vila Nova de Gaia, where the sweet fortified wine is aged in cellars and barrels.

  2. 2. Mesão Frio

    Mesão Frio means Cold Big House, a curious name for a town that looks over one of the warmest wine regions in Portugal. It sits on the steep north bank of the Douro, high above the river, at the point where the valley starts to be planted with terraced vineyards in earnest. The Douro region was formally demarcated in the eighteenth century, which makes it one of the oldest regulated wine regions in the world, and the stone terraces climbing every slope are the result of centuries of back-breaking work.

  3. 3. Peso da Régua

    Régua sits on the south bank of the Douro river, a working town of about five thousand people, and it is the capital of the oldest demarcated wine region in the world. In 1756 the Marquis of Pombal, the chief minister of Portugal, set the borders of the Port wine district to protect quality and prices, and the steep schist hillsides terraced with vines around the town have been cultivated for centuries. The landscape, with stone walls rising in steps from the river, was listed by UNESCO in 2001 as an example of a cultural landscape shaped by human labour, and it is one of the most beautiful in Europe.
