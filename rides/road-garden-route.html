Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mossel Bay

    The Garden Route is usually said to begin at Mossel Bay, and this is where the story of European contact with this coast begins too. In 1488 the Portuguese navigator Bartolomeu Dias anchored here after rounding the Cape, making the first known European landing on this part of Africa, and the town's museum complex keeps a full-size replica of his caravel. A famous old milkwood tree in the town, known as the Post Office Tree, recalls the tradition that sailors left letters in a shoe hung from its branches.

  2. 2. Wilderness

    Wilderness is a quiet beach village on the Garden Route that has quietly kept out of the way of the bigger towns on either side. It sits where the Touw River and a string of freshwater lakes meet the Indian Ocean, about twelve kilometres east of George, at the foot of the forested Outeniqua mountains. The coastline here is a mixture of long sandy beach, tidal lagoon, lake and indigenous forest, and it forms part of the Garden Route National Park, so the development is limited and the birdlife is rich. Kingfishers, fish eagles and herons are common around the lakes, and the Knysna loerie, a bright green forest bird with a crimson flash in its wings, can be spotted in the trees.

  3. 3. Knysna

    Knysna lies around a tidal lagoon on the Garden Route, with a pair of sandstone cliffs known as the Heads guarding the narrow channel to the sea. Forest runs down to the water, and the whole town has a green, relaxed look, with a population around sixty-nine thousand and a waterfront full of small shops and restaurants. The lagoon is an estuary, home to rare seahorses and a rich oyster industry, and every year there is an oyster festival in the winter that fills the town.

  4. 4. Plettenberg Bay

    The Portuguese called this bend of the coast Bahia Formosa, the beautiful bay, and nobody has seriously disagreed since. Plettenberg Bay, or Plett as everyone calls it, sits near the eastern end of South Africa's Garden Route, with a curved beach below forested hills and the dark rocky peninsula of Robberg standing out to sea. The Dutch governor Joachim van Plettenberg gave the town its present name in 1778, and the place grew through timber, whaling and later tourism.

  5. 5. Storms River

    Storms River is a village of a few houses and a petrol station tucked in a clearing of the Tsitsikamma forest, a place that would be easy to overlook if the setting were not so extraordinary. The Garden Route here runs along a narrow strip of land between the Indian Ocean and the mountains, with ancient indigenous forest, towering yellowwood trees, ferns and mist, and every river cutting a deep gorge on its way to the sea. The Khoi name Tsitsikamma is usually translated as place of much water, and the name is earned.
