Why this ride

From Puértolas it runs 440 km to Puértolas; allow about 7 h 13 of riding. It goes by way of Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña, Cueva de los moros. and back through Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña. It tops out around 1,750 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

The roads

Pyrenees N260 Route

Cueva de los moros. is 125 km from Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña on Autovía Mudéjar. It climbs 790 m to the top at 1,124 m and drops 1040 m again. Take a break before the climb, not halfway up.

The stops

  1. 1. Abrigo de la Ermita de San Urbez

    Start here.

    The Añisclo canyon is one of the deepest and narrowest in the Spanish Pyrenees, a cleft in the limestone of the Ordesa and Monte Perdido National Park, and the road that runs through its lower section is one of the most remarkable in Aragon. At its southern end, beside an old stone bridge over the Bellós river, stands the hermitage of San Úrbez, built half into the cliff, with a cave behind it where tradition says the saint lived as a hermit. Close by, rock shelters hold traces of prehistoric painting.

    Read the complete Abrigo de la Ermita de San Urbez write-up.

  2. 2. Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña

    101 km in.

    Just north of Huesca, where the flat cereal land of the Hoya suddenly rears up into the first limestone walls of the Pre-Pyrenees, lies the little village of Nueno and, above it, the rock shelter named after Santa Eulalia de la Peña. This is the southern edge of the Sierra de Guara country, a landscape of gorges, conglomerate cliffs and scrub where griffon vultures ride the thermals and the light comes hard off pale stone.

    Read the complete Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña write-up.

  3. 3. Cueva de los moros.

    226 km in.

    There is a Cueva de los Moros in half the valleys of Spain. Every unexplained cave, ruin or rock shelter used to be credited to the Moors, and this one, in the hills of the western Aragonese Pyrenees between the valleys of Hecho and Ansó, is a rock shelter with ancient traces. You should approach it as an excuse to spend time in two of the most beautiful and least spoiled valleys of the Pyrenees, rather than as a monument.

    Read the complete Cueva de los moros. write-up.
