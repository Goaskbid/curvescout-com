Why this ride

A 216 km round trip from Bárcabo - roughly 3 h 43 in the saddle. It goes by way of Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña, Chaves I, Forau del Cocho and back through Chaves I. The highest point is about 1,050 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mascún IV

    Huesca was the capital of the kingdom of Aragón until the end of the eleventh century, when King Pedro I captured it from the Muslim rulers and made it his base. Today it is a calm provincial city of more than fifty thousand people, with a Gothic cathedral that has a magnificent alabaster altarpiece and the Romanesque monastery church of San Pedro el Viejo. It is the last big town before the mountains, and it is where you will want to fill your tank before heading north into the Sierra de Guara. The shelter at Mascún IV lies about thirty kilometres away, deep in the Mascún valley, about fifteen kilometres from Alquézar. It carries painting in the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain, and the ride links it with the Cueva de los Moros.

  2. 2. Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña

    Just north of Huesca, where the flat cereal land of the Hoya suddenly rears up into the first limestone walls of the Pre-Pyrenees, lies the little village of Nueno and, above it, the rock shelter named after Santa Eulalia de la Peña. This is the southern edge of the Sierra de Guara country, a landscape of gorges, conglomerate cliffs and scrub where griffon vultures ride the thermals and the light comes hard off pale stone.

  3. 3. Chaves I

    The Cueva de Chaves, in the foothills of the Sierra de Guara in Huesca, was one of the most important Neolithic sites in the Iberian Peninsula, with deposits showing some of the earliest farming and herding in north-eastern Spain. In 2007, in an act that caused outrage, the landowner bulldozed the cave's floor deposits, destroying much of the archaeological record, and the case went to court. Rock art shelters in the area, including Chaves I and others in the Vero river valley, survive and are part of the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin listing, with paintings ranging from Palaeolithic to schematic art.

  4. 4. Forau del Cocho

    South of Huesca the land opens out. The Pyrenees are behind you, a snowy line on a clear day, and ahead stretches the gently rolling cereal country that eventually turns into the dry steppes of Los Monegros. Forau del Cocho lies in this transitional landscape, between Torres de Barbués, Almuniente and the outskirts of Huesca itself, a place where the only relief is the occasional low ridge or gully cut into soft rock.
