Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Guadiana River Dam

    The Guadiana is Spain's quiet river, wide and slow, and at Mérida it has been tamed since Roman times. The label on this stop speaks of a dam, and there is a low weir across the river here, just beside the old bridge and the Moorish Alcazaba, but the thing that really commands attention is the Roman bridge itself. It is about 790 metres long and carried by some sixty arches, one of the longest ancient bridges still standing anywhere, and for two thousand years it carried the road toward the north and west across this very stretch of water.

  2. 2. Old Town of Cáceres

    Cáceres has an old town so complete that film crews treat it as a ready-made set, and walking its streets at dusk, when the stone glows amber and the crowds have gone, you can see why. Inside the walls, built largely by the Almohads in the twelfth century, are dozens of fortified mansions, towers, churches and palaces, many built by noble families who made their fortunes in the conquest of the Americas. The towers once had battlements, but Queen Isabella ordered most of them cut down to curb the nobles' power. UNESCO listed the old town in 1986.
