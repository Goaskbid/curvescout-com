Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Peso da Régua river

    Régua sits where the Douro valley opens enough to hold a town, and it has been the commercial heart of the wine country for a very long time. In 1756 the Portuguese crown drew a boundary around the vineyards of the upper Douro, making it one of the first officially demarcated wine regions in the world, and the town's role as a market and shipping point goes back further. Port wine from the high terraces was loaded onto flat-bottomed rabelo boats here and floated down to the lodges at Vila Nova de Gaia, near Porto, a journey that took days and cost many a boatman his life in the rapids.

  2. 2. Pinhão vineyards

    Pinhão is a small town in the heart of the Douro wine country, and arriving by the river road feels like entering the engine room of Port. The little town sits where the Pinhão river meets the Douro, with terraced vineyards rising on all sides, and many of the great estates, called quintas, lie within a few kilometres. The scale of the landscape is hard to take in at first. Every slope is cut into terraces, held by dry stone walls that took generations to build, and the schist soil is so poor that the vines have to send their roots deep to find water.

  4. 4. Amarante return

    Amarante is a handsome town on the banks of the Tâmega, a river that runs out of the hills toward the Douro, and it is built around an old stone bridge. The bridge, dedicated to São Gonçalo, the town's patron saint, was the scene of a fierce stand during the Peninsular War, when local forces held back a French army under Marshal Soult for two weeks in 1809, and the town remembers it with pride. Behind the bridge rises the church and convent of São Gonçalo, with its fine cloister and a tall, tiered facade, and the houses along the river lean over the water with iron balconies and washing lines.
