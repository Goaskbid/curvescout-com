Why this ride

The stops in between: Auckland / New Zealand, near Parakai, Waiuku and Titirangi. The climbs: near Pukekohe East and near Papakura.

The stops

  1. 1. Fuel stop near Pakuranga

    Start here.

  2. 2. Warkworth

    128 km in.

    North of Auckland the coast road thins out and the land becomes greener and wetter, with dairy farms, tidal creeks and little harbours cut deep into the hills. The ride is the start of a long weekend towards the Bay of Islands.

    Read the complete Warkworth write-up.

  3. 3. Food stop near Parakai

    251 km in.

  4. 4. Waiuku

    393 km in.

    Waiuku sits at the head of a long, muddy arm of the Manukau Harbour, south-west of Auckland, and for centuries it was a crossroads. Māori travellers hauled canoes across the short portage between the Manukau and the Waikato River here, linking the harbours of the north with the great river system of the interior, and early European traders used the same route. Today it is a quiet country town serving farms in the surrounding hills, with a main street of shops and cafés and an easy, unhurried rhythm.

    Read the complete Photo stop near Waiuku write-up.

  5. 5. Near Titirangi

    460 km in.

    Titirangi calls itself the gateway to the Waitākere Ranges, and it fits the role: a bohemian village of artists, writers and bush-clad houses, built on steep slopes above the Manukau Harbour, where the suburbs of Auckland end and the native forest begins. The finish is a few kilometres from the village, on the harbour side towards Lynfield and Blockhouse Bay, and it serves as a calm finish for long rides back from Northland.

    Read the complete Fuel stop near Titirangi write-up.

  6. 6. High point near Pukekohe East

    519 km in. High point near Pukekohe East is the summit of this stretch.

    The soil around Pukekohe is a deep chocolate red, the gift of old volcanoes, and it grows a startling share of New Zealand's vegetables. Market gardens, many started by Chinese and Indian immigrants in the twentieth century, cover the slopes with neat rows of onions, potatoes and greens, and the area remains one of the most productive horticultural districts in the country. Pukekohe East is a rural community on the low hills to the east of the town, with long views over the fields.

    Read the complete High point near Pukekohe East write-up.

  7. 7. Scenic stretch near Papakura

    533 km in.

    Papakura grew up on the Great South Road, built by British troops in the early 1860s to move soldiers and supplies south for the invasion of the Waikato, a key episode of the New Zealand Wars. Redoubts and military settlements dotted the route, and Papakura was one of them. Today it is a busy town on the southern edge of Auckland, with a main street, a railway station and expanding suburbs.

    Read the complete Fuel stop near Papakura write-up.

  8. 8. Back to Pakuranga

    626 km in.

    Waiheke, a short ferry ride east of Auckland, is a hilly island of roughly nine thousand residents, with bays of white sand, wooded gullies and, over the last few decades, a collection of vineyards that makes it the country's most famous wine island.

    Read the complete Pakuranga write-up.

  9. 9. Murrays Bay

    691 km in.

    Murrays Bay is one of the East Coast Bays on Auckland's North Shore, a sheltered crescent of sand between rocky headlands, with a wharf, a sailing club and big pōhutukawa trees leaning over the beach. In December these trees burst into crimson flower, which is why New Zealanders call the pōhutukawa their Christmas tree. Out across the Hauraki Gulf, the symmetrical cone of Rangitoto Island, Auckland's youngest volcano, sits on the horizon, and on a calm morning the water is glassy and pale.

    Read the complete Murrays Bay write-up.

  10. 10. Near Wellsford, on the way back

    759 km in.

    West of Wellsford, the land falls away towards the Kaipara Harbour, the largest harbour in New Zealand, a vast, shallow, branching body of water that reaches deep into the farmland of Northland. Its eastern arms creep up into quiet valleys, and this stop is near one of them, a place to pause on the way back south, stretch out in the grass and cool off in the water when the tide is in.

    Read the complete Near Wellsford, on the way back write-up.

  11. 11. Rosebank

    839 km in.

    Rosebank is not where anyone goes for the view. It is a light industrial peninsula on the western side of Auckland, between the suburb of Avondale and the Whau River, a place of factories, warehouses, workshops and car dealers, with a single long road running down the middle. But it has a surprising history, and it is a practical, unpretentious place to start a ride into the Waitākere Ranges.

    Read the complete Rosebank write-up.

  12. 12. Photo stop near Waiuku

    927 km in.

    Waiuku sits at the head of a long, muddy arm of the Manukau Harbour, south-west of Auckland, and for centuries it was a crossroads. Māori travellers hauled canoes across the short portage between the Manukau and the Waikato River here, linking the harbours of the north with the great river system of the interior, and early European traders used the same route. Today it is a quiet country town serving farms in the surrounding hills, with a main street of shops and cafés and an easy, unhurried rhythm.

    Read the complete Photo stop near Waiuku write-up.

  13. 13. Near Mangere

    1003 km in.

    This stop looks out from the northern edge of the Manukau Harbour, the vast, shallow inlet that almost cuts Auckland in two, looking across the water to Māngere and its volcanic cone. Auckland is built on a field of some fifty small volcanoes, and you can see several of them from here: Māngere Mountain across the harbour, the terraced slopes of One Tree Hill to the north-east, and others dotted around the suburbs. Many were fortified pā sites in pre-European times, and their terraces and food storage pits are still visible on the grass.

    Read the complete Near Mangere write-up.

  14. 14. High point near Papakura

    1054 km in. The high point of this section.

    East of Papakura, the land climbs into the Hunua Ranges, the largest stretch of native forest close to Auckland and the source of much of the city's drinking water. Reservoirs fill the valleys behind tall dams, and the forest around them, of tawa, kauri, rimu and tree ferns, is home to birds such as the kōkako, whose haunting song was once almost silenced and is now recovering thanks to pest control. The ranges are a regional park, laced with walking tracks.

    Read the complete High point near Papakura write-up.

  15. 15. Scenic stretch near Pakuranga

    1078 km in.

  16. 16. Back to Pakuranga

    Back where you started, 1165 km.

    The island is a patchwork of vineyards, olive groves, pohutukawa-fringed bays and rolling pasture, with winding roads and sudden views of the blue gulf. About nine thousand people live here, many of them commuters, artists and wine makers, and in summer the island fills with visitors drawn by its beaches, restaurants and cellar doors. Pakuranga, on the Auckland side, is the closest large suburb across the water.

    Read the complete Pakuranga write-up.
