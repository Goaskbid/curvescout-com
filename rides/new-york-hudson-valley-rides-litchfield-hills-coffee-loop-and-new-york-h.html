Why this ride

A one-way ride of 730 km, New Dorp Beach to Parsippany - roughly 13 h 16 in the saddle. On the way it passes near Rossville, Bayonne, Scenic stretch in Little Falls and in Ridgewood. near Mendham is the high point. Long stretches are under trees.

  3. 3. Near Bayonne

    On the New Jersey side of New York Harbor, the Bayonne peninsula sticks out like a thumb between Newark Bay and Upper New York Bay, with the Statue of Liberty and the Manhattan skyline in view to the north-east. It is a working city of around sixty-five thousand people, built on oil refineries, shipyards and the port, and it still feels like a place where things are made and moved.

  6. 6. Levittown, on the way back

    Levittown is one of the most famous suburbs in the world, though it does not look like much at first glance. After the Second World War, the building firm Levitt and Sons bought potato fields on Long Island and built thousands of identical small houses on an assembly line, sold cheaply to returning soldiers and their families. It became the model for postwar suburbs across America, with its lawns, curving streets and shopping centres, and it also became a symbol of their darker side: for years the houses were sold only to white families, under covenants written into the deeds.

  8. 8. Back to Litchfield Hills

    Staten Island is the quiet borough, the one that New Yorkers from the other four tend to forget, and the stretch of shoreline near New Dorp Beach shows its slower side. Low houses sit behind a seawall, a long beach faces out across Lower New York Bay, and on a clear day the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge hangs in the distance. The island has the largest areas of woodland and marsh in the city, with a greenbelt of hills and ponds in its middle, and the pace in parts feels almost suburban.

  9. 9. Fuel stop in Parsippany

    Parsippany-Troy Hills is suburban New Jersey at its most typical: office parks, shopping centres and the enormous junction of Interstate 80 and Interstate 287, one of the busiest crossroads in the state. Nobody comes here for the scenery, but it is a useful launch point, because the good roads of northern New Jersey begin just beyond the town limits.

  10. 10. High point near Mendham

    The winter of 1779 to 1780 was one of the harshest on record, and the Continental Army spent it in huts at Jockey Hollow, a few miles east of Mendham, short of food, clothing and pay. Soldiers froze, starved and deserted, and some mutinied, yet the army survived. The site is now part of Morristown National Historical Park, with reconstructed huts, an old farmhouse and quiet woodland trails, and it is a moving place to stop.

  11. 11. Food stop near Shrewsbury

    Inland from Shrewsbury and the Jersey Shore, western Monmouth County is a surprise to anyone who thinks New Jersey is all highways and suburbs. This is farm country, with horse paddocks, sod farms, orchards and fields of corn and soybeans, crossed by quiet roads and dotted with small villages. Allentown, a few miles west, is a delightful old mill town with a millpond, a main street of eighteenth-century houses and antique shops.

  12. 12. Sea Girt

    A short red-brick lighthouse with a square tower stands on the corner of Ocean Avenue in Sea Girt, looking more like a Victorian house that grew a lantern than a proper beacon. It was built in the 1890s to fill a dark gap in the chain of lights along the Jersey Shore, and today volunteers keep it open for occasional tours. Around it, Sea Girt is a small, quiet, well-kept borough of shingled houses, wide lawns and shady streets, with a non-commercial boardwalk running along a clean, broad beach.

  13. 13. Scenic stretch in Old Bethpage

    Step into Old Bethpage Village Restoration and Long Island's suburbs vanish: a nineteenth-century farming village of houses, barns, a church, a tavern and a blacksmith's shop, many of them moved here from elsewhere on the island to save them from demolition. Costumed staff work the fields and crafts, and the contrast with the busy roads outside is striking.

  14. 14. Photo stop near Fort Salonga

    Fort Salonga takes its name from a British stronghold of the Revolutionary War. Fort Slongo stood on a bluff above Long Island Sound, and in 1781 American troops crossed the water from Connecticut and raided it. Nothing much remains, but the name lives on in this leafy corner of the North Shore, between Kings Park and Northport, where wooded hills drop to rocky beaches and the Sound glitters to the north.

  15. 15. Kearny

    Kearny is a Scottish town that happens to be in New Jersey. In the late nineteenth century, thread mills and linoleum factories run by Scottish companies drew workers from Scotland and Northern Ireland to this hilly strip of land between the Passaic and Hackensack rivers, and they brought with them their churches, pipe bands and, above all, football. For decades Kearny was known as Soccer Town USA, producing an unusual number of players for the US national team, and the sport still matters here more than in most American towns.

  16. 16. Back to North

    Parsippany is a large, sprawling township in Morris County, with more than fifty thousand people, a lot of offices and a patchwork of highways, and it belongs to the belt of suburbs that runs west of Newark into the New Jersey hills. Its name may come from a Native word of uncertain meaning. To the north and west, the landscape starts to change, with ridges, lakes and woods of the Highlands rising, and the point lies right on the transition.
