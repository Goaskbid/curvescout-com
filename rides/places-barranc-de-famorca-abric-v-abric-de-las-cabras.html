Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc de famorca- Abric V

    The Guadalest valley is the best-known inland stretch of the Alicante coast, and the village of Guadalest itself, with its castle perched on a pillar of rock and its bell tower on the cliff, is one of the most photographed places in the province. The shelter at Barranc de Famorca, Abric V, is not in the busy part. It lies about two kilometres from Castell de Castells and a few kilometres from Famorca, in the quiet valleys on the far side of the Serrella. Like the others in the ravine, it carries Levantine painting protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain.

  2. 2. Abric de la Penya

    Moixent, or Mogente in Castilian, stands at the head of a corridor that has carried traffic between the Valencian coast and the interior of Castile for as long as anyone has been moving goods. The old road, the railway and now the motorway all funnel through here, between low limestone ridges with vineyards and olive groves in the valley floor. The rock shelter known as the Abric de la Penya sits in this landscape a few kilometres from the town, close also to Vallada.

  3. 3. Abric de las Cabras

    The Millares country belongs to the Júcar, the long river that cuts through the limestone of inland Valencia and fills the reservoir at Tous before spilling out onto the orange-growing plain. Millares itself has fewer than six hundred people and sits in a landscape of gorges, ravines and dry hills, with Dos Aguas nearby in a similar fold of the land. The Abric de las Cabras is in the hills a few kilometres from the village, a rock shelter whose name suggests the animals that still use these slopes.
