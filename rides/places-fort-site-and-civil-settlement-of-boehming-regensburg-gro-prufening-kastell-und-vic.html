Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fort site and civil settlement of Boehming

    The Altmühl valley is one of the most beautiful river valleys in Bavaria, and near the village of Böhming, a short way upstream from Kipfenberg, the Romans built a small fort to guard the point where their frontier, the Raetian Limes, crossed the river. The Limes here came down from the plateau, crossed the valley and climbed again on the other side, and the fort, with its civil settlement, controlled the crossing and the route along the river. Little is visible today beyond outlines in the fields and a few marked spots, but a stone inscription found here and the finds displayed in the Roman and Bavarian museum in Kipfenberg bring the site to life.

  2. 2. Fort site and civil settlement of Kösching

    North of the Danube, on the edge of the Franconian Jura, the Romans built a string of forts behind the Raetian frontier to guard the river valley and the roads leading to it. Kösching, today a market town near Ingolstadt, was the site of one of them, an auxiliary fort with a civilian settlement around it, home to craftsmen, traders and the families of soldiers. Little is visible today, as the modern town has grown over it, but the site is part of the UNESCO Frontiers of the Roman Empire. The Danube valley was a busy corridor in Roman times, and remains so today.

  3. 3. Fort site and civil settlement of Eining

    The Danube here is already a serious river, and about two thousand years ago it was the edge of the Roman Empire. Abusina, the Roman fort at Eining, stood on the south bank, where a small tributary joins, and its soldiers watched the Germanic lands across the water. The site lies in a bend a few kilometres from Neustadt an der Donau, and what remains are the foundations of a fort that was rebuilt several times, and of a civilian settlement, with a bath house, whose walls you can see outlined in the grass. The fort ended its life in the fifth century, in the unsettled times when Rome let go of the frontier.

  4. 4. Regensburg Großprüfening - Kastell und Vic

    There is not much to see at Großprüfening, and that is worth knowing before you arrive expecting walls. The Roman fort and its civil settlement lie on the western edge of Regensburg, close to where the Naab flows into the Danube, and what survives is mostly under fields and houses, known from excavation and aerial photographs rather than from standing masonry. The site is part of the Danube Limes, the river frontier that UNESCO listed in 2021, and its value is in the position: a garrison watching a river junction, a natural route north into what the Romans considered barbarian country.
