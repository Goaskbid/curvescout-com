Why this ride

A 254 km round trip from Ocna Mureş - roughly 4 h 23 in the saddle. It goes by way of Village of Valea Viilor, Feldioara - Cetățeaua and back through Village of Valea Viilor. The highest point is about 1,150 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Războieni - Sat Războieni - Cetate - Sat

    There are two Razboienis in Romania, and the one on the Mures river has nothing to do with the famous battle in Moldavia. This village lies in Alba County, in the broad valley of the Mures where the river runs between the Transylvanian plateau and the western ridge of the Apuseni Mountains, close to the town of Ocna Mures. Ocna means salt mine, and the name is no accident: the ground here sits on enormous salt deposits, and salt has been taken from it since Roman times, with a number of flooded old workings now serving as lakes where people swim in the briny water.

  2. 2. Village of Valea Viilor

    Valea Viilor means Valley of the Vineyards, and the name is honest: the slopes around the village were planted with vines long before anyone thought of mapping them. The Saxons called it Wurmloch, and its fortified church, a tall Gothic hall with a defensive storey built over the choir and a ring wall around it, is one of the seven on the World Heritage list.

  3. 3. Feldioara - Cetățeaua

    The Făgăraș mountains are the highest wall in Romania, and from the Olt valley at Ucea they rise like a grey and white curtain across the southern sky, with Moldoveanu and Negoiu, the country's two highest peaks, hidden somewhere in the ridge. Down in the valley, near the villages of Ucea de Jos and Feldioara, the Romans had a fort at a site called Cetățeaua, guarding the Olt route that formed part of the frontier system of Dacia. The remains, earthworks and fragments in the fields, are included in the Frontiers of the Roman Empire listing for Dacia, added by UNESCO in 2024.
