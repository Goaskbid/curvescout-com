Why this ride

Blue water, Alpine edges and clean Bavarian rhythm. Out of Kochel and back again: 156 km, roughly 2 h 41 in the saddle. The stops in between: Walchensee road, Jachenau valley and Bad Tölz. The highest point is about 1,250 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kochelsee lakeside

    Kochel am See lies right at the point where the Bavarian flatlands end, as if somebody had stood the Alps up against the lake. The Kochelsee is a small, deep glacial lake of a few kilometres' length with reed beds along the north shore, and behind the village the ground rises sharply to the Herzogstand and Heimgarten, two wooded ridges that give the lake its backdrop. The village has about four thousand people, a handful of cafes, a promenade and, if you ride south from Penzberg, the first real sense of arriving in the mountains.

  2. 2. Walchensee road

    The Walchensee is one of the deepest and clearest Alpine lakes in Germany, and the road along its eastern and southern shore is one of the most photographed in Bavaria. The water is a turquoise that looks almost Caribbean when the sun hits the shallows, and it is fed by cold springs, which keep it chilly all summer. Around it rise forested slopes, the Herzogstand to the north and the peaks of the Karwendel and the Estergebirge to the south, so the lake sits in a bowl, with its surface often creased by wind.

  3. 3. Jachenau valley

    Not many people know the Jachenau. It is a long, narrow side valley that runs east from the Walchensee region to the Isar, shut in by forested ridges and farmed by a few families on meadow terraces, and it has the quality of a place that the main roads missed. Lenggries, with its nine thousand inhabitants, is the nearest real town and the place for petrol, bakeries and shops, about eleven kilometres to the east.

  4. 4. Bad Tölz food

    Bad Tölz is the market town of the Isarwinkel, a place of seventeen thousand people on the Isar river with the Alps standing behind it, and it is the sort of town where a motorcyclist can eat very well. The old main street, the Marktstraße, climbs gently from the river and is lined with houses painted in bright colours and decorated with gables and painted fronts, a pattern that Bavarian towns have followed for centuries. It is a spa town in a modest way, known since the nineteenth century for the iodine-rich springs that gave it the name Bad, and people still come here for the cure.

  5. 5. Back to Kochelsee

    Kochel am See lies at the foot of the Alps, where the Bavarian lowlands finally give way to mountains, and the Kochelsee fills the basin in front of it with a sheet of green-blue water about six hundred metres above the sea. The town has about four thousand people and a long lakeside promenade, with the first big peaks rising behind. The painter Franz Marc, one of the founders of the Blue Rider group, lived in the village of Ried, just outside, and the Franz Marc Museum on the hill above the lake holds a lovely collection of his work and that of his circle. Marc died at Verdun in 1916.
