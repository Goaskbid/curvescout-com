Why this ride

A 84 km round trip from Nerpio - roughly 1 h 27 in the saddle. The stops in between: Prado del Tornero I, Diosa madre and back through Prado del Tornero I. The highest point is about 1,400 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abrigo de la Ventana II

    A window in the rock gives the shelter its name, and the Abrigo de la Ventana II lies about twelve kilometres from Nerpio in the high country of the Sierra del Segura, where limestone cliffs are pierced by natural openings and hollows. It is the second of two shelters of that name, and it appears on a route with the Diosa Madre, the Mother Goddess, which is the title that archaeologists have given to a rock painting that they interpret as a female figure. The whole group belongs to the Mediterranean rock art listed by UNESCO in 1998.

  2. 2. Prado del Tornero I

    Nerpio is the place the road seems to forget. It lies in the far south-west of Albacete province, tucked against the Sierra de Segura and the Murcian border, in a high valley where the headwaters of the Taibilla and other streams gather. It has fewer than two thousand residents and one of the densest clusters of prehistoric rock paintings in Spain, dozens of shelters scattered through the surrounding crags. These are part of the Levantine and schematic traditions, painted in red and black, and covered by the same World Heritage listing as the painted caves of the Levante coast.

  3. 3. Diosa madre

    Some places on a map feel remote; this one actually is. The point named Diosa Madre, mother goddess, lies in the mountains of the Sierra de Segura, in the far southwest of Albacete province, roughly sixteen kilometres from Yeste and not far from the border with Jaén. The mountains themselves are the real experience.
