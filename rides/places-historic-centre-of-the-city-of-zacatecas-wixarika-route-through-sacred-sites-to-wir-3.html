Why this ride

From Zacatecas it runs 380 km to Zacatecas; allow about 6 h 33 of riding. The stops in between: Sanctuary of Plateros, Wixárika Route through Sacred Sites to Wir and back through Sanctuary of Plateros. The highest point is about 2,350 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Historic centre of the city of Zacatecas

    Silver built Zacatecas, and the city still wears it like a coat. In 1546, a few years after the Spanish crushed the Indigenous rising of the Mixtón War, prospectors found a rich vein in the hills here, and within a generation the town was one of the wealthiest in the Americas, shipping silver across the Atlantic and bankrolling the Spanish crown. The money went into churches and mansions, and the centre, listed by UNESCO in 1993, is a tight, steep tangle of pink-stone buildings in a ravine between two hills, 2,400 metres above the sea.

  2. 2. Sanctuary of Plateros

    Plateros is a small town on the edge of the high desert plateau in Zacatecas, a few kilometres from the silver city of Fresnillo, and it exists, more or less, because of one small figure on an altar. The Santo Nino de Atocha, a child dressed as a pilgrim and carrying a basket and a staff, is revered here as a protector of travellers, prisoners and the sick. The devotion came from Spain, where the original image belongs to the shrine of Atocha in Madrid, and it was carried across the Atlantic by the Spanish miners who came for Zacatecas silver. The sanctuary at Plateros became one of the most important pilgrimage sites in Mexico.

  3. 3. Wixárika Route through Sacred Sites to Wir

    West of Jerez, in Zacatecas, the land starts to fold into the long ridges of the Sierra Madre Occidental, and somewhere in these hills runs the path that Wixárika pilgrims follow from their homeland in the mountains of Jalisco and Nayarit toward Wirikuta, their sacred desert far to the east. The route, listed by UNESCO in 2025, is not a road in the usual sense but a chain of sacred places, springs, hills and caves where the pilgrims stop to pray, leave offerings and remember the journey of their ancestors, who according to tradition walked this way at the beginning of the world.
