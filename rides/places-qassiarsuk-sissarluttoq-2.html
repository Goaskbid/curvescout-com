Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Qassiarsuk

    Qassiarsuk is a sheep farming settlement of a few dozen people on the green side of a fjord in southern Greenland, and for a few years around the year 1000 it was the centre of European Greenland. The Norse chieftain Erik the Red, banished from Iceland for killing, arrived here in the 980s, built a farm that he named Brattahlíð, and became the leader of a colony that would eventually number several thousand. His son Leif, called the Lucky, sailed from here to the shores of North America.

  2. 2. Igaliku

    A motorcycle is an awkward guest in southern Greenland, and Igaliku is the reason to think about it carefully. There is no road network linking the settlements here; you reach them by boat, helicopter, or on foot, and the tracks between the farms are short and local. Nevertheless this is one of the great historical landscapes of the Arctic, and it deserves a place in any ride that dreams about the edge of the map.

  3. 3. Sissarluttoq

    Greenland has no roads between its towns, and in the south, where the green fjords of Kujataa lie, that fact is both a limit and a charm. The Norse came here in the late tenth century, led by Erik the Red, who settled at Brattahlíð, now the farm of Qassiarsuk, and for nearly five hundred years there were farms, churches and a bishop at Garðar, today's Igaliku. The settlers raised cattle, sheep and goats and hunted seals and walrus, and then, around the fifteenth century, they vanished, for reasons still debated, among them climate, trade and conflict. The Inuit farmers and hunters who live there now raise sheep on the same pastures, and UNESCO listed the Kujataa landscape in 2017 as an example of farming at the edge.
