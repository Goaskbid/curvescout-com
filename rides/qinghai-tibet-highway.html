Why this ride

A one-way ride of 3099 km, Xining to Lhasa - roughly 42 h 13 in the saddle. The stops in between: Golmud, Tanggula Pass and Nagqu. The highest point is about 5,250 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Golmud → Tanggula Pass

The ground tilts up and stays tilted for 1009 km. 5231 m of it, taken steadily rather than in one wall — a steady rhythm suits this better than bursts. The height arrives whether you hurry or not.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Xining

    Xining stands on the edge of the Tibetan Plateau, at about 2,200 metres, and it is the capital of Qinghai Province, a city of more than seven hundred thousand people in a valley between high, dry hills. It is a meeting point of cultures: Han Chinese, Tibetan, Hui Muslim and Mongol communities all live here, and you can see it in the street food, the mosques and the monasteries. The Dongguan Great Mosque, one of the largest in northwest China, stands near the old centre, and the smell of grilled lamb and baking bread drifts from the Muslim quarter.

  2. 2. Golmud

    Golmud is a city built on a salt flat, and it exists because of the road. The name comes from a Mongolian word for river, which sounds optimistic for a place on the edge of the Qaidam Basin, a vast, high desert of salt lakes, gravel plains and oil wells. At about 2,800 metres, with the Kunlun mountains rising to the south, it is a modern, wide-avenued city of several hundred thousand people that grew from almost nothing in the 1950s, when engineers began pushing a highway and later a railway across the Tibetan plateau.

  3. 3. Tanggula Pass

    Tanggula Pass is a place of thin air and big silence. At 5,231 metres on the Qinghai-Tibet Highway, it is one of the high points of the road that crosses the plateau from Golmud to Lhasa, and it marks the boundary between Qinghai and the Tibet Autonomous Region. There is little here but a stone marker, a wide view and a landscape of bare brown hills dusted with snow. You will feel the altitude as soon as you step off the bike: a pounding head, short breath and a strange slowness in your limbs.

  4. 4. Nagqu

    Nagqu is the biggest town you will meet on the northern half of the Tibetan plateau, and it feels like a frontier. It sits at roughly 4,500 metres on the open grassland between the Tanggula range and Lhasa, a high, cold country of yak herders, black tents and wide rolling pasture, and the Chinese name for it is Naqu. The Tibetan name means the black river, and the pastures around it have fed nomadic families for centuries.

  5. 5. Lhasa

    Lhasa lies at about 3,650 metres in a wide, dry valley beside the Kyichu River, and almost every rider who arrives there from the east is a little dizzy from the altitude, a little sunburnt and more than a little humbled. The G318, known as the Sichuan-Tibet Highway, runs over two thousand kilometres from Chengdu, crossing a series of passes above 4,000 metres, plunging through gorges and following the great bend of the Yarlung Tsangpo before it finally settles on this plateau. Reaching the city is the culmination of one of the world's classic overland roads.
