Why this ride

From Klagenfurt am Wörthersee it runs 185 km to Klagenfurt am Wörthersee; allow about 3 h 11 of riding. It goes by way of Church of St. Francis of Assisi, Plečnik’s Žale - Garden of All Saints, Promenade along the Embankments and Bridge and Green Promenade along Vegova Street. It crosses from Austria into Slovenia - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Klagenfurt

    Klagenfurt has a dragon, and the dragon is the first thing to see. The Lindwurm fountain on the Neuer Platz, carved from a single block of local stone in the late sixteenth century, is the symbol of the city, and the legend says a monster once lived in the marshes here before the founders drained them. The city of ninety thousand people is the capital of Carinthia, built on flat ground just east of the Wörthersee, with the long wall of the Karawanks to the south marking the border with Slovenia. Robert Musil and Ingeborg Bachmann were both born here, an unexpected literary pedigree for a quiet provincial capital.

  2. 2. Church of St. Francis of Assisi

    Ljubljana's Church of Saint Francis of Assisi stands in the Šiška district, north-west of the old centre, and it is one of the buildings by Jože Plečnik that UNESCO listed in 2021. Plečnik was born in Ljubljana in 1872, trained in Vienna under Otto Wagner, and worked in Prague, where he renovated the castle for the first Czechoslovak president, before returning to his home city in 1921. He spent the next decades remaking Ljubljana in his own style, with bridges, markets, a library and gardens, mixing classical forms with a personal, almost playful imagination. The church in Šiška, built in the late 1920s, is among his strangest and most original buildings.

  3. 3. Plečnik’s Žale - Garden of All Saints

    It is the city's central burial ground, laid out on the eastern edge of town, and in the late 1930s the architect Joze Plecnik added a series of chapels, arcades and entrance structures that turn it into a quiet landscape of white walls, columns and calm courtyards. He called it the Garden of All Saints, and the name fits. Plecnik was Slovenia's great architect of the twentieth century, a man who also shaped the bridges, markets and riverbanks of Ljubljana, and his works in the city were added to the UNESCO list in 2021.

  4. 4. Promenade along the Embankments and Bridge

    Ljubljana is a small capital with a big reputation for charm, and much of that comes from the river. The Ljubljanica runs through the centre in a shallow curve, with cafes along both banks, willows trailing in the water and bridges at every few steps. The architect Joze Plecnik, who worked here for much of the first half of the twentieth century, shaped the embankments, the market colonnade and the bridges, and his work was listed by UNESCO in 2021 as part of a series of his buildings.

  5. 5. Green Promenade along Vegova Street

    Vegova Street is one of the quieter corners of Plecnik's Ljubljana, and the green promenade that runs beside it was one of the places where he turned a piece of ordinary city into something calm and ordered. The area lies in the district between the old town and the university, where the new National and University Library stands, and the planting, avenues of trees, paths and small spaces have been laid out with the same care he gave to the grand squares. It is a modest thing, but modest is what makes it special.
