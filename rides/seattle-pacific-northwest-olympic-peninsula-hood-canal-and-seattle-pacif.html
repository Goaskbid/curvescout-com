Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Seattle

    Seattle runs on water. The city is squeezed between Puget Sound on the west and Lake Washington on the east, and is cut through by canals and bridges, so that every day involves a shore of some kind. Elliott Bay holds the working waterfront, with the ferry terminal at Colman Dock and the old piers, and ferries still carry commuters and tourists to Bainbridge Island and Bremerton, past a skyline that looks best from the deck.

    Read the complete Seattle write-up.

  2. 2. Rainier

    Despite the name, the town of Rainier is not on the mountain. It is a small place in south Thurston County, Washington, strung along State Route 507 between Tenino and Yelm, a farming and commuter town of a couple of thousand people on the prairies south of Puget Sound. On a clear day, though, the mountain it is named after looms on the eastern horizon like a second sky, white and enormous, and you understand the choice.
