Why this ride

It goes by way of Tianxiang, Qixingtan beach and Taroko Gorge. The climb of the day is Hehuanshan high road. It tops out at about 3,300 m. It is a high-mountain day.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Taroko Gorge gate

    Taroko Gorge is the great cleft in Taiwan's eastern flank, where the Liwu river has cut through a mass of marble and schist for several million years, and where the mountains rise almost vertically from the Pacific coast to over three thousand metres. The gate at the mouth lies some twenty kilometres north of Hualien, and from there the road follows the river into a gorge so narrow in places that the walls seem to lean together overhead. The marble is grey, white and streaked, the water is a vivid turquoise, and the sound of the river echoes off the rock.

  2. 2. Tianxiang

    Tianxiang is a small settlement deep inside the Taroko Gorge on Taiwan's east coast, and it is where the Central Cross-Island Highway stops being a canyon road and turns into a mountain one. Just before you reach it, the road threads the marble gorge of the Liwu river, one of the most remarkable canyon landscapes in Asia, where grey and white cliffs rise hundreds of metres, and tunnels, galleries and a narrow ledge cut by hand carry the traffic. At Tianxiang, the valley widens, and the road splits, one branch continuing up toward the high mountains and the other leading into a side valley.

  3. 3. Hehuanshan high road

    Wuling, the pass just beyond the Hehuanshan high road, is the highest paved road crossing in East Asia, at 3,275 metres, and the ride there from the lowlands is a journey through half the climates of the world. In Puli the air is warm and smells of fruit and betel nut, and by the time you reach the pass you are in a landscape of alpine meadow, bamboo grass and wind-sculpted fir, with temperatures that can hover near freezing even in summer. The road is Provincial Highway 14A, a spur off the main east-west highway, and it climbs from the valley of Puli in a long succession of curves for more than eighty kilometres.

  4. 4. Qixingtan beach

    Taiwan's east coast does not have sand beaches in the postcard sense. Qixingtan, whose name means Seven Star Lake, is a long curve of grey pebbles on the Pacific side of Hualien, where the water goes from shallow to deep in a few steps and the waves arrive with real weight. Swimming is discouraged because of the undertow, and signs make that clear, but sitting on the stones with a drink while the sea rolls in is a very good way to end a ride. Behind you the Central Range rises almost straight from the coast, with cloud caught in the ridges, and on a clear morning the sun comes up out of the ocean in front of you.

  6. 6. Hualien

    Hualien lies on Taiwan's wild east coast, squeezed between the Pacific and a wall of mountains that rise to over three thousand metres within a few dozen kilometres of the sea. The city is relaxed, with a lively night market, sea-view cafes and the slightly weathered air of a place that is regularly shaken by earthquakes and typhoons. It is also the gateway to Taroko Gorge, where the Liwu river has cut a deep canyon through marble.

  7. 7. Shitiping

    Shitiping means roughly stone steps terrace, and that already tells you a lot about the place. It sits on Taiwan's east coast, where the Coastal Mountain Range drops almost straight into the Pacific, and the highway squeezes itself between rock and sea. The area belongs to the long, wild stretch of Provincial Highway 11, the road that runs south from Hualien towards Taitung with the ocean on one side nearly all the way.

  8. 8. Sanxiantai

    Sanxiantai means Terrace of the Three Immortals, and the name comes from a legend of three Taoist immortals who are said to have rested on the offshore island here. It lies on Taiwan's east coast, near the town of Chenggong, where the sea turns a startling deep blue, and a long arched footbridge, shaped like a dragon's back, runs out to the island across a lagoon. Seen from the shore, with the waves breaking on dark volcanic rock, it has a wild, sculptural look.

  9. 9. Dulan

    Dulan sits on Taiwan's east coast, where the mountains drop almost straight into the Pacific. The village, in Taitung County about fifteen kilometres north of Taitung City, lies along Provincial Highway 11, a ribbon of road that runs between the Coastal Mountain Range and the sea, and the combination of mountain, surf and open sky has made it a haven for surfers, artists and refugees from the island's crowded west.

  10. 10. Taitung

    Taitung is a quiet, easy-going city on Taiwan's south-eastern coast, with about a hundred and ten thousand people, a long Pacific shoreline, and a pace of life noticeably slower than the busy west coast. Behind it, the Coastal Range runs north to south on one side and the Central Mountain Range on the other, with the long East Rift Valley in between, a flat strip of farmland that follows the line where two tectonic plates meet. For a rider who has spent the day on the road, it is an easy and friendly place to end.
