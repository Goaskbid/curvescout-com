Why this ride

A 302 km round trip from Cheremule - roughly 5 h 12 in the saddle. On the way it passes Funerary Tradition in the Prehistory of Sa, Archaeological Park of Pranu Mutteddu and back through Funerary Tradition in the Prehistory of Sa. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Petroglyph Park-a

    The north-west of Sardinia is a country of low volcanic hills, pasture, cork oak and stone, and the villages of the Logudoro are small, quiet and far older than they look. This area, around Cheremule and Giave, is dotted with traces of a world that predates the Romans by a very long time. The island's prehistoric people carved tombs into soft rock, the domus de janas, which translates as fairy houses, and built round stone towers called nuraghi, of which there are thousands.

  2. 2. Funerary Tradition in the Prehistory of Sa

    The high country of central Sardinia, between the Tirso river and the hills of the Barbagia, is a landscape of basalt plateaus, cork forests, small stone villages and long, empty views. The nearest places on our list, Nughedu Santa Vittoria and Neoneli, are small villages with only a few hundred people each, and the town of Macomer is some twenty-five kilometres away. The prehistoric story here is about how the island's early people treated their dead, and the title reflects that.

  3. 3. Archaeological Park of Pranu Mutteddu

    The south of Sardinia is not all beaches. Inland, on the edge of the Gerrei hills, lies Pranu Mutteddu, a flat upland of granite, cork oak and scrub that holds one of the island's more striking prehistoric sites. The name means something like the plain of the little stones in Sardinian, and the place is known for its menhirs, standing stones raised several thousand years ago by Neolithic people, many of them still upright and arranged in rows or groups across the clearing.
