Why this ride

A 256 km round trip from Bárcabo - roughly 4 h 25 in the saddle. The stops in between: Forau del Cocho, Abrigo de Los Gitanos and back through Forau del Cocho. The highest point is about 1,100 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cueva Peña Miel II

    The Sierra de Guara is cut by gorges so deep that sunlight reaches the bottom only at midday, and the Cueva Peña Miel lies on the edge of that wild country, in the foothills of the Pyrenees in Huesca. It is part of the Río Vero Cultural Park, an area where painted shelters, some in the Levantine tradition and others in a later, more abstract schematic style, are scattered through the canyons and cliffs. The park is a UNESCO site, and Peña Miel, which translates as Honey Rock, is one of the smaller ones, a fact that keeps it quiet.

  2. 2. Forau del Cocho

    South of Huesca the land opens out. The Pyrenees are behind you, a snowy line on a clear day, and ahead stretches the gently rolling cereal country that eventually turns into the dry steppes of Los Monegros. Forau del Cocho lies in this transitional landscape, between Torres de Barbués, Almuniente and the outskirts of Huesca itself, a place where the only relief is the occasional low ridge or gully cut into soft rock.

  3. 3. Abrigo de Los Gitanos

    The road north-west out of Huesca toward Pamplona runs along the foot of a dramatic wall of rock. To the north are the Pre-Pyrenean sierras, and studded along them are some of Aragón's most striking sights: the Castle of Loarre, a superbly preserved Romanesque fortress on a spur, and the Mallos de Riglos, huge red conglomerate towers rising straight out of the hillside. The rock shelter known as the Abrigo de los Gitanos lies in this country, between Ayerbe, Loarre and Luesia.
