Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Near Heggenes

    Beitostølen is a ski resort with a long summer afterlife, sitting high on the edge of the Valdres valley at the point where farmland gives way to open mountain. The village is a collection of hotels, chalets, shops and lifts, and in summer it serves as the gateway to the Valdresflye, the high plateau road that crosses toward Jotunheimen, Norway's highest mountains. Heggenes and the rest of Øystre Slidre lie further down the valley, among farms, lakes and forests.

  2. 2. Norwegian Scenic Route Valdresflye point 2

    Bygdin is a long, narrow mountain lake lying like a blade between steep slopes, and the Valdresflye road runs along part of its edge. At roughly 1,060 metres above the sea it is already in the zone where the trees thin out and the weather takes over, and on a calm day the surface reflects the peaks so cleanly that you have to look twice to see which way up the world is.

  3. 3. Norwegian Scenic Route Valdresflye point 3

    Gjendesheim is the place where Norwegian mountain romance meets Norwegian mountain reality. The long, green lake of Gjende lies below, coloured by glacial flour into a shade that looks like it came from a paint chart for a particular kind of dream, and above it rises the Besseggen ridge, one of the most famous walks in the country. Ibsen sent his hero Peer Gynt riding across it on the back of a reindeer, and generations of schoolchildren have never quite forgiven him for making it sound easy.

  4. 4. Vågåmo

    Vågåmo is a village in the Gudbrandsdalen valley, in the middle of Norway's mountain heartland, and it sits beside the river Otta, with the Jotunheimen range to the west. The village has about fifteen hundred people, a church, a school, shops and a fuel station, and it is the place where the Valdresflye road comes down to meet the main valley. The Vågåvatnet lake lies just beside the village, and the farms stretch in lines along the hillsides. The name is thought to derive from an old word for a bay or inlet, and the lake was once much larger.
