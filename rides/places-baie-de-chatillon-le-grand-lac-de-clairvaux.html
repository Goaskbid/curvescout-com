Why this ride

A 329 km round trip from Châtillon - roughly 5 h 26 in the saddle. It goes by way of Immeuble Clarté, Le Grand Lac de Clairvaux and back through Immeuble Clarté. It crosses from France into Switzerland - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

The roads

Jura Crest Scenic Road

Le Grand Lac de Clairvaux is 91 km from Immeuble Clarté, mostly on D 678. A proper up-and-over: 940 m of climbing, 1,184 m at the top, 790 m down. Top up fuel at the bottom - pumps thin out on the way up.

The stops

  1. 1. Baie de Châtillon

    Start here.

    At the northern tip of the Lac du Bourget, the largest natural lake entirely in France, the shore curves into a shallow bay below the village of Châtillon and its castle. In the shallows, divers and archaeologists have found the remains of prehistoric lake villages, and the Baie de Châtillon is one of the French sites included in the UNESCO listing of prehistoric pile dwellings around the Alps. The castle, perched on a rocky hill above the bay, was the seat of local lords in the Middle Ages and has a commanding view over the lake.

    Read the complete Baie de Châtillon write-up.

  2. 2. Immeuble Clarté

    74 km in.

    In 1932, while most of Geneva was building in stone, Le Corbusier put up an apartment block made almost entirely of steel and glass. The Immeuble Clarté, in the Eaux-Vives district on the east side of the city, was revolutionary for its time: a prefabricated steel frame, huge windows that flood the apartments with light, duplex flats with double-height living rooms and a structure that allowed flexible layouts. The name means clarity, and the building lives up to it. Since 2016 it has been part of the UNESCO listing of the architectural work of Le Corbusier, alongside the Villa Savoye, the Unité d'Habitation and other buildings on several continents.

    Read the complete Immeuble Clarté write-up.

  3. 3. Le Grand Lac de Clairvaux

    165 km in.

    The Jura plateau behind Clairvaux-les-Lacs is a country of limestone, cows and lakes, and the Grand Lac de Clairvaux is the largest of the lakes that give the town its name. Prehistoric people lived on its shore on pilings, in villages that rose and fell for centuries, and the waterlogged mud preserved their wood and tools. The sites on Clairvaux's lakes are among the pile-dwelling places inscribed on the UNESCO list in 2011. The lake today is a quiet blue sheet, ringed by reeds and low wooded hills, with a beach where families swim in July.
