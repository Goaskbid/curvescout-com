Why this ride

From Krimml it runs 27 km to Gerlos; allow about 28 min of riding. The stops in between: Krimml Waterfalls and Gerlos Pass. The highest point is about 2,150 m. Gerlos Pass is subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Gerlos Alpine Road

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 1507 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance. Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Krimml

    Krimml sits at the very top of the Salzach valley in Pinzgau, where the Hohe Tauern close in and the river is still a loud young thing. The village is small, under a thousand people, and it exists largely because of water: the Krimml Falls drop in three stages for something like 380 metres, which makes them the highest in Austria and among the tallest in Europe. You hear them before you see them, a low roar that gets into the car park, and in the afternoon the spray hangs in the trees like fog.

  2. 2. Krimml Waterfalls

    You hear the Krimml falls before you see them. A low, constant roar rolls down the valley long before the road reaches the village, and on a warm day in early summer, when meltwater from the Hohe Tauern is at its peak, the spray hangs in the air like a fine drizzle. The Krimmler Ache drops about 380 metres in three steps, which makes this the highest waterfall in Austria and one of the highest in Europe.

  3. 3. Gerlos Pass

    The Gerlos Pass is a handsome, civilised crossing between the Zillertal in Tyrol and the Pinzgau in Salzburg province, and at 1,507 metres it is low enough to be open for most of the year, though not low enough to ignore. It is a toll road, a fact that the locals accept with a shrug, and the money pays for a surface that is smooth, wide and well marked, and for snow clearing that begins early in the winter.

  4. 4. Gerlos

    Gerlos is a ski village strung along a high Tyrolean saddle, roughly 1,200 metres up, between the Zillertal to the west and the Salzach valley to the east. It is the sort of place that is busiest in February, and in summer feels pleasantly half asleep, with farmhouses and chalets, cows with bells, and a handful of cafés that serve strudel to people in leathers at an unhurried pace. The nearest town of any size is a long way off, so think of it as a stop for coffee and a stretch rather than a place to do errands.
