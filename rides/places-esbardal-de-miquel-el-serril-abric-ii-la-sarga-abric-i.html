Why this ride

From Castell de Castells it runs 66 km to Castell de Castells; allow about 1 h 08 of riding. On the way it passes Barranc de frainos- Abric I, La Sarga- Abric I and back through Barranc de frainos- Abric I. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Esbardal de miquel el Serril- Abric II

    Castell de Castells sits in a valley with running water in it, which in dry Alicante counts for a good deal. Springs, small pools and irrigated terraces give this corner of the Serrella a green freshness that comes as a surprise in such country. The Esbardal de Miquel el Serril is a rocky outcrop and shelter a kilometre and a half from the village, in a landscape of cliffs, pine and almond, and it is one of the closest shelters to Castell de Castells, with Benimantell four kilometres away.

  2. 2. Barranc de frainos- Abric I

    The mountains behind Benidorm are a world away from the high-rise hotels and the beaches. Inland from the Costa Blanca, the land rises quickly into a jumble of limestone ridges, the highest of which, the Serra d'Aitana, reaches over fifteen hundred metres, the roof of the province of Alicante. Between Sella, Benilloba and Relleu, deep in this rugged country, the Barranc de Frainos holds a rock shelter with prehistoric paintings, part of the UNESCO listing of Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula. The sites here are small and fragile, and access is usually limited to guided visits, but the landscape is worth the journey.

  3. 3. La Sarga- Abric I

    Alcoy, or Alcoi in Valencian, is a city of bridges, built where three rivers meet in deep ravines, in the mountains of northern Alicante. It was one of the cradles of Spain's industrial revolution, with textile and paper mills powered by its rivers, and its streets have fine modernist buildings from that era. It is also famous for its Moors and Christians festival in April, one of the most spectacular in Spain, with parades, music and mock battles in honour of Saint George. The rock shelters of La Sarga lie in the hills about eight kilometres from the city, toward Ibi.
