Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Near Baden-Baden

    Baden-Baden is where the Schwarzwaldhochstrasse begins its climb, and this stretch just south-east of the spa town is the moment the road leaves the parks and villas behind and starts to work. The B500 rises out of the valley through mixed woods of beech and fir, gaining height steadily in long, well-cambered bends, with the occasional tighter hairpin and glimpses back over the Rhine plain. Within a few kilometres you have gone from a town of colonnades and casinos to the cool, resin-scented air of the northern Black Forest.

  2. 2. Near Biberach

    This is the high middle of the Schwarzwaldhochstrasse, where the road runs along the crest at more than a thousand metres and the Hornisgrinde, the highest summit of the northern Black Forest, rises just to the north. Right beside the road lies the Mummelsee, a small dark lake of glacial origin set in a hollow of fir trees. It is the stop everyone makes, which is both its charm and its problem: on summer weekends the car park is full, coaches line up, and the lakeside hotel and shop do a roaring trade in Black Forest cake and cuckoo clocks.

  3. 3. Near Oppenau

    South of the Ruhestein the Schwarzwaldhochstrasse runs along the high ground between the Schliffkopf and the Kniebis plateau, and the forest feels wilder here than further north. Much of this ridge lies in the Black Forest National Park, created in 2014, where large areas are being left to grow without forestry. You see the results from the saddle: grey snags of storm-felled trees, dense young growth, open patches of heather and grass on the old high pastures called Grinden, where cattle once grazed. It is not a manicured landscape, and that is the point.
