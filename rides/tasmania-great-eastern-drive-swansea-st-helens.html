Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Swansea

    Swansea sits on the shore of Great Oyster Bay, halfway up Tasmania's east coast, and its position is one of the best on the island. The bay curves away to the east, and across the water the pink granite peaks of the Hazards rise above the Freycinet Peninsula, glowing in the sunset like a stage set. Behind the town the land climbs to the Eastern Tiers, and the Meredith River runs through farmland to the sea. It is one of the oldest settlements on this coast, and the stone buildings, old warehouses and quiet streets reflect its long history of farming, whaling and sheep.

  2. 2. Freycinet

    Freycinet is the peninsula on Tasmania's east coast where a granite spine called the Hazards rises pink and orange out of the sea, the kind of rock that looks stained by the last sunset, and the colour comes from the feldspar in the stone. The famous curve of Wineglass Bay lies on the far side, a white arc of sand reached by a steep walking track of about an hour from the car park, and a good viewpoint is on the way up; it is a fair climb in motorcycle boots, so plan to change shoes.

  3. 3. Bicheno

    Bicheno is a small fishing village on Tasmania's east coast, built around a tiny harbour known as the Gulch, where fishing boats shelter behind a rocky breakwater. The town has a quirky, cheerful feel, with a blowhole that spouts when the sea is rough, rock pools full of life and the island of Diamond Island, which you can walk to at low tide. The sea here is clear and cold, and the marine reserve off the coast teems with fish and sea life, making it a favourite of divers.

  4. 4. St Helens

    St Helens sits at the head of Georges Bay on the north-east coast of Tasmania, and it is the biggest town on this stretch, though that still means about two thousand people. The bay is a wide, calm inlet where fishing boats come and go, and the town is built around it, with a long waterfront, a few pubs, a bakery or two and the quiet hum of a place that survives on fish, tourists and a bit of timber. The coast here has a reputation for game fishing, and in summer the harbour fills with boats chasing tuna and marlin further out.
