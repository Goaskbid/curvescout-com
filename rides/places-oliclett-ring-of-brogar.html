Why this ride

From Wick it runs 538 km to Wick; allow about 10 h 15 of riding. On the way it passes The Flow Country, Ring of Brogar and back through The Flow Country. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

The stops

  1. 1. Oliclett

    Start here.

    Caithness is the flat northeastern corner of mainland Scotland, a country of big skies, peat and flagstone, and Oliclett lies in it west of Wick, among scattered farms and low fields. The soil is thin and the wind is constant, and the trees that grow bend away from the sea. But beneath it, the county is thick with prehistory: chambered cairns, standing stones and broch ruins by the hundred. The Grey Cairns of Camster, near Lybster, are among the oldest, built over five thousand years ago, and you can crawl into one of them along a low passage.

  2. 2. The Flow Country

    100 km in. The ride passes about 8 km from it.

    Most people who first see the Flow Country think it is empty. It is not; it is simply flat, wet and enormous, and it takes a while to understand what you are looking at. Spread across Caithness and Sutherland in the far north of Scotland, this is the largest expanse of blanket bog in Europe, a landscape of peat built up over thousands of years, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2024. The surface is a patchwork of sphagnum mosses, cotton grass and heather, studded with countless small pools called dubh lochans that shine like broken mirrors when the sun comes out. Under your feet, the peat holds an extraordinary amount of carbon.

    Read the complete The Flow Country write-up.

  3. 3. Ring of Brogar

    268 km in.

    The Ring of Brogar is a perfect circle of standing stones on a flat spit of heather between the Loch of Stenness and the Loch of Harray, about a hundred and four metres across, ringed by a rock-cut ditch. Sixty stones are thought to have stood here originally, and twenty-seven remain, some of them toppled or broken, with the tallest about four and a half metres high. It was built perhaps four and a half thousand years ago, probably a little later than Stenness, and nobody knows precisely what it was for. The suggestions range from astronomy to gathering, and standing in the middle you can believe any of them.
