Why this ride

A 209 km round trip from Cuenca - roughly 5 h in the saddle. The stops in between: Segment Llaviuco - Llaviuco, Segment San Antonio - San Antonio and back through Segment Llaviuco - Llaviuco. It tops out around 3,800 m. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Avenue of the Volcanoes

From Historic Centre of Santa Ana de los Ríos d to Segment Llaviuco - Llaviuco is 19 km on Vía Cuenca - Molleturo - Puerto Inca. You climb most of the way: 750 m, up to 3,293 m. Cold up there even in July - pack a warm layer and check it is open.

The stops

  1. 1. Historic Centre of Santa Ana de los Ríos d

    Start here.

    High in the southern Ecuadorian Andes, at about 2,500 metres, Cuenca was founded by the Spanish in 1557 on a grid plan, on the site of an Inca city called Tomebamba, which was itself built over an earlier Cañari settlement. The colonial centre, with its cobbled streets, whitewashed houses with wooden balconies, churches and plazas, was inscribed by UNESCO in 1999, and the blue-tiled domes of the new cathedral are the city's landmark. The Tomebamba river runs through the city, lined with parks and old houses perched on the bank.

    Read the complete Historic Centre of Santa Ana de los Ríos d write-up.

  2. 2. Segment Llaviuco - Llaviuco

    19 km in.

    The Incas built a road across the Andes that stretched for thousands of kilometres, linking Quito, Cusco and far beyond, and parts of it still survive in Ecuador. Llaviuco is a short surviving segment, listed by UNESCO as part of the Qhapaq Ñan, the great Andean road system. It lies inside Cajas National Park, in a landscape of high moorland, small lakes and cloud forest, above Cuenca. The stones of the old road are set in the ground by hand, and walking them gives a direct sense of how this empire moved people, messengers and llamas through the mountains.

    Read the complete Segment Llaviuco - Llaviuco write-up.

    99 km in.

    What I can say is that the point lies on the steep western slope of the Andes, in the country between the highlands near Cuenca and the coastal plain, with Naranjal the nearest real town, thirty-odd kilometres off on the lowlands.
