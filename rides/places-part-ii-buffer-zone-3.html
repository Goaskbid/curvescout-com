Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Part II

    Pico is an island that is mostly mountain. The great cone of Mount Pico rises to about 2,350 metres from the Atlantic, the highest point in Portugal, and its slopes sweep down in black lava to the sea. The summit, often wrapped in cloud, appears from nearly everywhere on the island and from the neighbouring island of Faial, and climbing it is a long, steep day on loose volcanic rock, with an extraordinary view if the weather behaves.

  2. 2. Landscape of the Pico Island Vineyard Cult

    The vines on Pico grow in a basalt maze. For five hundred years, the islanders have built tiny walled enclosures called currais out of black volcanic stone, each one just big enough for a few vines, to protect the plants from salt wind and sea spray, and the result is a landscape of thousands of small rectangles covering the rough coastal lava plain, mile after mile, with parallel walls running down toward the sea. UNESCO listed this vineyard culture in 2004, and the sight of it from the coastal road is among the strangest and most beautiful in Europe. The grapes ripen in the heat that the black stone holds, and the wines, especially the white Verdelho, were once shipped to the courts of the Russian tsars.

  3. 3. Buffer zone 3

    Pico Island rises out of the Atlantic in the central Azores as a single enormous volcano, and its summit, at about 2,351 metres, is the highest point in Portugal. It is a country of black lava, small stone-walled fields and a sea that is always close, with the great cone usually wrapped in cloud above you.
