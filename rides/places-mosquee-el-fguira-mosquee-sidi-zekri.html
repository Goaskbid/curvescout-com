Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mosquée El Fguira

    El Fguira stands in the south of Djerba, a long way from the bustle of Houmt Souk, in a part of the island where the farms are bigger and the roads quiet. The label puts it among the more remote of the mosques that make up the Djerba listing, and the journey is a pleasure in itself. The mosque is a small, whitewashed building with strong buttresses and a rounded roof, standing among olive trees and palms, and its shape is as simple as a child's drawing of a house. Like the others, it is still used for prayer, so it should be seen from outside and with respect.

  2. 2. Synagogue La Ghriba

    La Ghriba stands on the island of Djerba, just outside the village of Erriadh, and it is among the oldest Jewish places of worship in North Africa. The name means the Stranger, or the Marvellous, and the stories about its beginning stretch back to a priest who fled Jerusalem after the destruction of the First Temple, bringing a door or a stone. Whatever the truth of that, the building you see today is much later, a nineteenth-century structure with a whitewashed exterior and an interior of blue, turquoise and gold tiles, carved wood and low lamps that give the prayer hall an underwater glow.

  3. 3. Mosquée Sidi Zekri

    Sidi Zekri is one of the many small religious buildings of Djerba, an island where it sometimes seems that every field has a whitewashed dome in it. The name suggests a local holy man, the sort of marabout who is buried under a domed tomb and whose tomb draws believers seeking help with illness or family troubles. Many such places are small and quiet, and are looked after by neighbours rather than by any big institution.
