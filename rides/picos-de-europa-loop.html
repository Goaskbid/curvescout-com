Why this ride

106 km round from Cangas de Onís - plan on about 1 h 43 of riding. On the way it passes Covadonga, Lakes of Covadonga, Fuente De and Potes. The highest point is about 1,350 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cangas de Onis

    This is where Spain's story of reconquest is told, at least the way it is told in Asturias. A few kilometres up the road at Covadonga, the local lord Pelayo is said to have beaten a Muslim force around 722, an event that became the founding legend of the Kingdom of Asturias, and Cangas de Onís was that kingdom's first capital. So a small town of about six thousand people carries rather a lot of history, and it shows in the Roman Bridge, which is actually medieval, with its high single arch over the Sella and a cross hanging beneath it.

  2. 2. Covadonga

    Ask a Spaniard where their country's story starts again after the Moorish conquest and many will say here, in a green fold of the Picos de Europa, a few kilometres up the valley from Cangas de Onís. The tradition says a Visigothic noble named Pelayo and a small band of Asturians beat a Muslim force in the hills around 722, and the kingdom of Asturias grew from it. Whether the battle was as grand as the legend or a skirmish blown up later, historians still argue, but the place carries the weight regardless. Pelayo is buried in the cave.

  3. 3. Lakes of Covadonga

    The climb from the basilica to the lakes is a short road with a long reputation. Cyclists know it from the Vuelta, where it has been a summit finish with a brutal gradient, and the ramps are steep enough that you will use second gear more than you expect. Over about a dozen kilometres you rise from valley meadows to a high, rolling limestone shelf, and the road threads between steep grassy slopes with cow bells clanking somewhere in the mist. Expect hairpins, a narrow carriageway, and cattle that regard the tarmac as theirs.

  4. 4. Fuente De

    Fuente Dé is the end of the road, in the best sense. The CA-185 climbs up a narrow glacial valley from Potes, the little capital of the Liébana region, and finishes in a vast amphitheatre of limestone, a cirque of grey cliffs that rise nearly vertically above the meadow and block off the sky. There is nowhere further to ride, so you park, tip your head back, and look up.

  5. 5. Potes

    Potes is the capital of Liébana, a hidden-away valley in Cantabria that is walled in by the Picos de Europa on three sides and so mild and sheltered that it grows fruit and grapes at the foot of mountains that still carry snow in June. The town sits where the rivers Deva and Quiviesca meet, under a square medieval tower, the Torre del Infantado, with stone bridges, arcaded houses and narrow streets climbing away from the water. It has only a little over a thousand residents, which is why everybody on the street seems to know everybody else.

  6. 6. Arenas de Cabrales

    Arenas de Cabrales is the capital, if that is the word, of one of Europe's most pungent cheeses. Cabrales is a blue made from cow's milk, sometimes blended with sheep and goat, and it matures in natural limestone caves in the hills above the village where cool, damp air does the work that a refrigerator would elsewhere. It looks like something that has been left in a cellar, and it tastes like a punch to the tongue followed by a hug. Locals eat it with cider, or spread on bread, or melt it over beef. A late-summer cheese fair in the village celebrates it. You can buy a wedge here to take home, though think about where it will ride.
