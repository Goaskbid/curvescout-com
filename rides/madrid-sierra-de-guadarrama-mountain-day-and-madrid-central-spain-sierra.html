Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Navacerrada Pass

    At 1,858 metres, Puerto de Navacerrada is the pass Madrid runs to when it wants to cool off. It is the crossing between the Madrid and Segovia sides of the Sierra de Guadarrama, and the road, the M-601 on the south side, climbs from Cercedilla and Navacerrada village in a series of tight bends through pine forest before bursting out onto the bare, rocky ridge. At the top there is a small ski area, a few ventas and cafés, a car park that is too small on every sunny weekend, and a view of the high peaks and, in winter, a rather charming scrum of skiers and bikers sharing the same tarmac.

  2. 2. Segovia old town

    Segovia announces itself with a Roman aqueduct, and nothing else in Spain quite prepares you for it. Built around the first or second century, the granite blocks are stacked in two tiers of arches without any mortar, and the whole thing strides through the middle of the town like something from a film set. It carried water from the mountains for nearly two thousand years, and in the oldest parts of the city you can still see how it dictated the shape of the streets around it.

  3. 3. La Granja palace

    Philip V was a homesick king. Born in France and raised at Versailles, he came to the Spanish throne in 1700 and spent much of his reign longing for the gardens of his childhood, so in the early eighteenth century he built himself a retreat at La Granja de San Ildefonso, on the northern slope of the Sierra de Guadarrama, and filled the grounds with French-style avenues, clipped hedges and an extravagant series of fountains and statues. The result is a baroque palace that looks out on a mountain wall rather than a French plain.

  4. 4. Rascafría food

    Rascafría is the main village of the upper Lozoya valley, a green, high, glacier-carved trough on the northern side of the Sierra de Guadarrama where the Lozoya river runs down from the peaks. It is a small place, around sixteen hundred people, but it feels bigger than it is because of what surrounds it: the great ridge of Peñalara, the highest summit in the range at a little over two thousand four hundred metres, lies just to the west, and the old pine forests close in on all sides.

  11. 11. Food stop near Navalcarnero

    Navalcarnero has one of the finest arcaded squares in the Madrid region, the Plaza de Segovia, with wooden balconies, stone columns and a lively atmosphere on market days. The town has royal connections too: in the seventeenth century, King Philip IV's wedding to Mariana of Austria was celebrated here, and the house where the event took place is still pointed out. Today it is a busy market town on the southwestern edge of the Madrid metropolitan area.

    These are tidy, prosperous commuter towns with business parks, wide roads and a lot of roundabouts. Tres Cantos was a planned new town of the 1970s, a bit of an experiment in suburban living, and it has more green space than you might expect, thanks to the surrounding Monte de Viñuelas and woodland.
