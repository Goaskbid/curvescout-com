  2. 2. The Monastery of Saint Thaddeus

    On a bare hillside near the border between Iran and Turkey, close to the foot of Mount Ararat, stands a black-and-white church that has survived earthquakes, wars and neglect for most of a thousand years. The Monastery of Saint Thaddeus, known in Iran as Qara Kelisa, the Black Church, is one of the oldest and most revered Armenian churches in the world. It is built of alternating bands of black and white stone, in a way that gives it a striking, almost chequered appearance, and it stands in a high, windy valley of the north-west.

  3. 3. The main chapel

    Black and white stone, in alternating courses, climbs the walls of a church that stands alone on a hillside in the far north-west of Iran. The Armenians call it the monastery of Saint Thaddeus, and the Persians know it as Qara Kelisa, the black church, a name that comes from the dark stone of its lower walls. Tradition says that a chapel was founded here in the first century by the apostle Thaddeus, who was martyred nearby, and that this makes it one of the oldest places of Christian worship in the world. The buildings that stand today are mostly of the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, rebuilt after an earthquake, and they are among the finest examples of Armenian architecture outside Armenia.

  4. 4. The village

    The village near the black church is a world of mud, stone and apricot trees. Low houses with flat roofs are built into the slope, with piles of fuel cakes drying against the walls, smoke curling from chimneys in the evening, and the sound of sheep and dogs echoing in the valley. People here live much as they have for generations, growing grain and fruit, herding animals and trading with the markets in the towns below. This corner of West Azerbaijan is a mix of Azeri and Kurdish villages, with Armenian history layered underneath.
