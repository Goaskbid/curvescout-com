Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Palma

    Palma is a city built around a cathedral that looks like a stone cliff. La Seu, as everyone calls it, rises directly above the seafront, a Gothic mass of pale limestone begun in the early fourteenth century by King James II of Mallorca, with a huge rose window and buttresses that seem to leap toward the sea. It took centuries to build, and in the twentieth century the architect Antoni Gaudí was called in to rework the interior, hanging a great canopy above the altar. The reflection of the building in the long pond in front, at dawn, is one of the great sights of the Mediterranean.

  2. 2. Cultural Landscape of the Serra de Tramunt

    The Serra de Tramuntana is a wall of limestone running along the north-west of Mallorca, and it is the island's backbone, its rain catcher and its best motorcycling country. UNESCO listed it as a cultural landscape in 2011, and the label fits, because every slope has been shaped by people. Terraces of dry-stone walls climb the hills in pale steps, planted with olives and almonds, and old mule paths, water channels and stone huts show how a mountain was farmed for centuries by hand. Puig Major, at about 1,445 metres, is the highest summit, with a military radar station on top, and the whole range holds a surprising amount of rain and mist for a Mediterranean island.
