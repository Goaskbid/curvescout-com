Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Dinosaur Colorado

    The little town of Dinosaur, Colorado, has streets named for its famous residents: Brontosaurus Boulevard, Stegosaurus Freeway, Triceratops Terrace. It lies in the arid country of the far north-west corner of the state, just east of the Utah line, in the Uinta Basin, a land of red rock, juniper and dust. The surrounding hills are banded in pink, grey and rust, and the sky is huge. Rangely, about twenty-four kilometres east, is the nearest sizeable settlement and the practical place for fuel and supplies.

  2. 2. Vernal

    The town has about eleven thousand people, with a long main street, a few motels and diners, and a museum of natural history with a collection of dinosaur skeletons, including a life-size model garden with creatures that appear to lurk beside the road. The surrounding country is red and tan rock, sagebrush, and dry river valleys, with the high, snowy Uinta Mountains standing to the north.

  3. 3. Moab

    Moab sits in a green strip of valley beside the Colorado River, surrounded by red sandstone cliffs, and it is the main base for exploring the canyon country of southeastern Utah. At about 1,200 metres, it is a small town of five thousand people, though in the busy seasons it feels much larger. Arches National Park lies just five kilometres north, Canyonlands is a short ride southwest, and the roads in and out are as scenic as any in the country, with long views of red rock, blue sky and the La Sal Mountains, which stay capped with snow into early summer.
