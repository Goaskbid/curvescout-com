Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Naurzum State Nature Reserve - NSNR Main A

    Kazakhstan is mostly steppe, and this is the northern part of it, an enormous open country of feather grass, lakes and wind. The Naurzum reserve, in the Kostanay region, protects a patch of that landscape, together with an oddity that surprises everyone, an island of ancient pine forest sitting in the steppe. The pines grow on sandy ground left by an old river, and they form a relict stand that survived when the climate changed. Together with the nearby Korgalzhyn reserve it was recognised by UNESCO as the Saryarka, the Steppe and Lakes of Northern Kazakhstan, and the lakes are a major stopover for migratory birds on the flyway from Africa and India to Siberia.

  2. 2. Naurzum State Nature Reserve - NSNR Eco-Co

    In the steppes of northern Kazakhstan, in the Kostanay region, islands of pine forest rise out of the open grassland like a mirage, and between them lie shallow lakes full of birds. The Naurzum State Nature Reserve is one of the two parts of Saryarka – Steppe and Lakes of Northern Kazakhstan, listed by UNESCO in 2008 together with the Korgalzhyn reserve far to the east.

  3. 3. Naurzum State Nature Reserve - NSNR Sypsyn

    The steppe at Naurzum is the kind of place where the horizon is a straight line in every direction. This is northern Kazakhstan, in Kostanay region, a flat country of feather grass, salt lakes and shimmering heat, and in the middle of it a protected area that holds one of the last stretches of unploughed steppe, together with a pine forest that has no right to be there. The reserve was listed by UNESCO as part of Saryarka, Steppe and Lakes of Northern Kazakhstan, in 2008.

  4. 4. Naurzum State Nature Reserve - NSNR Tersek

    In the steppe of northern Kazakhstan, the Naurzum reserve covers a patch of country that, from a distance, looks like nothing and, from close up, turns out to be a world of its own. It lies in the Kostanay region, in the dry heart of the Turgay plains, and is made of grassland, salt lakes, birch groves and, remarkably, a strip of old pine forest called Naurzum Karagai that survives here, far south of the main taiga. Tersek is one of the separate sectors of the reserve. Together with Korgalzhyn it forms a UNESCO site, the steppe and lakes of northern Kazakhstan, inscribed in 2008.
