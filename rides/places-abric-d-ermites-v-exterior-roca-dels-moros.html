Why this ride

163 km from Ulldecona to Ulldecona, about 2 h 49 of riding. On the way it passes Cova dels Rossegadors o del Polvorin, Prop de la Cova Pintada, Roca dels moros and back through Prop de la Cova Pintada. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abric d'Ermites V exterior

    The Montsià is a small, rocky massif that rises abruptly above the Ebro delta, the last real mountain before the river dissolves into rice paddies and lagoons, and the hills around Ulldecona are its northern neighbours. From their ridges you can look across the flat delta to the sea, a view that changes from silver in the morning to gold in the evening. Abric d'Ermites V exterior lies about four and a half kilometres from Ulldecona, on the slopes of the Serra de Godall, and carries painting in the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain. The ride links it with Valcomuna, a site name that sounds like a village meeting place.

  2. 2. Cova dels Rossegadors o del Polvorin

    The Tinença de Benifassà is one of the emptiest corners of the Valencian Community, a group of tiny stone villages scattered across rugged limestone mountains in the far north of Castellón, right where Valencia, Catalonia and Aragón meet. The Cova dels Rossegadors, also called the Cova del Polvorí, lies on the edge of this country near Rossell, with Mas de Barberans and Canet lo Roig the other nearby villages.

  3. 3. Prop de la Cova Pintada

    The Ports massif rises abruptly at the meeting point of Catalonia, Aragón and Valencia, a tangle of limestone peaks, deep gorges and pine forest that feels surprisingly wild given how close it is to the Ebro delta and the coast. Prop de la Cova Pintada, meaning near the painted cave, sits on its southern side, between Mas de Barberans, Valderrobres and the old city of Tortosa.

  4. 4. Roca dels moros

    In 1903, a young man from Calaceite named Juan Cabré, who would go on to become one of Spain's pioneering archaeologists, found painted figures of deer and other animals in a rock shelter in the Calapatá ravine, between Calaceite and Cretas. The Roca dels Moros became one of the first sites of Levantine rock art identified in Spain, and it helped open up a whole new field of study. Today it is part of the UNESCO listing of rock art of the Mediterranean Basin, and a visit here is a small pilgrimage to the beginnings of that story.
