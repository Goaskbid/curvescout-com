Why this ride

From la Granja d'Escarp it runs 171 km to la Granja d'Escarp; allow about 2 h 57 of riding. The stops in between: Val de Caballé I, Abrigo del Plano del Pulido, Abric del mas de Barberà and back through Abrigo del Plano del Pulido. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abric del Barranc de Canà o de la mina fed

    Before the reservoir and the anglers' boats, Mequinenza was a coal town. The seams of lignite that run under the hills around the confluence of the Segre and the Ebro were dug for generations, and the coal was shipped down the river to feed the furnaces and power stations of a growing Spain. When the mines faded and the old town was submerged, a good part of the population left, and the people who remained are proud of a heritage that is half underground and half underwater. The Barranc de Canà shelter lies five kilometres out, in dry hills between the town and the road to Fraga.

  2. 2. Val de Caballé I

    Here the Ebro stops being a river and becomes a lake. Behind the great dams at Mequinenza and Riba-roja, the water backs up for kilometres into the dry hills of eastern Aragon, and locals call the reservoir the Mar de Aragón, the sea of Aragon. Val de Caballé is a gully in the hills south of the water, between Mequinenza, Nonaspe and Fabara, part of a scatter of prehistoric rock shelters in this corner where Aragon, Catalonia and the Matarranya meet.

  3. 3. Abrigo del Plano del Pulido

    South of Caspe, the lower Aragón country is a dry, sculpted landscape of ochre hills, flat-topped mesas and gullies cut into soft sandstone, with almond and olive groves clinging to the valley floors. In these sandstone outcrops, prehistoric people found shelter and left paintings, and the Abrigo del Plano del Pulido is one of the rock shelters of the area included in the World Heritage site of the rock art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula. The painted figures, in the reddish tones of Levantine art, are small and faded, and as at most of these sites, access is controlled and visits may need to be arranged in advance.

  4. 4. Abric del mas de Barberà

    Alcañiz is the capital of the Bajo Aragón, a region of olive groves, cereal fields and dry hills crossed by the Guadalope river in the south-east of Aragón. The town is crowned by a castle built for the Order of Calatrava, now a parador, and its main square, with a Gothic loggia and the town hall, is one of the finest in the region. The shelter of the Mas de Barberà lies in the hills just outside town, toward Castelserás and Calanda.
