Why this ride

77 km from Erriadh to Erriadh, about 1 h 20 of riding. On the way it passes Zone Côtière inhabitée, Mosquée Imghar and back through Zone Côtière inhabitée. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Zone Hara Sghira

    Hara Sghira means the little quarter, and for centuries it was the name of the Jewish village on Djerba's north coast, a few minutes from Houmt El Souk across flat olive and palm country. Today the modern town around it is called Erriadh, and it has two reputations. One is religious: the El Ghriba synagogue, which locals will tell you is the oldest in Africa, draws pilgrims every spring for a festival of candles and song, and visitors go through security checks at the gate, which is a sign of the troubled recent history rather than anything to be nervous about. The other is artistic, for in 2014 well over a hundred artists from dozens of countries covered the whitewashed walls of the village with murals, and the project, called Djerbahood, turned a sleepy street grid into an open-air gallery.

  2. 2. Zone Côtière inhabitée

    Most of Djerba is cultivated, and then the cultivation stops. On the island's northern shore, where the olive groves and palm gardens run out, a strip of shallow sea, salt flat, sand and scrub stretches along the coast with hardly a building on it, and this is the uninhabited coastal zone that the island's heritage listing includes in its protected landscape. In 2023, UNESCO recognised Djerba for the way its people shaped a territory without rivers or springs, and the empty shore is part of that story, because it shows what the settlers started with and what they chose to leave alone.

  3. 3. Mosquée Imghar

    Djerba's mosques look like small fortresses, which is more or less what they once were. Imghar, in the interior of the island, is one of the group of Ibadi mosques whose whitewashed walls, thick buttresses and low domes give them the air of a farmhouse that has been trained for war. Ibadism is a branch of Islam that predates the Sunni-Shia split, and it survives today mainly in Oman, parts of North Africa and here on Djerba, where it has shaped the island's culture for over a thousand years.
