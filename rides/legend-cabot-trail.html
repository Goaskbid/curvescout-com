Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cabot Trail point 1

    Baddeck is a small village on the shore of the Bras d'Or Lake, the big inland saltwater sea that nearly splits Cape Breton Island in two, and it is a gentle place to begin a loop around the Cabot Trail. The Bras d'Or is a strange body of water, tidal but sheltered, fringed with low green hills and dotted with sailboats, and in the early morning the surface can be perfectly still. Baddeck has a main street of low wooden buildings, a small harbour, a handful of cafes and a bakery, and the unhurried air of a place accustomed to summer visitors.

  2. 2. Cabot Trail point 2

    Margaree Harbour is the point where the Cabot Trail meets the Gulf of St. Lawrence for the first time, and the sudden change in scale is striking. After the sheltered green valleys inland, you come out onto a coast of open water, long beaches and low cliffs, with a salty breeze and gulls overhead. The little village at the mouth of the Margaree River is quiet, with a few houses, a small harbour and a fishing wharf, and the feeling of the edge of the world.

  3. 3. Cabot Trail point 3

    Pleasant Bay sits in a steep valley where the highlands tumble to the sea, and it has a name that is not an exaggeration. The village is small, with a few houses, a fishing harbour and a view across the Gulf of St. Lawrence, and it is surrounded by high, forested slopes. It is a Scottish and Acadian community, and its fortunes have long been tied to fishing and, more recently, whale-watching. Pilot whales come close to shore in summer, and boat trips set out from the wharf.

  4. 4. Cabot Trail point 4

    Cape North is where the island's highlands finally give up. After the long, steep climbs of the national park, the road drops into a broad valley that opens toward Aspy Bay, and the landscape changes from mountain to rolling farmland and low hills. The small community here has a few shops, a school and a scattering of farms, and it sits at a crossroads: one road leads to the north tip of the island, another to the east coast and the long ride south.

  5. 5. Cabot Trail point 5

    Ingonish is not one village but a string of them, spread around two bays on the Atlantic coast, and it is the gateway to the most celebrated section of the east side of the trail. Behind it rises Cape Smokey, a huge headland that the road climbs in a series of long, curving bends, with the sea far below. The ascent is steep, the views are huge, and the descent into Ingonish is among the most memorable rides on the island.

  6. 6. Cabot Trail point 6

    Coming back into Baddeck after the loop feels like stepping off a ship. The long ride around the highlands and coast is behind you, your arms are tired and your jacket smells of salt and woodsmoke, and the village has something almost ceremonial about it. The Bras d'Or Lake lies calm ahead, and the whole trail seems to resolve into a few quiet streets and a bench by the water.
