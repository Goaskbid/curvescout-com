Why this ride

A 95 km loop that starts and ends near Andermatt, about 1 h 35 of riding. The stops in between: Furka Pass, Grimsel Pass, Innertkirchen and Susten Pass. The highest point is about 2,450 m. Furka Pass, Grimsel Pass and Susten Pass are subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off.

Road summary

A alpine pass loop based around Andermatt, Switzerland, via Furka Pass. Key character: glaciers; hairpins; high passes. Surface: paved.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Andermatt

    Andermatt sits at 1,444 metres in the Urseren valley, a high, wide, treeless hollow in the heart of the Swiss Alps, and it has been a crossroads for centuries. Three great passes meet here: the Gotthard, which runs south to Italy and Ticino at 2,106 metres, the Furka, which climbs west over 2,429 metres toward the Valais, and the Oberalp, which leads east at 2,044 metres into Graubünden. For a motorcyclist it is a kind of Mecca, since the three roads are among the most famous in the Alps, and the town fills with bikes on any fine summer weekend.

  2. 2. Furka Pass

    Furka is one of the Alps' great stage roads: 2,429 metres of switchbacks linking Uri and Valais, with the Rhône Glacier at the western foot. From Andermatt the road climbs through the high Urseren valley, past Realp, up a series of hairpins to a pass where the air is thin and cold, and the views unfold across rock, snow and sky. The mountains here are bare and grand, and the sense of altitude is real.

  3. 3. Grimsel Pass

    The Grimsel is a road made of granite. At 2,164 metres, the pass sits at the junction of the Bernese Alps and the Valais, in a bare, polished landscape of rounded boulders, tiny lakes and big reservoirs, where the ice of the last glaciation scraped the rock smooth. There is almost nothing growing at the top, and the sky seems close enough to touch.

  4. 4. Innertkirchen

    Innertkirchen sits at 625 metres where the Haslital opens out, and in practice it is a junction. Two famous passes start here, the Grimsel climbing southeast toward the Rhône glacier and the Susten rising east toward the Reuss valley, and the roads divide at the edge of the village. The Aare river runs through, fresh from the glaciers, and just downstream the Aare Gorge cuts a narrow slot of polished limestone that can be walked on timber galleries. The valley is also the heart of the Oberhasli hydropower scheme, with turbines and tunnels bored deep inside the mountains, which explains the large grey buildings you pass.

  5. 5. Susten Pass

    Susten is a pass built for driving, and that is rare. Where most Alpine crossings follow old mule tracks or trade routes, this one was planned in the 1930s and finished in the 1940s as a modern road, with smooth gradients, wide curves and sturdy retaining walls, and the result is a ride that flows in a way few others do. It connects Wassen in Uri with Innertkirchen in the Bernese Oberland, crossing the ridge at 2,224 metres through a short tunnel.

  6. 6. Wassen

    Wassen is a village you have probably seen without knowing it, because it is the star of one of the most photographed railway curiosities in Europe. The Gotthard line climbs the narrow Reuss valley in a series of spiral tunnels, and the little church of Wassen, perched on a mound, appears three times from the train window at three different heights and angles. Riders get the same trick on the road, where the old Gotthard route zigzags up the valley and the church tower comes round again and again.
