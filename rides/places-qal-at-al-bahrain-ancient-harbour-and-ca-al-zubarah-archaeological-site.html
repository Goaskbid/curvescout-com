Why this ride

A 150 km round trip from Jidd Ḩafş - roughly 2 h 43 in the saddle. On the way it passes Al Zubarah Archaeological Site and Qal’at al-Bahrain - Ancient Harbour and Ca. It crosses from Bahrain into Qatar - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Qal’at al-Bahrain - Ancient Harbour and Ca

    Qal'at al-Bahrain is a hill, and the hill is made of cities. For about four thousand years, people have built on this spot on the north coast of Bahrain, and every generation has laid its houses and walls on top of the one before, until the mound, called a tell, rose over the surrounding palm gardens. Archaeologists have peeled back the layers to find the capital of the Dilmun civilisation, a trading power of the Bronze Age that linked Mesopotamia with the Indus valley, with Bahrain as the entrepot where copper, pearls, timber and grain changed hands. UNESCO listed the site in 2005.

  2. 2. Al Zubarah Archaeological Site

    Out on the north-west coast of Qatar, where the desert slides into a shallow turquoise sea, stand the low ruins of Al Zubarah, once one of the busiest pearling and trading towns in the Gulf. In the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the town thrived on pearls dived from the oyster beds offshore, traded on to India, Persia and beyond. Then it declined, was abandoned in the early twentieth century, and the sand covered it. Archaeologists have since uncovered streets, houses, a souk, mosques and a long town wall running in an arc around the old harbour. UNESCO inscribed the site in 2013.
