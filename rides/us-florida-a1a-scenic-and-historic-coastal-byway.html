Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ponte Vedra Beach

    The beach at Ponte Vedra is a wide band of pale sand that drops gently into the Atlantic, backed by dunes with sea oats and low scrub, and the coast road behind it, State Road A1A, runs for miles in a sequence of palm trees, big houses and glimpses of the water. It is an affluent place now, with golf courses, tennis clubs and gated communities, and home to the headquarters of the PGA Tour and the TPC Sawgrass course, where the Players Championship is played each year and the par-three seventeenth hole sits on an island green that has humbled the best golfers in the world.

  2. 2. Flagler Beach

    Flagler Beach is what much of the Florida coast used to be like. The little town stretches along a narrow barrier island between the Atlantic and the Intracoastal Waterway, with a long, broad beach of hard-packed sand, low buildings, a pier and a laid-back, unhurried feel that the high-rise resorts to the south left behind long ago. The population is about five thousand, and it is a place where people still walk to the beach, fish from the pier and eat at small seafood restaurants. State Road A1A, the coastal highway, runs right along the shore, with the sea on one side and the town on the other, and it is one of the most relaxed roads in the state.

  3. 3. Ormond Beach

    Ormond Beach is a small city with a big place in the history of going fast. Early in the twentieth century the hard-packed sand here, together with its neighbour Daytona to the south, was where the world's first speed records on wheels were chased, long before anyone thought of building a speedway. Henry Flagler's railway brought the winter crowds, and his grand hotel in town drew millionaires; John D. Rockefeller wintered a short walk from the shore in a house called The Casements. A motorcyclist rolling in today is following a very old tradition.
