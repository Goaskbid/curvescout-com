Why this ride

A one-way ride of 436 km, Kentfield to Pacifica - roughly 7 h 36 in the saddle. On the way it passes Stinson Beach, Point Reyes Station, Tomales Bay and Mount Tamalpais.

The roads

Pacific Coast Highway

26 km from Stinson Beach to Point Reyes Station. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 5 and 128 m. Best in the early light, before the traffic.

The stops

  1. 1. Mount Tamalpais

    Start here.

    Mount Tamalpais is the hill every San Francisco rider eventually points a front wheel at. It stands just across the Golden Gate in Marin County, with the east peak at roughly 780 metres, which sounds modest until you remember that it rises almost straight out of the bay, so the whole thing reads as a proper mountain. The Miwok people saw a sleeping woman in its outline, and from certain angles in the city she still lies there, flat on her back against the sky. Kentfield, Mill Valley and Larkspur lie at its foot, and any of them will sell you petrol and coffee before you climb.

    Read the complete Mount Tamalpais write-up.

  2. 2. Stinson Beach

    11 km in.

    Stinson Beach is where the Pacific finally gets its way with Marin. After the long curves of Highway 1 down from the ridge, the road drops to sea level and a pale crescent of sand runs out for about three miles, with a spit of dunes at one end and the steep green slope of the mountain at the other. The village behind it is tiny, a scatter of beach houses, a few cafes, a grocery and a bookshop, and it feels both sleepy and expensive, as seaside places near big cities often do.

    Read the complete Stinson Beach write-up.

  3. 3. Point Reyes Station

    37 km in.

    Point Reyes Station is a single street of low wooden buildings in a green valley north of San Francisco, and it has more good food per metre than most cities. It sits on the Pacific side of Marin County, at the southern end of Tomales Bay, beside Highway 1, with dairy farms and cattle ranches stretching away toward the hills. The town has a bakery that draws queues on weekend mornings, a famous creamery that makes cheese from local milk, an old-fashioned feed store and a bookshop, and the pace is slow and friendly.

    Read the complete Point Reyes Station write-up.

  4. 4. Tomales Bay

    51 km in.

    Tomales Bay is a long, thin finger of water that points north-west from Point Reyes, and it lies exactly on the San Andreas Fault. The Pacific plate on the western side is moving north, slowly, against the North American plate on the east, and the straight, narrow shape of the bay is the scar left by the rift. In 1906, the fault slipped here with such violence that fences and roads were displaced by several metres, and you can still see where in places near Point Reyes Station.

    Read the complete Tomales Bay write-up.

  6. 6. Fuel stop in Pacifica

    160 km in. Pacifica · San Ramon and Lucas Valley-Marinwood Loop is the fuel stop for this leg.

    For decades the most nervous stretch of Highway 1 south of San Francisco was Devil's Slide, a ledge carved into crumbling cliffs above the Pacific between Pacifica and Montara, which slid into the ocean so often that the road was closed for months at a time. In 2013 twin tunnels finally bypassed it, and the old road became a walking and cycling trail where you can stand on the cliff edge and see what the engineers were up against. Pacifica sits at the northern end of that story, a string of beach neighbourhoods tucked into coves and valleys between the coastal hills and the surf.

    Read the complete Near Pacifica write-up.

  7. 7. Montara

    169 km in.

    For years, the stretch of Highway 1 just north of Montara was the road everyone worried about. Devil's Slide, a crumbling headland of rock above the Pacific, kept sliding into the sea and closing the highway, sometimes for months. Since 2013 the traffic has passed through a pair of tunnels bored through San Pedro Mountain, and the old road along the cliff has become a walking and cycling trail with some of the wildest views on the San Mateo coast. If you have twenty minutes, walk a piece of it.

    Read the complete Montara write-up.

  8. 8. Near Daly City

    192 km in.

    South of San Francisco's city limits, the land runs out abruptly. Daly City's houses, the ones with the colourful matching boxes that inspired the song Little Boxes, cover the hills right up to the edge of high, crumbling sea cliffs, and below them the Pacific pounds a long empty beach.

  11. 11. Near San Ramon, on the way back

    355 km in.

    Mount Diablo dominates everything here. The mountain rises alone to 1,173 metres above the eastern side of San Francisco Bay, its brown summer slopes dotted with dark oaks, and on a clear winter day, after rain, the view from the top is said to be one of the widest in the world, reaching the Sierra Nevada to the east and the Golden Gate to the west. San Ramon, Danville and the gated enclave of Blackhawk spread along its western foot, a valley of tidy suburbs, office parks and golf courses.

    Read the complete Near San Ramon, on the way back write-up.

  12. 12. Food stop near Fairview

    376 km in.

    The hills behind Hayward rise steeply from the flat bay plain, and Fairview sits right on top of them, a semi-rural community of ranches, winding lanes and houses with long views across San Francisco Bay. On a clear day, you can see the bridges, the city skyline and the Peninsula hills beyond. It is a peaceful spot above one of the busiest urban areas in the country.

    Read the complete Fuel stop near Fairview write-up.

  13. 13. Back to Sonoma Coast

    The end: 436 km.

    Pacifica is the last proper coastal town before San Francisco, a long strip of fog, surf and low houses squeezed between the Pacific and the steep ridge of the Coast Range. It is a place that feels slightly overlooked, which is part of its appeal. The beach at Linda Mar is one of the Bay Area's best-known surf spots, and on a gray winter morning you can watch wetsuited figures bobbing in the breaks while commuters stream by on Highway 1.

    Read the complete Sonoma Coast write-up.
