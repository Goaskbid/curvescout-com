Why this ride

From Saint-Étienne it runs 548 km to Vienne; allow about 10 h 23 of riding. It goes by way of Monts d’Ardèche roads, Vallon-Pont-d’Arc canyon stop and Ardèche swim window. Col de l’Oeillon ridge is the high point.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Saint-Étienne fuel gate

    Saint-Étienne wears its history in layers of coal, iron and cloth, and from the ring roads on the north side you can see how a city spread across a series of ridges, with the hills of the Pilat to the south and the mining basin of the Gier to the east. This is the second time the rides use it as a base, and it makes sense: with nearly a hundred and seventy-six thousand people, it has fuel on every approach, early bakeries and a tram that runs through the centre. The suburbs of Saint-Genest-Lerpt and Saint-Priest-en-Jarez, each with about six thousand people, climb the hills on the north side and make the quickest exit onto quieter roads.

  2. 2. Monts d’Ardèche roads

    The Monts d'Ardèche are not tall, but they are steep, and that is what makes them interesting. Around the valley of the Eyrieux, between Saint-Sauveur-de-Montagut, Le Cheylard and Privas, the land is a tangle of narrow ridges and deep, wooded gorges, falling from the high plateau toward the Rhône. Chestnut woods cover most of the slopes, and on the lower ground the old terraces, built by hand to hold soil on the hillsides, still step down in grey stone walls, many of them abandoned and overgrown.

  3. 3. Vallon-Pont-d’Arc canyon stop

    The first sight of the Pont d'Arc is the sort of thing that makes you stop without being asked. A single arch of pale limestone, about sixty metres wide, spans the Ardèche river like a doorway cut by water, with the river running through and canoes drifting in the shade. It is a natural bridge, shaped over a very long time as the river found a shortcut through a narrow meander, and the little town of Vallon-Pont-d'Arc sits close beside it, full of campsites, bakeries and shops that sell kayaks and ice cream in about equal numbers.

  4. 4. Ardèche swim window

    Sometimes the best reason to stop is simply that it is hot. The Ardèche river just downstream of Vallon-Pont-d'Arc is a broad, shallow, clear stretch of water over pale stones, with small gravel beaches along the banks, and in summer it is the natural place to cool off. The label says swim window, and that is accurate: for an hour in the middle of the day, the heat of the gorge makes the river feel like a gift, and riders in boots and jackets quickly start thinking about paddling.

  6. 6. Vienne fuel gate

    Vienne has been a city for two thousand years and it still shows it. On the east bank of the Rhône, about thirty kilometres south of Lyon, the Romans built a prosperous colony here, and the temple of Augustus and Livia still stands in the centre, as complete as when it was built, with its Corinthian columns facing a small square of cafés. Nearby a Roman theatre climbs the hillside, and every July it fills with music for a jazz festival that has become one of the best known in Europe.

  7. 7. Col de l’Oeillon ridge

    On the map the Pilat looks like a small bump south of Saint-Étienne, but on the ground it is a proper mountain massif, with a high ridge, deep forests and views down into the Rhône valley. The Col de l'Oeillon sits at around thirteen hundred metres, on the road that climbs from Pélussin up toward the main crest, and it is one of the best-known climbs for riders in the Lyon region. The highest point in the range, the Crêt de la Perdrix, is a short walk away at about fourteen hundred and thirty metres.

  8. 8. Pilat forest bends

    Down in the forest on the southern and western flanks of the Pilat, the road forgets about views and concentrates on bends. This stretch, near Saint-Julien-Molin-Molette and Bourg-Argental, runs under big stands of beech and fir, with the light falling in patches on the tarmac and the air noticeably cooler and damper than anywhere on the Rhône side. If the high ridge is the postcard, this is the quiet backstage, and some riders will tell you it is the better ride.

  9. 9. Condrieu food by river

    Condrieu is a small town on the right bank of the Rhône, and its fame rests on a grape. Viognier, a fragrant white variety with notes of apricot and blossom, was almost lost here in the twentieth century, when only a few hectares survived on the steep granite terraces above the town, and the wine from this tiny area has since become one of the most sought after in France. The vines cling to slopes so steep that they are held by dry-stone walls, and in autumn the terraces blaze yellow above the river.
