Why this ride

A 392 km round trip from Albocàsser - roughly 8 h 15 in the saddle. On the way it passes mas d'en Badenes, Coveta de la Cornisa, Las Rozas I and Abric del mas de Barberà. The stored terrain estimate reaches about 1,250 m; a measured road-elevation profile is not available. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cova de Rull

    Cova de Rull sits in the same dry country as its neighbours in the northern Valencian interior, in the hills around Albocàsser, and it carries a different feeling from the great ravine galleries. A cova is a cave, and this one is a modest opening in the rock with painted figures that belong to the Levantine tradition recognised by UNESCO. Archers, animals and signs are rendered in the red pigment that the style is known for, and what survives gives a hint of how many more once decorated the cliffs of this region.

  3. 3. Coveta de la Cornisa

    Morella is one of the most dramatic towns in Spain: a walled medieval city wrapped around a conical hill, with a castle on the summit and its walls running unbroken around the base. It stands in the mountains of the Ports in the north of Castellón province, at around a thousand metres, and its silhouette, seen from the roads that climb toward it, is unforgettable. The Coveta de la Cornisa, the little cave of the ledge, lies in the hills about four kilometres away.

  4. 4. Las Rozas I

    The upper valley of the Guadalope in Teruel is a place where limestone has been folded, cut and carved into an extraordinary array of shapes: cliffs, gorges, natural arches and pinnacles, with the river winding between them in places of startling green. Near Castellote, the painted shelter labelled Las Rozas I is one of the sites of Levantine rock art listed by UNESCO. Its figures are faint and fragile, but they speak of people who hunted deer and ibex in these same valleys thousands of years ago.

  5. 5. Abric del mas de Barberà

    Alcañiz is the capital of the Bajo Aragón, a region of olive groves, cereal fields and dry hills crossed by the Guadalope river in the south-east of Aragón. The town is crowned by a castle built for the Order of Calatrava, now a parador, and its main square, with a Gothic loggia and the town hall, is one of the finest in the region. The shelter of the Mas de Barberà lies in the hills just outside town, toward Castelserás and Calanda.
