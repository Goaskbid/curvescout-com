Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Holmavik

    Hólmavík is a small fishing village on the eastern shore of the Westfjords, and for many riders it is the gateway to the wildest part of Iceland. It sits on a little peninsula in Steingrímsfjörður, with a harbour full of boats, a church and a handful of colourful houses, and it has barely three hundred and fifty people. The landscape around it is stark and beautiful: low, rounded hills, deep fjords and bare, dark mountains beyond. In summer the light barely fades, and at midnight the sky glows orange over the water.

  2. 2. Isafjordur

    Isafjordur is the largest town in the Westfjords, with about two and a half thousand inhabitants, and it sits on a narrow gravel spit that juts into the fjord Skutulsfjordur, with steep mountains rising almost vertically on three sides. It was settled early as a fishing and trading port, and it grew rich on cod. The oldest part of town has some of the finest surviving timber buildings in the country, some of which date from the eighteenth century, and the Westfjords Heritage Museum is housed in one of them, with exhibits on fishing, boats and daily life.

  3. 3. Dynjandi

    Dynjandi is a waterfall that spreads like a bridal veil down the side of a mountain in the Westfjords, one of the most remote and least visited parts of Iceland. The water falls about a hundred metres in a series of steps, narrowing at the top to about thirty metres and widening at the bottom to about sixty, so that the whole thing looks like a great white fan, and at the foot of the main fall there are half a dozen smaller cascades, each with its own name, that you can climb past on a short path.
