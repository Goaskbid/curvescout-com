Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hanmer Springs

    Hanmer Springs is a small alpine village in the Hurunui district of north Canterbury, set in a broad basin among forested hills about an hour and a half's drive from the Pacific coast. The hot springs here were found by European settlers in the 1850s and have been in use ever since, and the thermal pools at the centre of the village, with their rock pools, sulphur baths and water slides, are what draw most visitors. Around them the village has cafes, restaurants, a handful of shops and a long list of places to stay, and about a thousand people live here year-round.

  2. 2. Lewis Pass

    Lewis Pass is a low, unhurried crossing of the Southern Alps, only 864 metres high, but it feels grander than that because the whole route runs through unbroken beech forest and wild river valleys. State Highway 7 links the Canterbury plains with the West Coast, and it does so by following the Waiau and Lewis rivers up through a corridor of mountains, past the Lewis Pass Scenic Reserve, and then down along the Maruia River toward Murchison. It was named for Henry Lewis, a surveyor who crossed it in 1860, though Māori had used it for centuries to reach the pounamu, the greenstone, of the West Coast.

  3. 3. Murchison

    Murchison is a small town with a big memory. On 17 June 1929, a powerful earthquake, around magnitude 7.8, struck the area, killing seventeen people and triggering enormous landslides that blocked valleys and rivers and cut roads and telegraph lines for weeks. The land was reshaped, and some of the new lakes and scars from that day are still visible. The Murchison Museum tells the story with photographs, and it is quite moving.
