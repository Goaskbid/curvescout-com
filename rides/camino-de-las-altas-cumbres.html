Why this ride

A one-way ride of 195 km, Córdoba to Mina Clavero - roughly 2 h 37 in the saddle. On the way it passes Copina and Pampa de Achala. The highest point is about 2,200 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Camino de las Altas Cumbres

Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it. The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2200 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cordoba

    Córdoba is Argentina's second city by size and first by claims to learning, a university town where the baroque churches of the Jesuits stand beside student cafés and traffic that never seems to stop. The Jesuits arrived in the sixteenth century and built what is now called the Jesuit Block, a group of church, college and residences that UNESCO listed in 2000. Their university, founded in 1613, is one of the oldest in South America, and in 1918 students here launched a reform movement that spread through Latin American universities. The city is also where Argentines drink fernet with cola, an acquired taste that locals will defend with some force.

  2. 2. Copina

    Copina is a small hill village on the eastern flank of the Sierras de Córdoba, on the road that climbs from the lowlands toward the high plateau, and it is a place where the temperature starts to drop and the road starts to bend. The village lies in granite country, with huge rounded boulders scattered across the hillside and the soil thin and stony. It is a quiet place of a few houses, a chapel and a handful of small guesthouses, and the main attraction is the air, which is clear and cool compared with the plains.

  3. 3. Pampa de Achala

    The Pampa de Achala is a high plateau at about 2,200 metres, a windswept expanse of grass and granite that sits at the top of the Sierras de Córdoba. After the climb from the lowlands, the landscape changes completely. The trees disappear, the sky grows enormous, and the land rolls away in smooth, bare hills dotted with great grey boulders. It feels more like Patagonia or the Scottish moors than central Argentina, and the air is thin, cold and very clear.

  4. 4. Mina Clavero

    Mina Clavero is a small river town on the western side of the Sierras de Córdoba, in the long, dry Traslasierra valley, and it is where the Camino de las Altas Cumbres comes to rest after crossing the mountains. You arrive from the east after a climb through grassland and rock that tops out above two thousand metres, then a descent in a long run of bends that opens suddenly onto a view of the whole valley, flat and golden, with the Sierra de Comechingones low and blue beyond it.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Cordoba to Copina

Cordoba → Copina is 66 km of getting across. Ride it tidily and keep the enthusiasm for what comes after.

Copina to Pampa de Achala

Copina → Pampa de Achala climbs about 2200 m in 19 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Pampa de Achala to Mina Clavero

Pampa de Achala → Mina Clavero drops about 2200 m over 21 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
