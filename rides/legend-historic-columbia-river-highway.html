Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Troutdale

    Samuel Lancaster, the engineer who built the Historic Columbia River Highway between 1913 and 1922, wanted a road that would show off the gorge instead of conquering it, and Troutdale is where it begins. The town sits where the Sandy River meets the Columbia, on the eastern edge of the Portland area, with a tidy main street, an old railroad depot and a hotel that began life as a poor farm. For a rider the gateway is easy: you leave the suburban edges, cross the river, and within a few miles the road starts to climb.

  3. 3. Historic Columbia River Highway point 3

    Multnomah Falls drops about two hundred metres in two main tiers, and it is the most visited natural site in Oregon, which tells you what to expect from the car park. The classic stone lodge stands at the bottom, the arched footbridge spans the lower pool, and a path zigzags up to a viewing point above it. From a motorcycle seat you cannot see the whole thing at once, so the usual routine is to park, take off your jacket and walk the few minutes to the base.

  4. 4. Hood River

    Wind is the reason Hood River became what it is. Where the Columbia River Gorge narrows between the cool, wet west and the dry eastern plateau, the air funnels through with such force that windsurfers and kiteboarders gather by the hundreds, and the town has grown into a capital of outdoor sports. On a summer afternoon the river is dotted with colourful sails, and the bridge across to Washington State hums in the wind. Behind the town, Mount Hood rises to 11,249 feet, snow-covered through most of the year, and in front of it lies a valley full of orchards.
