Why this ride

From Cortina d'Ampezzo it runs 30 km to Cortina d'Ampezzo; allow about 29 min of riding. It goes by way of Passo Giau, Selva di Cadore and Passo Falzarego. The highest point is about 2,250 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Passo di Giau · Dolomites

Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it. The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2236 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cortina d Ampezzo

    The peaks of the Tofane, Cristallo, Pomagagnon and Sorapiss surround it, and the whole area is part of the UNESCO Dolomites World Heritage Site. The town has about five thousand residents, and many more in the holidays, with elegant shops, bars and a long history as a mountain resort.

  2. 2. Passo Giau

    Giau is one of those passes that makes you slow down on purpose. At 2,236 metres it crosses between the valley of Selva di Cadore and the Cortina side of the Dolomites, climbing out of dark spruce forest onto an open saddle of short grass, boulders and scattered chalets, with the jagged ridge of Ra Gusela and the table-like top of Nuvolau rising to the north. The villages below, Selva di Cadore and Colle Santa Lucia, are tiny and Ladin-speaking, and the ancient Ladin culture of these valleys gives this part of the Dolomites a different flavour from the German-speaking Alto Adige on the far side of the range.

  3. 3. Selva di Cadore

    Selva di Cadore is a quiet village on the road between two big names, in the Fiorentina valley, with Monte Pelmo on one side and the great wall of the Civetta on the other. It is not a place that most people choose as a destination, and that is part of its appeal. A handful of houses, a church, a few hotels and a ski lift sit in a green bowl, and above them the Pelmo, with its flat, throne-like summit, dominates the view. The mountain is known locally as the throne of God, and when the light catches it at the end of the day, it is easy to see why.

  4. 4. Passo Falzarego

    Falzarego is a pass that rewards the effort of the Dolomites, a bare grey saddle at 2,105 metres between Cortina d'Ampezzo and the high valleys of the Cordevole, ringed by pale, ruined-looking peaks that glow pink at sunset. The mountains here are sheer, jagged and strangely close, with the great cliffs of Lagazuoi and the Tofane on one side and the stacked towers of the Cinque Torri on the other. There is almost no vegetation at the top, only rock, scree and a few huts, and the sky feels very large.
