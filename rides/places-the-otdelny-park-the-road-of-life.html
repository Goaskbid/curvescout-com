Why this ride

A 347 km round trip from Pushkin - roughly 5 h in the saddle. It goes by way of The Maximov's Datcha, The Road of Life and back through The Maximov's Datcha. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

56 km from The Otdelny Park to The Maximov's Datcha, mostly on КАД. Fairly level - 13 to 67 m end to end. Watch for sand and spray near the water.

The stops

  1. 1. The Otdelny Park

    Start here.

    Pavlovsk and Pushkin sit side by side on the plain south of Saint Petersburg, and both grew from the imperial taste for country palaces. Pushkin was once Tsarskoye Selo, the Tsar's Village, where Catherine the Great had a palace of blue and white with a facade that stretches for hundreds of metres, and where the Amber Room once stood. Pavlovsk was a smaller palace for her son Paul, set in one of the largest landscape parks in Europe. The name means separate or detached, and it points to a part of the landscape set apart from the main royal grounds.

    Read the complete The Otdelny Park write-up.

  2. 2. The Maximov's Datcha

    56 km in.

    The south shore of the Gulf of Finland west of St Petersburg is a strip of dachas, palaces and old military ground, and the place marked here as Maximov's Datcha sits within it, a couple of kilometres from the town of Lomonosov.

    Read the complete The Maximov's Datcha write-up.

  3. 3. The Road of Life

    170 km in.

    On the south-eastern shore of Lake Ladoga, east of Saint Petersburg, a road ends at the water. This is the start of the Road of Life, the supply route across the ice that kept Leningrad alive during the siege of 1941 to 1944. The siege lasted 872 days, from September 1941 to January 1944, and it cut the city off from nearly everything except the lake. In the first winter, when Ladoga froze, trucks and horse-drawn sledges began to cross the ice with food and fuel, and on the return journey they carried out women, children and the sick. Lorries broke through the ice, and bombing and artillery attacked the route, but it kept going.

    Read the complete The Road of Life write-up.
