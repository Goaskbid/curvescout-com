Why this ride

A one-way ride of 875 km, Kochel to Rottach-Egern - roughly 15 h 39 in the saddle. On the way it passes Walchensee road, Jachenau valley, Bad Tölz and Kochelsee. near Navis is the high point. It tops out at about 1,300 m.

Water arrives beside the road and stays for 39 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go — slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kochelsee lakeside

    Kochel am See lies right at the point where the Bavarian flatlands end, as if somebody had stood the Alps up against the lake. The Kochelsee is a small, deep glacial lake of a few kilometres' length with reed beds along the north shore, and behind the village the ground rises sharply to the Herzogstand and Heimgarten, two wooded ridges that give the lake its backdrop. The village has about four thousand people, a handful of cafes, a promenade and, if you ride south from Penzberg, the first real sense of arriving in the mountains.

  2. 2. Walchensee road

    The Walchensee is one of the deepest and clearest Alpine lakes in Germany, and the road along its eastern and southern shore is one of the most photographed in Bavaria. The water is a turquoise that looks almost Caribbean when the sun hits the shallows, and it is fed by cold springs, which keep it chilly all summer. Around it rise forested slopes, the Herzogstand to the north and the peaks of the Karwendel and the Estergebirge to the south, so the lake sits in a bowl, with its surface often creased by wind.

  3. 3. Jachenau valley

    Not many people know the Jachenau. It is a long, narrow side valley that runs east from the Walchensee region to the Isar, shut in by forested ridges and farmed by a few families on meadow terraces, and it has the quality of a place that the main roads missed. Lenggries, with its nine thousand inhabitants, is the nearest real town and the place for petrol, bakeries and shops, about eleven kilometres to the east.

  4. 4. Bad Tölz food

    Bad Tölz is the market town of the Isarwinkel, a place of seventeen thousand people on the Isar river with the Alps standing behind it, and it is the sort of town where a motorcyclist can eat very well. The old main street, the Marktstraße, climbs gently from the river and is lined with houses painted in bright colours and decorated with gables and painted fronts, a pattern that Bavarian towns have followed for centuries. It is a spa town in a modest way, known since the nineteenth century for the iodine-rich springs that gave it the name Bad, and people still come here for the cure.

  5. 5. Back to Kochelsee

    Kochel am See lies at the foot of the Alps, where the Bavarian lowlands finally give way to mountains, and the Kochelsee fills the basin in front of it with a sheet of green-blue water about six hundred metres above the sea. The town has about four thousand people and a long lakeside promenade, with the first big peaks rising behind. The painter Franz Marc, one of the founders of the Blue Rider group, lived in the village of Ried, just outside, and the Franz Marc Museum on the hill above the lake holds a lovely collection of his work and that of his circle. Marc died at Verdun in 1916.

  6. 6. Fuel stop near Rottach-Egern

    At the southern end of the Tegernsee, Rottach-Egern sits where the lake meets the mountains, and it has long been one of the most comfortable addresses in Upper Bavaria. Painted houses, carved balconies and well-kept gardens line the shore, and in summer the lake is busy with sailors and swimmers. The Wallberg rises steeply behind the village, and on a clear day its summit looks close enough to touch.

  7. 7. High point near Navis

    Navis lies in a long, quiet side valley that branches east from the Wipptal, the great Brenner corridor south of Innsbruck. Down on the main valley floor the motorway and the railway carry an endless stream of traffic towards Italy; turn off at Matrei or Steinach and climb into Navis, and within minutes the noise is gone. What remains is a scattered farming village strung along the valley for several kilometres, with steep meadows, wooden barns and a church that seems to keep watch from its slope.

  8. 8. Scenic stretch near Schwabmünchen

    South of Augsburg the land flattens into the Lechfeld, a broad gravel plain shaped by the Lech river as it runs north from the Alps. Schwabmünchen is the market town of this stretch, a practical place with a long main street, a handful of churches, and all the shops and services a rider needs. It is not a postcard town, and it does not pretend to be, but it makes a reliable pause between Augsburg and the Allgäu.

  9. 9. Photo stop near Donauwörth

    Donauwörth stands where the Wörnitz flows into the Danube, a meeting of waters that made the town important for trade and crossings since the Middle Ages. Its main street, the Reichsstraße, is lined with colourful gabled houses, churches and the old town hall, and it was once part of an important trade route between Nuremberg and Augsburg. Today it is a stop on the Romantic Road, the tourist route that runs from Würzburg to Füssen.

  10. 10. Near Johanniskirchen, on the way back

    The Rottal, in Lower Bavaria, is a land of rolling hills, small woods and wide fields, with farmhouses scattered across the slopes and onion-domed church towers rising above the villages. It is far from the famous Alpine scenery of the south, and few tourists come here, but it has a quiet, pastoral beauty that many riders find refreshing. Johanniskirchen is a small village in the hills between the Vils and the Rott, on the way back towards Munich, and the lanes around it are as calm as any in Bavaria.

  11. 11. Food stop near Arnstorf

    Arnstorf is a small market town in the Rottal, the gently rolling country of Lower Bavaria between the Isar and the Inn. It is farming country through and through: maize, wheat, hop gardens here and there, big Bavarian farmsteads and onion-domed churches on every rise. The Rottal is also famous for horse breeding, and you may see paddocks of the sturdy Rottaler horses along the lanes.

  12. 12. Near Planegg

    The end of a Bavarian lakes day usually means the Würm valley, and this finish lies on its edge, between Planegg, Krailling and Neuried, where Munich's south-western suburbs meet the forests. The Würm flows north out of Lake Starnberg through a gentle valley of meadows and villages, and the Forstenrieder Park, a large forest of beech and spruce, stretches to the east. It is a pleasant, green corner of the city's fringe.

  13. 13. Back to Allgäu High Roads

    Bad Wiessee lies on the western shore and Tegernsee town on the eastern side, with the mountains of the Mangfall range dropping steeply into the water behind them. It is a place where Munich's money goes for the weekend, and the villas and boat houses show it, but it is also a real valley with cows, hay meadows and a long history.
