Why this ride

A 106 km one-way ride from near Martigny-Ville, about 1 h 45 of riding. On the way it passes Sion, Salgesch and Leuk. The highest point is about 2,500 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Grand Tour of Switzerland

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 37 km this is Grand Tour of Switzerland, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Martigny

    Martigny stands at the elbow of the Rhône, the place where the river turns from a westward run through the Valais to a northward one toward Lake Geneva, and it has been a crossroads since the Romans. They called it Octodurum, built a town, a temple and an amphitheatre here, and the road over the Great St Bernard, which starts at the edge of town, was one of the main routes over the Alps. You can still visit the amphitheatre, and a museum on the Roman site has a collection that is unusual for a town this size.

  2. 2. Sion

    Sion is the capital of the Valais, the Swiss canton that runs along the upper Rhône between two walls of high mountains, and it is dominated by two hills. On one, the ruins of the Tourbillon castle crown the rock, and on the other, the fortified church of Valère holds what is said to be the oldest playable pipe organ in the world, dating from around the fifteenth century. You climb to it by a steep stone lane, and in summer the organ is used for a festival. The two hills rise straight out of the town and give Sion a theatrical appearance, like a backdrop for something medieval.

  3. 3. Salgesch

    Salgesch is the wine village at the end of the German-speaking world, or at least at its edge in the Rhône valley. Just downstream, in Sierre, people speak French. In Salgesch, they speak a Valais German dialect and make some of the best Pinot Noir in Switzerland, and the language border runs between them like an invisible fence that nobody has ever bothered to build. The vines grow on steep south-facing terraces above the village, with the Rhône flat and silvery below and the peaks of the Bernese Alps on the other side.

  4. 4. Leuk

    Leuk sits on a small hill above the Rhône, with a castle at the top, a church with a tall spire, and a street plan that has changed very little since the Middle Ages. In German it is Leuk, in French Loèche, and the double name tells you where you are: on the language border of the Valais, where the French-speaking lower valley gives way to German. For centuries the town was the seat of a regional government, and the old houses along the main street, with their carved wooden balconies and painted shutters, reflect a prosperous past.

  5. 5. Brig

    Brig is a small town with a big palace, and its position at the foot of the Simplon Pass gives it a significance out of proportion to its size. It sits in the upper Rhône valley in the canton of Valais, where the river runs west to Lake Geneva, and a route over the mountains to Italy has existed here since Roman times. The Stockalperschloss, the biggest private residence in Switzerland when it was completed in the late seventeenth century, was built by Kaspar Jodok von Stockalper, a merchant who controlled the Simplon trade and became the richest man in the country. With its three onion-domed towers, named for the Three Kings, it dominates the old town and is open to visitors.
