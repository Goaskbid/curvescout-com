Coral Coast Highway: Perth to Kalbarri

Why this ride

From Perth it runs 1274 km to Kalbarri; allow about 17 h 28 of riding. It goes by way of Pinnacles Desert and Geraldton. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Perth

    Perth is the most isolated big city on Earth, and you can feel it in the way people talk about the rest of Australia as a different country. About two thousand kilometres of desert separate it from Adelaide, and the next nearest city of any size is across the Indian Ocean. The place has always lived by its own rules and its own sunshine, which is claimed to be the most of any Australian capital. The Swan River, broad and slow, loops past the city centre, and Kings Park, a huge area of bushland and gardens on a hill above, gives a clear view of the skyline and the water.

  2. 2. Pinnacles Desert

    The Pinnacles are thousands of limestone pillars standing in a field of yellow sand, in Nambung National Park, about two hundred kilometres north of Perth, on the Indian Ocean coast of Western Australia. Some are knee-high, others taller than a person, and they rise out of the desert like a ruined city or a field of tombstones, glowing orange at sunset. Nobody is entirely sure how they formed, but the leading theory is that they are the remains of a limestone layer made from ancient seashells, with the pillars forming where plant roots channelled water and hardened the rock, then exposed when the sand blew away.

  3. 3. Geraldton

    Geraldton sits on Champion Bay, about four hundred kilometres north of Perth, and it is the largest city on the Coral Coast, with more than twenty-six thousand people. It has a deep working harbour, long white beaches and a stiff southerly wind that makes it a magnet for windsurfers and kitesurfers, who flock to the beaches near the city in summer. The air smells of salt and diesel, and the light is brilliant.

  4. 4. Kalbarri

    Kalbarri is a small town on the mouth of the Murchison River, where the river meets the Indian Ocean, and it is the gateway to a national park of the same name. The park covers a huge area of sandstone plateau, cut by deep gorges that expose layers of red and white rock laid down hundreds of millions of years ago. Lookouts such as Nature's Window, a natural arch framing the river below, and the Z Bend, where the gorge folds back on itself, are the main attractions, and the Skywalk platforms stick out into the void for those with a head for heights.
