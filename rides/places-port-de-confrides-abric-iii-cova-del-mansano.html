Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Port de Confrides- Abric III

    Fog sometimes fills the valleys below the Port de Confrides while the summit shines in the sun, and when that happens the effect is like standing on the shore of a white sea. It is a lovely sight in autumn and winter, and it is a good reason to check the forecast before you set out. The Port de Confrides is a pass on the road between the Guadalest valley and the Alcoi plain, in the shadow of Aitana, and the shelter lies a short way from it, in the rough hillside of barrancos and scrub that falls towards Benimantell.

  2. 2. Pla de Petracos- Abric I

    Pla de Petracos is one of the most important prehistoric art sites in Spain, and it looks like nothing much: a row of shallow shelters in the side of a ravine near Castell de Castells, in the high interior of the Marina Alta. What makes it special is the style. The paintings here are among the clearest examples of what archaeologists call Macroschematic art, large, bold, almost abstract human figures with raised arms and serpentine forms, painted by the first farming communities of the region in the early Neolithic. Elsewhere in the Valencian mountains you find Levantine hunting scenes; here you find something stranger and more symbolic. A short path and viewing platforms make the panels accessible, and the information boards help you see what the eye misses at first.

  3. 3. Cova del mansano

    Every cyclist in Europe seems to know the Coll de Rates, and that is a good hint for motorcyclists too. The climb starts in Parcent, a few kilometres from the Cova del Mansano, and winds up the mountainside in a long sequence of smooth, well surfaced bends to a pass with a view over the whole Vall de Pop and the sea beyond. The rock shelter itself sits in the hills around Jalón, Alcalalí and Parcent, a short walk from the road, and its paintings belong to the same World Heritage group as the other Levantine shelters of the coast.
