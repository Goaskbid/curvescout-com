Why this ride

From Nerpio it runs 197 km to Nerpio; allow about 3 h 24 of riding. The stops in between: Canalejas de Abajo, Letreros de los mártires, Cañada de la Cruz and back through Letreros de los mártires. The highest point is about 1,400 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abrigo de la Ventana II

    A window in the rock gives the shelter its name, and the Abrigo de la Ventana II lies about twelve kilometres from Nerpio in the high country of the Sierra del Segura, where limestone cliffs are pierced by natural openings and hollows. It is the second of two shelters of that name, and it appears on a route with the Diosa Madre, the Mother Goddess, which is the title that archaeologists have given to a rock painting that they interpret as a female figure. The whole group belongs to the Mediterranean rock art listed by UNESCO in 1998.

  2. 2. Canalejas de Abajo

    Canalejas de Abajo is a name on the map rather than a place with a church and a bar: a scattering of farms and old cortijos in the upper valley of the Taibilla river, south of Nerpio, in the mountains where Albacete, Jaén and Granada meet. The Sierra de Segura and the Sierra de las Cabras rise all around, high limestone country cut by deep valleys, and the region is known among archaeologists for its painted rock shelters. The Abrigo del Sabinar and others along the same valley hold some of the most important prehistoric paintings of the Spanish south east, drawn on the soft overhangs that the river and the wind have shaped.

  3. 3. Letreros de los mártires

    Out in the high north-east of Granada province, Huéscar feels a long way from the Alhambra and the beaches of the Costa Tropical. This is the altiplano, a broad upland of wheat fields, almond trees and pale badlands at around a thousand metres, ringed by mountains, with the great limestone hump of La Sagra rising to the north. The rock shelter called Letreros de los Mártires lies in this country, a dozen or so kilometres from the town.

  4. 4. Cañada de la Cruz

    The Cañada de la Cruz is a high valley or drovers' pasture in the Segura mountains; cañada is the word for the traditional livestock routes and grazing grounds used by shepherds moving their flocks between summer and winter pastures, and this part of Spain has a long history of transhumance.
