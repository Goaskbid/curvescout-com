Why this ride

National Scenic Byway. foothills, historic sites, mountain views. From Golden it runs 39 km to Golden; allow about 43 min of riding. It goes by way of Evergreen and Indian Hills. The highest point is about 2,650 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Golden

    Golden is where Denver's western edge hits the mountains. The town sits at the mouth of Clear Creek Canyon, squeezed between the twin flat-topped bulks of North and South Table Mountain, and its main street still looks like a western town that grew up respectable. Golden was the territorial capital of Colorado in the 1860s before Denver took over, and it has kept a few reminders, including the banner that arches over Washington Avenue welcoming visitors, and the Colorado School of Mines, the college that trains mining and petroleum engineers. Coors Brewing has made beer here since 1873, and the brewery still dominates one end of town, with a tour that finishes at a tasting room.

  2. 2. Near Golden

    Here the plains of Denver finish and the Rockies begin, and they do not ease into each other. The point lies in the foothills west of Golden, near Indian Hills, in a steep, pine-dotted landscape where red sandstone fins stick up from the slopes and the city's glow is only a few miles behind the ridge. Evergreen, the nearest sizeable town, sits in a mountain valley beyond.

  3. 3. Evergreen

    Evergreen is the mountain town that Denver residents escape to, and you can feel it as soon as you arrive. Pine trees close in around the road, the elevation has crept above seven thousand feet, and the lake in the middle of town reflects aspen and sky. After the open prairie and the busy suburbs, this feels like a different state, and it takes only forty minutes by bike to get from one to the other.
