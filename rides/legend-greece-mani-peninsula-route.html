Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kalamata

    Kalamata is the doorway to the Mani, and the olive capital of Greece, as the dark purple Kalamata olive is known the world over. Groves of olive trees fill the plain of Messenia, and the city sits at the head of the gulf with the great wall of the Taygetos range rising behind it, its summit at Profitis Ilias well over two thousand metres, often capped with snow into spring. The city is a lively mix of seafront, markets and a pedestrian old town, and was rebuilt after a damaging earthquake in 1986.

  2. 2. Kardamýli

    The Mani is the middle finger of the Peloponnese, a rugged, rocky peninsula that points south toward Africa, and Kardamyli sits on its western coast, at the foot of the Taygetos range. The mountains rise steeply behind the village, with the highest peak at about two and a half thousand metres, and the sea sits right in front, so the whole place has the air of a stage set. Olive groves cover the slopes between the two, and the stone houses and towers of the old village climb the hill above the new one.

  3. 3. Greece Mani Peninsula Route point 3

    Around Areopoli the Mani stops being a pleasant coastal road and starts to look like what it is: a rugged spur of limestone pushing south into the Mediterranean, with the high ridge of Taygetus behind and a dry, stony landscape in front. The soil is thin, the stone walls run in every direction, and the villages are clusters of grey towers that look more like a fortress than a home. Gytheio, the nearest sizeable town on the Laconian side, lies about nineteen kilometres away.

  4. 4. Greece Mani Peninsula Route point 4

    Further south the Mani gets harder and more beautiful in about equal measure. The southern part of the peninsula, often called the Deep Mani, is a thin finger of limestone with almost no trees, scrub in place of woodland, and a bare, silvery look in the afternoon light. The sea is always close, a bright band on either side, and the wind seldom drops. Gytheio, the only larger town in range, is more than thirty kilometres behind.

  5. 5. Greece Mani Peninsula Route point 5

    The end of the line is a place with the name Taenaron, or Matapan in the older sea charts, the southernmost tip of mainland Greece. Few places in Europe feel so much like the edge of something. The land narrows to a spine of rock, scrub and wind, the sea opens to the south, and on a clear day the horizon seems to curve. There are no towns in range, only a handful of tiny settlements, so treat it as a place to reach prepared and unhurried.
