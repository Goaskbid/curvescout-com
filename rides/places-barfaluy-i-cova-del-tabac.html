Why this ride

A 224 km round trip from Bárcabo - roughly 3 h 52 in the saddle. The stops in between: El Remosillo, Les Coves, Cova del Tabac and back through Les Coves. The highest point is about 1,050 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barfaluy I

    There is more than one reason to come to the Barfaluy ravine, but the clearest is the sudden change in the land. From Alquézar the road runs along a plateau of fields and scrub, and then the ground simply drops away into a deep, narrow gorge where the walls rise sheer on either side and sunlight reaches the floor only briefly. Barfaluy I, one of the shelters in the series catalogued along it, lies five kilometres from the village, and as with others in the Vero valley it forms part of the Levantine rock art listed by UNESCO in 1998.

  2. 2. El Remosillo

    If you ride north from Barbastro toward Graus, the Ésera river eventually forces its way through a ridge of limestone, and the result is the Congosto de Olvena, a short, deep gorge with the road squeezed between rock and water. Up on the cliffs around here are rock shelters with prehistoric paintings, and El Remosillo is one of the best known in this corner of Aragón, between Estadilla, Graus and Fonz.

  3. 3. Les Coves

    The western edge of Catalonia, around Lleida, is fruit country. Irrigation canals bring water from the Pyrenean rivers to a broad, flat plain of orchards growing apples, pears and peaches, laid out in neat lines that stretch to the horizon. Les Coves lies in this area, close to the Aragonese border, between Alfarràs, Albelda and Algerri, where the valley of the Noguera Ribagorçana spills out of the hills and onto the plain.

  4. 4. Cova del Tabac

    Above the village of Camarasa, where the Segre comes out of the pre-Pyrenean hills onto the plain of Lleida, a cave in the limestone holds some of the most interesting rock paintings in Catalonia. The Cova del Tabac, the tobacco cave, is a wide shelter with schematic paintings, simplified human figures, animals and symbols in red, and it is one of the sites included in the UNESCO listing of Mediterranean rock art on the Iberian Peninsula. A short walk leads up from the road to the shelter, which also offers a fine view over the valley, and on a clear day the Pyrenees rise white on the northern horizon.
