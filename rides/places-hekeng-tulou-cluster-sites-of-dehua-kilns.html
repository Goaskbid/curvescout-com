Why this ride

On the way it passes Fujian Tulou, Xiacaopu Iron Production Site of Qingyang, Sites of Dehua Kilns and back through Xiacaopu Iron Production Site of Qingyang. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Hekeng Tulou Cluster

    Start here.

    Hekeng is a hamlet in Nanjing County, tucked into a steep valley where a river drops through a series of small gorges, and its tulou stand among terraces and bamboo thickets so close to the water that you can hear the stream from the courtyards. The setting is the thing to notice. Unlike the lowland villages, Hekeng lies hard against the hills, and the buildings are packed along the slope in a way that seems almost improvised, with narrow lanes and stone steps between them. The cluster includes a number of earthen houses, among them a large round one and several square ones, built by families of the Hakka and local Minnan communities from the nineteenth century onward.

    Read the complete Hekeng Tulou Cluster write-up.

  2. 2. Fujian Tulou

    147 km in.

    Picture a giant ring of rammed earth, three or four storeys high, with almost no windows on the outside and a single heavy gate. Inside, galleries of wooden rooms face a courtyard with an ancestral hall at its heart, and up to several hundred members of one extended family once lived within those walls. That is a tulou, and the hills of south-western Fujian are dotted with them, round and square, some centuries old, many still inhabited.

    Read the complete Fujian Tulou write-up.

  3. 3. Xiacaopu Iron Production Site of Qingyang

    203 km in.

    In the green hills of Anxi county, inland from the port city of Quanzhou in Fujian, the Xiacaopu site preserves the remains of an iron industry that flourished during the Song and Yuan dynasties, roughly from the tenth to the fourteenth centuries. Archaeologists found furnaces, slag heaps, ore-roasting pits and workshops on a hillside, evidence of large-scale production. The iron produced here was shipped down the rivers to Quanzhou and exported to Southeast Asia and beyond, and that is why the site is part of the World Heritage listing of Quanzhou, Emporium of the World in Song-Yuan China, inscribed in 2021. At that time, Quanzhou was one of the largest ports in the world, visited by Arab, Persian and Indian merchants, and described by Marco Polo and Ibn Battuta.

    Read the complete Xiacaopu Iron Production Site of Qingyang write-up.

  4. 4. Sites of Dehua Kilns

    276 km in.

    Dehua, a county in the mountains of Fujian province, in south-eastern China, has been making porcelain for over a thousand years. Its white porcelain, known in the West as blanc de Chine, is famous for its pure, creamy white colour and its fine figures, especially statues of Guanyin, the goddess of mercy, and other Buddhist and Taoist figures. The ancient kiln sites of Dehua, including long dragon kilns built on hillsides, are part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site of Quanzhou: Emporium of the World in Song-Yuan China, inscribed in 2021.
