Why this ride

From Al Muharraq it runs 134 km to Al Muharraq; allow about 2 h 26 of riding. It goes by way of Al Zubarah Archaeological Site and back through Al Zubarah Archaeological Site. It crosses from Bahrain into Qatar - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Murad House

    The Murad family were pearl merchants, and their house in Muharraq belongs to the generation that saw the trade at its peak and then watched it collapse. The business boomed in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, as demand for natural pearls rose in Europe and India, and the merchants of the Gulf grew rich. Then, in the 1930s, the Japanese perfected the cultured pearl, prices fell, and the industry went into a decline from which it never recovered. In Bahrain the crash was cushioned by oil, struck in 1932, the first oil strike on the Arab side of the Gulf.

  2. 2. Al Zubarah Archaeological Site

    Out on the north-west coast of Qatar, where the desert slides into a shallow turquoise sea, stand the low ruins of Al Zubarah, once one of the busiest pearling and trading towns in the Gulf. In the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the town thrived on pearls dived from the oyster beds offshore, traded on to India, Persia and beyond. Then it declined, was abandoned in the early twentieth century, and the sand covered it. Archaeologists have since uncovered streets, houses, a souk, mosques and a long town wall running in an arc around the old harbour. UNESCO inscribed the site in 2013.
