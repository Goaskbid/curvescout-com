Why this ride

A 152 km round trip from Pushkin - roughly 3 h 30 in the saddle. It goes by way of Peterhofskaya Road, Historical Centre of the Town of Peterhof, The Coastal fort “Shanetz” and back through Historical Centre of the Town of Peterhof. The coast is the point of it.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

It is 22 km from Peterhofskaya Road to Historical Centre of the Town of Peterhof on Санкт-Петербургское шоссе. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 16 and 30 m. Watch for sand and spray near the water.

The stops

  1. 1. Historical Centre of the Town of Pushkin

    Start here.

    The history of the Pushkin district begins with a Swedish manor and a Russian victory, and the story of what grew there is the story of the Russian court itself. The town was long called Tsarskoye Selo, the Tsar's Village, and the great Catherine Palace, with its long blue and white facade and gilded decoration, was the summer residence of the empresses. Inside, the Amber Room, once panelled with amber, was looted during the Second World War and has been recreated in recent decades, to the delight of visitors.

    Read the complete Historical Centre of the Town of Pushkin write-up.

  2. 2. Peterhofskaya Road

    24 km in.

    Peterhofskaya Road was once the grandest road out of St Petersburg. Running southwest from the city along the shore of the Gulf of Finland toward Strelna and Peterhof, it was lined in the eighteenth century with country estates, dachas and palaces built by the nobility, who wanted to be close to the imperial residences along the coast. Peter the Great started the trend, granting plots to courtiers along the road, and the result was a chain of estates with gardens, parks and ponds stretching for miles. Many of these estates survive in some form, and the road and its surroundings are part of the UNESCO listing of the historic centre of St Petersburg and related groups of monuments.

    Read the complete Peterhofskaya Road write-up.

  3. 3. Historical Centre of the Town of Peterhof

    46 km in.

    Peter the Great wanted a palace by the sea that would rival Versailles, and he chose this stretch of the southern shore of the Gulf of Finland, about thirty kilometres west of Saint Petersburg. What became Peterhof is a town built around an imperial residence: the Grand Palace on a terrace above the sea, the Grand Cascade of gilded statues and fountains falling toward a canal that runs straight to the water, and parks full of pavilions, including Peter's own small Dutch style house, Monplaisir, right on the shore. The fountains run on gravity, fed by springs on the higher ground inland, a feat of eighteenth century engineering.

    Read the complete Historical Centre of the Town of Peterhof write-up.

  4. 4. The Coastal fort “Shanetz”

    74 km in.

    Shanetz sounds like a word for a small redoubt, and that is roughly what these coastal forts were: low, squat batteries put on islets and spits to cover the fairways into the Gulf of Finland. This one lies in the western approaches of Kotlin, on the shoals north of the island, and it belongs to the great ring of defences that earned Kronstadt its nickname as the key to Petersburg. The forts earned their keep during the Crimean War, when Allied ships came up the Baltic, took a look at the defences and decided not to risk an attack.

    Read the complete The Coastal fort “Shanetz” write-up.
