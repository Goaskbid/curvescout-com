Why this ride

A 435 km round trip from Alquézar - roughly 7 h 27 in the saddle. On the way it passes Cueva de Revilla, Eglésia de Santa Maria de l'Assumpció and back through Cueva de Revilla. It tops out around 1,850 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Pyrenees N260 Route

The 107 km between Covacho de Palluala and Cueva de Revilla, mostly on A-138. Up about 780 m to 1,092 m, then 470 m back down the far side. Fill the tank before the climb.

The stops

  1. 1. Covacho de Palluala

    Start here.

    Covacho is the Aragonese word for a small cave or overhang, and Palluala is one of the hillside patches near Alquézar that carry the old farming names of the valley. The shelter, a mere kilometre and a bit from the village, is probably the nearest to it of all the painted rocks on this list, which means you can leave the motorbike in the village and walk instead of making a project of it. As with its neighbours it belongs to the Levantine rock art of the Spanish Mediterranean, listed by UNESCO in 1998.

    Read the complete Covacho de Palluala write-up.

  2. 2. Cueva de Revilla

    107 km in.

    Revilla is one of the most spectacularly placed hamlets in the Spanish Pyrenees, perched on a ledge above the Garganta de Escuaín, a deep gorge cut into the limestone of the Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park. It looks down into the canyon from a terrace of meadows, with the high peaks behind. The Cueva de Revilla is a cave in the cliffs near the hamlet, and the area between Bielsa, Plan and Boltaña is some of the wildest country in Aragón.

    Read the complete Cueva de Revilla write-up.

  3. 3. Eglésia de Santa Maria de l'Assumpció

    220 km in.

    Valleys in the high Pyrenees of Catalonia and Aragon are full of small Romanesque churches, and the area around the Alta Ribagorça is among the richest. They were built in the eleventh and twelfth centuries by craftsmen working in what is called the Lombard style, with plain stone walls, round arches and tall, slender bell towers decorated with blind arcades, and they stand in villages that often have only a few houses.
