Why this ride

504 km from Lares to Lares, about 10 h of riding. The stops in between: Segment Plaza Inca Hanan: Hauk’aypataqolla, Segment Portada Rumiqolqa-ch’Uspitacana, Segment Cusipata-Santa Cruz de Occobamba N and Segment Machaqmarka-Raqchi-Qquea. It tops out around 4,450 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Sacred Valley Route

The 105 km between Segment Patacancha-Huacahuasi and Segment Plaza Inca Hanan: Hauk’aypataqolla, mostly on CU-593. A proper up-and-over: 1650 m of climbing, 4,218 m at the top, 1510 m down. Above 2,000 m the weather turns fast; have a layer and a plan B.

The stops

  1. 1. Segment Patacancha-Huacahuasi

    Start here.

    Above Ollantaytambo, where the Sacred Valley of the Incas narrows, a side valley climbs north toward the high passes of the Urubamba range, following the Patacancha river. The segment between Patacancha and Huacahuasi is part of the Qhapaq Ñan, the Inca road system, and it crosses some of the most beautiful country in the Cusco region: steep slopes covered with potato fields and terraces, small villages of stone and adobe houses, herds of llamas and alpacas grazing in high pastures, and snow-capped peaks above. The communities of this area, like Patacancha and Huacahuasi, are known for their weaving, with intricate designs and bright colours, and the women's traditional dress is among the most striking in the Andes.

    Read the complete Segment Patacancha-Huacahuasi write-up.

  2. 2. Segment Plaza Inca Hanan: Hauk’aypataqolla

    105 km in.

    Every road of the Inca empire began here. The great square of Cusco, which the Incas called Hauk'aypata, was the ceremonial heart of Tawantinsuyu, the land of the four quarters, and from it the main roads radiated out to the four parts of the empire, to Quito in the north, to Chile and Argentina in the south, to the jungle in the east and the coast in the west. The square was much larger than today's Plaza de Armas and was covered, according to the chroniclers, with sand brought from the coast, in which offerings of gold and silver were buried. Here, mummies of past rulers were brought out for festivals, and the Inca received ambassadors and celebrated victories.

    Read the complete Segment Plaza Inca Hanan: Hauk’aypataqolla write-up.

  3. 3. Segment Portada Rumiqolqa-ch’Uspitacana

    148 km in.

    South-east of Cusco, the Vilcanota valley narrows at a place called Rumicolca, and a massive stone gateway stands across the old road. Built originally by the Wari, the empire that preceded the Inca, as part of an aqueduct, and later converted by the Inca into a controlled entrance to the Cusco valley, the gateway of Rumicolca marked the checkpoint where travellers and goods were inspected. The segment of the Qhapaq Ñan between Rumicolca and Chuspitacana is part of the Andean road system inscribed on the UNESCO list in 2014, and its stones still carry the marks of the people who walked it.

    Read the complete Segment Portada Rumiqolqa-ch’Uspitacana write-up.

  4. 4. Segment Cusipata-Santa Cruz de Occobamba N

    215 km in.

    South of Cusco, the Vilcanota River runs through a broad, fertile valley flanked by high mountains, and along it ran one of the most important stretches of the Qhapaq Ñan, the Inca road system that linked an empire stretching from Ecuador to Chile. The route from Cusco to Lake Titicaca followed this valley, and sections of the original road, some paved with stone, some cut into the hillside, still survive. The segment between Cusipata and Santa Cruz de Occobamba went onto the World Heritage list in 2014 as one strand of the Qhapaq Ñan.

    Read the complete Segment Cusipata-Santa Cruz de Occobamba N write-up.

  5. 5. Segment Machaqmarka-Raqchi-Qquea

    252 km in.

    The Vilcanota valley south of Cusco is the old highway of the Andes, and the Inca road ran along it towards Lake Titicaca and the southern provinces of the empire. The segment between Machaqmarka, Raqchi and Qquea is one of the sections of the Qhapaq Ñan listed by UNESCO, and it passes one of the most impressive Inca sites outside Cusco. At Raqchi, near San Pedro de Cacha, the Temple of Wiracocha, dedicated to the creator god, was an enormous building with a central wall of stone and adobe that still rises around fifteen metres high, flanked by rows of columns. Around it are the remains of storehouses, the round qollqas, lined up in rows, houses, baths and fountains, and a lava field from the nearby volcano of Kinsach'ata, which according to legend erupted when Wiracocha passed by. The village holds a festival in June, with music and dancing.
