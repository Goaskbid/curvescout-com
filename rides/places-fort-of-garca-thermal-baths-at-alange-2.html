Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fort of Garça

    The Fort of Graca sits on a hill north of Elvas, like a stone crown, and it was built after a lesson. In the 1750s and 1760s, Europe had been rocked by the Seven Years War, and Portugal, which had been dragged into conflict with Spain, found that its border defences were out of date. The government called in a foreign expert, the Count of Lippe, a German soldier of fortune and military reformer, who designed a new fortress on the highest hill overlooking the town. Work began in the 1760s and continued for decades, and the result was the most modern piece of the entire system, a fortress designed for the age of heavy artillery, with a star-shaped plan, deep ditches and underground galleries.

  2. 2. Proserpina Dam

    The Romans built dams in Spain on a scale that still surprises engineers, and Proserpina, a few kilometres north of Mérida, is one of the best preserved. It was built in the first or second century AD to store water for Emerita Augusta, the provincial capital, which was fed by aqueduct from the lake. The dam is a massive earthen embankment faced with stone and backed by huge buttresses, some four hundred metres long, and it has held water for roughly nineteen centuries with repairs. The reservoir, a long green lake in a landscape of holm oak and rock, is now a place where local people fish, swim and walk.

  3. 3. Thermal Baths at Alange

    The Romans, who were serious about hot water, found a warm spring at Alange in what is now Extremadura and built a bathing complex around it, and the place is still used today. The Roman baths, about twenty kilometres from Mérida, include a domed chamber with stone benches and a pool fed by the spring, and the water comes out pleasantly warm, a gift in a dry country. The baths date from the time when Mérida, then called Emerita Augusta, was one of the great cities of Roman Spain, and wealthy citizens would travel here to relax and be cured.
