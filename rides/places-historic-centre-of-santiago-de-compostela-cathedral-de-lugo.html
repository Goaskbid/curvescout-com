Why this ride

From Santiago de Compostela it runs 319 km to Santiago de Compostela; allow about 5 h 30 of riding. On the way it passes Monaster de Sobrado Dos Monxes, Chemin de l’Intérieur, Cathédral de Lugo and back through Chemin de l’Intérieur. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Historic Centre of Santiago de Compostela

    The cathedral façade on the Obradoiro square is dark, baroque and enormous, but the thing that stays with you is the sound: a bagpiper in an archway, footsteps on wet granite and the tired cheers of pilgrims arriving after weeks on the road. Santiago has been the end of the Camino for over a thousand years, and arriving by motorbike is a very different pace, but the feeling in the square is the same. People hug, cry, take their boots off and lie flat on the paving stones.

  2. 2. Monaster de Sobrado Dos Monxes

    Pilgrims walking the Camino del Norte toward Santiago reach Sobrado dos Monxes tired and footsore, and the sight of the huge granite Baroque façade of the monastery, rising out of a quiet Galician village, must feel like a miracle. Sobrado was founded as a monastery in the tenth century, became Cistercian in the twelfth, and grew into one of the most powerful abbeys in Galicia. After the dissolution of the monasteries in the nineteenth century, it fell into ruin, until Cistercian monks returned in the 1950s and began restoring it. Today they run a pilgrim hostel in part of the buildings.

  3. 3. Chemin de l’Intérieur

    Pilgrims have been crossing this green upland of Galicia on their way to Santiago de Compostela for more than a thousand years, and the scallop-shell waymarkers still guide them through the villages around Vilalba and Guitiriz. This is the Terra Chá, the flat land, a high plateau in the north of the province of Lugo where the northern pilgrim routes come down from the Cantabrian coast toward the interior. The Ways of Saint James in northern Spain are part of a UNESCO listing that recognises not only the famous French Way but also the coastal and inland routes that run through this region.

  4. 4. Cathédral de Lugo

    Lugo is one of those cities that tells you its age before you have parked. A ring of Roman wall, two kilometres of it and still complete, circles the old town, and walking along the top is the best way to understand why UNESCO listed it. The Romans founded Lucus Augusti here at the end of the first century before Christ, drawn by hot springs on the Miño river, and the baths are still in use by the riverside. The wall's gates, rebuilt and re-cut many times, let you walk in and out of the old quarter as people have for nearly two thousand years.
