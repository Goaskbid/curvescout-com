Why this ride

74 km from Dahlonega to Blairsville, about 1 h 25 of riding. It goes by way of Turners Corner and Suches. The climb of the day is Wolf Pen Gap. It tops out at 900 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Scenic road chapters

Richard Russell Scenic Highway

The valley floor is behind you and the road starts working for its height at about 8.0%. Corners stack above one another where the ground allows nothing straighter — first and second gear more often than not. Look through each corner to the next one and let the engine do the holding.

Richard Russell Scenic Highway

900 m come off over 16 km at around 5.8%. The road gives back what the climb took, faster than it took it. Brakes early, eyes far ahead. A descent this long asks more of concentration than of pace.

  2. 2. Turners Corner

    Turners Corner is a crossroads, and in this corner of Georgia that is enough to make it a landmark. Where several of the old mountain roads meet in the foothills of the Blue Ridge, a small store and a gravel lay-by have served as a meeting point for riders for a long time, and on a Saturday morning there are always a few bikes parked in the lot, with their riders arguing amiably about which loop to take. It is not a place to spend an afternoon, but it is a good place to be at nine o'clock.

  3. 3. Suches

    Locals call Suches the valley above the clouds, which is both accurate and a good piece of marketing. It is a small high basin in the Blue Ridge, ringed by mountains and cool in summer, with a hamlet at its centre, a school, a few shops and not much else. It sits at an elevation that makes it one of the few places in Georgia where snow regularly lies, and in winter the surrounding roads can be icy long after the lowlands have thawed.

  4. 4. Wolf Pen Gap

    Wolf Pen Gap is a notch in the Blue Ridge of north Georgia, and the road that goes through it is a favourite of southern motorcyclists, with good reason. Georgia 348, the Richard B. Russell Scenic Highway, climbs out of the valley of the Chattahoochee watershed in a tight series of bends, shaded by hardwoods and rhododendron, then drops down the other side toward Suches. At around 900 metres, the gap is not high by western standards, but the road feels a lot more serious than the number, because the curves come quickly, one after another, with little straight in between.

  5. 5. Blairsville

    Blairsville is a small mountain town, the seat of Union County, and it sits in a bowl of the north Georgia hills at about 570 meters, in country that Georgians think of as the high mountains. The town square has an old courthouse and a few shops, and the surrounding roads lead to some of the best scenery in the state. In October the town holds the Sorghum Festival, a local tradition with syrup-making, music and crafts that draws crowds from across the region.
