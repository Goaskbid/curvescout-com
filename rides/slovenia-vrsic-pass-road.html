Why this ride

A 61 km one-way ride from near Kranjska Gora, about 1 h 02 of riding. The stops in between: Vrsic Pass and Trenta. The highest point is about 1,600 m. Vrsic Pass is subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Slovenia Vrsic Pass Road

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 1611 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance — cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Rider note

Check the pass is open before you commit to it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kranjska Gora

    Kranjska Gora is the last real town in the Upper Sava valley, a ski resort in winter and a base for hikers and cyclists in summer, tucked so close to the Italian and Austrian frontiers that you can see both countries on a clear afternoon. It is a small place, about fourteen hundred residents, with pastel chalets, a main street of cafes and shops, and the Julian Alps going straight up behind it. The tall wall of Triglav's neighbours dominates the sky, and the fields around the village are neat, green and full of hay racks.

  2. 2. Vrsic Pass

    Vršič is the highest road pass in Slovenia, at 1,611 metres, and it comes with fifty hairpins, each numbered with a little sign. Twenty-four of them are on the north side, from Kranjska Gora, and twenty-six on the south side toward Trenta, and some are still cobbled, which is memorable on a motorcycle in the wet. The road crosses the Julian Alps through a landscape of grey limestone towers, scree slopes and larch woods, and you feel the weight of the peaks pressing in from every side.

  3. 3. Trenta

    Trenta is the upper valley of the Soča, the river that runs emerald green out of the Julian Alps, and arriving here from the Vršič hairpins feels like being let out into a quiet room. The mountains rise on both sides, the road runs gently beside the water, and the villages are small clusters of stone and wooden houses scattered among meadows and larch woods. It lies inside Triglav National Park, Slovenia's only national park, and the whole area has the air of a protected landscape: clean, calm and generously green.

  4. 4. Bovec

    Bovec sits at the head of the Soča valley, in a wide bowl of green meadows ringed by limestone walls that look as if they were stacked there deliberately. Kanin rises to the south, the high Julian Alps close in to the east, and the river that gave the valley its name runs cold and unbelievably turquoise just down the road. It is a small town of around sixteen hundred people, but in summer its population of kayakers, rafters, paragliders and riders makes it feel much larger.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Kranjska Gora to Vrsic Pass

Kranjska Gora → Vrsic Pass climbs about 1611 m in 8 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Vrsic Pass to Trenta

Vrsic Pass → Trenta drops about 1611 m over 8 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Trenta to Bovec

Trenta → Bovec is 20 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
