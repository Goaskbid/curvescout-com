Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fort «Constantin»

    Fort Constantine sits off the western end of Kotlin Island, out in the Gulf of Finland, and the sea is the whole point of it. Kotlin is a long, thin strip of land with the island city of Kronstadt at its heart, built by Peter the Great as the base of the Baltic fleet and the shield for St Petersburg. The forts around it were built on shoals and artificial islands to close the channels, which means several of them can only be seen from a distance, or from the water.

  2. 2. Northern battery No. 7

    Not every stop on a route has a story that belongs to one fort, and this one belongs to a coastline. The north shore of the Gulf of Finland, between Sestroretsk and Lisy Nos, is a flat, sandy and pine-covered strip of land that was once Finnish territory, and the batteries and forts built on it, including the one the label calls Fort Constantin, were part of the same defensive system that guarded Kronstadt from the other side. The names Gorskaya and Tarkhovka belong to the settlements around, and the Dam, the huge flood barrier that links the island with the mainland, lands at this shore.

  3. 3. The Steinbok-Fermor's Estate

    On the north shore of the Neva Bay, at the edge of the modern city, lies Lakhta, a district that was a village of Finnish and Ingrian farmers when Peter the Great first built his capital. By the nineteenth century the waterfront was dotted with country estates for the St Petersburg aristocracy, and the Steinbock-Fermor family held one of them, a park of old trees and lawns with a manor house by the Gulf of Finland. The estate park, shaded and quiet, is the kind of place where the city's dacha culture began.
