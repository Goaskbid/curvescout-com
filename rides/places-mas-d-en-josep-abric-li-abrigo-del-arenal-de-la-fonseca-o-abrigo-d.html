Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. mas d'en Josep- Abric lI

    Rock art is easy to walk past. The paintings in the Maestrat shelters are small, usually a hand's breadth to a forearm's length, painted in a red that has faded to the colour of dried blood or brick, and often covered by a film of mineral crust or a scatter of lichen. The trick is to stop, let your eyes adjust, and look slowly, not for pictures but for shapes: a line that is too regular for a crack, a blob that has a head and legs, a slender figure with a bow. Raking light helps, so mid-morning or late afternoon is better than noon, and a torch held at a low angle can reveal what the sun hides. At Mas d'en Josep, Abric II, about six kilometres from Albocàsser, that patience is the whole game.

  2. 2. Villaroges

    Villaroges is one of those names that appears on a map of prehistoric sites rather than a road atlas, somewhere in the high country of the Alt Maestrat between Benassal, Culla and Vilafranca. It belongs to the extraordinary concentration of rock art in the mountains of Castellón, where small painted shelters are scattered through the ravines, protected as part of the UNESCO-listed rock art of the Mediterranean Basin. Exactly where the panel lies, and whether it can be visited, is a question for the local tourist office or the museum at Tírig; treat the name as an invitation into one of the emptiest and most beautiful corners of eastern Spain.

  3. 3. Barranco de Gibert II

    Up on the roof of the Maestrazgo, where Teruel and Castellón meet, the land rises above fourteen hundred metres into a bleak, beautiful plateau of pasture, pine and limestone. Mosqueruela, on the Aragonese side, is one of the highest villages in the area, and Vilafranca, on the Valencian side, sits in a valley below. The Barranco de Gibert cuts into the hills between them, close also to La Iglesuela del Cid, and its second shelter holds prehistoric paintings.

  4. 4. Abrigo del Arenal de la fonseca ó Abrigo d

    The Guadalope river cuts a deep canyon through the limestone of the Maestrazgo in eastern Teruel, and the Abrigo del Arenal de la Fonseca lies in this wild country near the hamlet of Ladruñán, between the villages of Castellote and Cantavieja. It is one of the important rock-art shelters of Aragon, part of the UNESCO listing of Mediterranean prehistoric art, with Levantine paintings of animals and human figures in red pigment on the rock. The setting is spectacular: a narrow gorge with towering cliffs, the river flowing below, and pine and holm oak clinging to the slopes. Access involves a walk, and the paintings are protected by a fence.
