Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Islamabad

    Seen as a base for day rides rather than a destination, Islamabad has two historic targets within reach, and they pull in opposite directions. West, about thirty-five kilometres away, lies Taxila, the ruins of a centre of Gandhara culture where Buddhism and Greek-influenced art met around two thousand years ago. The stupas at Dharmarajika and the monastery at Jaulian stand among dry hills, and the museum holds remarkable sculptures of the Buddha with a face that looks almost Mediterranean. South-east, along the Grand Trunk Road past Jhelum, stands Rohtas Fort, a vast sixteenth-century fortress built by the Afghan ruler Sher Shah Suri to guard against the Mughals, whose huge walls and gates follow the ridge above a river.

  2. 2. Giri complex of monuments

    The valley around Taxila is one of those places where the past is stacked in layers, and you can feel it as soon as you leave the road. Just north-west of Islamabad and Rawalpindi, in the foothills of the Margalla Hills, the broad plain was for more than a thousand years a crossroads for traders, armies, monks and scholars moving between Central Asia, India and the Persian world. UNESCO listed the ruins in 1980, and the Giri complex belongs to this wider landscape.

  3. 3. Giri Mosque and tombs

    Not everything in the Taxila valley is Buddhist, and the Giri mosque and tombs are a reminder of a later chapter. The Islamic centuries left their mark here too, in small mosques, tombs and shrines on the plain, built after the Muslim dynasties arrived from the north-west and the old monasteries had fallen into ruin. UNESCO's Taxila listing, from 1980, covers a long run of history, and the Islamic period is part of it.

  4. 4. Khanpur Cave

    Khanpur Cave sits in the hills north-west of Islamabad, close to the Khanpur Dam and the green reservoir that spreads below the town of Khanpur, and it is one of the older places on this ride. Archaeological work in the cave has found evidence of prehistoric occupation, with people sheltering here long before any of the cities and monasteries in the valley were built. It is a reminder that the Taxila region was inhabited for a very long time before it became famous.
