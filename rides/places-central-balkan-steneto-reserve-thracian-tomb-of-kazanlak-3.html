Why this ride

A 364 km round trip from Karlovo - roughly 9 h 10 in the saddle. It goes by way of Central Balkan - Peesh skali Reserve, Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak and back through Central Balkan - Peesh skali Reserve. It tops out around 900 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Central Balkan - Peesh skali Reserve to Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak

Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak is 93 km from Central Balkan - Peesh skali Reserve, mostly on road 5004. Up about 840 m to 1,074 m, then 990 m back down the far side. Take a break before the climb, not halfway up.

The stops

  1. 1. Central Balkan - Steneto Reserve

    Start here.

    Steneto is a deep, wild valley on the northern slopes of the Balkan range in central Bulgaria, enclosed by cliffs and covered in ancient beech forest, and it is one of the strict reserves of Central Balkan National Park, part of the Primeval Beech Forests World Heritage listing. Access is restricted, and nature is left to itself: old trees fall and rot, and the forest is home to bears, wolves, wild boar and a rich variety of birds, including several species of woodpecker and owl. The Cherni Osam river flows through the valley, and its clear waters are home to trout.

    Read the complete Central Balkan - Steneto Reserve write-up.

  2. 2. Central Balkan - Peesh skali Reserve

    89 km in. The ride passes about 6 km from it.

    Peesh skali means singing rocks, after the way the wind howls and whistles through the cliffs and crags of this reserve on the slopes of the central Balkan range. It lies on the northern side of the main ridge, not far from Botev, at 2,376 metres the highest peak of the Balkan mountains, and it protects beech and spruce forests, alpine meadows and rocky slopes, part of Central Balkan National Park. Chamois live on the crags, and bears, wolves and golden eagles roam the area. Access to the reserve is restricted, but trails in the surrounding park offer spectacular walks.

    Read the complete Central Balkan - Peesh skali Reserve write-up.

  3. 3. Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak

    181 km in.

    In 1944 soldiers digging a shelter on the edge of Kazanlak broke into a small brick chamber and found themselves looking at some of the finest ancient paintings in the Balkans. The Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak, built in the fourth century BC for a ruler of the Thracians, has a narrow corridor leading to a round, beehive-shaped burial chamber, its dome covered with frescoes of a funeral feast: the dead man and his wife seated at a table, servants bringing gifts, horses and chariots racing around the top. The colours and the tenderness of the figures are extraordinary. It was listed by UNESCO in 1979.
