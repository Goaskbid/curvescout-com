Why this ride

318 km from Annan to Annan, about 4 h 41 of riding. It goes by way of Willowford Roman temporary camp, The English Lake District and back through Willowford Roman temporary camp. It strings together lakeside roads.

The roads

Willowford Roman temporary camp → The English Lake District

The 99 km between Willowford Roman temporary camp and The English Lake District, mostly on M6. You lose around 310 m along the way; the top is 255 m. Water alongside for much of it - pick one good pull-out and stop.

The stops

  1. 1. Herd Hill North

    Start here.

    Bowness-on-Solway is the western end of Hadrian's Wall, a quiet village on a low headland above the estuary, and Herd Hill lies a little farther along the shore on the Cardurnock peninsula. Roman engineers built the wall to Bowness, where the fort of Maia stood, and then continued the defences south along the Cumbrian coast with milefortlets and towers. The northern watchtower on this hill was one of the chain that watched across the water to Scotland, at a place where the Solway is narrow enough to be forded at low tide.

    Read the complete Herd Hill North write-up.

  2. 2. Willowford Roman temporary camp

    60 km in.

    Rivers move, and the Irthing at Willowford proves it. When the Romans built the Wall here, they carried it across the river on a bridge, and the massive stone abutment of that bridge survives on the east bank, now well away from the water, because the river has shifted west over the centuries. Nearby, the earthwork outline of a Roman temporary camp, one of many marching camps built by units on campaign or on construction duty, lies in the fields. Temporary camps were ramparts of turf and ditches thrown up for a few nights or weeks, and their faint shapes are often best seen from the air or in low winter light.

    Read the complete Willowford Roman temporary camp write-up.

  3. 3. The English Lake District

    159 km in.

    Wordsworth wandered lonely as a cloud round here, and anybody who has ridden the Lake District on a wet bank holiday will understand the appeal of being lonely. The English Lake District, listed by UNESCO in 2017 as a cultural landscape, is a compact knot of mountains, lakes and valleys in Cumbria, shaped by glaciers and then by centuries of sheep farming, slate quarrying and, from the late eighteenth century, by poets, painters and tourists who came looking for the sublime.
