Why this ride

A 358 km round trip from Alquézar - roughly 6 h 10 in the saddle. The stops in between: Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña, Abrigo de Los Gitanos, Cueva de los moros. and Hayedos de Navarra - Aztaparreta. The highest point is about 1,450 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cueva de Regacens

    Alquézar takes its name from the Arabic for the fortress, al-qasr, and the name fits: a hill of honey-coloured rock above the Vero, crowned by a castle and a church, with a tangle of arcaded lanes tumbling down below. The Cueva de Regacens lies about eight hundred metres from the village, a shelter in the limestone that carries painting in the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain. It is one of the closest sites to a village of any on these rides, which makes it an easy and pleasant walk, though the paintings are small and faded.

  2. 2. Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña

    Just north of Huesca, where the flat cereal land of the Hoya suddenly rears up into the first limestone walls of the Pre-Pyrenees, lies the little village of Nueno and, above it, the rock shelter named after Santa Eulalia de la Peña. This is the southern edge of the Sierra de Guara country, a landscape of gorges, conglomerate cliffs and scrub where griffon vultures ride the thermals and the light comes hard off pale stone.

  3. 3. Abrigo de Los Gitanos

    The road north-west out of Huesca toward Pamplona runs along the foot of a dramatic wall of rock. To the north are the Pre-Pyrenean sierras, and studded along them are some of Aragón's most striking sights: the Castle of Loarre, a superbly preserved Romanesque fortress on a spur, and the Mallos de Riglos, huge red conglomerate towers rising straight out of the hillside. The rock shelter known as the Abrigo de los Gitanos lies in this country, between Ayerbe, Loarre and Luesia.

  4. 4. Cueva de los moros.

    There is a Cueva de los Moros in half the valleys of Spain. Every unexplained cave, ruin or rock shelter used to be credited to the Moors, and this one, in the hills of the western Aragonese Pyrenees between the valleys of Hecho and Ansó, is a rock shelter with ancient traces. You should approach it as an excuse to spend time in two of the most beautiful and least spoiled valleys of the Pyrenees, rather than as a monument.

  5. 5. Hayedos de Navarra - Aztaparreta

    Aztaparreta is a beech forest that has been left alone. High in the Pyrenees of Navarre, at the head of the Roncal valley, in the karst massif of Larra-Belagua on the border with France, this forest has never been logged, and its trees, some of them centuries old, grow, die and decay naturally. It is one of the components of the Ancient and Primeval Beech Forests of the Carpathians and Other Regions of Europe, a World Heritage Site that brings together beech forests from across the continent, from the Carpathians to the Pyrenees. Walking through it is like entering a cathedral, with tall grey trunks, a green canopy high above, and a thick carpet of moss and fallen leaves.
