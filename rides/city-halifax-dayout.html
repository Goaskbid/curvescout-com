Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Halifax

    Halifax sits on one of the great natural harbours of the world, and most of its history is a story of that fact. The British founded the city in 1749 as a naval base to counter the French fortress at Louisbourg, and the star-shaped Citadel on the hill above the harbour is its monument, with a view across the water that explains the choice at once. Today it is the largest city in Atlantic Canada, with about 360,000 people in the municipality, and Dartmouth across the water adds another hundred thousand. A ferry has linked the two shores for more than two centuries, and a ride across costs very little and gives the best view of the skyline.

  2. 2. Old Town Lunenburg

    Colour is the first thing that hits you in Lunenburg: wooden houses in mustard, barn red, teal and white, climbing the slope from the harbour in tidy rows. The town sits on Nova Scotia's South Shore, on a peninsula facing the Atlantic, and it is one of the best-preserved examples of a planned British colonial town in North America. It was founded in 1753, and the grid of streets laid out then is still the grid you walk now. The first settlers were largely Protestants from German-speaking lands, along with some Swiss and French, which is why you still hear names like Zwicker and Rhuland.

  4. 4. Landscape of Grand Pré

    The Bay of Fundy has the highest tides in the world, and at Grand Pré the sea comes in and out of the Minas Basin with a rush that can move the waterline across mudflats the size of a small town. Behind that shifting edge lies a broad, flat, green meadow that was not always land. The Acadians, French settlers who arrived in the seventeenth century, built earthen dykes with wooden sluice gates called aboiteaux to keep the tide out and turn salt marsh into rich farmland. The work took generations, and the dykes still protect fields today.
