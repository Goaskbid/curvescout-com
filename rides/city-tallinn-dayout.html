Why this ride

A 231 km round trip from Tallinn - roughly 3 h 59 in the saddle. The stops in between: Fortress of Suomenlinna, Finlandia Hall, Social Insurance Institution Main Office and back through Finlandia Hall. It crosses from Estonia into Finland - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Tallinn

    Tallinn is a medieval city that kept its walls, and the thick stone towers and steep red roofs of its old town make it among the best-preserved in northern Europe. It grew rich as a Hanseatic port, trading with Novgorod and Lübeck, and the merchants' houses and guildhalls built in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries still line the cobbled lanes. Above it all stands Toompea, the hill of the castle and the parliament, with the onion domes of the Alexander Nevsky Cathedral, built in the 1890s when Estonia was part of the Russian Empire, still dominating the view.

  2. 2. Fortress of Suomenlinna

    Suomenlinna is a fortress on a cluster of islands in front of Helsinki, reached in about fifteen minutes by public ferry from the Market Square. The Swedes began building it in 1748, when Finland was part of their kingdom and the Russian threat was growing, and they called it Sveaborg, the Fortress of the Swedes. The architect Augustin Ehrensvard designed the plan, and he is buried on the island. Granite ramparts, bastions, tunnels and storehouses are laid out among rocky islets, and a wide-angled view of the fortress from the sea is impressive.

  4. 4. Finlandia Hall

    Finlandia Hall stands on the shore of Töölö Bay in the middle of Helsinki, a long white building by Alvar Aalto that opened in 1971. Aalto was Finland's greatest architect, and the hall is typical of his late work, a sculpted block with a sloping roof and a facade of white marble that has a surprisingly soft quality in the Nordic light. The marble has had a hard time with Helsinki's climate, because the thin slabs bowed in the cold and had to be replaced in a restoration. The hall is a concert and congress centre, and it was here in 1975 that European leaders, together with the United States, Canada and the Soviet Union, signed the Helsinki Final Act, a milestone in Cold War diplomacy.
