  1. 1. Lima

    Lima sits on the edge of one of the driest deserts on earth, and yet it is a city of more than seven million people, held together by a grey sky that locals call la garúa, a damp, low cloud that hangs over the coast for much of the year and rarely produces rain. The city's coastline rises in cliffs above the Pacific, where the districts of Miraflores and Barranco look out to the surf, and parks, cafes and paragliders occupy the clifftops.

  2. 2. Canta Peru

    Canta is a small Andean town in the mountains northeast of Lima, a place where the coastal desert gives way to high, cold valleys and the sky turns a deep, thin blue. The town sits at about 2,800 metres on the banks of a river that tumbles down from the cordillera, with terraced fields climbing the hillsides and a plaza where life revolves around the church and the market. It is only a few hours from the Peruvian capital, but the atmosphere is completely different, with a slower pace, cooler air and a strong sense of rural Andean life.

  3. 3. Abra La Viuda

    Abra La Viuda is a high mountain pass in the Western Cordillera of the Peruvian Andes, and its name, the Widow's Pass, carries a sombre note that suits the scenery. The ground here is a pale, windswept grassland of ichu, the tough bunchgrass of the high Andes, with small lakes tucked in hollows and the glaciated peaks of the cordillera rising nearby. At this height, well above the tree line and above four thousand metres, the air is thin and cold, and clouds boil up over the ridges with the afternoon heat.
