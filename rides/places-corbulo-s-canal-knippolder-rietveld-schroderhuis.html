  1. 1. Corbulo's canal | Knippolder

    In AD 47, a Roman general on a quiet frontier had a problem, which was how to keep his legions from getting restless. Gnaeus Domitius Corbulo, newly appointed to command in Lower Germany, solved it, according to the historian Tacitus, by making his troops dig. The result was a canal, the Fossa Corbulonis, linking the mouth of the Rhine with the Maas, so that ships could pass between the two rivers on a sheltered inland route rather than risk the open North Sea. It is one of the earliest known large engineering works of its kind in the Netherlands.

  2. 2. Van Nellefabriek

    The Van Nelle factory, on the edge of Rotterdam, is one of the most beautiful industrial buildings ever made. Built between 1925 and 1931 for a company that processed coffee, tea and tobacco, it is a long, light building of glass and steel, with curtain walls that let daylight flood into the workspaces. The designers, Johannes Brinkman and Leendert van der Vlugt, created what became an icon of modernist architecture, a factory of light and air for its workers. It was added to the UNESCO list in 2014.

  3. 3. Woerden-Centrum

    Woerden is a small Dutch town on the Oude Rijn, the old Rhine, halfway between Utrecht and Leiden, in the middle of the Green Heart of Holland, a landscape of polders, meadows, ditches and cows. Its centre is a compact old town ringed by canals, with a castle, a big church and a windmill on the old ramparts. The Romans were here too: Woerden lies on the line of the Lower German Limes, and the remains of a Roman fort lie beneath the town centre.

  4. 4. Rietveld Schröderhuis

    On a quiet residential street in Utrecht, a few minutes from the centre by bicycle, stands a small box-shaped house made of white and grey planes, with black frames and touches of red, yellow and blue, which looks as though it had been made from a Mondrian painting. This is the Rietveld Schroder House, built in 1924 and inscribed by UNESCO in 2000 as a masterpiece of the De Stijl movement and of modern architecture. The old centre of Utrecht, with its cathedral tower and its canals, is under two kilometres away.
