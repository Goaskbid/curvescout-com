Why this ride

416 km one way from Yoshii - plan on about 7 h 34 of riding. The stops in between: Kashima-shi, near Kashima-shi and Chōfu. The highest point is about 800 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Photo stop near Yoshii

    One of the oldest written stones in Japan stands near Yoshii. The Tago stele, carved in the early eighth century to commemorate the creation of a new district, is part of a group of three ancient monuments in the area that were added to UNESCO's Memory of the World register in 2017. It is a modest stone under a protective roof, but its age and significance give pause in a landscape of fields and low hills.

  2. 2. Kashima-shi

    Kashima's great shrine claims to be one of the oldest in Japan, and its deity, Takemikazuchi, is a god of thunder and swords. A stone in the shrine forest, the kaname-ishi, is said to pin down the head of a giant catfish beneath the earth whose thrashing causes earthquakes. Only a small rounded top shows above ground; legend says that no amount of digging has ever reached its bottom. The forest around the shrine, with tall cedars, deer and a spring-fed pond, is a cool, dark refuge on a summer day.

  4. 4. Near Chōfu

    Studio Ghibli's Pom Poko tells the story of raccoon dogs fighting the bulldozers that turned the Tama hills into a new town in the 1960s and 1970s, and this finish lies right in that landscape, between Inagi, Chōfu and Tama. The hills are now covered with apartment blocks, roads and shopping centres, but pockets of woodland and farmland survive, and the Tama River runs through the valley below.

  5. 5. Back to Boso

    The Tokyo Bay ferry is the secret of this point. It carries motorcycles, and it is the pleasant way to link the two peninsulas, since the alternative is a long drive around the head of the bay or the tunnel and bridge of the Aqua-Line.
