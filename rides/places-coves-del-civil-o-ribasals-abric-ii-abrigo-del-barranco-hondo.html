Why this ride

A 248 km round trip from Albocàsser - roughly 4 h 17 in the saddle. On the way it passes La fenellosa, Abrigo del Barranco Hondo and back through La fenellosa. The highest point is about 1,200 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Coves del Civil o Ribasals- Abric II

    Think of the Coves del Civil, also called Ribasals, as a series of upturned bowls in a limestone wall. Abric II is the second shelter in the sequence, a little under six kilometres from the village of Albocàsser, tucked into a rocky ravine of the Maestrat. The wall above is pale and weathered, and the shelter faces east, so that it gets the early light. Its paintings are part of the Levantine art of the Spanish Mediterranean, protected by UNESCO since 1998, and are a short drive from the great Valltorta gorge.

  2. 2. La fenellosa

    People in Aragón like to call the Matarraña their Tuscany, and for once the comparison is not entirely marketing. It is a comarca of honey-coloured stone villages on hilltops, olive groves on terraces, almond trees and vineyards, with the limestone wall of the Ports de Beseit rising behind. La Fenellosa lies in this country, between Valderrobres, Fuentespalda and Mas de Barberans across the Catalan line, in the rough hills where the farmland gives way to the mountains.

  3. 3. Abrigo del Barranco Hondo

    Castellote is one of those villages in the Teruel Maestrazgo that seems to have been built by people who wanted to be left alone. Its houses pile up beneath a great rock wall topped by the ruins of a castle associated with the Knights Templar, and the Guadalope river winds below on its way into the Santolea reservoir. The Abrigo del Barranco Hondo, a painted rock shelter in a deep ravine outside the village, is part of the extensive collection of Levantine rock art scattered through these mountains, with small figures in red ochre that have survived on the stone for millennia.
