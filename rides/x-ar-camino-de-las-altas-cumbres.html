  1. 1. Cordoba Argentina

    Córdoba is Argentina's second city and its oldest university town, a sprawling, energetic place of about 1.4 million people on the edge of the Pampas, where the flat plains meet the first hills of the Sierras. It was founded in 1573 by Spanish settlers, and its colonial heart is among the best preserved in the country. The Jesuit Block, a group of churches, university buildings and a school that dates from the seventeenth century, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the National University of Córdoba, founded in 1613, is one of the oldest in the Americas. The city is nicknamed La Docta, the learned, and the large student population gives it a lively, youthful feel.

  2. 2. Mina Clavero

    Mina Clavero is a small river town on the western side of the Sierras de Córdoba, in the long, dry Traslasierra valley, and it is where the Camino de las Altas Cumbres comes to rest after crossing the mountains. You arrive from the east after a climb through grassland and rock that tops out above two thousand metres, then a descent in a long run of bends that opens suddenly onto a view of the whole valley, flat and golden, with the Sierra de Comechingones low and blue beyond it.
