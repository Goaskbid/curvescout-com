Why this ride

A 204 km round trip from Tulcán - roughly 3 h 31 in the saddle. It goes by way of Segment Mariscal Sucre - El Tambo, Segment Loma Virgen - Chiquito, Segment Pimán - Caranqui and back through Segment Loma Virgen - Chiquito. The highest point is about 3,450 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Segment Pulcas - Troya B

    Close to the Colombian border, at almost three thousand metres, the páramo above Tulcán is a cold, windy country of tussock grass and cloud. Tulcán, a town of over eighty thousand people, is the capital of Carchi province, and it sits at the very top of the northern Andes of Ecuador, within sight of the volcanoes that straddle the frontier. The air here is thin and cool, even at midday, and the rain is never far away.

  2. 2. Segment Mariscal Sucre - El Tambo

    Up in Carchi, Ecuador's northernmost province, a short stretch of old stone-edged road near San Gabriel is all that is left visible of something enormous. The Qhapaq Ñan, the Inca road system, ran from what is now southern Colombia to central Chile and Argentina, tens of thousands of kilometres of paths, stairways and causeways that tied the empire together. The segment between Mariscal Sucre and El Tambo is one of the pieces listed by UNESCO in 2014 as part of that network. A tambo was a waystation, a place where runners and travellers could rest and resupply, so the name itself tells you what stood here.

  3. 3. Segment Loma Virgen - Chiquito

    High in the Andes of northern Ecuador, between El Ángel and Pimampiro in the province of Carchi, a stretch of the Qhapaq Ñan, the great road network of the Inca Empire, crosses the páramo, the high grassland that blankets the mountains above the tree line. The Inca built more than thirty thousand kilometres of roads across their empire, from Colombia to Chile and Argentina, linking towns, temples and storehouses, and this northern section, near the empire's frontier, was among the last to be built before the Spanish conquest. The Qhapaq Ñan was inscribed on the UNESCO list in 2014 as a transnational site shared by six countries, with hundreds of individual segments, including this one.

  4. 4. Segment Pimán - Caranqui

    Ibarra sits in a high basin in northern Ecuador at around 2,200 metres, beneath the green slopes of Imbabura volcano, and the Inca road ran right through this valley. The Qhapaq Ñan, the Andean road system, stretched over 30,000 kilometres from Colombia to Chile and Argentina, linking the Inca empire, and it was inscribed on the UNESCO list in 2014. The segment between Pimán and Caranqui is one of the preserved sections in Ecuador, a stretch of old path through farmland and hills, with dry-stone edging in places and the kind of steady, sensible gradient Inca engineers favoured for runners and llama trains. Caranqui, now a suburb of Ibarra, was an important Inca centre, and local tradition claims it as the birthplace of Atahualpa, the last Inca emperor.
