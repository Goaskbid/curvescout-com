Why this ride

A 240 km round trip from George Town - roughly 4 h 08 in the saddle. On the way it passes Bukit Kepala Gajah, Bukit Gua Harimau, Bukit Jawa and Archaeological Heritage of the Lenggong Va.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Penang

    Penang is an island of the Strait of Malacca that behaves like a small country, with its own food, language habits and pride. The British East India Company's Francis Light took possession of it in 1786 and founded George Town as a free port, and the city quickly filled with Chinese, Indian, Malay, Arab, Armenian and European traders. The grid of narrow streets with shophouses, clan houses, temples, churches and mosques, many within a few steps of one another, earned UNESCO listing together with Melaka in 2008.

  2. 2. Bukit Kepala Gajah

    Bukit Kepala Gajah, Elephant Head Hill, is one of the limestone outcrops in the Lenggong Valley of Perak, a landscape where the Malay Peninsula's earliest people left their tools in the soil. The valley lies along the Perak river in the north of the state, among steep green karst hills, rubber plantations and small villages, and UNESCO inscribed it in 2012 as a site of archaeological heritage, giving it a reputation as one of the oldest in Southeast Asia.

  3. 3. Bukit Gua Harimau

    Gua Harimau means Tiger Cave, and the name hints at the stories that surround this limestone hill in the Lenggong Valley. The cave was excavated by archaeologists in the first decade of this century, and what they found was remarkable: a large number of human burials from the Neolithic and later periods, buried with pottery, tools and ornaments, which helped show that people lived and buried their dead here over thousands of years.

  4. 4. Bukit Jawa

    Bukit Jawa is one of the smaller hills of the Lenggong Valley, and like its neighbours it carries traces of very old human presence under tropical growth. The valley sits in northern Perak where the river Perak winds through a plain framed by limestone ridges, and the archaeology here suggests that people came to this region repeatedly, drawn by the river, the caves and the stone suitable for tools.
