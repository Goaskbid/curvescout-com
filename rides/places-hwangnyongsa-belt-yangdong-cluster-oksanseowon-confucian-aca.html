Why this ride

From Gyeongju it runs 151 km to Gyeongju; allow about 2 h 36 of riding. On the way it passes Oksan-seowon, Mt. Namsan Belt, Yangdong Cluster:Oksanseowon Confucian Aca and back through Mt. Namsan Belt.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hwangnyongsa Belt

    Gyeongju was the capital of the Silla kingdom for nearly a thousand years, and it is often called a museum without walls, because the tombs, temple sites and palace ruins lie scattered through a modern provincial city. Hwangnyongsa was once the greatest temple in the kingdom, begun in 553, and its nine-storey wooden pagoda, built in the seventh century after the monk Jajang came back from China, was one of the tallest wooden buildings in East Asia. The temple was burned down when the Mongols invaded in 1238, and what is left is a wide field of foundation stones, where the outline of the great hall and the pagoda base can still be made out.

  2. 2. Oksan-seowon

    Oksan-seowon sits in a quiet valley northwest of Gyeongju, beside a clear stream that tumbles over smooth granite slabs. It was founded in 1572 to honour Yi Eon-jeok, one of the great Neo-Confucian scholars of the Joseon dynasty, who lived in retreat nearby, and it served as a private academy where young men studied the Confucian classics, prepared for the state examinations, and performed rites in honour of the master. The buildings are arranged according to the strict logic of Confucian architecture: a lecture hall, dormitories for students, a library and, at the back, a shrine for the rites. The architecture is restrained, with wooden halls, tiled roofs and open courtyards, designed to harmonise with the landscape. Oksan is part of the UNESCO listing of the Seowon, Korean Neo-Confucian Academies, inscribed in 2019.

  3. 3. Mt. Namsan Belt

    Namsan, the southern mountain of Gyeongju, is a modest granite ridge rising just under five hundred metres above the old capital of the Silla kingdom, and it is crammed with more Buddhist art than almost any mountain in Korea. For centuries the monks and craftsmen of Silla, which unified much of the Korean peninsula in the seventh century and ruled from Gyeongju until the tenth, carved Buddhas into its boulders, built stone pagodas on its ridges and placed temples in its valleys. UNESCO took in the whole mountain when it listed Gyeongju's historic areas, and walking its trails is like moving through an open-air museum among the pines.

  4. 4. Yangdong Cluster:Oksanseowon Confucian Aca

    In the green hills east of Gyeongju, where the old Silla capital gives way to the rice fields and pine woods that lead toward the East Sea, the Joseon dynasty left a different kind of monument. Oksanseowon is a Confucian academy, a seowon, built in the sixteenth century to honour the scholar Yi Eon-jeok, known by his pen name Hoejae, who lived from 1491 to 1553. It belongs to the Yangdong cluster, part of the Historic Villages of Korea inscribed by UNESCO in 2010 together with Hahoe, and it shows how the Neo-Confucian elite wanted to live, study and be remembered.
