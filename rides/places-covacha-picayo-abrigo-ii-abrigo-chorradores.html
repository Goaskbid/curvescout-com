Why this ride

A 318 km round trip from Puçol - roughly 5 h 29 in the saddle. It goes by way of Barranc de la falaguera- Abric I, Abrigo de las Cañas II, Barranc de la falaguera- Abric II and Abrigo Chorradores. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Covacha Picayo- abrigo II

    The Sierra Calderona is the wooded ridge behind Valencia's northern suburbs, and it is the best surprise on this coast. It is a natural park of pine, holm oak and rosemary, with low summits, deep ravines and a sense of space that is hard to believe so close to a large city. Wild boar wander the slopes, along with foxes, genets and a growing population of birds of prey, and in spring the slopes are full of orchids and cistus flowers.

  2. 2. Barranc de la falaguera- Abric I

    Alginet sits in the Ribera Alta, the flat, fertile plain south of Valencia where the Júcar river spreads out among orange groves, rice fields and small towns. To the west, the plain meets the first hills of the interior, low ridges of limestone covered in pine and scrub. The Barranc de la Falaguera, the ravine of the fern, cuts into these hills near Alginet, Alfarp and Benifaió.

  3. 3. Abrigo de las Cañas II

    The Macizo del Caroig is one of the emptiest corners of the Valencian region, a high, rugged limestone massif cut by deep gorges, covered with pine, scrub and rosemary, and dotted with a few small villages. Bicorp, Millares and Quesa lie on its edges, and in the ravines around them are dozens of rock shelters with prehistoric paintings, part of the UNESCO-listed rock art of the Mediterranean Basin. The Abrigo de las Cañas is one of them, and the area as a whole is among the richest in Spain for this kind of art.

  4. 4. Barranc de la falaguera- Abric II

    South of Valencia the land flattens into the Ribera, a plain of orange and persimmon orchards cut by irrigation ditches, and then, quite suddenly, it crumples into low hills. Alginet and Alfarp sit on this edge, with the Albufera lagoon and its rice fields a little to the east. The Barranc de la Falaguera is a dry ravine in the dusty foothills, and Abric II is a small rock shelter recorded with a group of others nearby. These belong to the Mediterranean-arc rock art that UNESCO listed in 1998, and the Valencian interior holds a remarkable number of them.

  5. 5. Abrigo Chorradores

    Chorro is the Spanish word for a jet or spout of water, and a chorrador is a place where water spouts, so the name of this shelter suggests a spring or a waterfall that once poured from the rock nearby. It would have been a precious thing in this dry country, and probably explains why the painters chose the spot. The Abrigo Chorradores lies about two kilometres from Millares, a village of fewer than six hundred people in the rugged hills of inland Valencia, and carries painting in the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain. The ride links it with a site called Barranco del Cabezo del Moro.
