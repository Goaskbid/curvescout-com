Why this ride

371 km from Bolzano to Bolzano, about 7 h 39 of riding. The stops in between: Pale di San Martino San Lucano - Dolomiti, Pelmo-Croda da Lago, The Dolomites and Dolomiti Settentrionali Cadorine. It tops out around 1,850 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

The roads

Great Dolomites Road

Pale di San Martino San Lucano - Dolomiti is 94 km from Bolzano. A proper up-and-over: 2280 m of climbing, 1,736 m at the top, 1510 m down. Top up fuel at the bottom - pumps thin out on the way up.

The stops

  1. 1. Bolzano

    Start here.

    Bolzano lives with two languages and does so with a certain comfortable pride. It lies in a wide valley at the meeting of three rivers in South Tyrol, surrounded by steep slopes of vines and apples, with the pink limestone peaks of the Dolomites rising to the east. Until 1919 it belonged to Austria, and German is still the first language of most people in the province, though Bolzano itself is mostly Italian-speaking. Signs are in both, the cooking mixes dumplings and strudel with pasta and espresso, and the city feels like a conversation between two cultures.

    Read the complete Bolzano write-up.

  2. 2. Pale di San Martino San Lucano - Dolomiti

    94 km in.

    The Val di San Lucano is a deep, shadowed gash in the southern Dolomites, and the point here lies near Rivamonte and Voltago, in the Agordino, the upper valley of the Cordevole river. The big, pale walls on either side belong to the Pale di San Martino group, one of the nine sets of mountains included in the Dolomites World Heritage listing of 2009, and their sheer rock faces rise more than a thousand metres above the narrow valley floor.

    Read the complete Pale di San Martino San Lucano - Dolomiti write-up.

  3. 3. Pelmo-Croda da Lago

    139 km in.

    Monte Pelmo is the mountain that looks like a throne. Its huge limestone mass, rising to 3,168 metres, has two summits linked by a great curved basin, and the people of the Cadore call it the Caregon del Padreterno, the armchair of God. It stands alone between the valleys of the Boite and the Fiorentina, and in 1857 it became the first major Dolomite peak to be climbed, by the Irish naturalist John Ball. Across the valley, the jagged towers of the Croda da Lago rise above the little Lago Federa, and the two together form one of the components of the Dolomites World Heritage site.

    Read the complete Pelmo-Croda da Lago write-up.

  4. 4. The Dolomites

    185 km in.

    North of Cortina d'Ampezzo, the valley narrows between walls of pale rock, and the road toward Dobbiaco threads through a landscape that looks almost too dramatic to be real. Here, between the Cristallo group to the east and the high plateaus of Fanes and Sennes to the west, you are in the heart of the Dolomites, the mountains UNESCO inscribed in 2009 for their extraordinary beauty and geology. The rock is dolomite, a magnesium-rich limestone formed from ancient coral reefs in a tropical sea some two hundred and fifty million years ago, and the way it catches the evening light, turning pink and orange, has a name of its own in the local Ladin language: enrosadira.

    Read the complete The Dolomites write-up.

  5. 5. Dolomiti Settentrionali Cadorine

    Same place as the previous stop.

    North of Cortina d'Ampezzo, the Dolomites change character. The soft meadows and pine woods of the Ampezzo basin give way to long grey screes, vertical walls and the pale towers that make the range so unmistakable, and the road toward Cimabanche runs through a valley framed by some of the most dramatic rock in Europe. This area belongs to the northern Cadore group in UNESCO's Dolomites listing of 2009, nine separate systems recognised for their landscape and their geology.

    Read the complete Dolomiti Settentrionali Cadorine write-up.
