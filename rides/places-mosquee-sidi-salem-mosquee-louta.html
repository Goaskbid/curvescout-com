Why this ride

75 km from Houmt El Souk to Houmt El Souk, about 1 h 18 of riding. The stops in between: Mosquée Medrajen, Mosquée Louta and back through Mosquée Medrajen. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mosquée Sidi Salem

    Sidi is the title given to a local holy man, and many Djerban mosques and small domed tombs carry a saint's name, with a whitewashed cube or dome standing beside a tree at the edge of a field. Sidi Salem sits in the north-west of the island, between Houmt Souk and the sea, in an area of olive groves and market gardens where the land is so flat that you can see every dome for miles. On the coast not far away, the Borj Ghazi Mustapha, a square sixteenth-century fort that the Spanish built and the Ottomans took, stands over the shallow water at the old harbour entrance.

  2. 2. Mosquée Medrajen

    The east of Djerba is the island's holiday coast, with long beaches, a line of big hotels and the busy market town of Midoun just inland, but the older island is not far behind. The Mosque of Medrajen, in the countryside between Midoun and the sea, belongs to the group of fortified Ibadi mosques that dot the interior, and it reminds visitors that the island was farmland and olive groves long before it was a resort.

  3. 3. Mosquée Louta

    In the central and southern parts of the island, among olive groves and sandy lanes, stand small whitewashed mosques of the Ibadi tradition, with thick walls, simple domes and sometimes a short minaret and an outer stair. Some were built as places of refuge, with storerooms and wells inside, to serve scattered communities in times of raids, and others as neighbourhood mosques for a cluster of farms. Together they are among the features for which the island of Djerba was added to the UNESCO list in 2023.
