Why this ride

curated scenic road. forest, canyon, monasteries. 142 km from Yerevan to Alaverdi, about 2 h 35 of riding. It goes by way of Dilijan and Vanadzor. The highest point is about 2,200 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Yerevan

    Yerevan is a pink city. The stone that built it is volcanic tuff, in shades from rose to apricot to grey, and when the evening light hits the apartment blocks and the opera house it glows as if lit from inside. It is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, founded as the fortress of Erebuni in 782 BC, and yet most of what you see was laid out in the Soviet years by the architect Alexander Tamanian, in a ring plan around a great central square. A little over a million people live here, about a third of the country, and the capital feels like the engine room of Armenia.

  2. 2. Dilijan

    Dilijan is the town the Armenians call their Little Switzerland, which is a generous description of what is really a small spa town buried in the forest. It sits in the Tavush province, in a green valley of the Lesser Caucasus, at roughly 1,400 metres, with beech and oak woods climbing the slopes all round. It is wetter and cooler than the rest of the country, which is mostly dry plateau, and the sudden greenery after the bare hills of the Armenian highlands is a shock. The air carries a smell of wet leaves and wood smoke.

  3. 3. Vanadzor

    Vanadzor is Armenia's third city, and it makes no attempt to charm. It sits in a bowl of the Lesser Caucasus, at the confluence of the Pambak and Vanadzor rivers, with mountains on all sides and the old Soviet industrial landscape of chemical plants, apartment blocks and wide avenues in the middle. The city was known as Kirovakan in Soviet times, after a Bolshevik leader, and it took on its present name in 1992. In December 1988, the great Spitak earthquake devastated the region, and Vanadzor suffered serious damage, some of which can still be seen.

  4. 4. Near Alaverdi

    Alaverdi is squeezed into the bottom of the Debed canyon, in the north of Armenia, so tightly that the houses seem to be stacked on top of each other up both sides. The river runs below, the railway and the road run beside it, and above rise steep green slopes with cliffs and monasteries. The town grew in the Soviet period around a copper smelter, which worked for decades, and the industry has left a distinctive atmosphere: apartment blocks, rusting pipes, and a feeling of a place that has seen better days but has not given up.
