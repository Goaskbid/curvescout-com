Why this ride

A 108 km loop that starts and ends near Silver City, about 1 h 45 of riding. It goes by way of Pinos Altos, Lake Roberts and Gila Cliff Dwellings. The highest point is about 2,350 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Silver City

    Silver City is the kind of place that makes people wonder why they did not find it sooner. It sits at about 5,900 feet on the edge of the Gila country in southwest New Mexico, a few miles from the Continental Divide, and its brick downtown still carries the mark of the mining boom that created it in the 1870s. A flood in the 1890s carved out the main street into a ravine, and the Big Ditch park now runs where Main Street once was, which is one of those local facts that stops visitors in the middle of the pavement.

  2. 2. Pinos Altos

    Pinos Altos means tall pines, and the little old mining town above Silver City lives up to the name. It sits at about 2,000 metres in the Gila National Forest, in a cool bowl of ponderosa pine and oak, and the main street has the unhurried, slightly crooked look of a place that has seen a gold rush and decided to stay on. The first strike was made here in 1860, drawing prospectors and a ragged frontier population, and for a while it was a hard, violent camp, fought over by miners and the Apache, on whose land they were digging.

  3. 3. Lake Roberts

    Lake Roberts is a small reservoir in a pine-and-juniper valley in the Gila National Forest, at about 2,000 metres, and it has the quiet, slightly secret air of a place that most travellers drive straight past. The lake is under a hundred acres, edged with reeds and ponderosa pine, with a campground and a small marina on one side and rounded hills on the other, and it is popular with anglers after trout and bass. Mornings are still, with mist rising off the water, and evenings bring elk and deer to the shore.

  4. 4. Gila Cliff Dwellings

    The last miles to the Gila Cliff Dwellings are the reason the Trail of the Mountain Spirits has its reputation. From Silver City the road climbs through the old mining village of Pinos Altos and then settles into a long, tight conversation with the mountains: pine, juniper, a ridge, a drop, another ridge. It is slow going, and nobody who rides it fast enjoys it. Count on a couple of hours for the last stretch, and be glad of every turnout, because the corners come in strings and the surface is the sort of patched asphalt that rewards a rider who looks well ahead.
