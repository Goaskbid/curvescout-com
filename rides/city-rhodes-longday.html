Why this ride

From Ródos it runs 279 km to Ródos; allow about 4 h 49 of riding. The stops in between: Letoon, Xanthos bis outlier, Xanthos and Xanthos-Letoon. The highest point is about 900 m. It crosses from Greece into Türkiye - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Rhodes

    Rhodes is an island that has been fought over for three thousand years, and the walls of its old town still carry the marks. In 1309 the Knights of St John, a crusading order driven from the Holy Land, took the island and spent two centuries turning its capital into one of the strongest fortresses in the Mediterranean. They built the Palace of the Grand Master, a vast castle of pale stone, and the Street of the Knights, a long cobbled lane lined with the headquarters of the order's language groups. In 1522 the Ottoman sultan Suleiman besieged the city with a great army and, after six months, forced the knights to leave.

  2. 2. Letoon

    Letoon is a sanctuary in a flat green plain, and what you find is mostly water and frogs. The ruins of three temples, dedicated to Leto and her children Apollo and Artemis, stand in the valley of the Eşen river, near the coast of ancient Lycia, and in wet seasons the foundations are flooded, so that the old columns stand in pools full of croaking frogs. According to the story, Leto came here with her twins, thirsty and hunted by Hera, and when local shepherds refused her water, she turned them into frogs.

  3. 3. Xanthos bis outlier

    The Eşen valley has long been a farming country, and the landscape around the ruins of Xanthos is one of green fields, red earth and bare limestone hills. This stop lies close to the main ruins, among villages and orchards, and the rock tombs and carved pillars of the Lycians stand on the slopes above the road. Lycian tomb builders liked a view: they cut their graves in the cliffs, often in the form of wooden houses, and raised tall pillar tombs, topped with burial chambers, near the city.

  4. 4. Xanthos

    Xanthos was the greatest city of ancient Lycia, and its story is one of defiance. In 540 BC the Persian general Harpagus attacked it, and according to the historian Herodotus the Xanthians fought to the last, setting fire to their own homes and perishing together rather than surrender. Centuries later, in 42 BC, Brutus besieged the city during the Roman civil wars, and again the people chose destruction over capture. It is a grim record, and the city remains the symbol of Lycian stubbornness.
