Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bludenz

    Bludenz sits at the meeting of several valleys, and that is its whole story. The Walgau runs northwest toward the Rhine, the Klostertal heads east to the Arlberg, the Brandnertal climbs south to the Lünersee, and the Montafon carries the Ill river up to the Silvretta. At 588 metres, the town is a low point in a landscape of high peaks, with the Rätikon walls rising behind the old centre and a compact medieval core of arcades and painted houses. It is an ideal place to begin a ride into the mountains.

  3. 3. Near Galtür

    Galtür stands at about 1,600 metres at the head of the Paznaun valley, and the first thing you notice is a concrete wall. It is long, curved and tall, and it was built after the avalanche of February 1999, when a wall of snow roared down into the village and killed thirty-one people. The wall now shields the centre, and a museum beside it, the Alpinarium, tells the story of the disaster and, more broadly, of living under snow. It is a serious place, and a thoughtful stop even for riders who normally only want coffee.

  5. 5. Landeck

    Landeck sits at the meeting of valleys in the Tyrolean Alps, where the Inn river swings from its long upper valley into a gorge and the Sanna comes tumbling down from the Arlberg side. At around 800 metres, it is a modest height for the Alps, but the mountains rise steeply on all sides, and the old castle on its rock above the town gives the place the look of a doorway. Burg Landeck dates from the thirteenth century and now houses a museum. The town has about 7,700 people, and the old centre is small, with painted houses and cafes.
