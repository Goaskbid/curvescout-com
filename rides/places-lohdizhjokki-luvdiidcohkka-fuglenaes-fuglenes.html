Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. LOHDIZHJOKKI Luvdiidcohkka

    Alta calls itself the northern lights town, and it has some right to the claim. The little town on the Alta fjord, about a degree and a half south of the North Cape, has some of the clearest, most dependable aurora in Norway, and visitors travel from across the world in winter to stand in the cold and wait. In summer, the same sky never gets dark, and the midnight sun lights the fjord and the hills for weeks. The nearest large settlement to this stop on the plateau, Alta has about twelve thousand people, and it is thirty-six kilometres away.

  2. 2. LILLE-REIPAS Raipas

    Some of the most important monuments in the world are very small. Lille-Reipas, on a hill above the town of Alta in far northern Norway, is one of them. It is one of the points of the Struve Geodetic Arc, a chain of survey stations running from Hammerfest on the Arctic Ocean to the Black Sea, about 2,800 kilometres, that was used in the nineteenth century to measure the size and shape of the Earth. UNESCO listed the surviving points in 2005, and a simple marker is all that stands at most of them.

  3. 3. FUGLENAES Fuglenes

    Fuglenes is the headland at Hammerfest where one of Europe's long scientific ideas comes to an end. In the early nineteenth century the astronomer Friedrich Struve ran a chain of survey points stretching from the Black Sea to the Arctic Ocean to measure the shape of the Earth, and the northern end of that chain is marked here by a stone monument. It is a modest column on a rocky point, but it carries a World Heritage listing, and the idea of someone carrying a triangulation across ten countries makes you look at the little stone differently.
