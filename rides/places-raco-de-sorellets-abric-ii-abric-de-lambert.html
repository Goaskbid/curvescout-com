Why this ride

A 259 km round trip from Castell de Castells - roughly 4 h 28 in the saddle. On the way it passes Pinós, Coves Santes de Dalt, Abric de Lambert and back through Coves Santes de Dalt. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Racó de Sorellets- Abric II

    Vall d'Ebo is one of those places that history has swept clean. In 1609, when the crown expelled the Moriscos, this valley lost almost all of its inhabitants, and the villages had to be resettled with newcomers from elsewhere, so the terraces and irrigation channels you see on the hillsides were built by people whose descendants were sent away. The Racó de Sorellets is on the heights nearly five kilometres from the village, in a landscape of cliffs and ravines where the shelter walls carry much older marks than any of that.

  2. 2. Pinós

    Inland from the busy coast of the Marina Alta, behind Benissa and Calp, the land folds into a series of steep, terraced valleys where almond and olive trees cling to dry-stone walls and white farmhouses sit among the pines. Pinós is a rural area in the hills near Benissa, Senija and Jalón, and the rides link it to the rock-art shelters of the barrancs, the ravines that cut through the limestone of this region, part of the UNESCO listing of Mediterranean prehistoric art. The exact contents of the nearby shelters are for specialists, but the landscape itself has a quiet, timeless beauty, especially in the soft light of late afternoon.

  3. 3. Coves Santes de Dalt

    The Montgó is the great limestone hulk that rises between Dénia and Jávea on the Costa Blanca, a mountain that looks like a sleeping elephant from some angles and dominates every view along this stretch of coast. On its flanks, in shelters and small caves, are prehistoric paintings, and the Coves Santes de Dalt, the upper holy caves, are among the sites in the area recorded as part of the UNESCO Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin. The paintings here are mostly schematic, simpler and more abstract than the hunting scenes found inland, and the shelters are protected, so viewing usually needs a guided visit.

  4. 4. Abric de Lambert

    Cullera is not where most people would look for prehistory. It is a busy beach town at the mouth of the Júcar, with high-rise apartments along the sand, a lighthouse on the point and rice fields stretching inland toward the Albufera lagoon. But the town has a mountain at its back, a limestone outcrop crowned by a castle and a sanctuary, and in that rock there are shelters, including the one known as the Abric de Lambert.
