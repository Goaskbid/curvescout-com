Why this ride

curated scenic road. Danube, vineyards, abbeys. 32 km from Krems an der Donau to Melk, about 35 min of riding. On the way it passes Dürnstein, Weißenkirchen in der Wachau and Spitz. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

  2. 2. Dürnstein

    Dürnstein is the best-known village on the Wachau stretch of the Danube, and it is easy to see why. The river bends below a ridge, the vineyard terraces climb the slope in neat rows, and the blue-and-white tower of the abbey church rises above the roofs like a piece of confectionery. A ruined castle crowns the cliff above, and the whole scene appears on postcards, in films and in the memory of anyone who has taken a boat down the river.

  4. 4. Spitz

    Spitz stands at the western end of the Wachau, where the Danube bends and the valley opens out a little. It is a small town in name and in size, about thirteen hundred people, but it carries itself like a place that has always known its worth. Painted houses front the main street, a medieval gate called the Rotes Tor stands at one end, and above the town the ruin of the Hinterhaus castle looks down from a wooded crag.

  5. 5. Melk

    Melk is dominated by one thing, and you can't miss it. The Benedictine abbey stands on a rock above the Danube like a yellow-and-white ship, with twin towers and a baroque dome, and it has watched over this stretch of river for nearly a thousand years. The monks took up the site in 1089, when Leopold II of Babenberg gave the hilltop to the order, and the building you see today is the product of an eighteenth-century rebuilding in full baroque style. It is still a working monastery with a school, and the library, with its painted ceiling and thousands of old volumes, is as handsome a room as you will see in Austria. Umberto Eco gave the name to his narrator in The Name of the Rose, Adso of Melk, which tells you something about the abbey's reputation.
