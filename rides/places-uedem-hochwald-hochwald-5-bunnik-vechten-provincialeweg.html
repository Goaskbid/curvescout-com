Why this ride

From Uedem it runs 248 km to Uedem; allow about 3 h 54 of riding. The stops in between: Arnhem-Meinerswijk, Bunnik-Vechten | Provincialeweg and back through Arnhem-Meinerswijk. It crosses from Germany into Netherlands - carry ID and check the toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Uedem-Hochwald | Hochwald 5

    Between April and midsummer, the Niederrhein belongs to asparagus. Farms around Uedem, Xanten and Kalkar sell white spears from roadside stalls and serve them in restaurants with new potatoes, ham and butter or hollandaise, and the season officially ends on the feast of St John, the twenty-fourth of June, when the growers let the plants rest. Hochwald 5 is a forest spot, about seven kilometres from Uedem, but it sits in a region where the fields that border the trees have the sandy, well-drained soil that white asparagus loves.

  2. 2. Arnhem-Meinerswijk

    The Rhine has always been a frontier here. In Roman times, the Lower Rhine marked the northern edge of the empire, and the Romans built a chain of forts, watchtowers and roads along its banks to guard it. At Meinerswijk, on the southern bank of the river opposite Arnhem, the remains of one of those forts lie buried in the floodplain. It is part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire, Lower German Limes, listed by UNESCO in 2021. There is little to see on the surface, but the floodplain itself, a wide expanse of meadows, ponds and willows, is a pleasant place to walk, and markers explain the history.

  3. 3. Bunnik-Vechten | Provincialeweg

    A modern fort and a Roman one stand within a couple of kilometres of each other here, and both exist because of the same river. Vechten, on the Kromme Rijn just east of Utrecht, was the Roman fort of Fectio, one of the best-known sites on the Lower Germanic frontier. It lay at a point where the Rhine split, with the branch that became the Vecht heading north, and that made it a perfect depot. Soldiers, grain, and building stone all went through here, and archaeologists have pulled a remarkable amount out of the ground, including altars, bronze statuettes and the debris of a busy harbour.
