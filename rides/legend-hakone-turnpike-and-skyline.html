Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Odawara

    Odawara is a castle town at the foot of Hakone, and for most riders it is the place where the mountains begin. The castle, which belonged to the Hojo clan until Toyotomi Hideyoshi besieged it in 1590, has a reconstructed keep, built in 1960, with a museum inside. The old town around it is quiet, with a modest shopping street and some traditional shops. It sits on Sagami Bay, with the coast road leading east and west and the Hakone mountains rising behind.

  2. 2. Near Hakone

    The southern end of Lake Ashi lies in the bowl of the Hakone volcano, a caldera ringed by grassy ridges, and the roads that run around its rim are some of the most ridden in Japan. Hakone town itself is a scattered place of hot-spring inns, shrines and lakeside piers, and on clear days, which come mostly in winter and early spring, Mount Fuji stands over the far end of the lake, perfectly symmetrical and white-topped. The rest of the year it hides in haze and cloud more often than the posters admit.

  3. 3. Hakone

    Hakone is where Tokyo goes to get away from itself. The mountain resort sits in the caldera of an old volcano southwest of the capital, about an hour and a half by road without traffic, with Lake Ashi in the middle and Fuji looming to the northwest on a good day. Odawara, on the coast below, is the practical town, with its castle and its fish markets, while the hot-spring villages scattered through the hills have catered to travellers for centuries. The old Tokaido highway crossed these mountains, and you can still walk stretches of its stone-paved surface and see a reconstructed checkpoint beside the lake, where officials of the Tokugawa shogunate once inspected every traveller's papers.

  4. 4. Near Odawara

    Odawara is where Sagami Bay meets the mountains, a city on the coast at the foot of the Hakone range, and for most riders heading up to the Turnpike it is the jumping-off point: a big station town with fuel on every arterial road, fish restaurants near the harbour and the last flat kilometres before the climb. The Hayakawa river runs out of the mountains here, and on its southern side the road begins to rise almost immediately.
