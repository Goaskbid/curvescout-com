Why this ride

176 km from Fukuoka to Fukuoka, about 3 h 12 of riding. On the way it passes Shimbaru-Nuyama Mounded Tomb Group, Hetsu-miya, Nakatsu-miya and Okitsu-miya Yohaisho.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fukuoka

    Fukuoka is the big city of Kyushu, home to about one and a half million people, and it feels closer to Seoul and Shanghai than to Tokyo in the way it turns its face to the sea. The harbour of Hakata has been a gateway to the continent for two thousand years, and a gold seal found nearby, dated to the first century AD, is said to record a gift from the Chinese emperor. In 1274 and 1281 the Mongols landed here with huge fleets, and the stone wall built to resist them survives in places.

  2. 2. Shimbaru-Nuyama Mounded Tomb Group

    In Fukuoka prefecture, on the shore of the Genkai Sea, a group of burial mounds records the power of the Munakata clan, the family who were priests and sea-lords of the strait between Kyushu and Korea. Shimbaru-Nuyama is a cluster of tombs from the fifth and sixth centuries, small and large mounds scattered over a low ridge, and it belongs to the World Heritage listing for Okinoshima and the associated sites of the Munakata region, inscribed in 2017. Okinoshima is a remote island in the channel where ritual offerings were made for centuries to pray for safe voyages, and no women are allowed to land, and men must purify themselves in the sea before setting foot on it.

  3. 3. Hetsu-miya

    Hetsu-miya is the third of the Munakata shrines, and the only one you can drive to. Munakata Taisha honours three goddesses, the daughters of the sun goddess Amaterasu in the old myths, and each lives in a different place along the sea lane between Japan and Korea: one on the remote island of Okinoshima, one on Ōshima and one here on the mainland, at Tashima, beside a calm pine grove close to the shore. The grounds are plain, with a simple wooden hall and an air of sea and pine, and in autumn a festival draws crowds and boats.

  4. 4. Nakatsu-miya

    Munakata Taisha is not one shrine but three, spread across the sea off northern Kyushu, and Nakatsu-miya is the middle one. The boat leaves from the little harbour of Kōnominato and takes roughly a quarter of an hour, crossing water that was once the main sea lane between Japan and the Korean peninsula. Shrines were built at three points along that lane to protect sailors, and the middle one honours the goddess Tagitsuhime.

  5. 5. Okitsu-miya Yohaisho

    Some holy places are meant to be seen from a distance. Off the coast of Munakata, in Fukuoka Prefecture on the island of Kyushu, the island of Okinoshima rises from the Genkai Sea about sixty kilometres offshore. It is a sacred island where rituals were performed from the fourth to the ninth century, and tens of thousands of offerings, including mirrors, jewels and gold rings, were left on its rocks. Ordinary visitors are not allowed to land, so on the island of Oshima, closer to the mainland, stands the Okitsu-miya Yohaisho, a hall from which worshippers can pray towards Okinoshima across the sea.
