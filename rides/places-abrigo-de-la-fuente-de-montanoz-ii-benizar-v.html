Why this ride

From Nerpio it runs 164 km to Nerpio; allow about 2 h 50 of riding. The stops in between: Diosa madre, Abrigo del Sabinar, Benizar V and back through Abrigo del Sabinar. The highest point is about 1,400 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abrigo de la fuente de montañoz II

    Montañoz may sound like a village, but as a place name it belongs to a spring and a stretch of hillside south of Nerpio, up where the Segura headwaters break the land into steep slopes. The shelter called Abrigo de la Fuente de Montañoz II is one of many in the district, ten kilometres from Nerpio, and it belongs to the extraordinary group of painted overhangs that make this part of Albacete one of the most important rock art regions in Spain. Some of the paintings around here are in a distinctive style, with stylised human figures, and the UNESCO listing of 1998 covers several.

  2. 2. Diosa madre

    Some places on a map feel remote; this one actually is. The point named Diosa Madre, mother goddess, lies in the mountains of the Sierra de Segura, in the far southwest of Albacete province, roughly sixteen kilometres from Yeste and not far from the border with Jaén. The mountains themselves are the real experience.

  3. 3. Abrigo del Sabinar

    The Abrigo del Sabinar lies in the remote mountains of north-western Murcia, near Moratalla, where the Sierra de Segura and the Sierra del Taibilla meet, and its paintings of human figures and animals, including deer and goats, are among the finest examples of Levantine rock art in the region. The shelter is part of the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin listing, and the area around Moratalla and Nerpio is particularly rich in painted shelters. The landscape is wild and rugged, with pine forests, limestone cliffs and deep river valleys, and the villages are few and far between.

  4. 4. Benizar V

    Benizar is one of those places that only a handful of maps bother to name. It is a hamlet belonging to Moratalla, in the hilly north-west of the Murcia region, close to where the province meets Albacete and the first ridges of the Sierra de Segura begin. Around it, in the rock faces and gullies, archaeologists have recorded a cluster of painted shelters, and Benizar V is one in this series: a stone overhang with faint prehistoric figures, part of the Levantine rock art that runs along Spain's eastern mountains.
