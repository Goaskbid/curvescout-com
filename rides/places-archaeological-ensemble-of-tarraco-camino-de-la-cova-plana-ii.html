Why this ride

From Tarragona it runs 255 km to Tarragona; allow about 4 h 24 of riding. On the way it passes Albi II / Balma dels Punts, Camino de la Cova Plana II and back through Albi II / Balma dels Punts. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Archaeological Ensemble of Tarraco

    Tarraco was Rome's first permanent foothold in Spain. The Scipio brothers landed on this coast in 218 BC to cut Carthaginian supply lines and built a fortified base on the rock above the harbour, whose huge stone walls, in part from that early period, still stand along the edge of the upper town. Under Augustus the city became the capital of the largest Roman province in Spain, Hispania Tarraconensis, and the emperor himself lived here for a time in 26 and 25 BC, ruling the empire from a Spanish town while he recovered his health and led a campaign in the north.

  2. 2. Albi II / Balma dels Punts

    L'Albi is a small village on a hill in Les Garrigues, the dry, olive-covered district southeast of Lleida, and its name appears in the archaeological record thanks to rock shelters in the area, including the Balma dels Punts, the shelter of the dots. The name suggests schematic paintings in the form of dots and lines, typical of the later phase of prehistoric art in the region, and the site is part of the UNESCO listing of Mediterranean rock art. The shelters are small and fragile, and some can only be visited with a guide, so check with the town hall or a local interpretation centre before heading out.

  3. 3. Camino de la Cova Plana II

    Three rivers come together near Mequinenza: the Ebro, flowing in from the west, and the Segre and Cinca, arriving together from the Pyrenees to the north. The meeting point, now swallowed by the reservoirs behind the dams, has always been a strategic place, and the much-restored castle on the hill above the town has watched over it for centuries. The Camino de la Cova Plana is a track in the hills east of town, towards the Catalan border, and the site recorded here is one of the many prehistoric rock shelters scattered across this dry, rugged landscape.
