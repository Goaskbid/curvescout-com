Why this ride

A 425 km round trip from Mérida - roughly 5 h 46 in the saddle. The stops in between: Old Town of Cáceres, Royal Monastery of Santa María de Guadalup and back through Old Town of Cáceres. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Sta. Eulalia Obelisk → Old Town of Cáceres

The 75 km between Sta. Eulalia Obelisk and Old Town of Cáceres on Autovía Ruta de la Plata. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 228 and 437 m.

The stops

  1. 1. Sta. Eulalia Obelisk

    Start here.

    Eulalia was about twelve years old, the story goes, when she walked into the governor's court in Emerita Augusta and told him to his face that his gods were worthless. This was in the year 304, under the persecutions of Diocletian, and what followed made her the patron saint of Mérida. She was tortured and killed, and the city built a shrine over her grave that grew, over the centuries, into one of the great pilgrimage places of Roman and Visigothic Spain. The obelisk in the centre of town, a tall granite shaft by the old basilica quarter, is a reminder that devotion to her goes back to the days when Mérida was a provincial capital and the road north was the Via de la Plata.

  2. 2. Old Town of Cáceres

    75 km in.

    Cáceres has an old town so complete that film crews treat it as a ready-made set, and walking its streets at dusk, when the stone glows amber and the crowds have gone, you can see why. Inside the walls, built largely by the Almohads in the twelfth century, are dozens of fortified mansions, towers, churches and palaces, many built by noble families who made their fortunes in the conquest of the Americas. The towers once had battlements, but Queen Isabella ordered most of them cut down to curb the nobles' power. UNESCO listed the old town in 1986.
