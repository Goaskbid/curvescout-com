Why this ride

A proper distance ride to rivers, hills and old-town corners. The best part sits around Hann. Münden three rivers, Weserstein photo stop, while Northeim fuel gate and Göttingen food stop keep the day practical instead of turning it into a fuel hunt.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hannover south edge

    Pattensen is a small town on the southern fringe of Hannover, in the Calenberger Land, where the flat plain of the city meets the first gentle swells of the hill country that runs south toward the Weser. Its old centre has half-timbered houses and a medieval church, and the streets have a sleepy, slightly farm-like atmosphere even though the suburbs of the city are barely a few kilometres away. For a rider wanting to head south, this is the right place to start, because the main roads out of the city are behind you and the quiet roads begin almost at once.

  2. 2. Hann. Münden three rivers

    Hannoversch Münden is where two rivers lose their names. The Werra and the Fulda meet here, and from this point on the water is called the Weser, which flows north for more than four hundred kilometres to the North Sea. The town sits at the meeting point, with water on three sides, and has so many half-timbered houses, more than seven hundred, that it feels like a stage set, with carved beams, painted gables and a cobbled main street.

  3. 3. Weserstein photo stop

    There is an old verse on the stone: where the Werra and the Fulda kiss, they lose their names, and from the kiss the Weser flows, German to the sea. The Weserstein stands on the tip of the land where the two rivers meet, in a small park on the edge of the old town of Hann. Münden, and it has been put up for a long time, so the stone has the patina of weather and moss.

  4. 4. Göttingen food stop

    Göttingen is a university town that has never been shy about it. The Georg-August University was founded in 1737 and became one of the leading centres of science in Europe, and the roll of people who studied or taught here includes the mathematician Carl Friedrich Gauss, the physicists Max Planck and Werner Heisenberg, the Brothers Grimm, and a long list of Nobel laureates. The student population gives the city a lively, young feel, with bicycles everywhere.

  5. 5. Northeim fuel gate

    Northeim is a Hanseatic town that gets passed by, which is a pity. It sits on the Leine river, in the broad valley between the Harz mountains to the east and the Solling hills to the west, and it has a long history, with its origins in the early Middle Ages and a time as a member of the Hanseatic League. The medieval counts of Northeim were once powerful in the region, and one of them, Otto, became Duke of Bavaria in the eleventh century.

  6. 6. Einbeck return road

    Einbeck is where bock beer began. In the Middle Ages the town was one of the great brewing centres of the Hanseatic League, and its strong, dark beer was exported across northern Europe. When Bavarian brewers copied it, the name Einbecker was heard as ein Bock, a billy goat, and so a beer style got its name. The town still has a brewery, and the goat appears on the labels.

  7. 7. Back to Hannover

    Hannover is not the first name that comes to mind when riders talk about great destinations, and it would probably say that is fine. It is a big, calm, practical city of half a million people on the northern edge of the German hills, where the flat North German Plain begins to roll up toward the Deister and the Weser Uplands.
