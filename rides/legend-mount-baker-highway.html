Why this ride

National Scenic Byway. volcano, alpine meadows, forest. From Bellingham it runs 64 km to the finish; allow about 1 h 10 of riding. The highest point is about 1,300 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Scenic road chapters

Mount Baker Highway

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 20 km this is Mount Baker Highway, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bellingham

    Bellingham is the last substantial city before the Canadian border, a place of eighty-five thousand people on a sheltered bay in the north-west corner of Washington state. It is a university town, with a lively cafe and brewing scene, a revived waterfront, and the kind of outdoorsy, independent character you find in many Pacific Northwest cities. The old Fairhaven district, a Victorian district with brick buildings and bookshops, is a nice place to wander, and the ferry terminal for Alaska sits at its edge.

    Here the valley narrows and the forest closes in, with towering Douglas firs and red cedars draped in moss, and the river rushes over boulders beside the road. Mount Baker itself is hidden for now, but you can feel it ahead.

  3. 3. Mount Baker Highway point 3

    Picture Lake earns its name every clear morning. This small pond sits beside the Mount Baker Highway in the Heather Meadows area, just below the ski area, and on a still day it holds a perfect mirror image of Mount Shuksan, a jagged, glacier-draped peak rising to the east. It is one of the most photographed views in the United States, and once you have seen it, you will understand why. Shuksan's sharp summit, its hanging glaciers and the dark forest around the lake form a scene that looks almost too perfect to be real.

  4. 4. Mount Baker Highway point 4

    Artist Point is where the Mount Baker Highway ends, at about 1,550 metres, on a high ridge between two volcanoes. To the west, Mount Baker rises to 3,286 metres, its massive dome of glaciers and snowfields filling the sky. To the east, Mount Shuksan stands sharp and jagged, its glaciers hanging off its steep faces. And all around you are meadows, lakes and rocky ridges, with views stretching in every direction. It is one of the most spectacular viewpoints in the Cascades, and arriving there on a motorcycle feels like a triumph.
