Why this ride

A 370 km round trip from Hellín - roughly 6 h 39 in the saddle. On the way it passes Cueva del Esquilo, Cueva Ambrosio and back through Cueva del Esquilo. It tops out around 1,200 m.

The stops

  1. 1. Barranco de la mortaja I

    Start here.

    Hellín is a sizeable town of thirty thousand in the south-east of Albacete province, a dry, open country of hills, almond groves and stony plains that belongs more to Murcia than to La Mancha in feeling. The Barranco de la Mortaja is a ravine in the sandstone hills near the town, and the shelter that gives this stop its name belongs to the rock art of the Mediterranean Basin, a group of sites that UNESCO listed in 1998. The region is among the best for early paintings, with the shelters of Minateda close by, where figures of hunters and animals, in red and black, were painted on the walls of rock overhangs thousands of years ago.

    Read the complete Barranco de la mortaja I write-up.

  2. 2. Cueva del Esquilo

    113 km in.

    The border between Albacete and Murcia runs through a tangle of mountains here, and the villages of Socovos, Férez and Letur sit among them like small islands. The Cueva del Esquilo is one of the painted rock shelters in these hills, a reminder that people lived and hunted in the Segura mountains thousands of years before any border existed. Like most of the rock art in the region, it is protected and best visited with local guidance; the town halls and tourist offices of the surrounding villages are the place to ask.

    Read the complete Cueva del Esquilo write-up.

  3. 3. Cueva Ambrosio

    192 km in. The ride passes about 8 km from it.

    Cueva Ambrosio belongs to the high, empty north of Almería province, on the dry plateau where Andalusia runs into Murcia, fifteen or so kilometres out of Vélez-Blanco. This is a landscape of broad, pale fields, almond and cereal farms, low limestone ridges and long skylines, with the pines of the Sierra María to the south and almost nothing built on the horizon.
