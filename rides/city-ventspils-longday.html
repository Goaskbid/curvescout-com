Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ventspils

    Ventspils is a port town on the western coast of Latvia, at the mouth of the Venta River, and it has two faces. One is the working harbour, which handles oil, coal and timber and has done since the Hanseatic League, and the other is a handsome, tidy waterfront town with a long white beach and a big public park. The beach has held the Blue Flag for many years, and the water is shallow and cold, with fine pale sand. The town's older centre is small, with wooden houses and cobbled streets around the Livonian Order castle, built in the thirteenth century and one of the best preserved in the country.

  2. 2. Historic Centre of Riga

    Riga is the biggest city in the Baltic states, and its old centre reads like a history of northern trade. The city was founded in 1201 by a German bishop, soon became a leading member of the Hanseatic League, and grew rich on timber, flax, furs and grain moving between Russia and western Europe. The medieval core, packed with merchants' houses, guild halls and church spires, still has the layout of those days, with narrow cobbled lanes and squares, and the House of the Blackheads, a brotherhood of unmarried merchants, with its ornate gabled facade, stands in the Town Hall Square.
