Why this ride

From Como it runs 208 km to Como; allow about 6 h 22 of riding. On the way it passes Monte San Giorgio, Sacri Monti of Piedmont and Lombardy, Sacro Monte della Beata Vergine del Soccor and back through Sacri Monti of Piedmont and Lombardy. It tops out around 1,100 m. It crosses from Italy into Switzerland - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules.

The roads

Lake Como Scenic Circuit

The 31 km between Como and Monte San Giorgio on ex strada militare del monte Orsa-Pravello. It gains around 750 m and ends high, near 892 m. Top up fuel at the bottom - pumps thin out on the way up.

The stops

  1. 1. Como

    Start here.

    Lake Como is shaped like an upside-down Y, with the city of Como at the end of one leg, and it is the deepest lake in Italy, plunging to over four hundred metres. The Alps rise straight from the water, with villas and gardens on the shores, and ferries zigzagging between the villages. Como itself is a small and elegant city, with a Duomo that took centuries to build and mixes Gothic and Renaissance styles, a walled old town and a lakefront with a funicular climbing to the hill village of Brunate. It was the birthplace of Alessandro Volta, the inventor of the battery, and the unit of electrical potential carries his name.

    Read the complete Como write-up.

  2. 2. Monte San Giorgio

    31 km in.

    Monte San Giorgio is a wooded, pyramid-shaped mountain on the shores of Lake Lugano, straddling the border between Switzerland and Italy. To the eye, it is a pleasant green hill with villages on its slopes. To palaeontologists, it is one of the most important fossil sites in the world. About two hundred and forty million years ago, during the Middle Triassic, this area was a tropical lagoon, and its sediments preserved an extraordinary range of marine reptiles, fish, invertebrates and plants. The mountain was added to the UNESCO list in 2003 on the Swiss side, and in 2010 on the Italian side.

  4. 4. Sacri Monti of Piedmont and Lombardy

    104 km in.

    On the western shore of Lake Como, above the village of Ossuccio and facing the little Isola Comacina, a cobbled path climbs the hillside through olive groves and woods, past fourteen small chapels, to a sanctuary at the top. This is the Sacro Monte of Ossuccio, one of nine sacred mountains in Piedmont and Lombardy listed together by UNESCO. Built in the seventeenth century, the chapels illustrate the Mysteries of the Rosary with life-size terracotta and plaster figures arranged in dramatic scenes, each one a small piece of theatre.

    Read the complete Sacri Monti of Piedmont and Lombardy write-up.

  5. 5. Sacro Monte della Beata Vergine del Soccor

    Same place as the previous stop.

    The Madonna of Succour sits above Ossuccio, on the western shore of Lake Como, about halfway between Como and Menaggio. A cobbled path climbs through the village with fourteen small chapels along it, each holding painted terracotta figures that act out a scene from the life of Mary, and at the top there is a sanctuary with a terrace and a view across the water. It is one of the Sacri Monti of Piedmont and Lombardy, a group of hillside devotional complexes that UNESCO listed together in 2003. These were built in the Counter-Reformation as walkable theology: you could not read, so you walked and looked. The Ossuccio chapels date from the seventeenth century, and the little island you see below the slope is the Isola Comacina, the only island on the lake, with its own long and rather stormy history.
