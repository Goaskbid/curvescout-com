Why this ride

A 94 km one-way ride from near Arnold, about 1 h 33 of riding. The stops in between: Lake Alpine and Ebbetts Pass. The highest point is about 2,650 m. Ebbetts Pass is open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Arnold California → Lake Alpine

Water arrives beside the road and stays for 49 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go — slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did.

Ebbetts Pass Scenic Byway

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2663 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance. Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Arnold California

    Arnold is a mountain town strung out along Highway 4 in the Sierra Nevada foothills, a place of log cabins, pine trees and holiday homes at about four thousand feet. It is the first real settlement on the way up the Ebbetts Pass road, and many people use it as a base for the summer, thanks to its cool air and the lakes nearby. The town has cafes, a supermarket and gas stations, and is a sensible place to prepare for the mountain road.

  2. 2. Lake Alpine

    Lake Alpine is a high, clear reservoir in the Sierra Nevada, about 2,200 metres above sea level, and it sits like a cupped hand among granite and lodgepole pine. Highway 4 runs along its edge, and for a few months of the year it is a delightful stop: a lodge with a deck, a small boat ramp, and a view down the water to bare rock. It is the sort of place where you take your helmet off, buy a cold drink, and for a few minutes forget that the road beyond is a serious one.

  3. 3. Ebbetts Pass

    Ebbetts Pass is one of the narrowest, steepest and least-visited paved passes in the Sierra Nevada, and for that reason it is a favourite with riders who like their mountains a bit raw. It tops out at 2,663 metres, on Highway 4 between the western foothills and the eastern desert, and the road that climbs it is a patchwork of single-lane sections, tight hairpins, steep gradients and rough surfaces. It was named for Major John Ebbetts, who explored the route in the 1850s hoping to find a place for a railway, and the railway never came, which is why the road is the way it is.

  4. 4. Markleeville

    Markleeville is the seat of Alpine County, which has fewer residents than any other county in California, and the town feels like a mountain hamlet that has never been in a hurry. A few hundred people live along the East Fork of the Carson River, with a courthouse, a small museum, a general store, two or three cafes and the rest hidden in pine and aspen. At around fifty-five hundred feet, in the eastern Sierra, the air is clear and cool, and the sound of the river runs through the village.

Arnold California to Lake Alpine

Arnold California → Lake Alpine is 49 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Lake Alpine to Ebbetts Pass

Lake Alpine → Ebbetts Pass climbs about 2663 m in 23 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Ebbetts Pass to Markleeville

Ebbetts Pass → Markleeville drops about 2663 m over 21 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
