Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sandnessjoen

    Seven mountains stand in a row along the coast of Alsten island, and the people of Sandnessjøen call them the Seven Sisters. They are jagged, steep and dark, rising straight out of the sea to over a thousand metres, and a Norwegian legend gives each one a name and a story involving trolls and a pursuing suitor. With around five thousand six hundred people, it is the biggest place on this stretch of the coast.

  2. 2. Jektvik

    The Jektvik to Kilboghamn crossing is part of the Helgeland coastal route, Fv17, and the boat carries cars and bikes over a sound that marks, at its centre, the line of 66 degrees 33 minutes north, so a motorcycle can cross into the Arctic on a ferry deck with a coffee in hand. Beyond the water, the mountains of Meløy and the great Svartisen glacier lie waiting.

  3. 3. Glomfjord

    Glomfjord sits at the end of a short fjord arm in Nordland, on the Helgeland coast just south of the Arctic Circle, and it is a company town in the way that only Norway can manage: a place built around hydropower, in a landscape that looks as if it were designed for a postcard. The power station was built in the early twentieth century to use the water from the mountains, and an industrial plant followed, so a village of about twelve hundred people stands where the steep slopes meet the water.
