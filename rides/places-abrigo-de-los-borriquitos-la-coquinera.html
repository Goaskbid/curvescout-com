Why this ride

94 km from Alacón to Alacón, about 1 h 37 of riding. The stops in between: Recodo de los Chaparros II, La Coquinera and back through Recodo de los Chaparros II. The highest point is about 1,150 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abrigo de los Borriquitos

    Borriquitos means little donkeys, and whoever named this shelter presumably saw something long-eared and four-legged in the red paint, though Levantine painters drew deer and goats far more often than donkeys. The name is a reminder that these places were named by farmers, long before they were studied by archaeologists, and that what a shepherd sees on a wall may not be what a professor sees. The shelter is near Alacón, in the Martín valley in Teruel, and belongs to the Parque Cultural del Río Martín.

  2. 2. Recodo de los Chaparros II

    The Río Martín cuts a deep, narrow canyon through the dry limestone of the Bajo Aragón, in the province of Teruel, and the Recodo de los Chaparros, whose name means roughly the bend of the dwarf oaks, sits on that river a few kilometres from Albalate del Arzobispo. The land is rolling and almost treeless, covered in olive groves, almond trees and pale rocky fields, and it can feel like a different planet from the green north of Spain.

  3. 3. La Coquinera

    You reach La Coquinera through some of the emptiest country in Spain. The rock shelter lies in the hills of the Río Martín Cultural Park, in the triangle between Oliete, Estercuel and Alacón, where the Martín river and its side ravines have sliced the Teruel plateau into terraces, cliffs and narrow gorges. The paintings here belong to the same Mediterranean tradition as the dozens of other panels in the park, figures of animals and people in ochre red, small enough that you have to stand close and let your eyes adjust before they emerge from the stone.
