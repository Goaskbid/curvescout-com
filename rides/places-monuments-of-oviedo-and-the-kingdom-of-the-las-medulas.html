Why this ride

A 394 km round trip from Oviedo - roughly 6 h 48 in the saddle. The stops in between: Chemin Primitif, Las Médulas and back through Chemin Primitif. The highest point is about 1,150 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Monuments of Oviedo and the Kingdom of the

    Oviedo is a capital that started as a monastery. In the early ninth century King Alfonso II of Asturias moved his court here, built a church dedicated to the Saviour, and began to create a little kingdom out of the mountains that had resisted the Moors, and the buildings that followed form one of the most distinctive groups of early medieval architecture in Europe. UNESCO lists the Monuments of Oviedo and the Kingdom of the Asturias, and the best of them stand on the slopes of Monte Naranco, above the city: Santa María del Naranco, a palace hall of King Ramiro I built in the 840s and later used as a church, and San Miguel de Lillo, a slender church nearby.

  2. 2. Chemin Primitif

    Tineo stands on a hillside above the green valleys of western Asturias, and it is one of the main stops on the Camino Primitivo, the original Way of St James. According to tradition, it was King Alfonso II of Asturias who first walked from Oviedo to Santiago in the ninth century to see the tomb, newly found, of the apostle, and the route he took became the Primitivo. It is the hardest of the Caminos, crossing mountain after mountain, and the pilgrims you meet here, wet, muddy and cheerful, are a special breed.

  3. 3. Las Médulas

    Walk into Las Médulas on a clear autumn morning and you meet a strange kind of silence. The red crags stand around you like the ruins of a cathedral built from clay, and the chestnut trees, some of them centuries old, drop their husks onto the path. It is a landscape made by an empire and then forgotten by it, left to grow over for nearly two thousand years.
