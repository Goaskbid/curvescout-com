Why this ride

A one-way ride of 1491 km, La Crosse to St. Louis - roughly 20 h 26 in the saddle. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. La Crosse

    Grandad Bluff gives La Crosse its postcard view: a limestone headland rising about 150 metres over the city, from which the whole valley lies open, the Mississippi sliding past in several braided channels, the wooded bluffs of Minnesota on the far side, and the Wisconsin river towns lining the shore. On a clear autumn day the colour is extraordinary, the oaks and maples turning red and gold along miles of ridgeline. A road climbs to the top, and the view is free.

  2. 2. Dubuque

    Dubuque is an old river city built on a limestone bluff above the Mississippi, in the north-east corner of Iowa, where the state meets Illinois and Wisconsin. About fifty-nine thousand people live here, and the town's history goes back to 1788, when a French-Canadian trader named Julien Dubuque, with the permission of the Meskwaki people who lived here, began mining the lead that lay close to the surface. He died in 1810, and his grave, a stone tower above the river at a site called Mines of Spain, is now a state recreation area with hiking trails and views over the water.

  3. 3. St Louis

    St. Louis is a river city with a very large arch in it. The Gateway Arch, designed by Eero Saarinen and finished in 1965, rises 630 feet on the west bank of the Mississippi, a catenary curve of stainless steel that is the tallest monument in the United States and one of the most recognisable. It marks the city's historic role as the gateway to the West, the starting point for settlers, trappers and explorers, including Lewis and Clark, who set out up the Missouri in 1804 from a camp not far north. At its feet, a museum tells the story of westward expansion, and a tram carries visitors to the top.
