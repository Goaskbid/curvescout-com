  1. 1. Pasto

    Pasto sits high in the Andes of southern Colombia at about 2,500 metres, under the smoking cone of Galeras volcano, and it is the last big city before the Ecuadorian border. About 380,000 people live here, in a city that feels more like the Andes than the Caribbean, with Indigenous and colonial influences in its food, crafts and festivals. Puerres is a smaller town in the southern highlands, and the roads out of Pasto lead to some of Colombia's most surprising sights.

  2. 2. Las Lajas

    No photograph prepares you for this one. The Santuario de Las Lajas is a neo-Gothic church built across a gorge, its tower and spires rising from a bridge that springs from the rock walls of the Guáitara river canyon, close to the Ecuadorian border in the far south of Colombia. From the viewing paths above, it looks like a fantasy castle hung between the cliffs, with waterfalls on one side and a deep green drop on the other.

  3. 3. Laguna de la Cocha

    Cold, high and often wrapped in cloud, Laguna de la Cocha is the largest lake in the south-west of Colombia, and it feels like a different country from the dry canyons around it. Also known as Lake Guamuez, it lies at around 2,800 metres in the Andes east of Pasto, and the shores are lined with wooden houses painted in bright colours, small jetties, and boats waiting to take visitors across to the Isla de la Corota, a tiny forested island that is a protected sanctuary and a wetland of international importance.
