Why this ride

From Teruel it runs 333 km to Teruel; allow about 5 h 44 of riding. The stops in between: Abrigo de los Dos Caballos, Castellón de los machos, Barrio del Castillo and back through Castellón de los machos. The highest point is about 1,450 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mudejar Architecture of Aragon

    Teruel is one of the coldest and least visited provincial capitals in Spain, and a campaign slogan, Teruel Existe, was coined to remind the rest of the country that it is still there. It stands at around nine hundred metres on a flat-topped hill above the Turia river, in the Aragonese uplands, and its old town holds a group of brick-and-tile towers that UNESCO lists as part of Mudéjar architecture in Aragon. Mudéjar is the style created by Muslim craftsmen working for Christian patrons after the reconquest, and in Teruel it took the form of tall square towers decorated with geometric brickwork and glazed green and white ceramic.

  2. 2. Abrigo de los Dos Caballos

    Red rock and green pine make an unusual pair in Spain, and Albarracín has built a landscape out of it. The hills west of the town are the Pinares de Rodeno, a protected area where rodeno sandstone, a rusty red, has weathered into rounded boulders and little overhangs under a cover of pines. Those overhangs, sheltered from rain and wind, are where prehistoric painters did their work, and the Abrigo de los Dos Caballos is one of the shelters the Albarracín area is known for. The paintings belong to the Levantine tradition, part of the group inscribed by UNESCO in 1998, and they show animals, hunters and humans in rust-red pigment on the sandstone.

  3. 3. Castellón de los machos

    High in the Serranía de Cuenca, among pine woods and sandstone outcrops near the village of Villar del Humo, prehistoric hunters painted figures on the rock shelters thousands of years ago. The site called Castellón de los Machos is one of a cluster in this area that belongs to the vast UNESCO listing of rock art of the Mediterranean Basin of the Iberian Peninsula, which covers hundreds of painted shelters from Catalonia to Andalusia. The images here are small, reddish and stylised, and finding them takes patience and good eyes.

  4. 4. Barrio del Castillo

    Cuenca is built on a spur of rock between two gorges, and the Barrio del Castillo is its highest quarter, the old castle district at the narrow tip of the ridge. The Huécar river runs along one side and the Júcar along the other, each in a deep cleft with limestone cliffs, and the old town clings to the top between them like a ship's prow. The castle that gave the quarter its name has largely gone, though a gate and a few walls remain, and the Torre de Mangana, a clock tower that was once part of the fortress, stands nearby. The whole walled town is on the UNESCO list as an unusual example of a medieval fortress city that kept its shape.
