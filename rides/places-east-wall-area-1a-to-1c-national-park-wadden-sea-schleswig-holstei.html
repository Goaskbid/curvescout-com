Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. East Wall Area 1A to 1C

    When the Vikings built their trading town of Hedeby at the head of the Schlei, a long narrow inlet of the Baltic, they chose a spot with a short portage to the North Sea rivers, making it one of the great crossroads of Northern Europe. To protect the isthmus between the two seas, the Danes built a long earthwork called the Danevirke, begun in the eighth century and strengthened for centuries after, with a rampart, a ditch and in places a brick wall added in the twelfth century by King Valdemar. Hedeby and the Danevirke are together a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The East Wall, the Ostwall, is the eastern arm of the system, running through the countryside near Kosel above the Schlei.

  2. 2. Archaeological Border complex of Hedeby an

    Hedeby, or Haithabu in German, was a Viking town on the shore of the Haddebyer Noor, a small branch of the Schlei, and for about three centuries it was one of the most important trading places in northern Europe. It was founded in the early 800s and grew into a market town where Frisian, Saxon, Slavic, Danish and Swedish merchants met, trading furs, amber, iron, glass and slaves. At its height it may have had a thousand or more inhabitants, and a semicircular rampart, open to the water, protected the whole settlement. In 1066 it was sacked and burned by a Slavic force, and the surviving population moved across the water to Schleswig.
