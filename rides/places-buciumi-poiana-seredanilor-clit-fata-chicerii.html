Why this ride

From Stârciu it runs 216 km to Stârciu; allow about 3 h 43 of riding. On the way it passes Poieni - Râmbușoi, Mirșid - Dealul Mare 1, Cliț - Fața Chicerii and back through Mirșid - Dealul Mare 1. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Buciumi - Poiana Șeredanilor

    Bees do well in the hills around Buciumi, where acacia, linden, wildflower meadows and fruit orchards flower in turn from April to August, and in many gardens a few painted hives sit under the plum trees. Poiana Șeredanilor, which one might loosely translate as the glade of the Șeredan family or people, is a clearing name near the village, about five kilometres from Stârciu. It is a place on a map more than a viewpoint, but it gives you the right country for talking about honey.

  2. 2. Poieni - Râmbușoi

    West of Cluj, the valley of the Crișul Repede cuts through the hills toward the Hungarian plain, and the main road between Transylvania and the west follows it. The village of Poieni and the hill of Râmbușoi lie in this valley, between Huedin and Ciucea, with the forested slopes of the Vlădeasa massif, part of the Apuseni mountains, rising to the south. In Roman times, the frontier of Dacia ran through the hills to the north, along the Meseș mountains, and this corridor was a natural route to watch; hilltop positions in the area formed part of the defensive network, and the larger fort at Bologa guarded the valley nearby.

  3. 3. Mirșid - Dealul Mare 1

    Mirsid is the kind of village you pass on the way somewhere else, until you notice that it sits at the foot of the greatest Roman frontier in Transylvania. The ridge of the Meses mountains rises behind it in a long, dark line of beech and oak, and the Romans built a series of watchtowers along the crest, looking north across the border into the lands of the free Dacians. The name Dealul Mare, Big Hill, with the number one attached, suggests the first in a sequence of those lookout points, and in a few of them you can still make out the square footprint of a tower in the grass.

  4. 4. Cliț - Fața Chicerii

    Northern Sălaj is quiet country: low wooded hills, hay meadows, plum orchards and villages strung along the Someș river and its side valleys, with wooden churches and haystacks built around poles in the fields. Cliț is a small village near Băbeni, and the point named Fața Chicerii lies in the hills above it. Names in this list follow the pattern of the Romanian archaeological record, a village and then the local name of a hillside, and in this part of Sălaj many such points belong to the line of watchtowers and small forts of the Roman frontier of Dacia, which UNESCO recognised in 2024.
