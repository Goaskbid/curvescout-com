Why this ride

A 428 km round trip from Santa María - roughly 8 h 24 in the saddle. The stops in between: Nuestra Señora de Santa Ana, San Ignacio Mini, Jesuit Mission of La Santísima Trinidad de and Nuestra Señora de Loreto. It crosses from Argentina into Paraguay - carry ID and check the toll rules. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

The stops

  1. 1. Santa María la Mayor

    Start here.

    Red earth is the first thing you notice in Misiones, the thin finger of northeastern Argentina squeezed between Paraguay and Brazil. The soil is the colour of a brick, the forest is subtropical, and the roads cut through it leave long scarlet stripes on the verge. Santa María la Mayor, a small town of under two thousand, lies in this country, and its main attraction is a ruin the forest nearly swallowed.

    Read the complete Santa María la Mayor write-up.

  2. 2. Nuestra Señora de Santa Ana

    85 km in.

    Deep in the red earth of Misiones, in the north-eastern tip of Argentina, the forest is slowly reclaiming a mission town. Nuestra Señora de Santa Ana was one of the Jesuit reductions, settlements where Jesuit priests gathered the Guaraní people in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, partly to protect them from slave raiders and partly to convert them. The towns were laid out around a central plaza with a church, a school, workshops and houses, and the Guaraní built, carved and played music in a form that mixed European baroque with their own skills. Santa Ana, founded in the 1630s, is among the lesser known, and much of it lies in ruin under trees and vines.

    Read the complete Nuestra Señora de Santa Ana write-up.

  3. 3. San Ignacio Miní

    103 km in.

    Red sandstone, carved with angels, vines and saints, rises out of the green jungle of northeastern Argentina at San Ignacio Miní, the best preserved of the Jesuit missions in the province of Misiones. Founded in the early seventeenth century and moved to its present site in the 1690s, the mission was a town for several thousand Guaraní people, organised by Jesuit priests, with a great church, workshops, houses, a school and a plaza. The church's facade, in a style often called Guaraní Baroque, combines European forms with local motifs carved by Guaraní craftsmen. The mission, with others in Argentina and Brazil, became a World Heritage Site in 1983 and 1984.

    Read the complete San Ignacio Mini write-up.

  4. 4. Jesuit Mission of La Santísima Trinidad de

    154 km in.

    In the south-east of Paraguay, near the Parana river and the Argentine border, a field of red earth, tall trees and golden sandstone walls marks the site of La Santisima Trinidad de Parana, one of the largest of the Jesuit missions built among the Guarani people in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. The ruins are about nine kilometres from Hohenau, a small town of just over five thousand people, and not far from Trinidad village and Jesus, where another mission stands.

    Read the complete Jesuit Mission of La Santísima Trinidad de write-up.

  5. 5. Nuestra Señora de Loreto

    214 km in.

    The ruins of Loreto lie in a clearing in the forest of Misiones, in the north-east tip of Argentina, and the forest is slowly winning. Strangler figs and giant roots creep over the red sandstone walls of what was once a church, and the grass grows over the plaza where thousands of Guarani people once lived. Loreto was one of the Jesuit missions, the reducciones, that the Society of Jesus founded in the early seventeenth century in the lands of the Guarani, in what is now Argentina, Paraguay and Brazil. This one was moved to its present site in 1632 after raids by slave hunters from Brazil.

    Read the complete Nuestra Señora de Loreto write-up.
