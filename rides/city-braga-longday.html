Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Braga

    Braga is Portugal's most religious city, and also one of its oldest. It was founded by the Romans as Bracara Augusta in the first century BC, became a Christian bishopric in late antiquity, and has been a seat of the Church ever since. Its cathedral, begun in the eleventh century, is the oldest in the country and mixes Romanesque, Gothic and Baroque, and the city's skyline is a thicket of church towers. During Holy Week, its processions, with hooded penitents and torchlit marches, are among the most intense in Portugal.

  2. 2. Alto Douro Wine Region

    The Douro river has been carving a gorge through the north of Portugal for millions of years, and for about two thousand years people have been planting vines on its slopes. The terraces, held up by stone walls, climb steeply from the water and rise through schist and slate in rows, and the effect, in the right season, is of a vast green staircase. The Alto Douro was the first wine region in the world to be officially demarcated, by the Marquis of Pombal in 1756, and UNESCO listed it in 2001. Port wine, the sweet fortified red that made the region famous, is still made here.

  4. 4. Quinta do Fariseu

    Quinta do Fariseu is a farm in the Coa valley in northern Portugal that sits on one of the great stories of European archaeology. In the 1990s, engineers preparing a dam on the Coa River came upon thousands of engravings carved on the schist rock faces along the valley, figures of horses, aurochs, ibex and deer, scratched into the stone by hunters thousands of years ago. Many date to the Upper Palaeolithic, some twenty thousand years old or more, which makes them as old as the paintings in the great caves of France and Spain. The find caused a public campaign, and the dam was cancelled, a rare triumph of heritage over engineering. Today the Coa Valley is a UNESCO site, together with its Spanish counterpart at Siega Verde.

  5. 5. Fonte Frieira

    The setting is austere and beautiful: steep schist hills, terraced slopes planted with vines, almond and olive, and a sky that is very big and very blue in summer. This is the upper Douro, a landscape shaped by centuries of wine, and the terraces of the Port country, which lie a short distance west, are a World Heritage landscape in their own right.
