Why this ride

From Bregenz it runs 455 km to Bregenz; allow about 7 h 51 of riding. The stops in between: Allensbach-Strandbad, Olzreute-Enzisholz, Monastic Island of Reichenau and back through Monastic Island of Reichenau. The highest point is about 1,200 m. It crosses from Austria into Germany - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bregenz

    Bregenz is where Austria touches Lake Constance, the Bodensee, and the town makes the most of a small piece of shoreline. The lake is huge, shared with Germany and Switzerland, and on a clear day the far shore melts into haze. Behind the town the Pfänder rises to just over a thousand metres, with a cable car that carries you from the harbour to a ridge in a few minutes, and from the top you see the whole lake, the Rhine delta, the Alps and, with luck, a good part of three countries at once.

  2. 2. Allensbach-Strandbad

    Lake Constance does not do dramatic. It does soft: a huge sheet of water, a far shore that comes and goes with the haze, and little towns with tidy promenades that seem to have been tidied again while you were not looking. Allensbach's Strandbad is one of those spots, a lido on the Gnadensee, the western arm of the lake, with a swimming area, grass banks and a view across to the Reichenau island and the Swiss shore beyond.

  3. 3. Olzreute-Enzisholz

    Some five thousand years ago, someone in Upper Swabia built a wheel out of a single maple plank, mounted it on an axle and used it to move loads across the soft ground near what is now Bad Schussenried. The wheel turned up during excavations at the moor settlement of Olzreute-Enzisholz and is among the oldest wheels ever found in Central Europe, dated to around 2900 BC. The settlement, a group of pile dwellings on the edge of a former lake, is one of the Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps listed by UNESCO in 2011, a transnational site across six countries.

  4. 4. Monastic Island of Reichenau

    Reichenau is a flat, green island in the Untersee, the western arm of Lake Constance, connected to the mainland by a long causeway lined with poplars. A wandering bishop named Pirmin founded the monastery here in 724, and over the next centuries it became one of the great centres of learning and art in Europe, famous for its scriptorium and its illuminated manuscripts. The island's three Romanesque churches survive, and UNESCO listed the whole as a World Heritage Site in 2000.
