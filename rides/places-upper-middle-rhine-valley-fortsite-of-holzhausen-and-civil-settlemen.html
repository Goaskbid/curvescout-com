Why this ride

A 71 km round trip from Sankt Goarshausen - roughly 1 h 21 in the saddle. It goes by way of Limessection between watchtowers WP 2/13 a, Fortsite of Holzhausen and civil settlemen and back through Limessection between watchtowers WP 2/13 a. It follows river valleys.

The roads

Rhine Romantic Route

It is 17 km from Upper Middle Rhine Valley to Limessection between watchtowers WP 2/13 a on Rheinstraße. Fairly level - 133 to 377 m end to end. Take it at your own pace.

The stops

  1. 1. Upper Middle Rhine Valley

    Start here.

    Between Bingen and Koblenz, the Rhine cuts a deep, winding gorge through the slate hills of the Rhenish Massif, and for centuries this has been one of the great landscapes of Europe: steep slopes covered with vineyards, castles on every crag, villages of half-timbered houses along the river, and barges gliding past. The Upper Middle Rhine Valley was listed by UNESCO in 2002, and Sankt Goarshausen sits at its heart, at the foot of the most famous rock on the river.

    Read the complete Upper Middle Rhine Valley write-up.

  2. 2. Limessection between watchtowers WP 2/13 a

    17 km in.

    Up on the plateau of the Hintertaunus, north of the Rhine gorge, the Roman frontier crossed a quiet landscape of fields and forest near Singhofen, and a section of it between two watchtowers survives here as a faint bank and ditch running through the woods. This is the Upper German Limes, which ran for more than five hundred kilometres from the Rhine to the Danube, marking the boundary between the Roman Empire and the free Germanic tribes from the second century until the Romans withdrew in the third. It was not a great wall like Hadrian's in Britain, but a line of watchtowers, forts and a timber palisade, later strengthened with a ditch and bank. Together with the Raetian Limes, it was listed by UNESCO in 2005.

    Read the complete Limessection between watchtowers WP 2/13 a write-up.

  3. 3. Fortsite of Holzhausen and civil settlemen

    36 km in.

    Up on the high, rolling plateau between the Rhine and the Lahn, where the Taunus fades into fields and beech woods, the Romans built one of the better preserved stone forts on the Upper German Limes. Holzhausen an der Haide is a quiet village of a little over a thousand people, and the fort sits just outside it in the trees, the outline of its walls still traceable on the ground, with the gates and corner towers marked out where the stone survives. It is part of the UNESCO-listed frontier that once ran from the Rhine near Koblenz all the way to the Danube, a line of palisade, ditch, watchtowers and garrisons meant less to stop armies than to control who came and went, and what they paid for the privilege.

    Read the complete Fortsite of Holzhausen and civil settlemen write-up.
