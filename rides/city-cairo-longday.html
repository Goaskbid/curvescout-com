  1. 1. Cairo

    Cairo is a city that makes sense of every superlative you have heard about it. With more than seven million people in the city proper and many millions more in the metropolitan area, it is the largest in the Arab world and one of the largest in Africa, and its streets are a river of cars, buses, donkey carts, bicycles and pedestrians, all moving at once to the music of horns.

  2. 2. Wadi Al-Hitan

    Forty million years ago, this arid corner of the Egyptian desert was a warm, shallow sea, and the creatures that swam in it left their bones in the sand. Wadi Al-Hitan, which means Valley of the Whales, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the Fayoum depression, inscribed in 2005 for what it reveals about one of the great stories of evolution: the transition of whales from land animals to fully marine creatures. The sand and rock are littered with the fossilised skeletons of early whales, among them the Basilosaurus, a long, snake-like predator that could grow as long as a bus.
