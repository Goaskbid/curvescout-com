Why this ride

An 85 km round trip from Kronstadt - roughly 1 h 18 in the saddle. The stops in between: The Maximov's Datcha, Oranienbaumsky Springboard and back through The Maximov's Datcha.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

From Northern battery No. 4 to The Maximov's Datcha is 24 km, mostly on КАД. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 0 and 27 m. Best in the early light, before the traffic.

The stops

  1. 1. Northern battery No. 4

    Start here.

    Kotlin Island lies in the Gulf of Finland, at the entrance to the Neva, and its job for three centuries has been to guard St Petersburg from the sea. Peter the Great founded the naval base of Kronstadt here in 1704, and the island, long and narrow, was ringed with forts, batteries and artificial islands. The northern batteries, of which No. 4 is one, belong to that long chain, built in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries as the guns grew bigger and the ships more dangerous. They are low, grass-covered earthworks and brick casemates facing the water, and on grey days the place has a lonely, military feel. The sea is shallow here and often frozen in winter.

    Read the complete Northern battery No. 4 write-up.

  2. 2. The Maximov's Datcha

    25 km in.

    The south shore of the Gulf of Finland west of St Petersburg is a strip of dachas, palaces and old military ground, and the place marked here as Maximov's Datcha sits within it, a couple of kilometres from the town of Lomonosov.

    Read the complete The Maximov's Datcha write-up.

  3. 3. Oranienbaumsky Springboard

    43 km in.

    The Oranienbaum Springboard is not a single building but a piece of ground, and once you know the story the low woods and fields south of the Gulf of Finland look entirely different. In September 1941, as German and Finnish forces closed the ring around Leningrad, a pocket of coast around Oranienbaum, today's Lomonosov, held out. It was cut off from the city by land, supplied across the water and under the guns of Kronstadt, and it never fell. In January 1944 the Second Shock Army broke out of this pocket as one jaw of the offensive that finally lifted the siege.
