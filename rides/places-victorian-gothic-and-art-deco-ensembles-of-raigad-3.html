Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Victorian Gothic and Art Deco Ensembles of

    Mumbai built itself a seafront in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, and the result is one of the most unusual architectural neighbourhoods on earth. The Victorian Gothic and Art Deco Ensembles, inscribed by UNESCO in 2018, are two separate groups of buildings, facing each other across the Oval Maidan, a big open cricket ground in the south of the city. On one side are the Gothic Revival buildings of the British Raj: the Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus, which was Victoria Terminus, the High Court, the University and the Rajabai Clock Tower. On the other are the Art Deco cinemas, hotels and apartment blocks of the 1930s, along Marine Drive.

  2. 2. Khanderi Fort

    Khanderi is a small island fort in the Arabian Sea, a short boat trip off the Konkan coast south of Mumbai. Shivaji built it in the late seventeenth century to challenge the Siddis, the Abyssinian-descended admirals of Janjira, who held the neighbouring island of Underi, and the two forts glared at each other across a narrow stretch of water for decades. The Marathas built Khanderi first, and the Siddis answered by fortifying Underi, and the sea between them saw skirmishes and cannon duels. Today Khanderi carries a lighthouse and the remains of walls and bastions, and its rocks are surrounded by fishing boats.

  3. 3. Lohagad

    In the monsoon, Lohagad disappears into the clouds and reappears, drenched and green, with waterfalls pouring off every ledge. This hill fort stands on a ridge of the Western Ghats a short distance from Lonavla, between Mumbai and Pune, and its walls and gateways crown a basalt plateau a few hundred metres above the surrounding valleys. Its name means iron fort, and it is one of the forts associated with the Maratha ruler Chhatrapati Shivaji, which in 2025 were grouped together on the UNESCO list as the Maratha Military Landscapes.

  4. 4. Raigad

    Raigad is a flat-topped mountain in the Sahyadri, the Western Ghats of Maharashtra, with cliffs falling away on almost every side, and it was chosen by Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj as the capital of the Maratha kingdom. In 1674 he was crowned here, a ceremony that announced the Marathas as a sovereign power in the face of the Mughal empire, and the fort became the political heart of the young state. Today the summit holds the ruins of the palace, the market street with its rows of stone shop plinths, water tanks cut into the rock, and Shivaji's samadhi, which draws a steady stream of visitors who come as pilgrims rather than tourists.
