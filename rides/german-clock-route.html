Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Villingen Schwenningen

    Villingen-Schwenningen is two towns that were made to share a name in 1972, and they still do not quite agree. Villingen, to the west, is a medieval walled town in the Black Forest, with gates, towers and a minster, once part of Austria and proud of its carnival, the Fasnet, in which masked figures in elaborate wooden masks and painted costumes parade through the streets in a thunder of whips and bells. Schwenningen, to the east, is younger and more industrial, a clockmaking town that grew in the nineteenth century, and it has the source of the Neckar river in a small park, marked with a stone.

  2. 2. Triberg

    Triberg is built in a steep, narrow fold of the Black Forest, a town that seems to have been poured down a gorge and set hard. Houses climb both sides of the Gutach valley, rooftops overlap, and the main street runs steeply uphill past shops full of cuckoo clocks, carved wooden bears and cherry schnapps. It is touristy, no question, but the tourists come because the place is the real thing, the heartland of the Black Forest clock trade, where families have been making timepieces in workshops since the eighteenth century.

  3. 3. Schonach

    Schonach is the Black Forest at its most tightly folded, a village of about four thousand strung along a steep valley, with farms on every ledge and a small ski jump looming over the houses. Winter is its big season: the area is a regular stop for Nordic combined and ski jumping competitions, and the big hill, the Langenwaldschanze, is among the larger in Germany. In summer the same slopes fill with walkers and cyclists, and the church bells carry a long way in the clear air.

  4. 4. Titisee Neustadt

    Neustadt in the Black Forest has none of the lakeside bustle of its neighbour Titisee, and it is better for it. This is a working mountain town of about twelve thousand people on the Gutach river, with a solid church, a market square and a railway station where the Höllentalbahn from Freiburg arrives after its long climb. The line was an engineering feat when it was built in the nineteenth century, winding up the Höllental gorge with a steep rack section in its early decades, and the station is a handsome reminder of the days when the railway was the great modern invention.

  5. 5. Furtwangen

    Furtwangen is a Black Forest town high on a ridge, at about 850 metres, and it is the place where the story of the cuckoo clock is told. The Deutsches Uhrenmuseum, founded in the 1850s to inspire local craftsmen, holds one of the great clock collections in Europe, from early wooden Black Forest pieces with painted faces to elaborate astronomical machines and cuckoo clocks in every size. A clockmaking school was established here too, and it grew into a university of applied sciences, which is why a town of under ten thousand people has a fair number of students.
