Why this ride

A 246 km round trip from Vall de Gallinera - roughly 4 h 14 in the saddle. The stops in between: Abric de Gontrán, Cueva de la Vieja and back through Abric de Gontrán. The highest point is about 850 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc de Beniali- Abric V

    Beniali is another of the little hamlets of the Vall de Gallinera, tucked into the folds of the Alicante mountains between Villalonga and the head of the valley. The name looks Arabic and is, as with many place names here: the Beni- prefix means sons of, and recalls the clans who farmed these valleys during the centuries of Muslim rule. That legacy runs through the valley in the terraces cut out of the hillside, the irrigation channels, and the old way of growing fruit and almonds on thin soil.

  2. 2. Abric de Gontrán

    Head south-west from Xàtiva along the old road to Castile and you climb gently into a corridor between two mountain ranges, the Serra Grossa on one side and the Serra de Enguera on the other. The town of Moixent sits where the corridor narrows, with Vallada just downstream and the vineyards of Fontanars dels Alforins on the plateau beyond. The Abric de Gontran lies in the hills just outside Moixent.

  3. 3. Cueva de la Vieja

    In 1910 a local man led researchers to a shallow shelter above the plain of Alpera in Albacete, and the paintings they found there became some of the best known examples of Levantine rock art in Spain. The Cueva de la Vieja shows archers with feathered headdresses, women in long skirts, deer and goats, all painted with a sense of movement that still feels immediate. The nearby Cueva del Queso holds more figures. Both are part of the UNESCO listing for the rock art of the Iberian Mediterranean, and guided visits run from the village, so check times with Alpera's tourist office.
