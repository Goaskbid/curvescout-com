Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Spearfish

    Spearfish stands at the northern edge of the Black Hills, where the prairie of South Dakota lifts into a dark, forested dome of ancient rock and pine. The town, with about eleven thousand people, is a university town as well as a gateway, and its old brick main street, its trout stream and its neat tree-lined blocks give it a calmer feel than the more touristy places further south. The Black Hills themselves are sacred to the Lakota, who call them Paha Sapa, and the land was the subject of a long and bitter dispute after gold turned up in 1874.

  2. 2. Near Spearfish

    Spearfish Canyon cuts down through the northern Black Hills in a deep, narrow gorge of limestone cliffs, and US 14A follows it for about thirty kilometres from the town of Spearfish up to Cheyenne Crossing. It is one of the most beautiful canyons in the American Midwest.

  3. 3. Near Lead

    At Savoy, the midpoint of Spearfish Canyon, a narrower side canyon branches off to the west, and a short road leads up it to Roughlock Falls. This is Little Spearfish Canyon, a more intimate and secluded place than the main canyon, with spruce and pine forest, mossy boulders and a clear stream tumbling over a series of limestone ledges. The falls are reached by a short, easy trail from a picnic area, and they are particularly lovely in spring, when the water is high.

  4. 4. Near Lead

    The top of Spearfish Canyon is a different world from the bottom. Around Cheyenne Crossing, where US 14A meets US 85, the canyon opens out into high, rolling country of spruce and pine forest, meadows and small streams, at around 1,800 metres. From here, the road heads east over the hills to Lead, a historic mining town clinging to the steep slopes of a gulch, and Deadwood, its more famous neighbour, a few kilometres further on.
