  1. 1. Tbilisi

    Tbilisi spreads along the Mtkvari river in a bowl of dry hills, and its first impression is of balconies. Old houses lean over narrow streets with carved wooden verandas, sulphur bathhouses sit under domes of brick in the Abanotubani district, and on the ridge above, the ruined Narikala fortress watches the city. The name Tbilisi comes from a Georgian word for warm, and the story says that a hunting king found hot springs here and decided to found a city.

  2. 2. Mtskhetis Jvari

    Jvari, the Georgian word for cross, stands on a rocky bluff above the point where two rivers meet, the brown Mtkvari and the greener, faster Aragvi. The church dates from the end of the sixth century, and it is a small, dark, stern building, almost square, with a design that influenced much of Georgian architecture later. The reason it stands where it does is a legend: in the early fourth century, Saint Nino, the woman credited with bringing Christianity to Georgia, is said to have raised a cross of grapevine on this hilltop, and the king converted soon after. Georgia calls itself one of the first Christian nations, and this is where that story is anchored.

  3. 3. Historical Monuments of Mtskheta

    Mtskheta is the old heart of Georgia, and the whole town is listed by UNESCO. For hundreds of years before Tbilisi became important, it was the capital of the kingdom of Iberia, and here at the meeting of the Mtkvari and Aragvi rivers, in the early fourth century, King Mirian converted to Christianity. Archaeologists have found remains of a royal citadel, tombs and Roman-era graves in the surrounding hills, showing how much older the settlement is than the churches. The town is still the seat of the Georgian Orthodox Patriarch, and a place of pilgrimage.

  4. 4. Svetitskhoveli Cathedral

    Svetitskhoveli is the great cathedral of Georgia, and its name means the life-giving pillar. The story goes that after the crucifixion, a Georgian Jew named Elioz brought Christ's robe back to Mtskheta, where his sister Sidonia died of grief clutching it. The robe could not be removed from her hands, so she was buried with it, and a cedar grew from her grave. Saint Nino cut the tree down to make seven pillars for the first church, and the seventh is said to have risen by itself and hung in the air until a prayer brought it down. The present building was raised in the early eleventh century, in a time of great confidence in the Georgian kingdom, and it has been the burial place of many kings.
