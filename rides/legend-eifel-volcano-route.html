Why this ride

official tourism route. volcanic lakes, forest, castles. A one-way ride of 48 km, Kelberg to Gerolstein - roughly 52 min in the saddle. The stops in between: Daun and Manderscheid. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

  2. 2. Daun

    Daun is the capital of the Vulkaneifel, and the name is accurate: this is a country of dead volcanoes, and the evidence is all around the town. Instead of cones, the Eifel has maars, round lakes that fill the craters left when rising magma met groundwater and blew itself apart. Three of them lie within a few kilometres of Daun, the Gemündener, Weinfelder and Schalkenmehrener Maar, deep and dark and ringed with woods, and the sight of such perfectly round water in the middle of ordinary hills is oddly moving. The Weinfelder Maar has a small chapel on its shore that makes a very good photograph.

  4. 4. Manderscheid

    Manderscheid has two castles facing each other across a gorge, and the story of how they came to be there is a classic of medieval feuding. The Oberburg and the Niederburg stand on opposite ridges above the loops of the Lieser river, a hundred metres or so apart, and they were built by rival branches of the same family, who spent several centuries arguing about who owned what. Both are now ruins, atmospheric in the true sense, with broken towers and arches overlooking a deep wooded valley, and a short footpath leads between them. Of the two, the Niederburg is the more complete.

  5. 5. Gerolstein

    Gerolstein is best known for water, and the water is the story of the place. The town lies in a bowl of the Kyll valley, and under it flows mineral water from the volcanic ground, rich in carbonic acid and minerals, which is bottled and sold across Europe under the town's name. You can see the factory at the edge of town and taste the water at the public springs, where it comes out fizzy, cool and slightly metallic. About seven and a half thousand people live here, so fuel, shops and restaurants are plentiful.
