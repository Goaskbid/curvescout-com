Why this ride

From Guntramsdorf it runs 812 km to Petronell-Carnuntum; allow about 17 h 04 of riding. The stops in between: Purkersdorf, Scenic stretch near Tulln and in Großmugl. The climbs: near Heiligenkreuz and near Sopron. It crosses Austria, Czechia and Hungary - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. Long stretches are under trees.

  3. 3. Near Purkersdorf

    Purkersdorf sits at the eastern edge of the Wienerwald, where the Vienna Woods roll down into the city, and it is the last stop before the motorway delivers you back to Vienna. The town follows the narrow valley of the Wien river, and the hills around are covered with beech forest, meadows and small villages, a landscape of gentle curves and green shade.

  8. 8. Back to Salzkammergut

    This is the heart of the Thermenlinie, the line of hot springs and vineyards that runs south from Vienna along the foot of the hills, and the sunny south-east slopes grow two local white grapes found almost nowhere else, Zierfandler and Rotgipfler. Together they make a heavy, aromatic wine that has been prized since the Habsburg court. In the village centre, a Renaissance castle and a baroque church stand among the half-timbered houses.

  9. 9. Fuel stop in Petronell-Carnuntum

    Marcus Aurelius is said to have written part of his Meditations at Carnuntum, while campaigning against the Germanic tribes across the Danube in the second century. The city was then the capital of the Roman province of Upper Pannonia, a sprawling legionary fortress and civilian town of tens of thousands of people on the frontier of the empire. Today, the village of Petronell-Carnuntum sits on part of the site, and the archaeological park has reconstructed a Roman quarter with houses, baths and streets, built using ancient techniques, a remarkable way to imagine life on the frontier.

    Read the complete Fuel stop in Petronell-Carnuntum write-up.

  10. 10. Near Hustopeče, on the way back

    South Moravia is the warmest corner of the Czech Republic, and in summer the plains around Hustopeče shimmer in the heat, the vineyards and orchards dusty under a hard blue sky. The point lies near Šakvice, close to the Nové Mlýny reservoirs, a chain of three large artificial lakes on the Dyje river, and on the way back to Vienna it is a natural place to cool off in the water.

  11. 11. Food stop near Hodonice

    Hodonice lies in the vineyards and fields just east of Znojmo, in the southernmost corner of Moravia, a few kilometres from the Austrian border. The village is small and quiet, with a church and rows of houses, but the surrounding landscape is one of the most famous wine regions of the Czech Republic. The Znojmo area produces crisp white wines, and the cellars in the villages are often open for tasting.

  12. 12. Photo stop near Wilhelmsburg

    Porcelain with a lily trademark was once found in almost every Austrian kitchen, and much of it came from Wilhelmsburg, a small industrial town in the Traisen valley south of St. Pölten. The Lilien porcelain works were famous for their simple, practical designs, and the town has a museum devoted to the history of ceramics. Today, Wilhelmsburg is a quiet place, with a church and old factory buildings, set in a valley of meadows and forests.

  13. 13. Perchtoldsdorf

    Perchtoldsdorf lies on the very edge of Vienna, where the city's southern suburbs meet the first hills of the Vienna Woods, and it has the relaxed atmosphere of a wine village rather than a suburb. The hills behind the town are covered in vineyards, and the old streets are lined with Heurigen, the traditional wine taverns where producers serve their own new wine with simple cold food. When a Heuriger is open, it hangs a bunch of pine branches over the door, a sign that has been used for centuries.

  14. 14. Scenic stretch in Bad Sauerbrunn

    Bad Sauerbrunn is named after its acidic mineral spring, which has been used for healing since the nineteenth century, and it remains one of the smaller spa towns of Austria. It lies in northern Burgenland, at the foot of the Rosalia hills, a wooded range that marks the border between Lower Austria and Burgenland. The town is quiet and green, with villas, parks and a spa centre, and it has an air of gentle relaxation.

  15. 15. High point near Sopron

    Sopron calls itself the most loyal town, Civitas Fidelissima, because in 1921, when the region was being handed from Hungary to Austria after the First World War, its people voted in a plebiscite to remain Hungarian. The town became a Hungarian peninsula reaching into Austria, and its medieval centre, with the Firewatch Tower rising above the old walls, is one of the best-preserved in the country. The tower offers sweeping views over the rooftops and the surrounding hills.

    16. Back to Neusiedler See

    Despite the label, you are not on the lake. This point lies on the right bank of the Danube, about thirty kilometres east of Vienna, in Petronell-Carnuntum, and the lake is a good half hour's ride further south. What you have instead is something that most riders miss: the ruins of Carnuntum, once the capital of the Roman province of Upper Pannonia and a military base of the Danube frontier. In its time it held a legionary fortress, an amphitheatre and a city of tens of thousands, and several emperors passed through. Marcus Aurelius is said to have written part of his Meditations while on campaign here.
