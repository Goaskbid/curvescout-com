Why this ride

From Tarragona it runs 327 km to Tarragona; allow about 5 h 38 of riding. The stops in between: Britus I, La Vall de la Coma, Camino de la Cova Plana II and mas de Torres. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. The Roman Theatre

    Wherever you sit in the Roman theatre of Tarragona, you face the sea. The builders carved the seating into the slope of a hill that runs down to the shore, so that the audience looked past the stage toward open water, and the effect on a clear evening is of a stage set whose back wall is the Mediterranean. Only part of the structure survives, its curved terraces partly rebuilt and its stage area cluttered with the foundations of the later buildings that stood on it, and yet the shape of the whole is easy to read. It lies in the lower town, a few hundred metres from the harbour, and is one of the monuments of the Tàrraco ensemble listed by UNESCO in 2000.

  2. 2. Britus I

    Between Montblanc and Vimbodí, in the Conca de Barberà, the land rises toward the Prades mountains and the Serra de la Llena, and in the sandstone and limestone outcrops of these hills, prehistoric hunters left paintings in rock shelters. Britus I is one of these sites, with figures painted in the distinctive style of Levantine rock art, which shows scenes of hunting, gathering and daily life with lively human and animal figures. The rock art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula was inscribed by UNESCO in 1998, covering hundreds of sites from Catalonia to Andalusia. Many are small and fragile, and access is often restricted to protect them.

  3. 3. La Vall de la Coma

    The Garrigues is the dry heart of Lleida province, a land of terraced hills, stone walls and endless olive groves, and La Vall de la Coma is one of its quiet valleys, in the municipality of L'Albi. In a few rock shelters here, prehistoric people painted small figures in red, part of the Levantine rock art of the Mediterranean basin that is recognized as a World Heritage Site. The paintings are fragile and modest in size, and access is usually organized through local guides or interpretation centres, so plan ahead rather than expecting to wander in.

  4. 4. Camino de la Cova Plana II

    Three rivers come together near Mequinenza: the Ebro, flowing in from the west, and the Segre and Cinca, arriving together from the Pyrenees to the north. The meeting point, now swallowed by the reservoirs behind the dams, has always been a strategic place, and the much-restored castle on the hill above the town has watched over it for centuries. The Camino de la Cova Plana is a track in the hills east of town, towards the Catalan border, and the site recorded here is one of the many prehistoric rock shelters scattered across this dry, rugged landscape.

  5. 5. mas de Torres

    The Monegros in high summer is the closest thing Europe has to a desert, a wide, dry plateau of gypsum hills, wheat stubble and steppe scrub under a hard, white sky. Near the village of Candasnos, close to the main road between Zaragoza and Lleida, the shelter labelled Mas de Torres is one of the rock art sites of the Mediterranean Basin listed by UNESCO, a reminder that people lived and hunted here long before the land dried out to its present state.
