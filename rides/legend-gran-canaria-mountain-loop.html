Why this ride

curated scenic road. volcanic mountains, ravines, island views. 51 km from Las Palmas de Gran Canaria to Maspalomas, about 56 min of riding. The stops in between: Teror, Tejeda, San Bartolomé de Tirajana and Santa Lucía. The highest point is about 1,950 m. The line favours views over speed.

  2. 2. Teror

    Teror is the spiritual capital of Gran Canaria, and the reason is a small statue of the Virgin. According to legend, in 1481, shepherds saw a light in a pine tree, and when they climbed up, they found an image of the Virgin Mary, who became Our Lady of the Pine, the patron saint of the island. The basilica built in her honour dominates the main square, a large pale-stone church with a broad front and a carved interior, and on 8 September each year, thousands of pilgrims walk through the night to reach it for the festival, carrying gifts of fruit and flowers.

  3. 3. Tejeda

    Tejeda sits in the crater-like bowl at the centre of Gran Canaria, with the great rock needle of Roque Nublo standing a short way to the south-west and the ancient shrine of Roque Bentayga showing on the skyline to the west. It is a white village of just over two thousand people, hung on a steep hillside at roughly a thousand metres, and it is regularly named among the loveliest in Spain for good reason.

  6. 6. Maspalomas

    Maspalomas is two things that do not quite match: a resort town of about thirty thousand people, and a nature reserve of dunes the size of a small desert. The dunes stretch for several kilometres along the south coast of Gran Canaria, a shifting sea of golden sand blown from the Atlantic, with a palm oasis at one end and a lighthouse, built in 1890, at the other. Camel rides carry visitors across the sand, the wind sculpts new ridges every day, and at sunrise or sunset the shadows are long and dramatic. The reserve protects a rare ecosystem, and walkers are asked to keep to marked paths.
