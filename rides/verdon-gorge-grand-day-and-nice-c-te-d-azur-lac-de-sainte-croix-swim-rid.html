Why this ride

From Grasse it runs 449 km to Grasse; allow about 8 h 09 of riding. The stops in between: Castellane, Verdon balcony road, Lake Sainte-Croix and Antibes. The highest point is about 1,550 m. It crosses from France into Italy - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It keeps close to the shore for long stretches.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Grasse fuel

    Grasse smells of something before you can see it. The old perfume capital of France climbs a steep limestone hillside about a dozen kilometres from the Mediterranean, high enough above Cannes to catch a cooler, more mountain air, and for generations the fields of jasmine, rose and tuberose on the terraces around the town fed its distilleries. Perfume came here from the leather trade: glovemakers in the seventeenth century began scenting their gloves, and the business outgrew the leather. Today the town still has big perfume houses and a handful of working fields, and in May the rose harvest and in late summer the jasmine are the times to be around.

  2. 2. Castellane food

    Castellane sits at the bottom of a bowl, with a huge limestone rock standing over it like a stopper. The rock, simply called le Roc, rises about a hundred and eighty metres above the old town, and a small pilgrimage chapel, Notre-Dame du Roc, perches on top, reached by a steep path through the pines. The town lies on the Verdon river a little upstream of the great canyon, and it has been a gateway to this country for as long as there have been roads.

  3. 3. Verdon balcony road

    The Verdon is a canyon for people who thought they had seen canyons. The river has cut a gorge through the limestone of Haute Provence, some twenty-five kilometres long, with cliffs that rise as much as seven hundred metres above the green water at the bottom, and the roads that run along the rims give you ridiculous views. This stretch, between Castellane and the area of Point Sublime, runs along the northern edge, and the balcony viewpoints along the road look down into a crack in the earth that appears to have no floor.

  4. 4. Lake Sainte-Croix

    This is where the Verdon stops being a canyon and becomes a lake. At the western end of the gorge, a dam was built in the early 1970s at Sainte-Croix-du-Verdon, and the water backed up through the valleys behind it to form the Lac de Sainte-Croix, a sheet of vivid turquoise about ten kilometres long that fills the end of the canyon and laps against the Valensole plateau. The village of Les Salles-sur-Verdon was drowned when the water rose, and the people were moved to a new settlement built higher on the shore; in very dry years, old walls and fields are said to emerge from the water.

  6. 6. Antibes

    Old ramparts rise straight from the sea at Antibes, and behind them a tight knot of lanes, markets and ochre houses climbs toward a castle on the shore. The town sits between Nice and Cannes on the bay of the same name, facing the snowy line of the Alps on clear winter days, with the long wooded peninsula of Cap d'Antibes to the south and the jazz town of Juan-les-Pins on its western flank.

  12. 12. Ospedaletti

    Palms, pastel houses and a bay that faces due south: Ospedaletti is the Riviera dei Fiori at its most relaxed, a small Ligurian town tucked between Sanremo and Bordighera with its feet in the sea and terraced hills behind it. The old coast railway has been moved inland into a tunnel, and its former line along the shore is now a cycle and walking path, so the seafront is quieter than you might expect on such a crowded coast.
