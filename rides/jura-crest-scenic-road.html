Why this ride

94 km one way from Nyon - plan on about 1 h 31 of riding. The stops in between: St Cergue, Col de la Givrine, Le Brassus and Sainte Croix. The highest point is about 1,250 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

First and second gear more often than not. Look through each corner to the next one and let the engine do the holding. The valley floor is behind you and the road starts working for its height at about 29.5%. Corners stack above one another where the ground allows nothing straighter.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Nyon

    Nyon was a Roman town before most of Europe had names, founded by Julius Caesar around 45 BC as Colonia Iulia Equestris, a settlement for veteran cavalry on the shore of Lake Geneva. Columns from the Roman forum still stand by the lake, and a small museum in the old basilica shows what remains. Today it is a pleasant town of about seventeen thousand people with a castle on the hill, a waterfront promenade and a market, and it has the quiet, wealthy air of a place that has been well looked after for two thousand years.

  2. 2. St Cergue

    Above Nyon and Lake Geneva, the Jura rises in long, wooded ridges, and Saint-Cergue sits on the first real shelf of them, about a thousand metres above sea level. The village is small, with just under two thousand people, but it has a surprisingly big view: on a clear day the lake lies below and the Alps are lined up beyond it, with Mont Blanc as the white presence at the end. Those who live in the Geneva basin come up here for cooler air and some snow, and the station at the heart of the village hints at why.

  3. 3. Col de la Givrine

    The Col de la Givrine tops out at 1,228 metres, which is hardly a heroic height, but the pass has a character all its own. It lies on the Jura ridge between Saint-Cergue and the French resort of Les Rousses, and it is one of those places where the border is a few paragraphs of paperwork and a different bakery. Bring your passport or ID card, because the checkpoint is real, even if you are often waved through.

  4. 4. Le Brassus

    Le Brassus is at the quiet top end of the Vallée de Joux, a long glacial trough in the Vaud Jura that holds the Lac de Joux and a handful of villages strung together along its length. It is a hard place to farm, cold and high, and over the centuries the people here turned to winter work indoors. That is how the valley became the home of Swiss watchmaking at its most complicated. Audemars Piguet, founded here in 1875, still has its roots in Le Brassus, and other prestigious names have workshops nearby. The houses look like ordinary farmhouses, but behind some of the windows people are assembling movements with hundreds of parts.

  5. 5. Sainte Croix

    Sainte-Croix lies on a sunny shelf of the Vaud Jura, with the Chasseron ridge rising behind it and the Swiss plateau stretched out far below. The town is about four thousand people, but it punches above its weight in one very specific area: mechanical things that make music. For more than a century this was the capital of Swiss music boxes and automata, and the industry grew into a wider tradition of precision engineering. The Paillard company, makers of Bolex cameras and Hermes typewriters, was founded here, and the town's wealth shows in the solid old buildings along the main street.

  6. 6. Creux du Van

    Creux du Van is a huge natural amphitheatre carved into the limestone of the Neuchâtel Jura, a curved cliff about a kilometre and a half long and well over a hundred metres high, ringing a deep bowl of scree and forest. It looks like a giant's quarry or a collapsed volcano, but it was made by ice and water, which worked away at the soft layers of rock over many thousands of years. From the rim, at about 1,450 metres, you look down onto treetops and across to the Alps on a clear day, and the Mont Blanc range is often visible on the horizon.

Nyon to St Cergue

Nyon → St Cergue is 12 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

St Cergue to Col de la Givrine

St Cergue → Col de la Givrine climbs about 1228 m in 4 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Col de la Givrine to Le Brassus

Col de la Givrine → Le Brassus drops about 1228 m over 20 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
