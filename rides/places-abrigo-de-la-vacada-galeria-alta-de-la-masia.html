Why this ride

A 154 km round trip from Bordón - roughly 2 h 39 in the saddle. The stops in between: Cingle de Palanques- Abrigo A, Coveta de la Cornisa, Cingle de Palanques- Abrigo B and Galeria alta de la masia. The highest point is about 1,250 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abrigo de la Vacada

    Castellote is a hill town in the Maestrazgo, the high, dry and wonderfully empty country of southern Aragon, and the shelter of La Vacada lies among the limestone gorges nearby. The name suggests a place where cattle were gathered. The area has some of the finest Levantine rock art in Teruel, and the figures here are part of the Mediterranean-arc ensemble UNESCO recognised in 1998: slender red archers, deer and sometimes a hunting scene, painted on the walls of shallow caves. The rock is pale and the cliffs are cut by the river Guadalope, which carves a sequence of narrow canyons through the plateau.

  2. 2. Cingle de Palanques- Abrigo A

    Els Ports is the high, empty country in the north of Castellón province, a land of stone-walled terraces, deep gorges, juniper and pine, and villages that cling to ridges above the river valleys. Palanques is one of the smallest of them, and above it, in a long cliff band known as the Cingle de Palanques, are shelters with prehistoric paintings, part of the UNESCO Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin. The Levantine paintings here show human figures and animals in the expressive style typical of this coast's mountainous interior, and like most such sites, they are protected and usually visited with a local guide.

  3. 3. Coveta de la Cornisa

    Morella is one of the most dramatic towns in Spain: a walled medieval city wrapped around a conical hill, with a castle on the summit and its walls running unbroken around the base. It stands in the mountains of the Ports in the north of Castellón province, at around a thousand metres, and its silhouette, seen from the roads that climb toward it, is unforgettable. The Coveta de la Cornisa, the little cave of the ledge, lies in the hills about four kilometres away.

  4. 4. Cingle de Palanques- Abrigo B

    Morella is one of the great walled towns of eastern Spain, a stack of grey stone houses wound round a conical hill in the high country where Castellón meets Teruel, and the whole thing is crowned by a castle that looks like it was carved from the summit. The medieval walls run for more than two kilometres, the main street is lined with arcades, and a Gothic basilica dedicated to Santa María holds a remarkable choir in the middle of the nave. Every six years the town holds a festival called the Sexenni, with processions and dancing in honour of the Virgin of Vallivana. Outside the festival years, the place keeps a quiet, mountain dignity, and the cheese, truffles and lamb are all good.

  5. 5. Galeria alta de la masia

    Morella is one of those hill towns that makes you pull over for a photograph before you even know what it is. Its castle crowns a steep crag, its walls run down the slope in a ragged line, and the old streets climb in a spiral to the top. It stands at around a thousand metres in the Els Ports highlands in northern Castellón, near the borders of Aragon and Catalonia, and the surrounding country is wide, cold, windswept and dotted with stone farmhouses. The rock shelters nearby preserve some of the most famous painted scenes of Levantine rock art, part of the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula, listed by UNESCO in 1998.
