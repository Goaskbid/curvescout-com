The stops

  1. 1. Ouarzazate

    Start here.

    Ouarzazate is a town built where the High Atlas gives way to the desert, and the light there is so strong and clear that the film industry came to rely on it. It sits on a high plateau at the southern foot of the mountains, with the pale red earth and flat-topped hills of southern Morocco spreading out in front, and it has been the location for films such as Lawrence of Arabia, Gladiator and numerous others. Huge studio complexes on the edge of town offer visits, with sets of ancient Egypt and Tibet standing in the dust, but the real attraction lies in the kasbahs.

    Read the complete Ouarzazate write-up.

  2. 2. Zagora

    The end: 159 km.

    A painted sign on the edge of Zagora famously announces Tombouctou 52 jours, the number of days it takes to reach Timbuktu by camel. Whether or not the journey was ever quite so precise, the sign sums up the town's place in history: the last major stop on the Draa valley route before the Sahara, where caravans carrying salt, gold, and slaves once gathered for the long crossing. Today Zagora has about thirty-five thousand people, a long avenue of shops and cafés, and a steady flow of visitors heading into the dunes.
