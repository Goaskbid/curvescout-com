Why this ride

curated scenic road. Pontic Mountains, extreme hairpins, remote valleys. A one-way ride of 79 km, Of to Bayburt - roughly 1 h 26 in the saddle. It goes by way of Çaykara and Uzungöl. The highest point is about 2,000 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Of

    Of is a name that sounds like an exclamation, and the town has had its fun with it. It is a busy Black Sea town of about thirty thousand people at the mouth of the Solaklı river, in the province of Trabzon, wedged between a steep green coast and the sea. The hills behind it are covered with tea bushes, hazelnut groves and dense forest, and almost everything is wet, because the eastern Black Sea gets some of the highest rainfall in Turkey.

  2. 2. Çaykara

    Çaykara means tea stream, which is a fair summary of what you see here. The town sits in a deep valley in the eastern Black Sea mountains of Turkey, in Trabzon province, and the slopes around it are covered with tea bushes in neat green terraces, clinging to hillsides that look too steep for any farmer. The air is damp, the clouds sit low, and the whole country smells of wet earth, woodsmoke and the faint bitter-sweet scent of tea leaves.

  3. 3. Uzungöl

    Uzungöl means long lake, and the lake in question was formed when a landslide blocked a river valley and the water backed up behind the barrier. It lies in a steep, forested valley in the Çaykara district, with spruce-covered slopes rising on every side and a mosque with a slender minaret sitting on the shore. On a still morning the mountains reflect in the water, and mist drifts across the surface, which is why the lake has become one of the best-known places on the Turkish Black Sea.

  4. 4. Bayburt

    Bayburt is a high, dry, windswept city on the upper Çoruh river, and after the green wetness of the Black Sea side it feels like arriving in another country. It sits at roughly fifteen hundred metres, in a plateau landscape of bare brown hills, wheat fields and clear skies, and its thirty thousand residents are used to winters that are long, cold and snowy. A castle dominates the town, a large fortress on a rocky ridge with walls that have been rebuilt many times, by Byzantines, Seljuks and Ottomans, and the view from the ramparts takes in the whole valley.
