Why this ride

A 182 km round trip from Samarkand - roughly 3 h 08 in the saddle. The stops in between: Jartepa II Temple, Sanjarshakh Settlement and back through Jartepa II Temple. The highest point is about 2,300 m. It crosses from Uzbekistan into Tajikistan - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Namazgoh Mosque

    Many Central Asian cities used to keep a namazgoh, an open prayer ground just outside the walls, where the whole male population gathered for the great feast days, in rows on the bare earth, led by an imam at a niche in a wall. The one at Samarkand lies on the southern fringe of the old town, a little way from the dense maze of lanes around the Registan, and it tells the story of how the city lived rather than how its rulers showed off. The structure, with its niche, a screen of arches and later additions, has been repaired and rebuilt over the centuries, so it is best seen as a layered place rather than a single period piece.

  2. 2. Jartepa II Temple

    The Zarafshan valley is the green artery of central Uzbekistan, carrying meltwater from the mountains of Tajikistan west past Samarkand and Bukhara into the desert. South of Samarkand, the land rises into the foothills of the Zarafshan range, and it is here, near Urgut and close to the Tajik border, that the Jartepa site lies, with remains of a temple from the Sogdian era.

  3. 3. Sanjarshakh Settlement

    Sanjarshakh lies in the Zarafshan valley of Tajikistan, a short ride from the town of Panjakent, and its low mounds and walls are the remains of a Sogdian settlement. The Sogdians were the great merchants of Central Asia, who spoke an Iranian language and carried silk, spices, paper and ideas along the Silk Road from China to Persia and beyond, and their language was the common tongue of the trade routes for several centuries. Their towns lay in the valleys of the Zarafshan and the Kashkadarya, and Panjakent, a few kilometres from here, is the best known, with houses decorated with wall paintings of epic stories, banquets and gods, that are now in the Hermitage in Saint Petersburg.
