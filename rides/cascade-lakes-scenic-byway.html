Why this ride

A 97 km loop that starts and ends near Bend, about 1 h 34 of riding. It goes by way of Mount Bachelor, Elk Lake, Lava Lake and Sparks Lake. The highest point is about 1,650 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Cascade Lakes Scenic Byway

Slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did. Water arrives beside the road and stays for 10 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bend Oregon

    Bend sits on the dry east side of the Cascades, where the rain shadow of the volcanoes gives way to ponderosa pine, juniper and sagebrush. The Deschutes River runs through the middle of town, and on one side of the city rises Pilot Butte, a cinder cone you can drive up for a view of the Three Sisters and the rest of the range to the west. The elevation is around 3,600 feet, so the air is thin and clear, and the sun is strong even in winter.

  2. 2. Mount Bachelor

    Mount Bachelor looks like a volcano drawn by a child: a clean, symmetrical cone that rises to just over nine thousand feet above the lava plains and pine forests of central Oregon. It is a young volcano, built in the last twenty thousand years or so, and sits at the northern end of a chain of cinder cones and lava flows that run along the eastern flank of the Cascades. Behind it, the Three Sisters and Broken Top fill the skyline with older, sharper, glaciated peaks.

  3. 3. Elk Lake

    Elk Lake lies at the foot of Mount Bachelor in a basin of fir and lodgepole pine, and it is one of the most reflective bodies of water in the Cascades. On a still morning, the cone of Bachelor and the pale spire of South Sister appear in the water as clearly as in the air, and the only disturbance is a fishing boat or a loon's call. The lake sits at around four thousand nine hundred feet, so the air is cool and the days short in spring and autumn.

  4. 4. Lava Lake

    The name tells you most of it. Lava Lake exists because a flow of molten rock once dammed the valley, trapping the spring water and snowmelt that seeped through the volcanic ground. It sits at about forty-eight hundred feet on the Cascade Lakes Scenic Byway, ringed by pine forest, with the Three Sisters visible on a clear day. Right beside it is Little Lava Lake, which is the headwaters of the Deschutes River, one of the best-known rivers in Oregon, and a quiet spring that gives birth to a major waterway.

  5. 5. Sparks Lake

    Sparks Lake is a photographer's lake, and the photographers know it. It is a shallow, irregular sheet of water on the Cascade Lakes Scenic Byway, with dead trees standing in the shallows and black lava rock along the shore, and at the far end rise the Three Sisters and Broken Top. At dawn, when the water is still and the light is soft, the whole range appears in the water with a clarity that almost looks staged. People arrive before sunrise with tripods and thermoses, and the lake is quiet and cold.
