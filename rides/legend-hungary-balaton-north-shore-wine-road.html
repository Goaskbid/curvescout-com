Why this ride

  1. 1. Keszthely

    The Festetics Palace, built in the eighteenth century by the Festetics family, has a famous library and a large park, and the town has a long academic tradition. The Georgikon, founded in 1797, is said to be the first agricultural college in Europe.

  3. 3. Tihany

    Tihany is a peninsula that pokes into Lake Balaton like a thumb, a hilly, volcanic outcrop in the middle of the largest lake in central Europe, and it narrows the lake almost to a strait. From the top, the water spreads out on both sides, with the vineyards of the north shore on one side and the low southern shore on the other, and on clear evenings the light on the water is unforgettable. The village sits on the ridge, a neat place of white houses and thatched roofs, with a Benedictine abbey at its heart.

    Read the complete Tihany write-up.

  4. 4. Balatonfüred

    Balatonfüred has been taking the waters since before anyone thought of calling it a holiday. On the north shore of Lake Balaton, Hungary's inland sea, this town of about thirteen thousand grew up around its mineral springs, which were known for treating heart conditions, and in the nineteenth century it became a fashionable resort for the aristocracy and writers. The old spa park and the promenade are still the heart of the place, and the long line of plane trees along the lakeshore makes a pleasant, shaded walk after a day on the bike.
