Why this ride

91 km round trip from Krasnoye Selo, about 1 h 49 of riding. It runs out through The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag, Oranienbaumsky Springboard, The Maximov's Datcha and The Alexandriysky Park and comes back by another road.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

The 19 km between The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag and Oranienbaumsky Springboard, mostly on 41К-274. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 61 and 126 m. Best in the early light, before the traffic.

The stops

  1. 1. Krasnoe Selo

    Start here.

    Readers of Anna Karenina will know Krasnoye Selo, even if they have never been within a thousand kilometres of it. It was here, at the summer camp of the imperial guards, that Tolstoy set the officers' steeplechase in which Vronsky rides his mare Frou-Frou to disaster in front of the court. For more than a century, from the early nineteenth century until the revolution, the guards regiments of the Russian army gathered each summer in this area south-west of Saint Petersburg for manoeuvres, parades and horse races, and the tsar and the court came to watch. It is listed as one of the monuments associated with the historic centre of Saint Petersburg.

    Read the complete Krasnoe Selo write-up.

  2. 2. The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag

    14 km in.

    Ropsha is one of those places where the scenery is gentle and the history is anything but. The village sits on the low Izhora Upland southwest of St Petersburg, between Krasnoe Selo and the coast road at Lomonosov, and the palace that gives the estate its name looks down over a chain of spring-fed ponds and a slow, wooded park. It was here, in July 1762, that the freshly deposed Emperor Peter III was held after his wife Catherine seized the throne, and here that he died a few days later under circumstances nobody at court cared to explain. Catherine became "the Great"; Ropsha became a footnote with a chill on it.

    Read the complete The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag write-up.

  3. 3. Oranienbaumsky Springboard

    32 km in.

    The Oranienbaum Springboard is not a single building but a piece of ground, and once you know the story the low woods and fields south of the Gulf of Finland look entirely different. In September 1941, as German and Finnish forces closed the ring around Leningrad, a pocket of coast around Oranienbaum, today's Lomonosov, held out. It was cut off from the city by land, supplied across the water and under the guns of Kronstadt, and it never fell. In January 1944 the Second Shock Army broke out of this pocket as one jaw of the offensive that finally lifted the siege.

    Read the complete Oranienbaumsky Springboard write-up.

  4. 4. The Maximov's Datcha

    51 km in.

    The south shore of the Gulf of Finland west of St Petersburg is a strip of dachas, palaces and old military ground, and the place marked here as Maximov's Datcha sits within it, a couple of kilometres from the town of Lomonosov.

    Read the complete The Maximov's Datcha write-up.

  5. 5. The Alexandriysky Park

    65 km in.

    The garden at Alexandria is a nineteenth-century romantic dream laid out at Peterhof for Tsar Nicholas I and his wife, Alexandra, a place of Gothic towers, winding paths and a small cottage palace by the sea. It lies a little to the west of the great fountains of the lower park, on the shore of the Gulf of Finland, and its mood is quite different: quiet, wooded and slightly melancholy. The Gothic Chapel, designed in the style of an English country church, stands among the trees, and the Cottage Palace beside it was a family retreat where the emperor could escape the formality of court life.
