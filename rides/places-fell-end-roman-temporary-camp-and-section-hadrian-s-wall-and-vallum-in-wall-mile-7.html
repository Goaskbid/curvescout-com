Why this ride

114 km round trip from Fell End Roman temporary camp and section , about 1 h 28 of riding. It runs out through Hadrian's Wall & vallum between field boun and Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 7 and comes back by another road.

The stops

  1. 1. Fell End Roman temporary camp and section

    Start here.

    Haltwhistle likes to call itself the centre of Britain, a claim based on measurements that no one outside the town quite trusts, but which has produced a very good tourist sign. The town sits in the South Tyne valley, a few kilometres south of Hadrian's Wall, and the wall and vallum between Fell End and the neighbouring crags form one of the most atmospheric sections of the whole frontier. The label here mentions a Roman temporary camp, and the district is full of such earthworks, built by marching legions as they moved through the area.

    Read the complete Fell End Roman temporary camp and section write-up.

  2. 2. Hadrian's Wall & vallum between field boun

    28 km in.

    Hadrian's Wall runs for about seventy-three miles across the north of England, from the Solway Firth in the west to the Tyne in the east, built on the orders of the Emperor Hadrian after his visit to Britain in 122 AD.

    Read the complete Hadrian's Wall & vallum between field boun write-up.

  3. 3. Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 7

    56 km in.

    It takes a little imagination to find the Roman frontier in Benwell, a suburb on the western hill of Newcastle, but it is there, tucked between semi-detached houses and bus stops. Wall mile 7 counts the distance from the Wall's eastern end at Wallsend, and in this part of the city the Romans built a fort called Condercum on the high ground above the Tyne, with fine views up and down the river. Most of it now lies under roads and housing, yet two small survivals make the detour worthwhile.
