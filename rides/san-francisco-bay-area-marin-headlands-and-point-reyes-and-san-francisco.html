Why this ride

842 km from Fairview to Richmond, about 14 h 18 of riding. The stops in between: Winters, in Kenwood, Bolinas and Daly City. The climbs: near Portola Valley and in Ashland.

The stops

  1. 1. Fuel stop near Fairview

    Start here.

    The hills behind Hayward rise steeply from the flat bay plain, and Fairview sits right on top of them, a semi-rural community of ranches, winding lanes and houses with long views across San Francisco Bay. On a clear day, you can see the bridges, the city skyline and the Peninsula hills beyond. It is a peaceful spot above one of the busiest urban areas in the country.

    Read the complete Fuel stop near Fairview write-up.

  2. 2. Near Winters, on the way back

    123 km in.

    West of Winters, the Sacramento Valley ends abruptly at the Vaca Mountains, and Putah Creek cuts a narrow canyon through them up to Monticello Dam and Lake Berryessa. This spot lies in that rugged, dry country a short way above Winters, where the valley floor of orchards and fields gives way to steep hills of oak, grey pine and golden grass, burned brown by summer and green after the winter rains.

    Read the complete Near Winters, on the way back write-up.

  3. 3. Food stop in Kenwood

    200 km in.

    Kenwood sits in the Valley of the Moon, the name Jack London gave to the Sonoma Valley, where he built his ranch and his ill-fated dream house, Wolf House, which burned before he could move in. The ruins and his grave lie in Jack London State Historic Park in nearby Glen Ellen, and the valley around them remains one of the most beautiful in California, with vineyards, oak woodland and the Mayacamas Mountains rising to the east.

    Read the complete Food stop in Kenwood write-up.

  4. 4. Bolinas

    297 km in.

    Bolinas does not want to be found, and it has made that clear for decades. Locals have a long tradition of removing the road sign that points to the town from Highway 1, so you may have to watch for the unmarked turnoff at the end of Bolinas Lagoon. The reward is a small, eccentric, salt-weathered village at the tip of a mesa on the Marin coast, with a few streets of old wooden buildings, a beach, surfers and an atmosphere that feels decades removed from the Bay Area just over the hill.

    Read the complete Bolinas write-up.

  5. 5. Near Daly City

    353 km in.

    South of San Francisco's city limits, the land runs out abruptly. Daly City's houses, the ones with the colourful matching boxes that inspired the song Little Boxes, cover the hills right up to the edge of high, crumbling sea cliffs, and below them the Pacific pounds a long empty beach.

    Read the complete Near Daly City write-up.

  6. 6. Half Moon Bay

    385 km in.

    Half Moon Bay is where the Peninsula's mountains finally meet the Pacific. The town sits on a wide coastal plain, backed by the green flank of the Santa Cruz Mountains and fronted by a long crescent of sand, and it feels like a different state from the busy bay side only twenty minutes away over Highway 92. Fog arrives most summer afternoons, so mornings are your best bet for sun.

    Read the complete Half Moon Bay write-up.

    420 km in. You top out here; the road stops climbing.

  8. 8. Back to Marin Headlands

    509 km in.

    That tells you something about how this ride ends: after a long day on the coast the route finishes on the far side of the water, among the suburbs that fill the shelf between the bay and the dry brown ridges behind. Fairview and Castro Valley are a few minutes away, Hayward a little further, and the whole district sits directly on the Hayward Fault, which is a fact most locals have made peace with.

  13. 13. South San Francisco

    690 km in.

    High on the side of San Bruno Mountain, giant white letters spell out "South San Francisco The Industrial City", a hillside sign put up in the 1920s and still the first thing most people notice when they arrive. It tells you the town's story: this was a place of meatpacking, steel, shipyards and factories, the working neighbour to the grand city to the north, and it has kept that practical, unpretentious feel even as its industries have changed.

    Read the complete South San Francisco write-up.

  14. 14. Montara

    710 km in. Richmond · Half Moon Bay and Montara Loop food stop is where this day stops to eat.

    For years, the stretch of Highway 1 just north of Montara was the road everyone worried about. Devil's Slide, a crumbling headland of rock above the Pacific, kept sliding into the sea and closing the highway, sometimes for months. Since 2013 the traffic has passed through a pair of tunnels bored through San Pedro Mountain, and the old road along the cliff has become a walking and cycling trail with some of the wildest views on the San Mateo coast. If you have twenty minutes, walk a piece of it.

    Read the complete Montara write-up.

  15. 15. Near Lagunitas-Forest Knolls, on the way back

    791 km in.

    The San Geronimo Valley runs west from Fairfax towards the coast, a narrow green valley of meadows, oaks and redwood groves, with small communities like Woodacre, Forest Knolls and Lagunitas strung along Sir Francis Drake Boulevard. It feels much further from San Francisco than it really is, and in the evening, when the fog creeps in from the ocean and the light goes soft and silver, it is magical.

    Read the complete Near Lagunitas-Forest Knolls, on the way back write-up.

  16. 16. Back to Big

    The end: 842 km.

    Richmond is the East Bay city that built ships for a war. Between 1941 and 1945 the Kaiser shipyards on the waterfront produced 747 vessels, more than anywhere else in the country, and tens of thousands of workers arrived from across the United States, many of them women and many of them Black, to fill jobs in the yards. The Rosie the Riveter WWII Home Front National Historical Park, at the old Ford assembly plant and along the shoreline, tells that story with museum displays and restored buildings, and it is a surprisingly affecting place to spend an hour.

    Read the complete Big write-up.
