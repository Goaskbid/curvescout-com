A 182 km round trip from Lamin - roughly 3 h 08 in the saddle. The stops in between: Six-Gun Battery, Saloum Delta and back through Six-Gun Battery. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. CFAO Building

    CFAO stands for the Compagnie Française de l'Afrique Occidentale, a big French trading company that built warehouses and shops across West Africa from the late nineteenth century, and its building at Albreda is the last layer of commerce in a place with many. By the time it was built, the slave trade was over, the French had given up the enclave to the British, and the money was in groundnuts, rubber and imported cloth, tinned goods and tools. The company's staff bought the crops from the farmers, graded them and loaded them on to river steamers, and in return sold a range of European goods, so the building was both a shop and a depot.

  2. 2. Six-Gun Battery

    The Gambia is a long, narrow country that follows its river, and Banjul sits at the mouth where the river meets the Atlantic, on a low, sandy island that feels like a sandbar with ambitions. The city was founded by the British in 1816 as Bathurst, a base to suppress the slave trade, and the Six-Gun Battery is one of the military remains from that era. It forms part of the UNESCO site called Kunta Kinteh Island and Related Sites, which also includes Fort Bullen on the opposite bank at Barra, and together they tell the story of the Gambia river as a route of the Atlantic slave trade and of the later effort to end it.

  3. 3. Saloum Delta

    The Saloum Delta is where the river Saloum and its arms dissolve into a maze of mangrove channels, sandbanks and low islands before meeting the Atlantic, about halfway between Dakar and the Gambian border. It is a flat, watery, shimmering place, where the horizon is a line of mangroves and the only things standing taller than a man are baobabs and the occasional village water tower. Locals call the narrow creeks bolongs, and they wind through the delta like veins.
