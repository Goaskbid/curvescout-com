Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Wellington SA

    Wellington is a farming town at the foot of the Groenberg mountains, and it sits at the start of one of the most beautiful passes in the Cape. It is known for wine, brandy and dried fruit, and long avenues of oak trees lead into its centre, with fields of vines and orchards beyond. Originally called Wagenmakersvallei, the Wagon-makers' Valley, it was renamed in the nineteenth century for the Duke of Wellington, and it grew into a centre of education and trade, with an old girls' seminary and a handsome Dutch Reformed church that give it a dignified air.

  2. 2. Bainskloof Pass

    Andrew Geddes Bain, a Scottish-born road engineer, built this pass in the middle of the nineteenth century, and a surprising amount of his work is still what you ride on. Bainskloof, completed in the early 1850s with the labour of convicts, links Wellington with Wolseley and the Breede River valley by cutting through the Limietberg mountains, and it was designated a national monument long ago. The stonework is the thing to notice: dry-packed retaining walls, culverts and bridges that have outlived most modern roads.

  3. 3. Ceres

    Ceres is fruit country, and the signs on the road say so, with apples, pears and peaches grown in the high valley of the Warm Bokkeveld. The town lies in a bowl surrounded by the Cape fold mountains, and the name is that of the Roman goddess of agriculture, which suits it. Orchards cover the floor of the basin, packing sheds and juice factories line the edges, and in blossom time in early spring the valley turns white and pink. Around forty thousand people live here, a mixture of farming families and seasonal workers.
