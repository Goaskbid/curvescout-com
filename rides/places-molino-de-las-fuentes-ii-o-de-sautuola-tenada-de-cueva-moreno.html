Why this ride

A 156 km round trip from Nerpio - roughly 2 h 41 in the saddle. On the way it passes Las Covachicas, Cueva del Gitano, Tenada de Cueva moreno and back through Cueva del Gitano. The highest point is about 1,400 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. molino de las fuentes II o de Sautuola

    Sautuola is a name that matters in this story. Marcelino Sanz de Sautuola, a landowner from Cantabria, was the man who in 1879 recognised that the bison on the ceiling of Altamira had been painted by people of the Ice Age, and he was laughed at for it, and he died without seeing himself proved right. A shelter near Nerpio carries his name alongside that of a mill, which is a rather lovely thing to find in a village in Albacete, some seven hundred kilometres from Santillana. It sits less than two kilometres from the village.

  2. 2. Las Covachicas

    The Sierra del Segura, in the far south of Albacete province, is a world of its own: deep valleys, rivers lined with poplars, terraces of olive and almond trees, and white villages that look more Andalusian than Castilian. Letur is one of the most beautiful, a village of Moorish origin built on a rock above a stream, with narrow alleys, arches and gardens watered by old irrigation channels. Las Covachicas, a group of shelters, lies in the hills nearby, between Letur, Socovos and Férez.

  3. 3. Cueva del Gitano

    Some places are remote on the map and remote in the gut, and the country around the Cueva del Gitano is both. It lies in the Sierra de Segura on the southern fringe of Albacete province, in the municipality of Nerpio, where the mountains of Albacete, Murcia, Jaén and Granada all run into one another. This is one of the richest areas for prehistoric rock art in Spain, with dozens of painted shelters along the valley of the Taibilla river and its side ravines, and the Cueva del Gitano is one of the smaller sites in that dense cluster. The art is faint, the walk can be rough, and the setting does most of the talking.

  4. 4. Tenada de Cueva moreno

    A tenada is a farm shed for livestock, the kind of low stone building that marks old transhumance routes, and the cave that goes with it is a local hollow in the limestone near Letur and Socovos in the south of Albacete province. This is the dry, hilly outer edge of the Sierra del Segura, where the pine woods give way to open scrub and cereal fields, and the horizon is a series of pale ridges. The label also links to the Letreros de los Mártires, a set of painted marks in a nearby shelter, which hints at the density of prehistoric art in this district.
