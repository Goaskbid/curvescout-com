Why this ride

A 73 km round trip from Spermezeu - roughly 1 h 16 in the saddle. The stops in between: Negrilești - Dealul Muncelului, Chiuiești - Dealul Podului and back through Negrilești - Dealul Muncelului. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Perișor - Ponoară în Vârf

    A ponoară, in the Romanian of these hills, is a sinkhole or swallow hole, a place where a stream vanishes into the ground. Ponoară în Vârf, which translates roughly as the sinkhole at the top, is a name that suggests limestone, and limestone country has its own character: dry ridges, hidden springs, small caves and sudden depressions in the meadows where the soil has collapsed.

  2. 2. Negrilești - Dealul Muncelului

    Bram Stoker never came to Transylvania, but he sent Jonathan Harker through Bistrița, and the town still enjoys the connection. Dealul Muncelului, a hill above the village of Negrilești, sits west of Bistrița in the Someș valley, and from here the road east leads through the Saxon town to the Tihuța pass, the Borgo Pass of the novel, where Count Dracula's carriage is supposed to have met his English visitor. It is a fine excuse for a ride, even if the vampire is fiction and the real history more interesting.

  3. 3. Chiuiești - Dealul Podului

    The hills above the Someș valley northeast of Dej roll away in long green waves, and the commune of Chiuiești sprawls across them in a scatter of hamlets, each tucked into its own fold of land. Dealul Podului, the Hill of the Bridge, is one of the ridges where traces of a Roman watchtower have been found, part of the chain of observation points that guarded the northern frontier of Dacia. The frontier was not a wall but a network of forts, towers and roads, and the Romans placed their towers where they could see far across the valleys and signal to one another. The site is one of the components of the World Heritage site of the Dacian frontier, although on the ground you will see little more than the shape of the hill and perhaps a low mound.
