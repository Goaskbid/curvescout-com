Why this ride

From Cieza it runs 92 km to Cieza; allow about 1 h 35 of riding. The stops in between: Abrigo de las Enredaderas, Cejo Cortado I and back through Abrigo de las Enredaderas. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Los Grajos II

    Before Cieza was Cieza, there was Siyasa. In the Middle Ages, when this part of Murcia was under Muslim rule, a fortified town stood on the hill above the Segura river, with a mosque, houses, a defensive wall and a castle, and it flourished from about the eighth to the thirteenth century. The ruins have been excavated and are now an archaeological site, with a few walls and foundations on the hillside, a reminder that the history of this valley goes much further back than the modern town. The ride also passes the Cejo Cortado, the Cut Ridge, a rocky outcrop in the same hills.

  2. 2. Abrigo de las Enredaderas

    Where the Río Segura squeezes through the sandstone and limestone ridges of northern Murcia, between Calasparra and Cieza, the valley turns into a canyon of orange and grey rock with narrow green strips of reeds and orchard along the water. The Abrigo de las Enredaderas, a rock shelter, lies twelve kilometres or so from both towns, in dry hills of pine, rosemary and esparto grass that smell sharply of resin on a hot day.

  3. 3. Cejo Cortado I

    Mula is a town in the heart of the Murcia region, built on the slopes of a hill crowned by a sixteenth-century castle, with an old quarter of narrow streets, noble houses and churches. Around it, a dry landscape of hills, ravines and badlands opens out, with orchards and olive groves along the river. The Cejo Cortado, a name meaning the cut brow, refers to a rocky escarpment with prehistoric shelters about seven kilometres from the town, between Mula, Albudeite and Ricote.
