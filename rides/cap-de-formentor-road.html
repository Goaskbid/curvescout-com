Why this ride

A one-way ride of 30 km, Port de Pollença to Port de Pollença - roughly 31 min in the saddle. The stops in between: Es Colomer and Formentor Beach. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Port de Pollenca

    Port de Pollença is the seaside half of a partnership, with the old hill town of Pollença six kilometres inland. The port sits on a wide, sheltered bay in the north of Mallorca, with the Tramuntana mountains on one side and the Formentor peninsula curving round the other, and the water is calm, shallow and a clear green-blue. A long, pine-shaded promenade runs along the waterfront, lined with cafés and ice-cream shops, and sailing boats and fishing vessels bob in the harbour. It has about six thousand people in winter, and many more in summer, and it feels like a holiday town without being too brash.

  2. 2. Es Colomer

    Es Colomer is the viewpoint that appears on every Mallorca postcard, and the postcards for once do not lie. The mirador stands on a bare rock ledge a couple of hundred metres above the Mediterranean, at the point where the Formentor peninsula narrows into a ridge of pale limestone, and the view down the cliffs to the little island of Illa des Colomer is dizzying. Gulls ride the updraughts at eye level. The water below is an unlikely shade between turquoise and navy, and the wind can be fierce.

  3. 3. Formentor Beach

    The beach at Formentor is a pocket of sand and pines in a bay on the north coast, facing the open sea on one side and the long finger of the peninsula on the other. It is not wild; the little hotel above it, the Hotel Formentor, was built in the late 1920s and became a rendezvous for writers, artists and well-heeled visitors. It later gave its name to a literary prize in the 1960s. But the setting is lovely, and the water is shallow, clear and cool.

  4. 4. Cap de Formentor Lighthouse

    The lighthouse at Formentor stands on the very end of the cape, some two hundred metres above the sea, in a spot that feels like the edge of everything. It was built in 1863, a squat white tower and keeper's house on bare rock, with only grass, thyme and a few twisted pines to soften the scene. On a clear day, you can see across the sea to Menorca on the horizon, and below your boots the cliffs fall in sheer walls to water so blue it looks artificial. The wind is almost always blowing, sometimes hard enough to rock a parked bike on its stand.
