Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Borobudur Temple Compounds

    The name Compounds is the clue to what makes this place unusual: Borobudur is not alone. The monument forms part of a larger sacred landscape in the Kedu Plain, where the river Progo and the river Elo meet, a setting that Javanese tradition regarded as a spiritual centre. Two smaller temples, Pawon and Mendut, stand to the east of the main monument in a straight line, and the three together were included in the UNESCO listing. Archaeologists still argue over exactly why they were aligned that way, but the idea that pilgrims once walked between them as part of a ritual route is widely accepted.

  2. 2. The Cosmological Axis of Yogyakarta and it

    Yogyakarta was laid out along a line. In the eighteenth century, Sultan Hamengkubuwono I founded the city on an axis running from the smoking cone of Mount Merapi in the north to the Indian Ocean in the south, with the Tugu monument, the Kraton palace and the Panggung Krapyak stage pavilion placed along it, embodying Javanese ideas about the cycle of life and the relation between people, ruler and the cosmos. The Cosmological Axis of Yogyakarta and its Historic Landmarks was inscribed on the UNESCO list in 2023, and the sultanate still functions: the sultan is also the governor of the Special Region of Yogyakarta.

  3. 3. Prambanan Temple Compounds

    Prambanan is the largest Hindu temple complex in Indonesia, a cluster of soaring stone towers on the plain between Yogyakarta and Solo, with the volcano Merapi brooding to the north. The central shrines were built in the ninth century and dedicated to the Trimurti: Shiva in the tallest tower, about forty-seven metres high, flanked by Brahma and Vishnu, with smaller temples for their mounts facing them. The walls are covered with reliefs of the Ramayana, carved so that you read the story as you walk clockwise around the galleries.
