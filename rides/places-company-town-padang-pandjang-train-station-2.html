Why this ride

From Tanjungampalu it runs 133 km to Tanjungampalu; allow about 2 h 18 of riding. The stops in between: Batu Tabal Train Station, Padang Pandjang Train Station and back through Batu Tabal Train Station. The highest point is about 900 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Company Town

    Sawahlunto is a town built in a bowl, and the company that built it made no secret about who was in charge. After coal was found in the valley in the 1860s, the Dutch colonial government set out to turn a handful of rice fields, which is what Sawahlunto means, into a mining centre, and the town grew up in layers around the pits. The officers' houses sat on the high ground with space and shade, while the labourers' quarters crowded closer to the work.

  2. 2. Batu Tabal Train Station

    High in the mountains of West Sumatra, a railway line built by the Dutch in the late nineteenth century snakes between volcanoes, lakes and rice terraces. It was laid to carry coal from the mines of Sawahlunto down to the port of Padang, and the whole system, mines, company town and railway, forms the Ombilin Coal Mining Heritage of Sawahlunto, listed by UNESCO in 2019. Batu Tabal is one of the small stations along the route, near the shores of Lake Singkarak, where the line runs right along the water's edge.

  3. 3. Padang Pandjang Train Station

    Padang Panjang is a cool, rain-washed town in the highlands of West Sumatra, in the land of the Minangkabau, at the foot of two volcanoes, Marapi and Singgalang, each rising to nearly three thousand metres. The railway station here is a relic of the Dutch colonial network built in the 1890s to carry coal from the mines of Sawahlunto to the port of Padang. The line had a difficult climb to make from the coast, and part of it used a rack system, with a toothed rail between the running rails and special locomotives gripping it to haul trains up the steep slope.
