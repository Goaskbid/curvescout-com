Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Andenes

    Andenes sits on the northern tip of Andøya, the northernmost island in the Vesterålen archipelago, and it is where the Arctic Ocean begins to feel close. The town is small, with about 2,700 people, a harbour, a lighthouse and a collection of low houses on a flat spit of land, and its main claim to fame is whales. Just off the coast, the continental shelf drops steeply, bringing deep water close to shore, and sperm whales feed there in summer, so boat trips run daily from the harbour. The Whale Safari is a well-established business, and sightings are common, though never guaranteed.

  2. 2. Near Andenes

    A few kilometres south of Andenes the road along the west side of Andøya drops to the shore at Bleik, and the view changes from flat bog to something close to the edge of the world. A long white beach curves away under steep peaks, the sea is a cold, startling turquoise on a bright day, and just offshore sits Bleiksøya, a steep pyramid of an island that holds one of the busiest puffin colonies on the Norwegian coast. In summer the birds wheel around it in thousands, and the boat trips from Andenes go out to see them.

  3. 3. Near Myre

    Down the outer coast of Andøya, between the bird cliffs of the north and the fishing village of Myre, the scenery gets quieter and more intimate. The road runs past a string of small settlements like Stave and Nordmela, each a cluster of houses, a few boats and a fish rack or two, and between them a coast of pale beaches, rocks polished by the surf and mountains that rise abruptly from the shore. It is one of those stretches where you want to stop every few minutes, and nobody minds if you do.

  4. 4. Norwegian Scenic Route Andoya point 4

    By now Andøya's boggy flatlands have given way to something more dramatic. The mountains of Vesterålen and the neighbouring island of Hinnøya crowd the view, and the road threads between water and rock, with fjords cutting in from the coast. The scene changes with every turn: bright blue water, steep scree slopes, pastures dotted with farm buildings, and above it all the cloud, which can sit low on the peaks or break open to show a sudden stripe of snow.
