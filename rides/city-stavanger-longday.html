Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Stavanger

    Stavanger is a town that has been rich twice. First it was sardines, when canneries lined the harbour in the nineteenth century and the smell of smoke hung over the waterfront. Then in 1969 the Ekofisk oil field was found in the North Sea, and the sleepy fishing and canning port became the oil capital of Norway. Today around 120,000 people live in the city itself and it feels cosmopolitan, with offices, restaurants and a sizeable international community.

  2. 2. Finnegården 1a-1b

    Finnegården is a name on the old wharf in Bergen, one of the properties in the long, gabled row of Bryggen, and it is worth stopping simply to understand what a property meant here. Each block was a tight cluster of wooden buildings running back from the quay, with storehouses, counting rooms and living quarters packed together, and each was owned and used by merchants from the Hanseatic League, the trading network of north German towns that dominated this coast.

  3. 3. Bryggen

    Bryggen is the face of Bergen, a row of narrow, tilting, brightly painted wooden gables along the north side of the harbour. UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site in 1979, and the postcards are accurate, though the reality is a little more interesting than the pictures because people still work and live in it.
