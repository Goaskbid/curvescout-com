Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. D707 north junction

    This is a point on the map rather than a town, in the dry country of southern Namibia where the Tiras Mountains lift out of the Namib's gravel plains. There is no village here, no shade and nothing with a name beyond the number of the road, and nobody lives close enough to appear on a list of nearby places. That is exactly the point. The D707 is a gravel road that runs through some of the emptiest and most beautiful terrain in the country, and the start of it feels like the moment the real trip begins.

  2. 2. Namtib Reserve

    The Namtib Biosphere Reserve lies in the southern Tiras Mountains of Namibia, on private farmland that has been set aside as a reserve for wildlife and desert landscape. There is no town nearby and no population to list, only red sand, rocky slopes and wide grass plains that run up against the mountains, with the Namib Desert lying to the west. The nearest place with real supplies is a long way off, which gives you an idea of the scale.

  3. 3. Sesriem junction

    Sesriem is the gate to the dunes. The word means six straps in Afrikaans, and the name comes from the early settlers, who tied six leather thongs together to lower a bucket into the narrow canyon here, which holds water long after the rains. The junction sits at the entrance to the Namib-Naukluft Park, in the heart of the Namib desert, and from it a tarred road runs sixty kilometres to Sossusvlei, a white clay pan surrounded by some of the tallest dunes in the world, which rise to around three hundred metres and glow orange and red at sunrise.
