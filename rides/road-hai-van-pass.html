Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Da Nang

    Da Nang is a port city on Vietnam's central coast, with a long beach, a wide river and a skyline that has changed dramatically in a generation. Where fishing boats and a French colonial quarter once stood, towers and bridges now line the Han River, and the Dragon Bridge, opened in 2013, spans the water in the shape of a golden dragon that breathes fire and water on weekend evenings. Behind the city, the green mass of the Son Tra peninsula rises above the sea.

  2. 2. Hai Van Pass

    Hai Van means sea cloud, and from the summit the name makes perfect sense: the pass is a saddle in the Truong Son range where the mountains tumble straight into the South China Sea, and the clouds gather on the ridge like steam from a kettle. At around five hundred metres, it is not high, but it marks a real boundary. To the north, the weather tends to be cool and damp, with grey drizzle in winter, and to the south, around Da Nang, it is warmer and brighter. Locals say the pass splits the country's climate in two, and for much of the year they are right.

  3. 3. Lang Co

    Lang Co is a long, thin sandbar of a village, with a lagoon on one side, the sea on the other, and the green wall of the Truong Son mountains rising behind both. It sits at the northern foot of the Hai Van Pass, on the road between Hue and Da Nang, and it has the relaxed, slightly sleepy air of a place that most travellers pass through on the way to somewhere else. That is a mistake, because the beach here is one of the loveliest in Vietnam, a wide curve of pale sand edged with casuarina trees, and the water is clear and warm for much of the year.
