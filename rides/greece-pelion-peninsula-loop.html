Why this ride

Out of Volos and back again: 47 km, roughly 45 min in the saddle. On the way it passes Makrinitsa, Chania Pelion, Tsagarada and Milies. The highest point is about 1,200 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Greece Pelion Peninsula Loop

The valley floor is behind you and the road starts working for its height at about 26.8%. Corners stack above one another where the ground allows nothing straighter — first and second gear more often than not. Look through each corner to the next one and let the engine do the holding.

Rider note

Check the pass is open before you commit to it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Volos

    Volos sits at the head of the Pagasetic Gulf, a calm sheltered bay in central Greece, and the Greeks say it was from here, from the ancient city of Iolkos, that Jason and the Argonauts set sail in search of the Golden Fleece. A replica of the ship Argo rests near the waterfront, a handsome piece of nonsense that still draws the eye. The modern town is largely newer than you would expect, because earthquakes in 1955 damaged much of it, and so there are many broad, practical concrete blocks. In the neighbouring Nea Ionia, the name recalls the refugees who arrived from Asia Minor in the 1920s and brought with them a taste for strong spirits and good food.

  2. 2. Makrinitsa

    Makrinitsa is called the balcony of Pelion, and the name is exact. The village hangs on a steep slope more than six hundred metres above the Gulf of Volos, and from its terraces and the central square you look straight out over rooftops and plane trees to the blue water, with the city of Volos small and distant on the shore. The square, shaded by a huge plane tree and cooled by a marble fountain, is the centre of village life, and the church of the Virgin, with its fine frescoes and carved iconostasis, stands beside it. In the late afternoon, when the sun slants through the leaves, it is one of the most pleasant places in Greece.

  3. 3. Chania Pelion

    Up above Makrinitsa, where the villages thin out and the forest takes over, the road reaches a wide mountain saddle called Chania at about twelve hundred metres. It is the closest Pelion has to a high alpine place, with a small ski area on the slopes and a scattering of cafés and lodges that serve walkers in summer and skiers in winter. The surroundings are a deep, cool forest of beech, fir and chestnut, dotted with clearings where mist drifts through the trees, and the air is crisp even in July. After the heat of the plains, it feels like a different country.

  4. 4. Tsagarada

    Tsagarada is not one village but four, strung down a steep wooded slope on the Aegean side of Pelion, each with its own square and church, and linked by lanes that wind through chestnut, plane and walnut trees. They climb from the sea toward the mountain crest, so that the highest hamlet has the smell of wet leaves and the lowest has a view over the water, and the whole thing feels more like a forest with houses in it than a village with trees. Streams run through the lanes and fountains splash everywhere. It is one of the wetter corners of Greece, and it shows.

  5. 5. Milies

    Milies owes its fame to a train. In 1903, the narrow-gauge railway from Volos to Milies opened, built by the Italian engineer Evaristo de Chirico, the father of the painter Giorgio de Chirico, who spent part of his childhood in Volos. The track is only sixty centimetres wide, and the little steam train, nicknamed Moutzouris, crawls up the mountain through tunnels, over stone bridges and around the slopes of Pelion, with views of the gulf. The most famous structure on the line is the high stone viaduct at Kalamaki, with its curved arches. The train still runs on some days in summer for tourists, and on those days the village fills with visitors.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Volos to Makrinitsa

Volos → Makrinitsa is 9 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Makrinitsa to Chania Pelion

Makrinitsa → Chania Pelion climbs about 1200 m in 4 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Chania Pelion to Tsagarada

Chania Pelion → Tsagarada drops about 1200 m over 14 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Tsagarada to Milies

Tsagarada → Milies is 9 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Milies to Volos

Milies → Volos is 23 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
