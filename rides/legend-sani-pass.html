Why this ride

A mountain pass from Underberg, South Africa to Sani Top, Lesotho, via South African border post. Key character: steep switchbacks; Drakensberg; high border. Surface: unpaved.

Scenic road chapters

Sani Pass

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 8 km this is Sani Pass, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

  2. 2. Sani Pass point 2

    The gravel begins where the comfort ends. Above Himeville the tar gives out and the Sani Pass road takes over, climbing along the valley of the Mkhomazana River toward the high wall of the Drakensberg. This is the lower part of the pass, close to the South African border post, and it is the stage where you find out how your bike likes loose stones, water crossings and ruts, before the real gradient starts.

  3. 3. Sani Pass point 3

    Somewhere in the middle of Sani Pass the road stops being a track and becomes a staircase. The gradient steepens, the hairpins come close together, and the whole route zigzags up a face of rock that looks too steep for any vehicle. This is the section the pass is famous for, where the gravel is at its roughest and the views down the valley are at their most dramatic.

  4. 4. Sani Pass point 4

    At the top of Sani Pass the ground levels off, and you are standing on the roof of southern Africa. The summit sits at around 2,874 metres, and the pass is the only road that climbs the Drakensberg escarpment between South Africa and Lesotho. The air is thin, the wind often sharp, and the landscape up here is open grassland and rock, without a tree in sight.
