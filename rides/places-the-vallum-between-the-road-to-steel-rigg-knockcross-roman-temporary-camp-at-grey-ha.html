Why this ride

A 382 km round trip from Haltwhistle - roughly 6 h 35 in the saddle. The stops in between: Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 7, Knockcross Roman temporary camp at Grey Ha and back through Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 7. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. The vallum between the road to Steel Rigg

    Steel Rigg is the place everyone photographs. The crag rises steeply from the road, with the Wall running along its top and dipping into a gap known as Sycamore Gap, where a single tree stood for generations in a perfect V of the hills, until it was felled one night in September 2023. The loss caused shock across the country, and the stump now sprouts new shoots. The vallum, the great ditch and mounds south of the Wall, runs beside the road at the foot of the crags, and here it is clearly visible, a long straight hollow in the field, with the mounds standing on either side.

  2. 2. Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 7

    It takes a little imagination to find the Roman frontier in Benwell, a suburb on the western hill of Newcastle, but it is there, tucked between semi-detached houses and bus stops. Wall mile 7 counts the distance from the Wall's eastern end at Wallsend, and in this part of the city the Romans built a fort called Condercum on the high ground above the Tyne, with fine views up and down the river. Most of it now lies under roads and housing, yet two small survivals make the detour worthwhile.

    The Solway Firth is shallow and wide on its Scottish side, and between Annan and Gretna the land is flat and green, with sheep and cattle, field boundaries lined with hawthorn, and long views across the water to the Lake District hills.
