The stops

  1. 1. Akhaltsikhe

    Start here.

    Akhaltsikhe means new fortress, and the fortress is the first thing you see: Rabati Castle, a large, restored walled complex on a rise above the Potskhovi river, with a golden-domed mosque, a church and a few towers, floodlit at night. The town of about seventeen thousand people lies in the south of Georgia, in the region of Samtskhe-Javakheti, where the green valleys of the Kura river meet the dry, high plateaus that run toward Turkey and Armenia. Vale, a smaller town, lies to the south.

    Read the complete Akhaltsikhe write-up.

  2. 2. Vardzia

    The end: 62 km.

    Vardzia is a whole town carved into a cliff. High above the Kura River, in the arid, rocky country of southern Georgia near the Turkish border, the face of Erusheti mountain is honeycombed with hundreds of rooms, passages, chapels and cells, the legacy of a monastery complex that was built in the twelfth century under King Giorgi III and his daughter, Queen Tamar. The idea was defence as much as devotion: a hidden refuge in the rock, tucked into the border zone against Mongol and Turkish threats. At its height it is said to have held thousands of rooms spread across many levels.
