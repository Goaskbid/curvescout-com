The stops

  1. 1. Roman Theatre

    Start here.

    The Roman theatre in Mérida is one of the best-preserved in the world, and it still does the job it was built for. It was commissioned around 16 to 15 BC by Marcus Agrippa, Augustus's right-hand man, and the semicircle of stone seats rising up the hillside could hold roughly six thousand spectators. Behind the stage, a tall double colonnade of Corinthian columns was rebuilt in the twentieth century from fallen fragments, and it is the part that most visitors photograph. The curved tiers face south-west, so the evening light turns the stone a deep gold.

  2. 2. Old Town of Cáceres

    71 km in.

    Cáceres has an old town so complete that film crews treat it as a ready-made set, and walking its streets at dusk, when the stone glows amber and the crowds have gone, you can see why. Inside the walls, built largely by the Almohads in the twelfth century, are dozens of fortified mansions, towers, churches and palaces, many built by noble families who made their fortunes in the conquest of the Americas. The towers once had battlements, but Queen Isabella ordered most of them cut down to curb the nobles' power. UNESCO listed the old town in 1986.
