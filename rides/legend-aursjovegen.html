  1. 1. Aursjovegen point 1

    The Aursjøvegen starts where the tarmac gets thin, and nobody has ever complained about that. It climbs out of the Sunndalen valley in a series of stone-lined hairpins, over bare mountainside and into a country of dammed lakes and wide views, with the peaks of Trollheimen and Dovrefjell on either side. Aursjøen itself is a reservoir, a long strip of dark water held by a dam, and the landscape around it has the stern, open quality of Norway's highest ground.

  2. 2. Near Sunndalsøra

    The valley climbing south out of Sunndalsøra toward the Aursjøen reservoir is one of the starting points of the Aursjøvegen, a mountain road that is less a scenic route than an engineering track from the age of hydropower. It runs from the steep, green valleys of Sunndal up onto a high, bare plateau, past reservoirs, dams and tunnels, and then down the other side toward Lesja in the Gudbrandsdalen. It is narrow, partly gravel, and open only in summer, and for riders who like adventure it ranks among the most memorable roads in central Norway.

  3. 3. Sunndalsøra

    Sunndalsøra is an industrial town in a spectacular setting, at the head of the Sunndalsfjord, where the steep valley of Sunndal opens out to the sea between mountains that rise more than 1500 metres on either side. It is dominated by the large aluminium plant of Hydro, one of the biggest and most modern in Europe, which has been the town's main employer since the mid-twentieth century and is powered by the hydroelectric stations in the mountains around it.
