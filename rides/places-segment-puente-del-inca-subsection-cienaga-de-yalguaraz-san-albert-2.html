Why this ride

On the way it passes Segment Ranchillos, Subsection Ciénaga de Yalguaraz-San Albert and back through Segment Ranchillos. The highest point is about 4,050 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Segment Puente del Inca

    The Puente del Inca is a bridge made by water and rock, and coloured by minerals. Over thousands of years, hot springs and the Las Cuevas river have deposited layers of orange, yellow and white sulphurous crust that formed a natural arch across the gorge, some fifty metres across. It stands in the high Andes of Mendoza province, at roughly 2,700 metres, on the main road between Argentina and Chile, and it was once part of the old trade route over the mountains.

  2. 2. Segment Ranchillos

    Ranchillos lies in the Uspallata valley in Mendoza province, Argentina, on the high road that runs west from the city of Mendoza toward Chile. The valley is a broad, dry basin at about 1,900 metres between the Precordillera and the High Andes, with poplars along the river, scrubby desert and bare peaks banded in rust, yellow and green. The famous Ruta 7, the road to the Cristo Redentor pass and the border, runs through it, and people have travelled this corridor for a very long time.

  3. 3. Subsection Ciénaga de Yalguaraz-San Albert

    Between the provinces of San Juan and Mendoza, the Andes of western Argentina stand up as a dry, enormous wall, and this stretch lies in their rain shadow, where the land is a mix of high steppe, stony ridges and green strips of irrigated valley. The air is clear and thin, the sky is a deep, flat blue, and the sun is fierce even in winter. To the south-west, over the border ridge, stands Aconcagua, the highest mountain in the Americas at almost seven thousand metres, though you will not always see it for the haze.
