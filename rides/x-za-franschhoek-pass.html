Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Franschhoek

    Franschhoek means French corner, and the story behind the name is one of the Cape's founding tales. In 1688, a group of Huguenot refugees fleeing religious persecution in France were granted land in this high valley by the Dutch East India Company, and they brought vines and winemaking skills with them. Their descendants still carry names like Du Plessis and Malherbe, the farms have French names, and a monument at the head of the village commemorates them with three tall arches and a statue.

  2. 2. Franschhoek Pass

    Long before there was a road, elephants walked this route, and the earliest track was known as the Olifantspad, the elephant path. The animals found the easiest way across the mountains between the Franschhoek valley and the high plains of the Theewaterskloof, and later hunters, traders and farmers followed them, until a proper pass was built in the nineteenth century. Today the R45 climbs the same line, and the thought of elephants lumbering up it gives the mountain an extra dimension.

  3. 3. Villiersdorp

    Villiersdorp is a small fruit town in a valley that remains out of most travellers' plans, which is part of the point. It lies in the Overberg region beyond the mountains from Franschhoek, near the large Theewaterskloof dam, with orchards of apples, pears and stone fruit stretching along the road, and a main street of low buildings and gardens. The town was laid out in the mid-nineteenth century and named for Pieter de Villiers, a local landowner, and it has kept the quiet, orderly air of a farming centre.
