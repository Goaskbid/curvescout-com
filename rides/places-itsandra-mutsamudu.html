Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Itsandra

    Itsandra is a town just north of Moroni on Grande Comore, the largest of the Comoros islands, and in old Comorian history it was a power in its own right. Before French rule, Ngazidja was divided among a number of sultanates, and Itsandra was among the most important, with its own ruler and a reputation for trade and seafaring. Today it is a coastal community of about three thousand people with a long beach, a mosque at the center and narrow lanes where children play football between walls made of volcanic stone.

  2. 2. Mutsamudu

    Mutsamudu, or Moutsamoudou, is the capital of Anjouan, called Ndzuwani by its people, one of the three islands of the Comoros, and it sits tucked against the north-west coast under steep green hills. The old town climbs the slope in a tangle of narrow alleys, with a medina of coral-stone houses, carved doors and whitewashed mosques, while the harbour below is busy with dhows, small cargo boats and fishermen. A fortress above the town recalls the days when Anjouan's sultans controlled a prosperous trade, and when the island had a reputation as a stopping place for ships on the route to India.
