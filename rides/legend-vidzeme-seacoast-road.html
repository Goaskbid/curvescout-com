Why this ride

The Gulf of Riga shore north-east from the city: pine, sand and fishing villages, with the sea on your left the whole way to the Estonian border.

Scenic road chapters

Vidzeme Seacoast Road

Slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did. Water arrives beside the road and stays for 12 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go.

  2. 2. Saulkrasti

    Saulkrasti means sun coast, which is a bold name for a place on the Baltic, and the town has spent more than a century living up to it. It lies about forty kilometres north of Riga, on a strip of white sand backed by dunes and tall pine woods, and it began as a string of fishing hamlets before the railway brought holidaymakers from the capital in the late nineteenth century. Today it is the main seaside resort of the Vidzeme coast, a town of wooden villas, a long beach promenade and a pier, with a gentle, slightly old-fashioned air.

  3. 3. Salacgrīva

    Salacgrīva sits where the Salaca river meets the Gulf of Riga, close to the Estonian border, and it has lived on fish for as long as anyone can remember. The river is known for its lampreys, eel-like fish that run upriver in autumn and are caught in traditional weirs, and the local people hold a lamprey festival and serve them grilled or smoked, an acquired taste that locals love. The harbour is full of boats, and the smell of smoke from the fish houses hangs over the town. Salmon, herring and sprats are also on the menu, and a plate of smoked fish with rye bread is the town's best advertisement.

  4. 4. Ainaži

    Ainaži is the last town in Latvia, and the road that ends here sits right on the border with Estonia, with the Estonian town of Ikla just across. It is a quiet place of about thirteen hundred people, set on the shore of the Gulf of Riga, with a harbour, a few streets of low wooden houses and a long, sandy beach backed by pines. The next town of any size is Salacgrīva, about twelve kilometres south, which has the fuel, shops and cafes, so top up there. Ainaži is the end of the Vidzeme Seacoast Road, and it feels like it: calm, a little windswept, and happily forgotten.
