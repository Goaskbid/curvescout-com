Why this ride

866 km from Valdez to the finish, about 11 h 58 of riding. On the way it passes Thompson Pass, Worthington Glacier and Glennallen. The highest point is about 1,200 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Valdez

    Valdez sits at the head of a fjord in Prince William Sound, shut in by mountains so steep and so high that the town gets some of the heaviest snowfall in Alaska. Sit on the harbour wall and glaciers hang on the peaks all around, with fishing boats, sea otters and the odd bald eagle in front of you. It is a small town of under four thousand people, but it has real weight, since it is the southern end of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline, where the oil pumped from the North Slope is loaded onto tankers.

  2. 2. Thompson Pass

    Thompson Pass sits at 855 metres, which sounds like nothing until you remember that the sea is only a few dozen kilometres behind you. The Chugach Mountains rise straight out of Prince William Sound, they catch every storm that comes in off the Gulf of Alaska, and this notch in the range is where the weather gets wrung out. The pass is famous for snow. It holds Alaska's records for snowfall, with a single winter in the early 1950s piling up close to a thousand inches, and the highway crews who keep it open will tell you stories you only half believe.

  3. 3. Worthington Glacier

    A few kilometres south of the top of Thompson Pass, the road swings round a bend and a river of ice is simply there, filling a side valley and hanging above a gravel bar. This is Worthington Glacier, and it is one of the few glaciers in Alaska you can reach on a motorcycle without anything more strenuous than putting the side stand down. There is a state recreation site with a parking area, a viewing platform and a short trail that leads up toward the terminus, close enough that you can see the blue colour in the crevasses.

  4. 4. Glennallen

    Alaska is big, and the Copper River Basin proves it. Glennallen sits in a broad, flat bowl of spruce forest and muskeg, ringed by some of the highest mountains in North America: the Wrangell Mountains stand to the east, with Mount Drum, Mount Sanford and the huge ice-clad bulk of Mount Wrangell, an active volcano that steams in cold weather. On a clear day the view from the highway is almost too large to take in, and a rider is aware of being a very small thing on a very long road.

  5. 5. Delta Junction

    Delta Junction is where two famous roads meet, and neither of them is in a hurry. The Richardson Highway, coming up from Valdez over the Alaska Range, joins the Alaska Highway here, and the milepost sign marks the official end of the wartime road that was built in 1942 to link the lower states with Alaska. A small visitor centre and a monument celebrate the achievement, and it is worth a stop to take in how recently that road was an engineering miracle.
