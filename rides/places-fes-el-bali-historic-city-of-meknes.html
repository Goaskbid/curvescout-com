Why this ride

A 317 km round trip from Fès al Bali - roughly 5 h 28 in the saddle. On the way it passes Archaeological Site of Volubilis, Historic City of Meknes, back through Archaeological Site of Volubilis and back through Historic City of Meknes. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fès El Bali

    Fès El Bali is the old walled city of Fez, founded at the end of the eighth century by Idris I and made a capital by his son Idris II, and it is often described as the largest car-free urban area in the world. Nine thousand lanes is the figure people quote for its maze, and anyone who has lost an hour there believes it. The medina fills a bowl in the hills, with roofs stepping down toward the Fez river, and from the heights above it the whole place looks like a pile of sand-coloured dice.

  2. 2. Archaeological Site of Volubilis

    Olive trees have surrounded Volubilis for two thousand years, and they still do. The Roman city, on a gentle slope below the Zerhoun massif north of Meknes, was the westernmost major town of the empire's province of Mauretania Tingitana, and it grew rich on olive oil, grain and the trade in wild animals for the arenas of Rome. Its ruins include a triumphal arch dedicated to the emperor Caracalla, a basilica, a forum, temples, and above all a series of houses with superb mosaics in their floors, showing Orpheus with his lyre, the labours of Hercules, Bacchus and Diana bathing. Volubilis became a World Heritage Site in 1997.

  3. 3. Historic City of Meknes

    Meknes is the city of Moulay Ismail, a sultan who ruled Morocco for fifty-five years from 1672 and decided that the capital needed to be as big as his ambitions. He built an imperial city of ramparts, gates, palaces, granaries and stables, using thousands of workers and captives, and the scale of it still startles. Bab Mansour, the great ceremonial gate, is covered in green and white tilework and marble columns taken from Roman Volubilis, and it stands over a large square called El Hedim, where storytellers and food stalls gather in the evening. The granaries, the Heri es-Souani, are a series of immense vaulted halls with walls several metres thick, where grain and animals were once stored, and the quiet inside is cool and heavy.
