Sichuan Tibet Highway G318

Why this ride

A one-way ride of 2870 km, Chengdu to Lhasa - roughly 38 h 36 in the saddle. On the way it passes Kangding, Litang, Batang and Nyingchi. The highest point is about 4,550 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Chengdu

    Chengdu is the sort of city where the main achievement is that people there have a good time, and the rest is background. The capital of Sichuan province sits in a wide, humid plain in western China, home to more than seven million people in the city proper and a lot more in the wider area. It has an easy, teahouse-and-mahjong rhythm, a pace the rest of the country envies, and a cuisine whose reputation is global. Sichuan pepper, the tingling spice that numbs the lips, flavours the hotpot, the mapo tofu and the dan dan noodles, and a rider arriving here should plan on eating well.

  2. 2. Kangding

    Kangding is where the Chinese road meets the Tibetan plateau. The town sits in a narrow gorge where two rivers join, at the foot of the Gongga massif, and for centuries it was the main trading point between Han China and Tibet, where tea from Sichuan was exchanged for horses, wool and medicinal herbs. The Tibetan name is Dartsedo, and the town remains a meeting place of two cultures, with Tibetan monasteries, prayer flags and markets beside Chinese shops and restaurants.

  3. 3. Litang

    Litang is one of the highest towns in the world, at roughly four thousand metres, a Tibetan settlement on a wide grassy plateau in western Sichuan, where the G318 highway crosses on its long journey from Chengdu to Lhasa. The mountains around it are rounded and bare, the sky is huge and the air is thin enough that every staircase becomes a conversation with your lungs.

  4. 4. Batang

    Batang lies on the edge of the Tibetan plateau, on a broad highland plain at about 2,600 metres, near the Jinsha river, the upper Yangtze, which forms the border with the Tibet Autonomous Region. The town is Tibetan in culture, with a large monastery, prayer flags on every hill and a population of Tibetan herders and traders, and it has been a staging post on the route between Sichuan and Lhasa for centuries.

  5. 5. Nyingchi

    Nyingchi is the green end of the Sichuan-Tibet Highway, and it comes as a shock after the bare plateau that precedes it. The Tibetan name is Nyingtri, and the town lies at about three thousand metres in the valley of the Niyang river, close to where the great Yarlung Tsangpo bends around the east end of the Himalaya. Here the air is thick and moist, the hillsides are covered in pine, cedar and rhododendron, and the nights smell of damp earth. It is often called the Switzerland of Tibet, a phrase that undersells it.

  6. 6. Lhasa

    Lhasa lies at about 3,650 metres in a wide, dry valley beside the Kyichu River, and almost every rider who arrives there from the east is a little dizzy from the altitude, a little sunburnt and more than a little humbled. The G318, known as the Sichuan-Tibet Highway, runs over two thousand kilometres from Chengdu, crossing a series of passes above 4,000 metres, plunging through gorges and following the great bend of the Yarlung Tsangpo before it finally settles on this plateau. Reaching the city is the culmination of one of the world's classic overland roads.
