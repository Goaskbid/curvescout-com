Why this ride

394 km from Oviedo to Oviedo, about 6 h 48 of riding. It goes by way of Chemin Primitif, Las Médulas and back through Chemin Primitif. The highest point is about 1,150 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cathédrale San Salvador et Chambre Sainte

    Oviedo wears its history lightly, and it is a surprisingly relaxed city, a green, rain-washed place of nearly a quarter of a million people at the foot of the Cantabrian mountains. Its Gothic cathedral, San Salvador, rises over the old town, but the real treasure is hidden inside: the Cámara Santa, the Holy Chamber, a small pre-Romanesque chapel built in the ninth century when King Alfonso II the Chaste made Oviedo the capital of the little Christian kingdom of Asturias. It holds the great relics of the Asturian monarchy, including two gold-and-jewel crosses, the Cross of the Angels and the Cross of Victory, which has become the symbol of the region, and a chest of relics that pilgrims came from across Europe to see.

  2. 2. Chemin Primitif

    Tineo stands on a hillside above the green valleys of western Asturias, and it is one of the main stops on the Camino Primitivo, the original Way of St James. According to tradition, it was King Alfonso II of Asturias who first walked from Oviedo to Santiago in the ninth century to see the tomb, newly found, of the apostle, and the route he took became the Primitivo. It is the hardest of the Caminos, crossing mountain after mountain, and the pilgrims you meet here, wet, muddy and cheerful, are a special breed.

  3. 3. Las Médulas

    Walk into Las Médulas on a clear autumn morning and you meet a strange kind of silence. The red crags stand around you like the ruins of a cathedral built from clay, and the chestnut trees, some of them centuries old, drop their husks onto the path. It is a landscape made by an empire and then forgotten by it, left to grow over for nearly two thousand years.
