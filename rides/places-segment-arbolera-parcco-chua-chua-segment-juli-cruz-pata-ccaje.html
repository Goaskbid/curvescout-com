Why this ride

From Zepita it runs 103 km to Zepita; allow about 2 h of riding. On the way it passes Segment Pomata-Chaca Chaca, Segment Juli-Cruz Pata-Ccaje and back through Segment Pomata-Chaca Chaca. It tops out around 4,100 m.

The stops

  1. 1. Segment Arbolera-Parcco Chua Chua

    Start here.

    At close to four thousand metres, on the Peruvian shore of Lake Titicaca, the air is thin enough that walking briskly feels like work and the light is so sharp it seems to sharpen the edges of everything. This stretch belongs to the Qhapaq Ñan, the great Andean road network that the Inca built and extended across their empire, and here it runs through Aymara farming country between the towns of Yunguyo and Desaguadero, both about twenty kilometres away, with the Bolivian border a short distance east.

    Read the complete Segment Arbolera-Parcco Chua Chua write-up.

  2. 2. Segment Pomata-Chaca Chaca

    28 km in.

    On the southern shore of Lake Titicaca, between Juli and the Bolivian border, the Inca road ran along the edge of the lake at nearly four thousand metres, linking Cusco with the lands of Collasuyu, the southern quarter of the empire. The Pomata section of the Qhapaq Ñan, part of the World Heritage listing of the Inca road system, follows the shore through Aymara villages, terraced hills and fields of potatoes, quinoa and broad beans, with the immense blue lake always beside you. The light at this altitude is intense, the air thin and dry, and the lake changes colour with every passing cloud.

    Read the complete Segment Pomata-Chaca Chaca write-up.

  3. 3. Segment Juli-Cruz Pata-Ccaje

    51 km in.

    Juli sits on the south-western shore of Lake Titicaca at close to 3,900 metres, and the air is so thin and clear that the far side of the lake, in Bolivia, seems close enough to touch. The town is nicknamed the Rome of the Americas for its cluster of colonial churches, built by Dominican and Jesuit missionaries in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, with carved stone façades and dim interiors full of paintings. Just outside town, the segment of Inca road between Juli and Cruz Pata Ccaje is part of the Qhapaq Ñan, the Andean road system inscribed as a World Heritage Site in 2014. This route, the Urcosuyu branch, ran along the lake towards what is now Bolivia.

    Read the complete Segment Juli-Cruz Pata-Ccaje write-up.
