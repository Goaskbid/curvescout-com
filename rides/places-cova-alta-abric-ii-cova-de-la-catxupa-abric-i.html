Why this ride

From Castell de Castells it runs 71 km to Castell de Castells; allow about 1 h 13 of riding. It goes by way of Abric de Seguili, Cova de la Catxupa- Abric I and back through Abric de Seguili. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cova Alta- Abric II

    On a clear day in this corner of Alicante you can see the long pale ridge of Aitana, the highest mountain in the province at about 1,558 metres, and the sea beyond it, which is a surprising combination for a country of rock shelters and mule tracks. Cova Alta II lies three and a half kilometres from Castell de Castells, in the high limestone country of the Serrella and Aitana massifs, and the name means high cave, which tells you what to expect: a climb. It is the second of the Cova Alta shelters, with Abric III and others nearby, and it forms part of the Mediterranean rock art listed by UNESCO in 1998.

  2. 2. Abric de Seguili

    The Vall de Pop, or Jalón valley as many foreign residents call it, is a sheltered valley in the north of Alicante province, a few kilometres inland from the coast at Dénia and Xàbia. It is a landscape of vineyards, almond and olive groves and orange trees, with white villages and the mountains rising on all sides. Alcalalí is one of these villages, with a medieval tower in its centre, and Parcent and Orba are nearby. The Abric de Seguilí lies in the hills just outside Alcalalí.

  3. 3. Cova de la Catxupa- Abric I

    The Marina Alta is the northern Costa Blanca at its most mixed: villas on every slope, a ribbon of coast running from Moraira to Jávea, and just behind it, farm terraces, dry stone walls and small rock shelters where prehistoric people left their mark. The Cova de la Catxupa, near Teulada and Benitachell, is one of them, a shelter in the hills a few kilometres from the sea, listed with the other Mediterranean rock art of the Iberian Peninsula.
