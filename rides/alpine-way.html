Why this ride

134 km from Jindabyne to the finish, about 1 h 52 of riding. On the way it passes Thredbo and Dead Horse Gap. The highest point is about 1,600 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Alpine Way

Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it. The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 1582 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Jindabyne

    Jindabyne has an odd secret: the town you drive through is not the original one. The old Jindabyne lies underwater beneath Lake Jindabyne, drowned in the 1960s when the Snowy Mountains Scheme dammed the Snowy River, and the whole community was moved uphill to its present spot. On a very dry year when the lake falls, you can sometimes see the old bridge piers. The lake itself is now the town's centrepiece, a big, blue, cold sheet of water backed by the Snowy Mountains and used for trout fishing, sailing and sitting on a bench with a pie.

  2. 2. Thredbo

    Thredbo is a ski village tucked into the upper valley of the Thredbo River in the Snowy Mountains of New South Wales, and in summer it has the slightly stunned air of a place waiting for snow. The chalets are built steeply on the hillside, the lifts stand idle, and the main street is quiet until the mountain bikers arrive. It sits inside Kosciuszko National Park, and Mount Kosciuszko itself, at 2,228 metres the highest mountain in Australia, is only a few kilometres away on foot.

  3. 3. Dead Horse Gap

    Dead Horse Gap is a saddle on the Alpine Way, high in the Snowy Mountains, where the road crests the ridge between the Thredbo valley and the country that drains toward the Murray. At about 1,580 metres it is the highest point on this road, and it feels like the edge of something. The trees thin out, the snow gums are bent and silvery, and the wind arrives straight from the Southern Ocean with very little to slow it.

  4. 4. Khancoban

    Khancoban is a town built for a purpose and still faintly wearing it. It was created in the 1950s to house the workers of the Snowy Mountains Scheme, the vast hydroelectric project that diverted rivers through the mountains and employed tens of thousands, many of them migrants from Europe. Today it is a small, tidy place on the western side of the range, close to the Murray River and the Khancoban Pondage, with a population of only a few hundred. It has what a rider needs, a pub, a shop, a place to eat and a few beds, but not much more, and the larger town of Corryong lies about twenty kilometres further west over the border in Victoria.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Jindabyne to Thredbo

Jindabyne → Thredbo is 37 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Thredbo to Dead Horse Gap

Thredbo → Dead Horse Gap climbs about 1582 m in 5 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Dead Horse Gap to Khancoban

Dead Horse Gap → Khancoban drops about 1582 m over 45 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
