Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Archipelago of San Lorenzo

    Out in the middle of the Gulf of California, in the channel that narrows between Baja California and the Sonoran coast, there is a scattering of rock islands that nobody lives on. San Lorenzo and San Pedro Mártir are two of them, protected as part of the Islands and Protected Areas of the Gulf of California. The Midriff Islands are famous among naturalists because the tides here move enormous volumes of water through narrow gaps, which churns up nutrients and feeds one of the richest marine systems on Earth. Whales, dolphins, sea lions, and clouds of seabirds all come for the harvest.

  2. 2. Islands and Protected Areas of the Gulf of

    Jacques Cousteau once called the Gulf of California the world's aquarium, and the number backs him up: more than two hundred and forty islands and a protected stretch of sea, listed by UNESCO in 2005, hold about a third of the world's marine mammal species. Blue whales and fin whales come into the gulf to feed in winter and spring, sea lions haul out on island rocks, dolphins ride the bow waves of boats, and whale sharks cruise in warmer months. The water is a startling turquoise against cactus-covered desert islands in the colours of rust, ochre and bone.
