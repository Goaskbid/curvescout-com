  1. 1. Tulcan

    Tulcán is the last big town in Ecuador, a high, cold border city at close to 3,000 metres with Colombia a few kilometres up the road. It is a place of traders, lorries and families crossing for the day, and its streets are busy with shops selling clothes, electronics and cheese from the surrounding dairy farms. The air is thin and crisp, the wind carries a chill, and a woollen layer is normal clothing even in the dry months.

  2. 2. El Angel

    The village of El Ángel is the gateway to one of Ecuador's strangest landscapes. It is a small farming town of around four thousand people in the northern province of Carchi, at roughly 3,000 metres, with a tidy main square, a church and streets lined with simple houses. A short ride above it lies the El Ángel Ecological Reserve, a protected area of páramo where thousands of frailejones, the tall, silver-green plants with furry leaves and yellow flowers, stand across the hills like a crowd of strange sentinels.
