Why this ride

From Barra it runs 220 km to Barra; allow about 3 h 48 of riding. On the way it passes Remains of Portuguese Chapel, Saloum Delta and back through Remains of Portuguese Chapel. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fort Bullen

    Take the ferry from Banjul and the first thing you see across the water is a low fort squatting at the river mouth, its cannon still pointing out toward the Atlantic. Fort Bullen guards the northern bank of the Gambia, in Barra, and it was built by the British in 1826 to keep watch on the slave ships that kept slipping past after Britain had outlawed the trade. The brick walls are modest and the guns are old, but the position says everything: whoever held this bank held the river, and the river was the Gambia's only real road into the interior.

  2. 2. Remains of Portuguese Chapel

    The Gambia is a country shaped like a river, a ribbon of land on either bank of a wide, slow stream that cuts deep into Senegal, and Albreda is a village on the north bank that tells the story of why Europeans came. The Portuguese were the first, in the fifteenth century, trading with the peoples of the river, and the remains of their chapel, a ruined stone and brick shell, stand among the houses. The French set up a trading post at Albreda in the seventeenth century, and the British, who held James Island, now Kunta Kinteh Island, a short boat ride away, fought them for control of the river trade. The trade was in gold, ivory, hides and, increasingly, human beings, and the slave trade from this stretch of the Gambia lasted some three centuries. UNESCO listed Kunta Kinteh Island and Related Sites in 2003.

  3. 3. Saloum Delta

    The Saloum Delta is where the river Saloum and its arms dissolve into a maze of mangrove channels, sandbanks and low islands before meeting the Atlantic, about halfway between Dakar and the Gambian border. It is a flat, watery, shimmering place, where the horizon is a line of mangroves and the only things standing taller than a man are baobabs and the occasional village water tower. Locals call the narrow creeks bolongs, and they wind through the delta like veins.
