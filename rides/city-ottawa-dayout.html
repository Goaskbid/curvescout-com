Why this ride

A 128 km round trip from Ottawa - roughly 1 h 49 in the saddle. The stops in between: Rideau Canal and back through Rideau Canal.

The stops

  1. 1. Ottawa

    Start here.

    Ottawa is a capital that still has the feel of a lumber town. It began in the 1820s as Bytown, named for Colonel John By, the army engineer who was sent to build a canal to link the Ottawa River to Lake Ontario, and the Rideau Canal, finished in 1832, still runs through the middle of the city. In winter it freezes and becomes one of the longest skating rinks in the world, with commuters gliding to work in scarves and hats, and in summer boats pass through the locks beside the grand Château Laurier hotel. The canal is a UNESCO site, and the whole waterway to Kingston is a good place to follow by bike.

    Read the complete Ottawa write-up.

  2. 2. Rideau Canal

    63 km in.

    Few canals in the world were built with such a mixture of fear and optimism. After the War of 1812, the British worried that the St. Lawrence, which formed the frontier with the United States, could be cut off in a new war, and so they decided to build an inland route from the Ottawa River to Lake Ontario at Kingston. Lieutenant-Colonel John By of the Royal Engineers was given the job, and between 1826 and 1832 thousands of labourers, many of them Irish, dug and blasted and built, working in swamp and forest and losing many to malaria. The result was 202 kilometres of linked rivers, lakes and cuts, with 45 locks, and it is the oldest continuously operated canal system in North America.
