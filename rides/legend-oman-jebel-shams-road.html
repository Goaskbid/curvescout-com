Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Nizwá

    Nizwá was the capital of Oman for centuries, and it still feels like the heart of the interior. About seventy thousand people live in this oasis city, at the foot of the Hajar Mountains, among date palms watered by falaj channels, the ancient irrigation systems that carry water from the hills by gravity, some of which have been in use for more than a thousand years. UNESCO has recognised the aflaj of Oman for their importance.

  2. 2. Oman Jebel Shams Road point 2

    Below the highest mountain in Oman, at the mouth of a great canyon, lies the village of Wadi Ghul, where the road towards Jebel Shams leaves the plains and begins to climb. An old mud-brick village crumbles on the rocky slope above the modern houses, its ruined walls the same colour as the mountain, and the wadi behind it is the start of a gorge so deep and steep that it is often called the Grand Canyon of Arabia. Jebel Shams, the Mountain of the Sun, rises above it to around three thousand metres, catching the first light each morning.
