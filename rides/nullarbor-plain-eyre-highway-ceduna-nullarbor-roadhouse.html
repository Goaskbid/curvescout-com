Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ceduna

    Ceduna is the last proper town before the long emptiness. It sits on the western side of the Eyre Peninsula in South Australia, facing Murat Bay, and for most people it is the place where the Nullarbor Plain crossing begins: more than a thousand kilometres of the Eyre Highway to Norseman in Western Australia, with roadhouses spaced out like stepping stones. The town itself is small, with around two thousand people, but it has the sort of weathered, practical atmosphere of a place that serves travellers and fishermen.

  2. 2. Penong

    Windmills, of all things, are what Penong is known for. This tiny settlement on the Eyre Highway, a couple of hundred people on the western edge of South Australia's Eyre Peninsula, pumps its water from underground using wind, and old farm windmills stand in rows along the roadside and in a small museum at the edge of town. In a place where the horizon is a straight line and the sky fills most of the view, they make a strangely graceful sight.

  3. 3. Nullarbor Roadhouse

    The Nullarbor Roadhouse stands in one of the emptiest stretches of the Eyre Highway, the sealed road that connects South Australia with Western Australia across the flat limestone plain. The name comes from the Latin for no trees, and the description is mostly accurate: the land is low, pale and covered in saltbush and bluebush, with a horizon so far away that it feels like the sea. The roadhouse is a small island of services in all that space, with fuel, food, accommodation and a campground, and it is a place where truckers and travellers meet and compare notes.
