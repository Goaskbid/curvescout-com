Why this ride

From Klingenstein it runs 300 km to Klingenstein; allow about 4 h 35 of riding. On the way it passes Fort site and civil settlement of Unterböb, Watchtowers WP 9/36 to 9/68 and back through Fort site and civil settlement of Unterböb.

The stops

  1. 1. Ehrenstein

    Start here.

    Ehrenstein is a small district on the western edge of Ulm, in the Blau valley, where the little Blau river flows from its spring at Blaubeuren towards the Danube. In the 1950s and 60s, archaeologists found here the remains of a Neolithic village, built on the damp ground of the valley floor around six thousand years ago, with wooden houses, hearths and remarkably well-preserved organic material. The site is part of the World Heritage list of prehistoric pile dwellings around the Alps, though there is nothing to see above ground today.

    Read the complete Ehrenstein write-up.

  2. 2. Fort site and civil settlement of Unterböb

    91 km in.

    The Rems valley runs east from Stuttgart towards the edge of the Swabian Jura, and Unterböbingen lies just outside the village of Böbingen, with Heubach three kilometres on and the long wooded wall of the Alb rising behind. The ground here is gently rolling farmland, orchards, small fields and the occasional church spire, the kind of country that looks entirely ordinary until you remember who once held it.

    Read the complete Fort site and civil settlement of Unterböb write-up.

  3. 3. Watchtowers WP 9/36 to 9/68

    149 km in.

    Between Öhringen and Mainhardt, the Upper German Limes does something a Roman engineer must have been proud of: it runs dead straight for many kilometres, up hill and down dale, ignoring the terrain as if drawn with a ruler on a map. The watchtower sites numbered along this section lie on that line, in the Hohenlohe country of northern Württemberg, between the open farmland around Pfedelbach and the dark wooded hills of the Swabian-Franconian Forest.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 9/36 to 9/68 write-up.
