  1. 1. Photo stop in Kukichūō

    Kuki is the kind of place that rarely appears in travel guides, a pleasant, low-rise city in the flat farmland of north-eastern Saitama, crossed by railways, rivers and expressways. Kukichūō is its central district, with shops, homes and offices, and it makes a practical stop on the long run north from Tokyo towards the mountains of Gunma and Tochigi.

  2. 2. Near Tokyo

    When the poet Bashō set out in 1689 on the journey that became The Narrow Road to the Deep North, he left from Senju, on the Sumida river, where friends saw him off. This finish lies in that same old northern edge of Edo, in Arakawa ward, a neighbourhood of narrow streets, small workshops and wooden houses that still feels more like shitamachi, the old downtown, than the glassy Tokyo of Shinjuku and Roppongi.

  3. 3. Back to Gunma

    Zama is home to an American army base, Camp Zama, which has been there since the 1940s and is the headquarters of the US Army in Japan. The Sagami river runs a few kilometres to the west, and Mount Oyama, a sacred peak, rises on the horizon on a clear day.
