Why this ride

170 km from Amman to Amman, about 2 h 51 of riding. On the way it passes Baptism Site “Bethany Beyond the Jordan”, Ancient Jericho/Tell es-Sultan, back through Ancient Jericho/Tell es-Sultan and back through Baptism Site “Bethany Beyond the Jordan”. It crosses from Jordan into Palestinian Territories - carry ID and check the toll rules.

The roads

Jordan Dead Sea Highway

Baptism Site “Bethany Beyond the Jordan” is 58 km from Amman, mostly on road 40. Mostly downhill - about 1320 m down, from a high point of 911 m. Water alongside for much of it - pick one good pull-out and stop.

The stops

  1. 1. Amman

    Start here.

    Amman is the starting point of Jordan's King's Highway, the old road that runs south through the country to the Red Sea, and it is also a city worth a day of its own. It is built on a series of hills, once seven, and the oldest part is the Citadel, which holds the pillars of a Roman temple to Hercules, a Byzantine church and an Umayyad palace, all on the same ridge. The ancient name was Philadelphia, after the Ptolemaic king who rebuilt it, and before that Rabbath Ammon, the capital of the Ammonites. Down in the valley below, a large Roman theatre, carved into the hillside and seating around six thousand people, still stages concerts.

    Read the complete Amman write-up.

  2. 2. Baptism Site “Bethany Beyond the Jordan”

    58 km in.

    Down here you are well below sea level, close to four hundred metres under it, and the air shows it: thick, warm and heavy, with a mineral haze rising off the Dead Sea a few kilometres to the south. The baptism site known as Bethany Beyond the Jordan, Al-Maghtas in Arabic, lies on the east bank of the river Jordan, the place many Christians identify with the baptism of Jesus by John. Across a narrow, muddy channel of water stands Qasr al-Yahud on the western side, close to Jericho, and the two banks look at each other across one of the world's most heavily watched borders.

    Read the complete Baptism Site “Bethany Beyond the Jordan” write-up.

  3. 4. Ancient Jericho/Tell es-Sultan

    85 km in.

    Jericho is one of the oldest continuously inhabited towns on earth, and at Tell es-Sultan, on its northern edge, archaeologists have dug down through layers of settlement more than ten thousand years old. Among the finds is a great stone tower, built in the Neolithic, around eleven thousand years ago, long before writing, metal or the wheel, which is often called the oldest such tower in the world. The tell, with its trenches and mud brick walls, was listed as a World Heritage Site in 2023.
