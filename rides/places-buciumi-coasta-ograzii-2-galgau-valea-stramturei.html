Why this ride

A 166 km round trip from Stârciu - roughly 2 h 52 in the saddle. It goes by way of Brebi - Dealul Mare 4, Cliț - Fața Chicerii, Gâlgău - Valea Strâmturei and back through Cliț - Fața Chicerii. It keeps close to the shore for long stretches.

Scenic road chapters

Buciumi - Coasta Ogrăzii 2 → Brebi - Dealul Mare 4

Slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did. Water arrives beside the road and stays for 24 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go.

  1. 1. Buciumi - Coasta Ogrăzii 2

    Plums are the quiet crop of Sălaj, and in the hills around Buciumi the orchards tell you so in every direction. In September, branches bend under purple fruit, the verges are littered with windfalls, and the smell of fermenting mash drifts from backyard stills, because this is where țuică is made. Țuică is the plum brandy of Romania, usually distilled twice, and served in small glasses with food. In Transylvania, the stronger, double-distilled version is often called pălincă.

  2. 2. Brebi - Dealul Mare 4

    The Meseș mountains are not high, but they once marked the edge of an empire. In the early second century the Romans conquered Dacia and built a line of defences along the ridge, with a chain of watchtowers, small forts and earthworks, to guard the north-western frontier of the new province. The big fortress at Porolissum, at Moigrad, was the headquarters, and in between were dozens of small posts on hilltops, each in sight of the next. Brebi, a village in Sălaj county, lies on this frontier, and Dealul Mare, the Big Hill, is the kind of name given to the high ground where such a tower would stand.

  3. 3. Cliț - Fața Chicerii

    Northern Sălaj is quiet country: low wooded hills, hay meadows, plum orchards and villages strung along the Someș river and its side valleys, with wooden churches and haystacks built around poles in the fields. Cliț is a small village near Băbeni, and the point named Fața Chicerii lies in the hills above it. Names in this list follow the pattern of the Romanian archaeological record, a village and then the local name of a hillside, and in this part of Sălaj many such points belong to the line of watchtowers and small forts of the Roman frontier of Dacia, which UNESCO recognised in 2024.

  4. 4. Gâlgău - Valea Strâmturei

    Strâmtură means a narrowing, and the name fits. Just outside Gâlgău, a small Sălaj village on the Someș, the valley pinches between wooded slopes, and the Roman frontier made use of exactly such pinch points to control movement along the river. The site listed as Valea Strâmturei belongs to that northern Dacian line, and like most of its neighbours it is earthwork and setting rather than standing masonry. You come for the geography and the sense of a border that ran through these quiet hills nearly two thousand years ago.
