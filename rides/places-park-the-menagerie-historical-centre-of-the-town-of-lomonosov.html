Why this ride

From Mariyenburg it runs 179 km to Mariyenburg; allow about 3 h of riding. On the way it passes Kievskoe Highway, The Monastery Troitse-Sergieva Pustin, Historical Centre of the Town of Lomonosov and back through The Monastery Troitse-Sergieva Pustin. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

Historical Centre of the Town of Lomonosov is 32 km from The Monastery Troitse-Sergieva Pustin, mostly on КАД. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 16 and 64 m. Water alongside for much of it - pick one good pull-out and stop.

The stops

  1. 1. Park «The Menagerie»

    Start here.

    Gatchina is one of the great imperial estates ringing St Petersburg, and the Menagerie, the Zverinets, is its wild back garden: a large walled tract of forest north of the palace that was laid out in the eighteenth century as a hunting reserve, with deer and other game kept for the court. Today it is mostly woods, ponds and paths, and on a hot day it is the coolest place within reach.

    Read the complete Park «The Menagerie» write-up.

  2. 2. Kievskoe Highway

    28 km in.

    South of Saint Petersburg, the flat, damp plain of Ingria runs off towards Pskov and the old western frontier, and the Kievskoe Highway is the road that heads that way, the route to Kyiv that gave it its name.

    Read the complete Kievskoe Highway write-up.

  3. 3. The Monastery Troitse-Sergieva Pustin

    54 km in.

    On the southern shore of the Gulf of Finland, between St Petersburg and Peterhof, the old road runs past a string of palaces and estates that the Russian elite built in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries to be near the sea and the court. Among them, near Strelna, stands the Trinity-Sergius Hermitage, a monastery founded in the eighteenth century as a dependency of the great Trinity Lavra of St Sergius near Moscow. It is part of the wide UNESCO listing of St Petersburg's historic centre and the monuments around it.

    Read the complete The Monastery Troitse-Sergieva Pustin write-up.

  4. 4. Historical Centre of the Town of Lomonosov

    86 km in.

    Lomonosov was called Oranienbaum until 1948, and its history is a story of ambition. Prince Alexander Menshikov, Peter the Great's close friend and favourite, began a grand palace here in the early eighteenth century, on the shore of the Gulf of Finland, and the name, meaning orange tree, came from its greenhouses. Later, the estate passed to the imperial family, and in the 1760s Catherine the Great had the Chinese Palace built in the park, a small, richly decorated pavilion with painted ceilings and exotic interiors. The ensemble is part of the UNESCO listing of Saint Petersburg and related monuments.

    Read the complete Historical Centre of the Town of Lomonosov write-up.
