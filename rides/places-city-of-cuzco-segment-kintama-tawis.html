Why this ride

From Cusco it runs 246 km to Cusco; allow about 4 h 14 of riding. On the way it passes Segment Patacancha-Huacahuasi, Segment Kintama-Tawis and back through Segment Patacancha-Huacahuasi. The highest point is about 4,200 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. City of Cuzco

    The Incas laid Cuzco out in the shape of a puma, they say, with the fortress of Sacsayhuamán as the head and the Tullumayo stream running along the spine, and while archaeologists argue about how literally to take the story, the city's plan still shows how seriously they took their capital. The name Qosqo means navel in Quechua, a statement of cosmology as much as geography: from here the empire's four quarters, the Tawantinsuyu, spread out in all directions.

  2. 2. Segment Patacancha-Huacahuasi

    Above Ollantaytambo, where the Sacred Valley of the Incas narrows, a side valley climbs north toward the high passes of the Urubamba range, following the Patacancha river. The segment between Patacancha and Huacahuasi is part of the Qhapaq Ñan, the Inca road system, and it crosses some of the most beautiful country in the Cusco region: steep slopes covered with potato fields and terraces, small villages of stone and adobe houses, herds of llamas and alpacas grazing in high pastures, and snow-capped peaks above. The communities of this area, like Patacancha and Huacahuasi, are known for their weaving, with intricate designs and bright colours, and the women's traditional dress is among the most striking in the Andes.

  3. 3. Segment Kintama-Tawis

    The Inca built a road network that stretched some thirty thousand kilometres along the Andes, from southern Colombia to central Chile, and much of it still exists as stone paths, staircases and causeways through the mountains. The Qhapaq Ñan, as it is called, was listed by UNESCO in 2014 as a set of segments across six countries. The Kintama-Tawis segment lies in the mountains north of the Sacred Valley of Cusco, in the high country between the valley and the cloud forests that descend toward the Amazon and the Manú national park.
