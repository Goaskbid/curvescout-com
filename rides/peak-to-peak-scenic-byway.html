Why this ride

86 km one way from Estes Park - plan on about 1 h 25 of riding. It goes by way of Nederland and Black Hawk. The highest point is about 3,150 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Estes Park

    Elk wander through the streets of Estes Park in autumn as if they had a lease on the place. During the rut, in late September and October, bulls bugle on the lawns and golf course, and visitors line up with cameras at a respectful distance, while the town itself, sitting at around 7,500 feet in a mountain bowl where the Big Thompson and Fall rivers meet, carries on with its cafes, fudge shops and outdoor stores. It is the eastern gateway to Rocky Mountain National Park and the starting point for Trail Ridge Road.

  2. 2. Nederland

    Nederland is a mountain town that has never entirely decided whether it is a ski-resort suburb, a hippie holdout or a mining camp that kept going. The answer is probably all three. It sits at about 2,500 metres where Boulder Canyon spills into the high country, at the edge of the Barker Reservoir, with the Indian Peaks on the horizon. Once you ride in from the canyon, the first thing you register is how cold the air has become, even in summer.

  3. 3. Black Hawk

    Black Hawk is the last place you would expect to find slot machines wedged into a gold-rush canyon, and yet there they are, stacked up the sides of Clear Creek in glass and brick. The town sits in Gilpin County, a short drop from Central City, in a steep cleft where gold was first struck in 1859. John Gregory found the lode that set off the Colorado rush here, and for a few years this narrow gulch was said to be among the richest bits of ground in the country. Mills clustered at Black Hawk to crush the ore, which is why milling, not gambling, was its first business.

  4. 4. Central City

    Central City climbs a steep gulch in the Colorado Rockies, a little brick town of a few hundred people at about eight and a half thousand feet, and it is a monument to the gold rush. In 1859, a prospector named John Gregory struck gold in the gulch, and within a few years, tens of thousands of people had arrived. The hills around were so full of ore that the town called itself the richest square mile on Earth, and the street names, the headframes, the tailings piles and the tilted miners' cottages still tell the story.
