Why this ride

From Alquézar it runs 106 km to Alquézar; allow about 1 h 50 of riding. It goes by way of Mascún V, Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña and back through Mascún V. The highest point is about 1,050 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Litonares E 3

    Every season has its own character in the Vero valley, and the riding is better in some than in others. In spring the almond blossom comes first, then the oak leaves open, and the hills turn the fresh green that makes the cliffs look more orange by contrast. The river is full from snowmelt in the Pyrenees, and the air smells of wet earth and rosemary. This is the best season, and Litonares E3, three kilometres from Alquézar, is a pleasant place to walk to in the morning light.

  2. 2. Mascún V

    The Barranco de Mascún is one of the great canyons of the Sierra de Guara, a limestone range in the pre-Pyrenees of Huesca, where rivers have carved deep, narrow gorges, caves and towers into the rock. The canyon, near the village of Rodellar, is famous among climbers for its overhanging walls and among canyoners for its pools and waterfalls, and it also holds traces of prehistoric painting in its shelters. Mascún V is one of these sites, part of the group of Mediterranean rock art sites listed by UNESCO, and the setting, with sheer orange and grey walls rising above the river, is extraordinary.

  3. 3. Abrigo de Santa Eulaliade la Peña

    Just north of Huesca, where the flat cereal land of the Hoya suddenly rears up into the first limestone walls of the Pre-Pyrenees, lies the little village of Nueno and, above it, the rock shelter named after Santa Eulalia de la Peña. This is the southern edge of the Sierra de Guara country, a landscape of gorges, conglomerate cliffs and scrub where griffon vultures ride the thermals and the light comes hard off pale stone.
