Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Les climats du vignoble de Bourgogne

    The Côte de Beaune and the Côte de Nuits are two ridges of gentle limestone, barely a hundred metres above the plain of the Saône, and they hold some of the most expensive agricultural land on earth. The phrase Les Climats du vignoble de Bourgogne refers to the small named vineyard plots, each with its own character of soil and exposure, that Burgundians have mapped and cultivated for centuries; the monks of Cluny and Cîteaux did much of the early work, noticing that wine from one plot tasted different from the next and writing it down. UNESCO recognised the whole system as a cultural landscape, and driving through it you can see why, because the walled vineyards, stone huts and village squares are all part of the same story.

  2. 2. Saline Royale d'Arc-et-Senans

    Claude-Nicolas Ledoux wanted to build an ideal city, and he started with a salt factory. The Saline Royale at Arc-et-Senans was built between 1775 and 1779 on the orders of Louis XV's administration, to boil brine into salt, and Ledoux designed it as a perfect half circle of buildings around the director's house, with the workers, the boiling halls and the management all arranged in a geometric order meant to express reason, hierarchy and efficiency. It never grew into the full circular city he imagined, but what was built is astonishing, and it was listed by UNESCO in 1982, later joined by the great saltworks at Salins-les-Bains.

  3. 3. Le Grand Lac de Clairvaux

    The Jura plateau behind Clairvaux-les-Lacs is a country of limestone, cows and lakes, and the Grand Lac de Clairvaux is the largest of the lakes that give the town its name. Prehistoric people lived on its shore on pilings, in villages that rose and fell for centuries, and the waterlogged mud preserved their wood and tools. The sites on Clairvaux's lakes are among the pile-dwelling places inscribed on the UNESCO list in 2011. The lake today is a quiet blue sheet, ringed by reeds and low wooded hills, with a beach where families swim in July.
