Why this ride

From Vall de Gallinera it runs 157 km to Vall de Gallinera; allow about 2 h 42 of riding. It goes by way of Barranc de Bolulla, Barranc del Salt- Abric l, Barranc de les Coves- Abric I and back through Barranc del Salt- Abric l. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barraac de Beniali- Abric Il

    Along the road through the Vall de Gallinera there are small signs for barrancs, ravines, and names such as Beniali, which most riders pass without a second glance. The Barranc de Beniali, a deep cleft in the limestone that runs down toward the Gallinera river, holds a number of shelters, and Abric II is one of them. About five kilometres from Villalonga, it lies in dry country of scrub and old terraced fields, where carob and almond trees grow wild among the rocks.

  2. 2. Barranc de Bolulla

    Bolulla is barely a village, a few hundred people tucked into a valley of the Marina Baixa behind Benidorm, and the ravine that carries its name cuts into the limestone hills just outside it. The mountains here are steep and close together, terraced for centuries with olives, almonds and loquats, and their caves and shelters hold traces of prehistoric painting that place this corner of Alicante within the wider family of Mediterranean rock art. What you see on the ground is a dry, rocky gully scented with pine and wild herbs, with the ruined castle of Bolulla perched on a ridge above, a reminder of the Moorish centuries when these valleys were densely settled.

  3. 3. Barranc del Salt- Abric l

    Benilloba is a tiny village in the Comtat, the mountainous district north of Alcoy in the province of Alicante, with fewer than a thousand people, a church and a cluster of stone houses among olive and almond groves. The Barranc del Salt, the waterfall ravine, cuts through the hills nearby, and its first shelter is one of the many painted sites in the region that are part of the UNESCO listing of Mediterranean rock art. The Comtat and the neighbouring Alcoià are particularly rich in this heritage, with all three main styles of prehistoric painting represented: Levantine, schematic and the older Macro-schematic, unique to this small area, with large, enigmatic figures.

  4. 4. Barranc de les Coves- Abric I

    Ibi is the toy capital of Spain, a fact that surprises everyone who rides through this mountain town in the hills of Alicante. Since the early twentieth century, workshops here have produced dolls, tin toys and plastic figures, and the Valencian Toy Museum tells the story. The rock shelter named Barranc de les Coves Abric I lies in the hills between Ibi and Alcoy, in a ravine whose name simply means the gully of the caves.
