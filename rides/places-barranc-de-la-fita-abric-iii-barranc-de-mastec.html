Why this ride

74 km from Famorca to Famorca, about 1 h 17 of riding. The stops in between: Coves de la Vila, Barranc de mastec and back through Coves de la Vila. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc de la fita- Abric III

    A barranc in this part of the Marina Alta is a dry ravine that carries water for a few hours after a storm, and the Barranc de la Fita has three shelters numbered along it. Abric III is the last of them, at the same spot as Abric I and Abric II, less than three kilometres from Castell de Castells. They are part of the Mediterranean rock art that UNESCO listed in 1998, and the density of such sites in a small area is one of the reasons the region is of such interest to archaeologists.

  2. 2. Coves de la Vila

    Planes sits in a cleft of the green hills above the Serpis river, in the Comtat, and the village of Beniarrés a few kilometres away is known for its reservoir, which fills the valley floor with a long, quiet sheet of water. The Coves de la Vila are a set of rock shelters in the cliffs above the village, and they belong to the Mediterranean Basin rock art that UNESCO listed in 1998. The paintings are in the Levantine style, red figures of hunters, goats and deer, and the shelters look out over the valley, giving you the same view as the people who painted them.

  3. 3. Barranc de mastec

    Between Cocentaina and Alcoy, in the hilly interior of Alicante province, a small ravine called the Barranc de Mastec holds rock shelters with prehistoric paintings, part of the UNESCO listing of rock art of the Mediterranean Basin. This corner of Spain is particularly rich in it, with styles ranging from the naturalistic Levantine figures of hunters and animals to the strange, abstract macro-schematic art that seems unique to these valleys. The ravine is surrounded by terraced olive and almond groves, pine woods and limestone crags.
