Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Anijima and peripheral reefs

    Anijima, whose name means elder brother island, lies a short boat ride off Chichijima in the Ogasawara or Bonin Islands, a chain of volcanic specks roughly a thousand kilometres south of Tokyo. Nobody gets here by accident. The only link to the mainland is the ferry that makes the journey of around twenty-four hours, running about once a week, and the whole group has never been joined to any continent. That isolation is why UNESCO listed it as a natural World Heritage Site in 2011, calling it an oceanic Galápagos.

  2. 2. Yomejima and peripheral reefs

    This is the one place on the list you cannot ride to, and it is worth being clear about that from the start. Yomejima is a small, uninhabited island in the Mukojima group, the northernmost cluster of the Ogasawara Islands, roughly a thousand kilometres south of Tokyo in the open Pacific. There are no roads, no people, no landing stage for visitors, and the reefs around it are part of the protected area that UNESCO inscribed as the Ogasawara Islands World Heritage site in 2011.

  3. 3. Nakodojima and peripheral reefs

    No road leads to Nakodojima, and for a good reason: it lies in the Pacific about a thousand kilometres south of Tokyo, in the Mukojima group of the Ogasawara or Bonin Islands, and it is a small uninhabited rock surrounded by reef. The name is a joke the old mariners would have enjoyed. In this group of islands, Mukojima means bridegroom island, Nakodo means matchmaker, and the nearby Yomejima means bride, so the three islets together make a wedding party. Access to the Mukojima group is restricted, and visitors need a permit or must go with a licensed tour.
