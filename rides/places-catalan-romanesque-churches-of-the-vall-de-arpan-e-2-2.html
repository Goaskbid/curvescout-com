Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Catalan Romanesque Churches of the Vall de

    There are nine churches in the Vall de Boí listed by UNESCO, and the surprise is that they are not museum pieces. They stand in working villages, with cows in the lanes, firewood stacked against the walls and the bells still ringing, and together they make one of the best-preserved groups of Romanesque churches in Europe. Sant Climent and Santa Maria de Taüll, the pair that everyone comes for, stand a few hundred metres apart on the hillside above the Noguera de Tor, and they were consecrated within a day of each other in December 1123. The others, in Boí, Erill la Vall, Cardet, Durro, Barruera and Coll, are quieter but each has a character of its own.

  2. 2. Cueva de Revilla

    Revilla is one of the most spectacularly placed hamlets in the Spanish Pyrenees, perched on a ledge above the Garganta de Escuaín, a deep gorge cut into the limestone of the Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park. It looks down into the canyon from a terrace of meadows, with the high peaks behind. The Cueva de Revilla is a cave in the cliffs near the hamlet, and the area between Bielsa, Plan and Boltaña is some of the wildest country in Aragón.

  3. 3. Arpán E 2

    The Río Vero is not a big river, but it has spent a very long time doing something clever with the limestone of the Sierra de Guara. Above Alquézar it has cut a gorge that narrows to a few metres in places and widens again into pools the colour of bottle glass, and the plateau on both sides is a dry country of box, holm oak and aromatic scrub that bears almost no relation to the water below. Arpán is a small side-valley three kilometres from the village, a short walk off the road, and its shelter is one of several in the Vero canyon system that carry painted figures from the ancient hunters of the Levantine tradition.
