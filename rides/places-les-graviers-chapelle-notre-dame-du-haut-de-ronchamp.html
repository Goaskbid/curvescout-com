Why this ride

From Peseux it runs 394 km to Peseux; allow about 6 h 31 of riding. It goes by way of Forêt de la Bettlachstock, Chapelle Notre-Dame-du-Haut de Ronchamp and back through Forêt de la Bettlachstock. It crosses from Switzerland into France - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Grand Tour of Switzerland

It is 132 km from Chapelle Notre-Dame-du-Haut de Ronchamp to back through Forêt de la Bettlachstock, mostly on N 19. It climbs 1500 m to the top at 1,081 m and drops 970 m again. Top up fuel at the bottom - pumps thin out on the way up.

The stops

  1. 1. Les Graviers

    Start here.

    Auvernier is a wine village with its feet in the water. Its old stone houses, cellars and narrow lanes climb from the shore of Lake Neuchâtel into vineyards, and the lake bed just off its little harbour holds the remains of prehistoric villages. Les Graviers is one of the sites in the UNESCO listing of prehistoric pile dwellings around the Alps, where archaeologists have found the traces of Neolithic and Bronze Age settlements, with wooden piles, tools, pottery and food remains preserved in the lake mud. Many of the finds from the region are displayed at the Laténium, a superb archaeology museum by the lake at Hauterive, on the other side of Neuchâtel.

  2. 2. Forêt de la Bettlachstock

    64 km in.

    Above the watchmaking towns of Grenchen and Bettlach, the first chain of the Jura rises like a wall out of the Aare plain, and on its steep southern flank grows the beech forest of the Bettlachstock. In 2021 it became one of the Swiss components of the UNESCO Ancient and Primeval Beech Forests of the Carpathians and Other Regions of Europe, a serial site that stretches across the continent to show how beech spread after the last Ice Age. What makes this patch special is that it has been left largely to itself for decades, on slopes so steep and rocky that nobody bothered to log them hard, and so the trees grow old, fall, rot and feed the next generation.

    Read the complete Forêt de la Bettlachstock write-up.

  3. 3. Chapelle Notre-Dame-du-Haut de Ronchamp

    198 km in.

    Notre-Dame-du-Haut sits on a hill above Ronchamp, at the foot of the Vosges, and it is one of the strangest and most beautiful buildings of the twentieth century. Le Corbusier designed it in the early 1950s and it was consecrated in 1955. The white walls curve and lean, the roof sweeps up like a hull or a hat, and the thick walls are pierced by small deep windows filled with coloured glass. Inside, light falls in coloured pools on the floor, and the whole room feels carved rather than built. There has been a pilgrim chapel on this hill for centuries, and the earlier church was destroyed in the war, which is what led to the commission.
