Why this ride

A 177 km round trip from Pego - roughly 3 h 03 in the saddle. It goes by way of Penyó de les Carrasques- Abric II, Barranc de les Coves- Abric I, Abric de la Penya and back through Barranc de les Coves- Abric I. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Benirrama- Abric II

    The Vall de Gallinera is a narrow valley in the mountains of northern Alicante, where eight small villages hang on the slopes among cherry trees, and Benirrama is one of them. The valley lies inland from the Costa Blanca, only an hour or so from the tourist beaches, yet it feels like another century. Terraced slopes, stone walls, irrigation channels and a thin, quiet road follow the Gallinera river, and in late winter and spring the cherry blossom covers the hillsides in white.

  2. 2. Penyó de les Carrasques- Abric II

    Cocentaina lies in the green hollow of the Comtat, a valley in the Alicante hills, with its palace and castle watching the Serpis river from the edge of town. Above and to the east rise limestone crags, among them the Penyó de les Carrasques, named for the carrasca, the holm oak that clings to dry slopes here. A rock shelter in the cliff, Abric II, preserves prehistoric paintings, part of the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin listed by UNESCO in 1998. As elsewhere in this tradition, the figures are small and fragile, and access can be restricted.

  3. 3. Barranc de les Coves- Abric I

    Ibi is the toy capital of Spain, a fact that surprises everyone who rides through this mountain town in the hills of Alicante. Since the early twentieth century, workshops here have produced dolls, tin toys and plastic figures, and the Valencian Toy Museum tells the story. The rock shelter named Barranc de les Coves Abric I lies in the hills between Ibi and Alcoy, in a ravine whose name simply means the gully of the caves.

  4. 4. Abric de la Penya

    Moixent, or Mogente in Castilian, stands at the head of a corridor that has carried traffic between the Valencian coast and the interior of Castile for as long as anyone has been moving goods. The old road, the railway and now the motorway all funnel through here, between low limestone ridges with vineyards and olive groves in the valley floor. The rock shelter known as the Abric de la Penya sits in this landscape a few kilometres from the town, close also to Vallada.
