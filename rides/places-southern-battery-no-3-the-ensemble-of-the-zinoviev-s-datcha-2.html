Why this ride

212 km from Kronstadt to Kronstadt, about 3 h 19 of riding. The stops in between: The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag, Krasnoe Selo, The Ensemble of the Zinoviev's Datcha and back through Krasnoe Selo. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

39 km from Southern battery No 3 to The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag, mostly on КАД. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 4 and 118 m. Best in the early light, before the traffic.

The stops

  1. 1. Southern battery No 3

    Start here.

    Sailors in the Gulf of Finland used to say that Kotlin Island was a long grey shape you saw only when you were already too close, and Southern Battery No. 3 is part of the reason the sailors came to respect it. It stands on the low, southern shore of the island, facing the channel that runs past Lomonosov on the mainland, one of a numbered line of coastal batteries that guarded the approach to Saint Petersburg from the sea. The batteries were built for guns and are now mostly quiet, grassy ruins of earth, brick and concrete.

    Read the complete Southern battery No 3 write-up.

  2. 2. The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag

    39 km in.

    Gostilitsy is a village on the Izhora Upland, west of Ropsha and south of Peterhof, and it is one of those places near St Petersburg where the imperial past hides behind fences and overgrown trees. The estate here is associated with Alexei Razumovsky, the Ukrainian Cossack singer who rose to become the favourite and, by many accounts, the secret husband of Empress Elizabeth in the eighteenth century. The palace and park ensemble that grew up around the manor included a house, a church, ponds and landscaped grounds fed by springs, and it is listed among the monuments of the historic centre of St Petersburg and related groups, a UNESCO site that includes many of the palaces and estates around the city.

    Read the complete The Palace and Park Ensemble of the Villag write-up.

  3. 3. Krasnoe Selo

    67 km in.

    Readers of Anna Karenina will know Krasnoye Selo, even if they have never been within a thousand kilometres of it. It was here, at the summer camp of the imperial guards, that Tolstoy set the officers' steeplechase in which Vronsky rides his mare Frou-Frou to disaster in front of the court. For more than a century, from the early nineteenth century until the revolution, the guards regiments of the Russian army gathered each summer in this area south-west of Saint Petersburg for manoeuvres, parades and horse races, and the tsar and the court came to watch. It is listed as one of the monuments associated with the historic centre of Saint Petersburg.

    Read the complete Krasnoe Selo write-up.

  4. 4. The Ensemble of the Zinoviev's Datcha

    106 km in.

    Here the Neva is a working river, broad and fast, carrying barges and the outflow of Lake Ladoga past factories and housing blocks on its way to the city. Yet on the left bank near Ust-Izhora, among the industry, survive the traces of a much older riverside life: the country houses, datchas in the old aristocratic sense, that Saint Petersburg's wealthy families built along the Neva in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, with parks running down to the water. The ensemble of the Zinoviev datcha is one of them, included in the long list of estates, parks and buildings that make up the World Heritage Site around the historic centre of Saint Petersburg.

    Read the complete The Ensemble of the Zinoviev's Datcha write-up.
