Why this ride

A 273 km round trip from Druento - roughly 4 h 34 in the saddle. The stops in between: Sacro Monte di S.Maria Assunta di Serralun, Grinzane Cavour Castle and back through Sacro Monte di S.Maria Assunta di Serralun. It tops out around 1,100 m. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Sacro Monte di S.Maria Assunta di Serralun → Grinzane Cavour Castle

It is 64 km from Sacro Monte di S.Maria Assunta di Serralun to Grinzane Cavour Castle on Autostrada Asti-Cuneo. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 114 and 331 m. This is the bit the ride is built around.

The stops

  1. 1. Borgo castello nel parco della Mandria

    Start here.

    The Parco La Mandria, north-west of Turin, is the largest remnant of the royal hunting estates of the House of Savoy, and the Borgo Castello at its centre was once the heart of the estate. The word mandria means a herd, and the park began as a stud and breeding farm for horses and cattle on the plain, with the woods kept for the hunt. Today the park is a protected area of about three thousand hectares, surrounded by a wall, where visitors walk or cycle between oaks, hornbeams and meadows, and where deer and wild boar still live.

    Read the complete Borgo castello nel parco della Mandria write-up.

  2. 2. Sacro Monte di S.Maria Assunta di Serralun

    72 km in.

    On a wooded hill above the vineyards of the Monferrato, between Casale and Asti, stands the Sacro Monte di Crea, one of the nine Sacred Mountains of Piedmont and Lombardy that are listed together as a World Heritage Site. The sanctuary of Santa Maria Assunta, a pilgrimage church, sits on the slope, and above it a path climbs through the woods past a series of chapels, each with terracotta statues and painted scenes from the life of the Virgin, to the Chapel of Paradise at the summit, with a crowd of figures depicting her coronation.

    Read the complete Sacro Monte di S.Maria Assunta di Serralun write-up.

  3. 3. Grinzane Cavour Castle

    136 km in.

    Count Camillo Benso di Cavour, the man who more or less engineered the unification of Italy, spent years as mayor of this little Langhe village before he went on to bigger things, and the square brick castle on its knoll still carries his name. He experimented with winemaking here too, at a time when Barolo was still finding its modern form, so the building sits at a neat crossroads of Piedmontese politics and Piedmontese wine.
