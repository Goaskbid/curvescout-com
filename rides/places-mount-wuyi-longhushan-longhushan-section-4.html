Why this ride

The stops in between: Longhushan: Guifeng Section, Longhushan: Longhushan Section and back through Longhushan: Guifeng Section. The highest point is about 850 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mount Wuyi

    Mount Wuyi is the home of some of the most famous teas in China, and the mountains that grow them are almost as remarkable as the leaves. Straddling the border between Fujian and Jiangxi provinces, the range is made of red sandstone and granite carved into steep cliffs, sharp peaks and deep gorges, with a winding river, the Nine-Bend Stream, flowing between them. The highest point, Huanggang Shan, is about 2,158 metres, the highest peak in south-east China. UNESCO listed the area in 1999 for both its nature and its culture.

  2. 2. Longhushan: Guifeng Section

    Sheer red cliffs rise out of the green hills of Jiangxi, and in some of them, high above a stream, there are dark rectangular holes in the rock. They are the cliff tombs of the Guyue people, who lived in this area about 2,600 years ago and who placed their dead in wooden coffins in caves and ledges on the face of a cliff above the Lushui river. How they hauled the coffins up the cliff is a puzzle that was never fully solved, and when archaeologists excavated some of them in 1979 they found well-preserved cloth, musical instruments and tools, as well as skeletons. Guifeng, the Ghost Peak, is the best-known site, and its name reflects the awe the place has long inspired.

  3. 3. Longhushan: Longhushan Section

    Longhushan means Dragon and Tiger Mountain, and the name is only slightly more dramatic than the scenery. In northeastern Jiangxi, thirty-odd kilometres from Guixi, the Luxi river winds between red sandstone cliffs and pinnacles that look like animals, towers and, with a little imagination, a dragon and a tiger staring each other down. The rock is Danxia, a reddish landform that China has recognised as a world heritage group of sites, and it glows when the sun is low.
