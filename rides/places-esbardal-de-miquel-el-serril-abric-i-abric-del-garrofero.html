Why this ride

A 209 km round trip from Castell de Castells - roughly 3 h 36 in the saddle. The stops in between: Barranc de les Coves- Abrigo l, El Pantanet, Abric del Garrofero and back through El Pantanet. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Esbardal de miquel el Serril- Abric I

    The Guadalest valley is one of the most photographed places on the Costa Blanca, and it is easy to see why. A narrow rocky spur rises from the middle of the valley, topped by the tower and ruined walls of the castle of Guadalest, and the village clings to the rock below, reached through a tunnel cut in the stone. Beyond it, a reservoir with a startling turquoise colour fills the valley floor, and the great grey wall of the Sierra de Aitana, at 1,558 metres the highest peak in the province of Alicante, rises behind. The village itself is crowded with visitors in summer, and many of the shops sell souvenirs, but at dawn the view across the valley is quiet and fine.

  2. 2. Barranc de les Coves- Abrigo l

    Castelló de Rugat sits on the southern rim of the Vall d'Albaida, a broad valley in the south of Valencia province hemmed in by mountain ridges. On one side rises the long saw-toothed crest of the Benicadell, on the other the Serra de la Solana, and between them lies a patchwork of vineyards, olive groves and small towns with church towers. The Barranc de les Coves, the ravine of the caves, cuts into the slopes near Castelló de Rugat, Ràfol de Salem and Beniarrés, and its first shelter holds painted figures.

  3. 3. El Pantanet

    Few riders have heard of the Serra de Mariola, and that is a pity, because this little limestone range between Alcoi, Bocairent and Ontinyent is one of the most rewarding corners of inland Valencia. El Pantanet, near the village of Alfafara, is a small rock shelter with prehistoric paintings, one of the many sites in the UNESCO listing of the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula. As with most of its sisters, it is protected and access may be limited, but the hills around it reward the trip on their own.

  4. 4. Abric del Garrofero

    Bicorp is a small village in the Canal de Navarrés, a valley in the interior of Valencia province walled in by rugged mountains and deep ravines. The surrounding Macizo del Caroig is one of the largest wild areas in the Valencian Community, a vast tableland of pine, scrub and gorges cut by the Júcar's tributaries. The Abric del Garrofero, the shelter of the carob tree, lies in the hills near Bicorp, Quesa and Navarrés.
