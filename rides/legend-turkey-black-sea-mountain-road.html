Why this ride

curated scenic road. tea country, Pontic Mountains, alpine lakes. A one-way ride of 130 km, Trabzon to Rize - roughly 2 h 22 in the saddle. On the way it passes Uzungöl and Çaykara. The highest point is about 2,200 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Scenic road chapters

Turkey D915 Derebasi Bends

Slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did. Water arrives beside the road and stays for 16 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Trabzon

    Trabzon looks north to the Black Sea and turns its back on the rest of Turkey, and the mountains behind it help. The Pontic Alps rise steeply from the coast, with cloud forests, tea gardens and hazelnut orchards, and the city climbs the slope in layers of apartment blocks and old houses. It was the capital of the Empire of Trebizond, a Greek Byzantine splinter state that formed in 1204 after the Fourth Crusade and survived until the Ottomans took it in 1461, outlasting Constantinople by eight years. The church of Hagia Sophia, a thirteenth-century building on a terrace above the sea with frescoes still visible inside, is the best reminder of that time.

  2. 2. Uzungöl

    Uzungöl means long lake, and the lake in question was formed when a landslide blocked a river valley and the water backed up behind the barrier. It lies in a steep, forested valley in the Çaykara district, with spruce-covered slopes rising on every side and a mosque with a slender minaret sitting on the shore. On a still morning the mountains reflect in the water, and mist drifts across the surface, which is why the lake has become one of the best-known places on the Turkish Black Sea.

  3. 3. Near Çaykara

    Çaykara means tea stream, which is a fair summary of what you see here. The town sits in a deep valley in the eastern Black Sea mountains of Turkey, in Trabzon province, and the slopes around it are covered with tea bushes in neat green terraces, clinging to hillsides that look too steep for any farmer. The air is damp, the clouds sit low, and the whole country smells of wet earth, woodsmoke and the faint bitter-sweet scent of tea leaves.

  4. 4. Rize

    There is hardly a hillside around Rize that is not covered in tea. The bushes grow in neat green terraces up to hundreds of metres above the coast, steep enough that pickers use ropes and the road clings to the slope in hairpins. Rize is the tea capital of Turkey, and this is also the wettest part of the country, with cloud and drizzle most of the year, which is exactly why the plants thrive. The town has a pleasant hill park above the centre where you can drink tea at a table with a view of the sea, and a small castle on the ridge above the centre gives a view over the roofs and the sea.
