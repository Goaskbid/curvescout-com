Why this ride

From Bloomfield it runs 357 km to Bloomfield; allow about 5 h 51 of riding. The stops in between: Aztec Ruins National Monument, Mesa Verde National Park and back through Aztec Ruins National Monument. It tops out around 2,450 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Twin Angels

    Start here.

    South of Bloomfield, on the edge of the badlands of Kutz Canyon, a small Chacoan great house sits on a ridge with views over the San Juan Basin. Twin Angels Pueblo is one of the outliers of Chaco Culture, a stone building with rooms and a kiva, built around a thousand years ago by people connected with the great centre at Chaco Canyon, about eighty kilometres to the south. It lies near the end of the Great North Road, the long, straight Chacoan road running north from the canyon, and it is part of the UNESCO-listed Chaco Culture. It is managed by the Bureau of Land Management, and reached by a rough dirt road and a short hike.

    Read the complete Twin Angels write-up.

  2. 2. Aztec Ruins National Monument

    38 km in.

    The Aztecs never came anywhere near Aztec, New Mexico. The ruins here were named by nineteenth-century settlers who assumed, wrongly, that such impressive buildings must have been made by the famous people of central Mexico. In fact, they were built by Ancestral Puebloans between about 1100 and 1300, as part of the great Chacoan world centred on Chaco Canyon, sixty miles to the south, and they are included with Chaco in the UNESCO listing of the Chaco Culture.

    Read the complete Aztec Ruins National Monument write-up.

  3. 3. Mesa Verde National Park

    180 km in.

    On a high sandstone plateau in the south-west corner of Colorado, the Ancestral Puebloan people lived for about seven hundred years, from roughly the sixth century to the end of the thirteenth, and in the last century or so of that time they built their homes in the shelter of the cliffs. Mesa Verde, the Green Table, takes its Spanish name from the pinon and juniper that cover the mesa top. The cliff dwellings, tucked beneath overhanging rock, are among the best preserved in North America, and Cliff Palace, the biggest of them, has around a hundred and fifty rooms and many circular ceremonial chambers called kivas.

    Read the complete Mesa Verde National Park write-up.
