Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sapa

    Sa Pa sits at about 1,500 meters in the Hoang Lien Son mountains of northwest Vietnam, and the mist that drifts in over its terraced valleys is half of its character. The French established a hill station here in the early twentieth century, building villas where colonial officials could escape the heat of the lowlands, and some of those stone buildings survive. Today it is a bustling market town for the Hmong, Red Dao, Tay and other mountain peoples, whose embroidered clothing, indigo-dyed cloth and silver jewelry make the Saturday market one of the most colorful in the country.

  2. 2. Cultural Landscape of Honghe Hani Rice Ter

    In the southern Yunnan prefecture of Honghe, on the slopes of the Ailao Mountains above the Red River, the Hani people have spent more than a thousand years carving rice paddies into the hillsides. The terraces climb from the valley floor at a few hundred meters to over a thousand, in layers so fine that from above they look like contour lines drawn on the land. UNESCO inscribed the Honghe Hani Rice Terraces as a Cultural Landscape in 2013, recognizing not just the beauty of the fields but the system that feeds them.
