Why this ride

From Birmingham it runs 330 km to Birmingham; allow about 5 h 41 of riding. It goes by way of Ironbridge Gorge, Derwent Valley Mills, back through Derwent Valley Mills and back through Ironbridge Gorge. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Birmingham

    Birmingham claims more miles of canal than Venice, a boast that is almost certainly true and that tells you something about the city. It grew in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries as a place that made things, buttons, guns, jewellery, steam engines, and the canals were the lanes that carried the raw metal in and the finished goods out. The thinkers of the Lunar Society, among them Matthew Boulton, James Watt and Joseph Priestley, met here at full moon so they could walk home by its light, and their dinner conversations helped shape the age of industry.

  2. 2. Ironbridge Gorge

    An iron bridge does not sound like much until you realise it was the first of its kind. In 1779, over the River Severn in Shropshire, the world's first major cast-iron bridge was completed, spanning thirty metres in a single semicircular arch, assembled with joints borrowed from carpentry. It was designed by Thomas Farnolls Pritchard and built by Abraham Darby III, whose grandfather, Abraham Darby I, had pioneered smelting iron with coke at nearby Coalbrookdale in 1709. That innovation lit the fuse of the Industrial Revolution, and the gorge that holds the works became one of its birthplaces. UNESCO listed the site in 1986.

  4. 4. Derwent Valley Mills

    The first factory in the modern sense stood at Cromford, in a narrow valley in Derbyshire, and the man who built it was a barber from Preston. Richard Arkwright's cotton mill of 1771 was the first to spin cotton with water power on a large scale, and it set a pattern that spread to the Midlands, Lancashire and across the world. The Derwent Valley Mills, the series of mills and villages that stretch from Cromford down to Derby, were listed by UNESCO in 2001 as the birthplace of the factory system.
