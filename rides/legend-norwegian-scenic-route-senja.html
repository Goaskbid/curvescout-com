Why this ride

Norwegian Scenic Route. Arctic coast, sharp mountains, fishing villages. 47 km from Gryllefjord to the finish, about 51 min of riding. It goes by way of Skaland and Norwegian Scenic Route Senja. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Gryllefjord

    Gryllefjord is a fishing village at the north-western end of Senja, Norway's second largest island, and it is about as far as you can go before the road gives out and the Atlantic takes over. It has fewer than five hundred inhabitants, a harbour, a small ferry quay and a handful of buildings, and the ferry runs from here across to Andenes on Andøya, a crossing of a couple of hours that is worth taking in summer. The mountains behind the village are jagged and steep, and the sea is a deep, cold blue. Finnsnes, on the mainland side of the island, is the nearest town of any size, nearly forty kilometres away.

  2. 2. Near Skaland

    Senja is Norway's second-largest island, and its outer coast is the closest thing the country has to Lofoten without the crowds: steep peaks rising straight out of the sea, small fishing villages tucked into the bays, beaches of pale sand, and a road that threads between them through tunnels and along ledges. The village of Skaland sits on the Bergsfjord on that outer side, and the road east of it climbs toward Bergsbotn, where a long wooden viewing platform reaches out from the hillside over the head of the fjord.

  3. 3. Near Skaland

    North of Skaland the outer coast of Senja gets wilder still. The road works its way along the shore between steep, bare peaks, cutting through tunnels where the mountains drop straight into the sea and emerging into small, sheltered bays where a handful of houses, a boat shed and a fish rack make up a village. Steinfjorden and Ersfjorden lie along this coast, each with its own narrow inlet and its own wall of mountains, and further north the road reaches Tungeneset, where a jagged ridge of peaks known as the Devil's Teeth rises from the sea.

  4. 4. Norwegian Scenic Route Senja point 4

    If you want to understand Senja, wait for the weather to change. Up here at nearly seventy degrees north the sky does most of the work, and a single afternoon can deliver sun, hail, a rainbow and a bank of cloud that rolls down the mountainsides like spilled milk. The fjords on this part of the island are deep and dark, and the peaks above them wear a skirt of low cloud more often than not.

  5. 5. Norwegian Scenic Route Senja point 5

    By now you have crossed to the eastern, more sheltered flank of Senja, and the character of the riding shifts. The mountains are still sharp, but the fjords are broader and the weather often a notch kinder than on the exposed western coast. The mainland town of Finnsnes lies about thirty kilometres away, linked to Senja by a long bridge that crosses the sound, and it is the natural hub of the area with shops, services and a proper selection of places to eat.
