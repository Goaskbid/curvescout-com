Why this ride

curated scenic road. high alpine, orchards, hairpins. From Merano it runs 85 km to Merano; allow about 1 h 33 of riding. The stops in between: Racines, Vipiteno and Sölden. The highest point is about 2,450 m. Passo Giovo and Timmelsjoch are open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

  1. 1. Merano

    Merano has a climate that surprises people. Sheltered by high mountains on three sides, it has mild winters and warm summers, and palm trees, cypresses and even a few vines grow in the town, which sits at about three hundred metres where the Passer river joins the Adige.

  3. 3. Vipiteno

    Vipiteno, called Sterzing in German, is a small walled town that has been collecting travellers for a very long time. It stands on the old route over the Brenner, where the road from the Eisack valley opens out into a broad basin before climbing to the pass, and it grew rich on trade and, in the late Middle Ages, on silver mining in the surrounding hills. The result is a main street that looks like a film set, with a long, curving line of arcaded houses in pastel colours, oriel windows, wrought-iron signs and, at one end, the Zwölferturm, a tall tower from the late fifteenth century that is the town's emblem.

  4. 4. Near Sölden

    Sölden lies at about thirteen hundred metres in the Ötztal, a long Tyrolean valley that runs south from the Inn into the high, glaciated mountains on the Italian border. In winter it is one of Austria's big ski resorts, and in summer it is a base for hikers, cyclists and riders heading for the Timmelsjoch. The village is a bit loud and a bit brash, with large hotels and plenty of apres-ski, but the setting is genuinely impressive, with peaks above three thousand metres on all sides and a river running clear and fast below.
