Why this ride

From Hengstdal it runs 70 km to Hengstdal; allow about 1 h 12 of riding. The stops in between: Kleve-Keeken, Till and back through Kleve-Keeken. It crosses from Netherlands into Germany - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. Open plateau country, wide and exposed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Nijmegen-Kops Plateau | East

    Under the suburbs of Nijmegen lies the oldest town in the Netherlands, at least according to Nijmegen, and on the Kops Plateau you can see why they say it. In the first century, the Romans chose this long ridge above the river Waal as a base for their legions. It was the perfect spot, a high sandy plateau with the broad river at its feet and a clear view across the flat country to the north. Their camp here, with ramparts, ditches and barracks for thousands of soldiers, was one of the largest military bases on the northern frontier of the empire.

  2. 2. Kleve-Keeken

    Here the Rhine stops being a German river. Just downstream of the village of Keeken, the great river crosses the border into the Netherlands, and a few kilometres further on it divides, with the Waal carrying most of the water west and the Pannerdens Canal leading to the Nederrijn and the IJssel. In Roman times, this was the northern frontier of the empire, the Lower German Limes, and Keeken is one of the sites listed as part of the UNESCO frontier, with traces of Roman military installations along the old river course.

  3. 3. Till

    The Lower Rhine around Kalkar and Kleve is wide, flat country: green polders, willows along drainage ditches, brick farmhouses and church spires visible for miles. Till is a small village here, part of Bedburg-Hau, just off the Rhine embankments between Kalkar and Kleve, with Emmerich across the river. It lies close to the line of the Roman frontier, the Lower German Limes, which followed the Rhine to the North Sea.
