Why this ride

From Benimassot it runs 148 km to Benimassot; allow about 2 h 33 of riding. The stops in between: La Sarga- Abric I, Barranc d'Alpadull- Abric I, La Sarga- Abric II and back through Barranc d'Alpadull- Abric I.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Coves Roges- Abric III

    Beniarrés is the key to this corner of the Alicante hills, a small town beside a reservoir in the mountains between Alcoi and the coast, and the shelter called Coves Roges, the Red Caves, lies a few kilometres to the north-east above the village of Planes. The name is honest: the rock here is stained in warm rust tones, and one of the overhangs carries painted figures in the old Levantine manner, listed by UNESCO along with the other rock art of eastern Spain. A neighbouring shelter, La Sarga, is the better-known site in the district and is often visited together with this one.

  2. 2. La Sarga- Abric I

    Alcoy, or Alcoi in Valencian, is a city of bridges, built where three rivers meet in deep ravines, in the mountains of northern Alicante. It was one of the cradles of Spain's industrial revolution, with textile and paper mills powered by its rivers, and its streets have fine modernist buildings from that era. It is also famous for its Moors and Christians festival in April, one of the most spectacular in Spain, with parades, music and mock battles in honour of Saint George. The rock shelters of La Sarga lie in the hills about eight kilometres from the city, toward Ibi.

  3. 3. Barranc d'Alpadull- Abric I

    The painted shelter of Barranc d'Alpadull sits in the folds of the Serra de Mariola, the limestone massif that rises between Bocairent, Alfafara and Agres in the inland corner where Valencia, Alicante and the old kingdom's hill country all meet. It is one of the many small overhangs in eastern Spain that carry Levantine rock art, the group of sites inscribed together by UNESCO as the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin. Do not expect a museum. These are faint, reddish figures under a lip of stone, usually fenced, often hard to read unless the light is low and raking, and part of the pleasure is simply knowing that hunters drew here thousands of years before anyone thought of building a road up this valley.

  4. 4. La Sarga- Abric II

    La Sarga is a deep, rocky ravine that cuts into the hills north of Alcoi, and it is one of the most important rock art sites in eastern Spain. The paintings here include both the lively red figures of the Levantine tradition and the simpler schematic signs of later peoples, and some show deer, goats and human figures with striking detail. The site belongs to the Mediterranean Basin rock art that UNESCO listed in 1998, and it is carefully managed, so check access conditions before travelling. Abric II, one of the shelters, is part of the cluster.
