Why this ride

A one-way ride of 448 km, Vic to Argentona - roughly 8 h 09 in the saddle. On the way it passes Ripoll mountain, La Molina overnight option and in Argentona. The climbs: Collada de Toses and la Barceloneta. It tops out at about 1,850 m. It is a high-mountain day.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Vic fuel gate

    Vic is the capital of Osona, a prosperous market town of nearly forty thousand people in a high, fertile basin about seventy kilometres north of Barcelona, and it is the natural stopping point on the way to the Pyrenees. The main square, the Plaça Major, is a huge cobbled space ringed by arcades and old houses, and on Tuesdays and Saturdays it fills with stalls selling vegetables, cheese and cured meats. The Saturday market has been a fixture for centuries, and it is a good place to buy a sausage for the road.

  2. 2. Ripoll mountain gate

    Ripoll is where Catalonia, in a sense, began. In 879 Guifré el Pelós, Wilfred the Hairy, the count credited with founding the Catalan dynasty, established the monastery of Santa Maria de Ripoll, and he is buried there. The carved Romanesque doorway, with its crowded scenes from the Bible and the lives of kings, is among the finest in Spain. Even if you only have time for a coffee, walk over and look at it.

  3. 3. Collada de Toses

    The Collada de Toses is a Pyrenean pass at about 1,800 metres, on the road between Ripoll and Puigcerdà, and it is one of those places where you feel the landscape change as you cross it. On the south side, the valley of the Freser is green and wooded. On the north, the high basin of the Cerdanya opens out, broad and bright, with snow-covered peaks to the horizon in winter and spring.

  4. 4. La Molina overnight option

    La Molina is the old ski village at the Catalan end of the Cerdanya, a high, sunny valley that the Pyrenees split between Spain and France. At roughly 1,400 metres, it is one of the early ski stations in Spain, with lifts that go back to the middle of the twentieth century, and today the slopes above it reach beyond 2,500 metres.

  9. 9. Castelldefels

    Castelldefels is where Barcelona goes to the beach, and where the coast suddenly stops being flat. Behind the town's long sandy shore, the limestone hills of the Garraf massif rise abruptly, and just south of town the coast road begins its famous run along the cliffs to Sitges. A castle on a small hill, rebuilt many times, gives the town its name and keeps watch over the plain of the Llobregat delta to the north.

  10. 10. Near El Prat de Llobregat

    The Llobregat delta is flat, green and noisy in equal measure. Jets climb out of Barcelona's airport over the sea, the city's port stretches along the coast to the north, and between them lie fields of artichokes, orchards, marshes and pine-backed beaches.

  12. 12. Vilassar de Mar

    Vilassar de Mar is a Maresme town with salt in its blood. It stretches along the coast northeast of Barcelona, between the railway, the main road and a broad sandy beach, with fishing boats pulled up on the sand and an old centre of narrow streets and low whitewashed houses. Behind the town, fields and greenhouses climb toward the wooded hills of the coastal range, and on a clear day you can see along the shore toward Mataró and back to Barcelona.

  13. 13. Back to Costa Brava

    A few minutes from the sea but already in the hills, Argentona and Cabrils are two of the pleasantest places on the Maresme. They lie in the folds of the Serralada Litoral, a low ridge of pine and cork oak behind the coast north-east of Barcelona, and their stone houses, old farmhouses and modernist villas speak of a time when wealthy Barcelona families built their summer homes here. The hills catch cooler air than the city, and the smell of pine resin hangs in the warm afternoons.
