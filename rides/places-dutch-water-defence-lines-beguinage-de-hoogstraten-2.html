Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Dutch Water Defence Lines

    The Dutch had a clever idea for defending their heartland: instead of building walls, they would drown the approaches. The Dutch Water Defence Lines, a World Heritage site, consist of a long belt of land around Amsterdam and Utrecht that could be flooded deliberately in wartime, with water deep enough to stop soldiers on foot and wagons, but too shallow for boats. Forts, dikes, sluices and pumping stations controlled the water. The New Dutch Waterline, built in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, ran south-east of Amsterdam through the area around Maarssen and the Loosdrecht lakes, and it was mobilised during the First and Second World Wars, though aircraft and modern artillery had made it largely obsolete by then.

  2. 2. Corbulo's canal | Knippolder

    In AD 47, a Roman general on a quiet frontier had a problem, which was how to keep his legions from getting restless. Gnaeus Domitius Corbulo, newly appointed to command in Lower Germany, solved it, according to the historian Tacitus, by making his troops dig. The result was a canal, the Fossa Corbulonis, linking the mouth of the Rhine with the Maas, so that ships could pass between the two rivers on a sheltered inland route rather than risk the open North Sea. It is one of the earliest known large engineering works of its kind in the Netherlands.

  3. 3. Béguinage de Hoogstraten

    A béguinage is one of the most peaceful places in the Low Countries: a walled enclosure of small houses around a green courtyard and a church, built in the Middle Ages for béguines, women who lived a religious life of prayer and work without taking permanent vows. They could own property, leave if they wished and support themselves by weaving, teaching or nursing, which made the béguinages a unique form of female independence in medieval Europe. The béguinage at Hoogstraten, in the north of Antwerp province near the Dutch border, is one of thirteen Flemish béguinages on the World Heritage list, and it is a quiet, charming place, with whitewashed houses, a baroque church and a garden where time seems to slow.
