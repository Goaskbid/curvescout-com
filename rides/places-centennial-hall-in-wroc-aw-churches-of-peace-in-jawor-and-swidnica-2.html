Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Centennial Hall in Wrocław

    A concrete dome sixty-five metres across looks like a modest thing to put on a World Heritage list, until you stand underneath it. The Centennial Hall in Wrocław was designed by the architect Max Berg and opened in 1913, to mark a hundred years since the Prussian uprising against Napoleon. At the time it was the largest reinforced-concrete building of its kind, and its ribbed dome, rising above a four-lobed hall and lit by a ring of windows, was a daring leap in engineering. Many early modernists took it as a sign of what concrete could do, and it still has the calm confidence of a building that did not need to apologise.

  2. 2. Lutheran Church of Peace under the invocat

    The Church of Peace at Świdnica is one of the most unusual churches in Europe, because the law demanded it be built cheaply and fast. After the Thirty Years' War, the Peace of Westphalia in 1648 gave Silesia's Lutherans, who lived under Catholic Habsburg rule, permission to build three churches, and only three, in the whole province, at Jawor, Głogów and Świdnica. They were not allowed to use stone or brick, nor to build a tower or a spire, and they had to be outside the town walls and finished within a year. The builders responded with timber, clay and straw, and with astonishing ingenuity. The church at Świdnica, completed in 1657, is a vast half-timbered building that can hold around seven thousand people, and its interior is a baroque wonder of galleries, painted panels and a gilded pulpit, more like a theatre than a church. UNESCO listed the churches of Świdnica and Jawor in 2001.

  3. 3. Churches of Peace in Jawor and Świdnica

    After the Thirty Years' War, Silesia belonged to the Catholic Habsburgs, and Lutherans in the region were allowed only the smallest favour. The Peace of Westphalia in 1648 allowed them three churches, built outside the town walls, from perishable materials, without towers or bells, and in a single year. The result, in Jawor, Świdnica and a third town that has since lost its church, was one of the most surprising buildings in Europe.
