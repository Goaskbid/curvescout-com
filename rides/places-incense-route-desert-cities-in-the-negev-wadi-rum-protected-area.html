Why this ride

The stops in between: Petra, Wadi Rum Protected Area and back through Petra. The highest point is about 1,150 m. It crosses from Israel into Jordan - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. Dry, open country - carry water.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Incense Route - Desert Cities in the Negev

    The Negev is not the empty wasteland it appears to be from a plane. From roughly the third century BC to the second century AD, the Nabataeans, a people of Arabian origin who made their capital at Petra, ran one of the most profitable trades in the ancient world through it, carrying frankincense and myrrh from southern Arabia to the Mediterranean ports of Gaza. They controlled the water, building cisterns, dams and terraced fields to farm in an area with almost no rain, and along the route they raised caravan stations, forts and small cities, among them Avdat, Haluza, Mamshit and Shivta, which UNESCO listed together in 2005 as the Incense Route Desert Cities.

  2. 2. Petra

    Petra is the most famous ruin in Jordan, and it earns the fame. The Nabataeans, an Arab people who grew rich on the incense and spice trade, carved their capital into the rose-coloured sandstone cliffs of a hidden valley more than two thousand years ago. You approach through the Siq, a narrow gorge about a kilometre long, with walls rising on either side until, at the end, the Treasury appears in a slice of light, a monumental facade cut straight out of the rock.

  3. 3. Wadi Rum Protected Area

    Wadi Rum looks like the moon dressed up in rust. Great walls of sandstone and granite rise sheer from a flat desert floor of red sand, cut with canyons and carved with arches, and the whole protected area covers hundreds of square kilometres south of Jordan's King's Highway and east of the Desert Highway. The nearest sizeable town is Quweira, about twenty kilometres away, and Aqaba lies roughly an hour south.
