Why this ride

Short alpine views with a lake-road finish. 84 km round from Zug - plan on about 1 h 36 of riding. It goes by way of Rigi foothills view, Brunnen lake and Axenstrasse awareness section. The climb of the day is Sattel high road. It tops out at 1,100 m. It is a high-mountain day.

Roadbook

  1. Zug lakeside exit photo · 413 m

    Zug sits on the northeast corner of its lake with the Alps behind it, and the first thing you notice is how neat everything is. The town has an old centre of lanes and arcades, a clock tower painted in blue and gold, the Zytturm, and a lakeside promenade that looks across the water to the long, green bulk of the Rigi. The town has become famous in recent decades as a corporate and financial centre, drawn by some of the lowest taxes in Switzerland, and a number of cryptocurrency firms have given the area the nickname of Crypto Valley. Yet the old streets still feel local and calm.

    Zug is also known for its cherry cake, the Zuger Kirschtorte, a layered sponge of almond meringue, buttercream and cherry brandy that is a local pride, and for the Röteli, a small char that lives in the deep, cold lake. Restaurants along the shore serve it fried or grilled, with a glass of white wine and a view of the sunset. The lake is clean enough to swim in during the summer, and the lakefront has pontoons, diving boards and shaded lawns.

    Across the lake, the Rigi, known as the queen of mountains, can be glimpsed on most clear days. In 1871 the first mountain railway in Europe opened on its slopes, at Vitznau, and trains still climb to the summit. The ride toward it from Zug runs along the lake and through orchards and farms, with views of the water and the peaks, and the roads bend gently between villages. Traffic can be busy near the town, particularly at commuting hours and on summer weekends, so aim for a quiet window.

    As the exit of a day's ride, Zug is a pleasure. At 413 metres it is warm and sheltered, and fuel stations and parking are easy to find. A coffee on the lakefront, with the sun low over the water and the Rigi in silhouette, is hard to beat. Locals stroll the promenade at dusk, and the evening feels slow and civilised.

  2. Rigi foothills view photo · 1100 m

    Above Arth, where the lower slopes of the Rigi begin to steepen, the land opens to a balcony over Lake Zug. At around eleven hundred metres you are not on the summit, which sits higher and attracts the crowds, but you are high enough to see the lake lying like a bent blade below, the rooftops of Arth and Immensee, and on a clear day the long line of Jura hills far to the west. The Rigi is a strange mountain, an isolated block of conglomerate rock rising from lowland lakes, so that views fall away on almost every side without any competing peaks.

    The Rigi earned the title Queen of the Mountains in the nineteenth century, when it became one of the first mass-tourist summits in Europe. The Vitznau-Rigi railway opened in 1871 as the first mountain rack railway on the continent, and the Arth-Rigi line followed a few years later. Travellers from Britain came in numbers to see the sunrise from the top, and Mark Twain wrote a funny account of climbing it. You can still catch that spirit at the old hotels and the wooden stations, though today the crowd is more likely to be hikers than Victorian gentlemen in boots.

    For a rider, the foothill roads above Arth are about tempo and outlook. They climb in steady gradients through orchards and meadows, with good tarmac, wide enough bends and farmhouses with big overhanging roofs. In spring the fruit trees bloom pale and pink, and in late summer the lower slopes smell of cut hay. Traffic is moderate, and walkers sometimes use the verges, so keep the speed sensible on blind corners.

    Arth, about three kilometres away, is the nearest town of size, with fuel, bakeries and places to eat. Goldau and Küssnacht are also close.

    The weather on the Rigi can be local and sudden. Low cloud, called the fog sea in autumn, often sits over the lakes while the hill stands in sun. If you wake to grey in the valley, ride up anyway. You may find yourself above the lid, with the whole world white beneath you and the peaks poking through like islands. Bring a layer, because the air at eleven hundred metres is cooler than it looks from the lake.

  3. Brunnen lake food food · 435 m

    Brunnen is a lakeside town on the Urnersee, the southern arm of Lake Lucerne, and it sits where the water bends sharply around the cliffs and the Alps appear in layers. The setting is dramatic. Mountains drop straight into the lake on both sides, the water runs deep green or blue depending on the sky, and the opposite shore with its steep forests and little white villages looks almost too composed. It is also a historic place. In 1315 the early Swiss cantons renewed their alliance here after the battle of Morgarten, an agreement known as the Pact of Brunnen, and the town has a long record of hosting meetings, merchants and, later, tourists.

    Today Brunnen is a resort town with a promenade, a harbour with steamers, and a main street of cafés and hotels from the age when visiting the Alps was fashionable. The lake climate is mild, and you will sometimes see palms and other warm-weather plants in gardens, a surprise in a landscape of cold peaks. The föhn wind, a warm dry gust that comes over the Alps, can be strong here, making the lake choppy and bringing sharp clear views of the mountains. Locals often claim to feel the föhn in their heads, and some blame it for bad moods.

    For riders, Brunnen is a natural food stop between the Rigi roads and the Axenstrasse. The streets can be busy on summer weekends, with tour buses, cyclists and families, but the lakeside is wide enough to find a bench or a terrace. Order something simple. Lake fish such as perch fillets are a classic in this region, and the cafés here usually have some form of rösti and local sausage if you want something filling.

    Fuel is easy, since Brunnen and neighbouring Ingenbohl have stations on the main road, and Schwyz is a few minutes inland. Public parking near the water gets full, so ride a block or two back from the front if you need a space, and keep valuables with you.

    It is worth walking to the water's edge even for ten minutes. Across the lake you can see the Rütli meadow, the traditional birthplace of the Swiss Confederation, and steamers cross it in summer. The light gets soft by late afternoon, and the mountains turn from grey to rose as the sun drops.

  4. Axenstrasse awareness section scenic · 440 m

    The Axenstrasse deserves a little respect before you ride it. It is a narrow road cut along the eastern shore of the Urnersee, between Brunnen and Flüelen, built in the 1860s when the canton of Uri and the canton of Schwyz decided they needed a road that did not depend on boats. Engineers blasted ledges into the cliffs, drove short tunnels through the headlands and added stone galleries wherever rocks threatened to fall. The result is a road that still feels like a piece of nineteenth-century ambition, squeezed between the mountain and the water.

    The section marked for awareness is the one where the corridor is tight and the tunnels come in quick succession. Some are lit, some dim, and they change from bright sunlight to near darkness in a second, so sunglasses off before the first one. The surface can be damp near the galleries, and gravel or small rock sometimes washes onto the carriageway after heavy rain. Rockfall is a genuine risk here, and the road has been closed on occasion while crews secure the slopes. Check conditions if the weather has been wild.

    It is also shared. Lorries, buses and cars use the same ribbon, though the motorway tunnel through the mountain takes most of the long-distance traffic now. On a summer weekend you can find yourself in a slow procession, and that is not a bad thing, because the views are the real reward. Through gaps in the rock you see the lake spread out below, deep and quiet, with the steep faces of the far shore coming down into it. A few pull-offs allow a stop, but use them with care and keep well clear of the carriageway.

    Brunnen and Ingenbohl, two kilometres or so back, are the places for fuel and food. Along the Axenstrasse itself, services are limited, with a few restaurants and viewpoints tucked into the cliffs, so arrive with a full tank and a plan.

    Keep to the speed limit, because the road is narrow and the tunnels have no room for error. Ride in the middle of your lane out of the galleries, since the corners tighten without warning, and let faster riders go by if they want to. It is not a place to race, and it repays patience.

  5. Sattel high road pass · 790 m

    Sattel is a small village on a saddle of land at about eight hundred metres, between the Rigi to the west and the Mostelberg ridge above. The name means saddle, and the place lives up to it, sitting in a gap between hills with views opening both ways. On a clear day you can look toward the Mythen peaks in one direction and the long shape of the Rigi in the other, with pasture and scattered farms between. It is one of those Swiss places that look as though a very tidy giant arranged them.

    The countryside is the heart of old Swiss history. Not far away is the shore of the Ägerisee, where in 1315 a force of Confederates ambushed a larger Habsburg army at Morgarten and won a victory that became a founding story of the nation. A monument marks the site. The battlefield is gentle now, with grass and a small chapel, but the narrowness of the ground helps you understand why a small force on the slope could beat knights on the road.

    The riding on the high road toward Sattel is superb for a relaxed afternoon. The route climbs from the lowlands through orchard and forest, with smooth bends and steady gradients that suit a touring pace. It is rarely congested, and the surface is good. In autumn the beech woods turn gold and the mist hangs in the valleys below, which makes the saddle feel like an island. In winter the area has small ski slopes and a cable car at Hochstuckli, so snow can linger on the shaded sections.

    Unterägeri, about seven kilometres away, is the nearest town with fuel and shops, and Steinen and Rothenthurm are close alternatives. In the village itself you can usually find a restaurant or bakery for a break, and the benches along the road make good rest points.

    Take a jacket even in summer, because the saddle catches wind and weather from both sides. If the clouds are coming in from the west, you will see them stacking on the Rigi before they reach you, which gives you time to put the rain gear on. It is a quiet, unpretentious stretch, and that is why it works.

  6. Back to Zug lakeside exit finish · 413 m

    The route ends here after 84 km. You lose 377 m on the way down to it, steeply.
