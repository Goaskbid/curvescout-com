Why this ride

From Albarracín it runs 534 km to Albarracín; allow about 9 h 10 of riding. It goes by way of Torre de la iglesia del Salvador, Abric del Rincón del Tío Escribano, Abric mas de los Perez and Balma de la fabriqueta. It tops out around 1,450 m. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Abrigo del Toro Negro

    Start here.

    Climbers have made Albarracín famous as well as painters. The Pinares de Rodeno, the red sandstone forest around the town, hold one of the best bouldering areas in Europe, and every year thousands of climbers come to scramble over the rounded blocks with their foam crash pads on their backs. The Abrigo del Toro Negro, the Black Bull shelter, lies four and a half kilometres from the town in the same stone, and its name probably comes from a painted bull, one of the animals that the prehistoric artists liked best. It belongs to the body of Mediterranean rock art protected by UNESCO since 1998.

    Read the complete Abrigo del Toro Negro write-up.

  2. 2. Torre de la iglesia del Salvador

    37 km in.

    Teruel spent decades as the Spanish joke about forgotten provinces, until its people adopted the slogan Teruel existe, Teruel exists, and turned it into a political movement. Ride into the old centre and you will agree it exists rather vividly. The Torre del Salvador is one of its four great Mudéjar towers, built of brick and glazed ceramic in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries by craftsmen trained in the Islamic tradition. The road, or rather a street, still passes through an archway at its base, and you can climb inside, between the outer and inner shells of the tower, up narrow stairs to the bells.

    Read the complete Torre de la iglesia del Salvador write-up.

  3. 3. Abric del Rincón del Tío Escribano

    135 km in.

    High in the interior of Valencia province, where the coastal orange groves give way to limestone plateaus and pine forest, the village of Titaguas sits on the road towards the Rincón de Ademuz. Close by lies the Abric del Rincón del Tío Escribano, a rock shelter with prehistoric paintings, one of the hundreds of sites that make up the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula, listed by UNESCO in 1998.

    Read the complete Abric del Rincón del Tío Escribano write-up.

  4. 4. Abric mas de los Perez

    214 km in.

    Between the Turia and the Palancia rivers, northwest of Valencia, the land rises into a maze of limestone mountains, pine forest and small villages that rarely see foreign visitors. The Abric del Mas de los Pérez is a rock shelter in the hills near Andilla, Viver and Alcublas, one of hundreds of painted sites in eastern Spain listed by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean Basin. These shelters were decorated by hunter-gatherers and early farmers thousands of years ago, and while some feature vivid hunting scenes, many are faint and hard to see without a trained eye. Respect any fences and signs, and never wet or touch the rock.

    Read the complete Abric mas de los Perez write-up.

  5. 5. Balma de la fabriqueta

    267 km in.

    The Serra Calderona is the green ridge that rises north-west of Valencia, separating the city's plain from the valleys of the interior. It is a natural park of pine forest, cork oak and rocky hills, close enough to the city for weekend walkers and riders, yet surprisingly wild once you get into it. The rock shelter of Balma de la Fabriqueta lies near the village of Olocau on its southern side, one of the painted shelters that make up the Levantine rock art inscribed by UNESCO. Access to many of these sites is restricted or guided, so check with the local tourist office before trying to visit.
