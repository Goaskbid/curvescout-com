Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Aktau

    Aktau is a city that grew out of the desert with the sea on one side and nothing on the other. Built in the Soviet era on the eastern shore of the Caspian, in the Mangystau region, it was first founded to support the mining of uranium and later oil, and for decades it was called Shevchenko, after the Ukrainian poet who had been exiled in the area. The name Aktau means white mountain, a reference to the pale chalk and limestone hills that rise behind. With roughly a hundred and fifty thousand people, it is the main port and regional hub.

  2. 2. Boszhira

    Boszhira is a landscape that looks like it was designed for a science-fiction film and then abandoned. In the Mangystau desert, on the edge of the Ustyurt plateau, wind and water have carved the pale chalk and clay into towers, ridges, amphitheatres and the famous jagged teeth that stand over a wide, empty basin. The colours change through the day, from dead white at noon to orange and pink in the evening. The rocks here were once the floor of an ancient sea, and fossil shells can still be found.
