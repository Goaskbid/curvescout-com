Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Butha-Buthe

    Butha-Buthe means place of lying down, and the name comes from the story of the Basotho people retreating to the flat-topped mountain above the town in the 1820s, when war and famine were tearing through the region, before their leader Moshoeshoe moved on to found his stronghold at Thaba Bosiu. Today it is a lively northern Lesotho market town of a few thousand people in its centre, with a few more in the surrounding villages, set in a wide valley under steep brown mountains. It is the first real stop after the border crossing from South Africa, a short distance away.

  2. 2. Katse Dam

    It takes a certain nerve to build a dam in a country this steep, and Katse is the proof that it can be done. Completed in the mid-1990s as part of the Lesotho Highlands Water Project, the dam is a thin concrete arch nearly 190 metres high that holds back the Malibamatšo river, creating a long, branching reservoir in the mountains of central Lesotho. Water from it flows through a tunnel of some forty kilometres to the Ash river in South Africa, supplying the Gauteng region, around Johannesburg and Pretoria, while Lesotho receives royalties for the water.

  3. 3. Thaba-Tseka

    Thaba-Tseka is the remote administrative town of the central highlands of Lesotho, a place that was founded in the 1970s to give government a foothold in the mountains and has the feel of a frontier outpost. The name means black mountain, and the dark ridge above town fits it. About five thousand people live here, in a scatter of government buildings, schools, shops and houses on a plateau more than two thousand metres up, where the air is thin and the nights are cold.
