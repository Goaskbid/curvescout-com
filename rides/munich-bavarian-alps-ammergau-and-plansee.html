Why this ride

Built for flow, views and useful stops rather than navigation busywork. 620 km round from Bad Aibling - plan on about 11 h 29 of riding. It goes by way of Scenic stretch in Peißenberg, near Dinkelscherben and near Schierling. near Kochel is the high point. It tops out at about 1,300 m.

Scenic road chapters

German Alpine Road

Slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did. Water arrives beside the road and stays for 64 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go.

  3. 3. Scenic stretch in Peißenberg

    Peißenberg owes its existence to coal. For more than a century a deep mine worked the seams of pitch coal under the town, and when it closed in 1971 the place had to reinvent itself. You can still see traces of the mining past in the old shaft buildings and a small museum, but the modern town is a pleasant, unpretentious market centre in the rolling foothills between the Ammersee and the Alps.

  4. 4. Photo stop near Dinkelscherben

    West of Augsburg, the land rises into a quiet country of wooded hills and valleys, protected as the Augsburg Western Forests nature park. Dinkelscherben is a small market town in the valley of the Zusam, with a church, a few shops and inns, and it makes a peaceful stop on a ride through the Swabian countryside, far from the busy roads around the city.

  6. 6. Near Mengkofen, on the way back

    Straubing lies a little to the northeast, and this stop sits in the great grain country around it, the Gäuboden. It is some of the most fertile land in Bavaria, a broad plain between the Danube and the Bavarian Forest covered in deep loess soil laid down by wind in the ice age, and the locals call it the breadbasket of the state. The villages of Leiblfing, Mengkofen and Geiselhöring each have a church with an onion dome and a ring of large farms. In summer the fields stand tall and golden, and the whole area smells of dust and warm straw.

  8. 8. Back to Ammergau

    Irschenberg is the hill that every driver on the Munich to Salzburg motorway knows, the long ridge where the road crests and the whole Alpine front suddenly appears, a row of grey peaks across the green foreland. On a clear day, the view from the old church on the hill, the pilgrimage church that is the village landmark, takes in the Wendelstein, the Kaisergebirge and the faraway Zugspitze.
