Why this ride

A 162 km one-way ride from near Bellingham, about 2 h 43 of riding. On the way it passes Glacier Washington and Heather Meadows. The highest point is about 1,300 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Mount Baker Highway

Water arrives beside the road and stays for 50 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go — slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bellingham

    Bellingham is the last substantial city before the Canadian border, a place of eighty-five thousand people on a sheltered bay in the north-west corner of Washington state. It is a university town, with a lively cafe and brewing scene, a revived waterfront, and the kind of outdoorsy, independent character you find in many Pacific Northwest cities. The old Fairhaven district, a Victorian district with brick buildings and bookshops, is a nice place to wander, and the ferry terminal for Alaska sits at its edge.

  2. 2. Glacier Washington

    Glacier, Washington, is more a crossroads than a town: a handful of buildings, a general store, a ranger station and a bridge, in a damp green valley where the pavement turns decisively toward the mountains. It is the last place of any kind on the Mount Baker Highway, which is why riders pause here. After this the road climbs for about twenty kilometres through old forest to the foot of a volcano, and nothing along the way is going to sell you a sandwich.

  3. 3. Heather Meadows

    The Mount Baker Highway ends in a small paradise of lakes, ridges and tilted meadows, and Heather Meadows is the part where the road gives up and the walking begins. You are at about 1,300 metres, which does not sound high until you realise how much of the scene is made of snow: Mount Shuksan is a great jagged pyramid straight ahead, Mount Baker is a white whale to the side, and pocket lakes flash in the hollows between. Picture Lake, with Shuksan reflected in it on a still morning, is one of the most photographed views in the state.

  4. 4. Artist Point

    The pavement ends at a car park on a ridge, and a short paved path leads to the best view in the North Cascades. Artist Point stands at around fifty-one hundred feet at the end of the Mount Baker Highway, and from the edge you look straight across a valley to Mount Shuksan, a jagged pyramid of dark rock and white ice, while the rounded, glaciated cone of Mount Baker sits close at hand to the west. Painters and photographers have been coming for a century, hence the name.
