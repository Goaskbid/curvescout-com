Why this ride

From Granada it runs 156 km to Guadix; allow about 2 h 15 of riding. It goes by way of Lanjaron, Pampaneira, Trevelez and Puerto de la Ragua. The highest point is about 2,050 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Alpujarras Mountain Road

For 17 km this is Alpujarras Mountain Road, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it. Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast.

Alpujarras Mountain Road

First and second gear more often than not. Look through each corner to the next one and let the engine do the holding. The valley floor is behind you and the road starts working for its height at about 6.6%. Corners stack above one another where the ground allows nothing straighter.

Alpujarras Mountain Road

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2041 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance — cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Granada

    Granada lies at the foot of the Sierra Nevada, the highest mountain range on the Spanish mainland, and the snowy ridge is visible from many streets, which is a surprise in a city that is baking hot in August. The Mulhacén, at 3,479 metres, is the highest peak of the Iberian Peninsula, and the summits stay white well into spring. Between the mountains and the plain, the city spreads across three hills, with the red towers of the Alhambra on one side, facing the whitewashed lanes of the Albaicín on another. The two hills look at each other across the river Darro.

  2. 2. Lanjaron

    Lanjarón is the front door of the Alpujarras, and the first thing it offers is water. The springs that rise from the Sierra Nevada above the town are among the best known in Spain, and the mineral water bottled here is sold across the country. The town has a spa tradition that goes back centuries, with a grand old bathing house and a long main street of cafés, shops and hotels, and a public fountain where you can taste the water for free. It is cold and light, with a faint mineral tang.

  3. 3. Pampaneira

    Pampaneira is the first of three white villages that cling to the sides of the Poqueira gorge, and it looks like something from the Atlas mountains, which is not an accident. The houses are flat-roofed cubes, whitewashed every year, with chimneys shaped like little pots, and their roofs are made of a grey clay called launa, laid over slate and beams, a building method brought from North Africa by the Moors. The village is steep, with narrow alleys and stepped streets, and the sound of running water from the channels that bring meltwater down the mountainside is everywhere.

  4. 4. Trevelez

    Trevélez is one of those places that people tell you about long before you see it. It hangs on the southern slope of the Sierra Nevada at around fifteen hundred metres, among the highest villages in Spain, below the bulk of Mulhacén, which at 3,479 metres is the highest peak on the Iberian mainland. The village is split into three barrios, one above the other, with whitewashed flat-roofed houses that look from the road like a staircase of sugar cubes. Above them is a valley of terraced orchards and chestnuts, and the cold, clear river that carries the snowmelt down toward the Mediterranean.

  5. 5. Puerto de la Ragua

    Spain's Sierra Nevada is the southernmost big mountain range in Europe, and the Puerto de la Ragua is the main paved road across it. At 2,041 metres it is not the highest road in the range, since the great summits of Mulhacén and Veleta both lie further west and rise to around 3,400 metres, but it is the easiest to love. It links Guadix, on the high dry plateau to the north, with the villages of the Alpujarras, the steep valleys on the southern side, and the change of scene from one side to the other is abrupt.

  6. 6. Guadix

    Guadix is a town where half the population lives in the hillside, and it manages this without any air of eccentricity. In the Barrio de las Cuevas, thousands of houses are dug into the soft, reddish clay of the hills, with whitewashed fronts, chimneys sticking out of the grass, and doors opening onto terraces. Inside, the temperature stays at around eighteen degrees all year, cool in the summer and warm in the winter, which explains why the tradition has lasted since the Moorish period. Some of the cave homes now serve as guesthouses, and one can stay in them.

Granada to Lanjaron

Granada → Lanjaron is 38 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Lanjaron to Pampaneira

Lanjaron → Pampaneira is 14 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Pampaneira to Trevelez

Pampaneira → Trevelez is 13 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Trevelez to Puerto de la Ragua

Trevelez → Puerto de la Ragua climbs about 2041 m in 31 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Puerto de la Ragua to Guadix

Puerto de la Ragua → Guadix drops about 2041 m over 28 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
