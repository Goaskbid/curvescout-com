  1. 1. Sani Pass top

    At the top of Sani Pass you are standing on the roof of the Drakensberg, close to 2,900 metres up, on the border between South Africa and the Kingdom of Lesotho. A small border post, a few huts, a stone chalet that claims to be the highest pub in Africa, and a view down the escarpment that makes your stomach lurch, are about all there is. On a clear day the plateau of Lesotho rolls away to the north, brown and treeless, while behind you the cliffs drop away into a haze of valleys.

  2. 2. Mokhotlong

    Mokhotlong is the remote capital of eastern Lesotho, a small town at about 2,200 metres where the road stops pretending to be easy. Its name is said to mean place of the bald ibis, after the rare, red-billed bird that nests on the cliffs in the area, and the town sits in a bowl of brown and green hills, with stone houses and tin roofs spreading up the slopes. In winter, snow often covers the highest ridges, and morning frost is a reliable companion, so riders should come prepared for cold.
