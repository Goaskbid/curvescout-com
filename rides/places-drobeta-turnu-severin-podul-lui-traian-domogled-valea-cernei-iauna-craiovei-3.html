Why this ride

A 160 km round trip from Drobeta-Turnu Severin - roughly 2 h 46 in the saddle. The stops in between: Domogled - Valea Cernei - Domogled-Coronin, Domogled - Valea Cernei - Iauna Craiovei and back through Domogled - Valea Cernei - Domogled-Coronin. The highest point is about 1,200 m. It crosses from Serbia into Romania - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Drobeta-Turnu Severin -Podul lui Traian

    When the emperor Trajan wanted to invade Dacia for the second time, he told his engineer to build a bridge across the Danube, and the engineer, Apollodorus of Damascus, made it happen. The bridge at Drobeta, completed in 105 AD, was a marvel, with twenty stone piers carrying timber arches over a river that is more than a kilometre wide at this point, and for a thousand years it was the longest arch bridge in the world. It was built to move the legions across the river in one season, and it did its job. The wars that followed ended with the conquest of Dacia in 106 and the creation of a new Roman province.

  2. 2. Domogled - Valea Cernei - Domogled-Coronin

    The river Cerna runs through one of the deepest and wildest valleys of the Romanian Carpathians, a narrow gorge between limestone cliffs, before emerging at Băile Herculane, one of the oldest spa towns in Europe. The Romans bathed in its hot springs nearly two thousand years ago and named the place after Hercules. In the nineteenth century, the Habsburgs built grand bathhouses, hotels and casinos in an ornate imperial style, and Emperor Franz Joseph and Empress Sisi came to take the waters. Many of these buildings now stand empty and decaying, a haunting sight, though restoration efforts are under way. The surrounding mountains form the Domogled-Valea Cernei National Park, and parts of its old beech forests are included in the UNESCO site of Ancient and Primeval Beech Forests.

  3. 3. Domogled - Valea Cernei - Iauna Craiovei

    The Cerna is a mountain river in south-western Romania, flowing through a long, deep valley between the Mehedinți and Cerna mountains before reaching the old spa town of Băile Herculane. The Domogled-Valea Cernei National Park protects this valley and the mountains around it, a landscape of limestone cliffs, gorges, caves and some of the largest old-growth beech forests in Europe, part of the UNESCO site for the ancient and primeval beech forests of the Carpathians and other regions of Europe.
