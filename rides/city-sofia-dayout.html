Why this ride

A 174 km round trip from Sofia - roughly 3 h in the saddle. The stops in between: \Grave of St. Ivan Rilski\ Complex, \St. Luke Hermitage\ Complex, Rila Monastery and Rila Monastery Complex. The highest point is about 1,700 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sofia

    Sofia sits under a mountain, and that is the first thing to understand about it. Vitosha rises right behind the southern suburbs, a broad, dome-shaped massif topping out at Cherni Vrah, around 2,290 metres, and on a clear morning the snow is visible from the boulevards. People ski there in winter and hike in summer, and the city's residents treat it like a back garden. The capital of Bulgaria is a plain, solid place of about one and a quarter million, with Soviet-era blocks, Ottoman remnants and an unexpected number of small parks.

  2. 2. \Grave of St. Ivan Rilski\ Complex

    Ivan of Rila was a hermit who, in the tenth century, withdrew into the forests of the Rila mountains to live in a cave and a hollow tree, and the monastery that grew up around his grave became the spiritual heart of Bulgaria. Rila Monastery sits in a valley at about 1,150 metres, surrounded by steep forested slopes, and the Rilska river runs beside it. The buildings you see today date mostly from the nineteenth century, after a great fire in 1833 destroyed the older ones, but within the courtyard stands a medieval tower, Hrelyo's Tower, from 1335, and the main church is covered in vivid murals.

  3. 3. \St. Luke Hermitage\ Complex

    Above Rila Monastery, in the folds of the Rila Mountains, a path leads to the hermit's cave where it all began. The monastery was founded in the tenth century by Saint Ivan of Rila, a monk who left the world to live as a recluse in these forests, and he gathered followers who built a community.

  4. 4. Rila Monastery

    The road to Rila Monastery climbs a long valley through forests of beech and spruce, and when the monastery appears the effect is theatrical. Its walls enclose a courtyard of striped arcades in black, white and red, with a church in the middle covered in frescoes, and behind it the green flanks of the Rila mountains rise towards peaks of more than two and a half thousand metres. It is the largest and most important monastery in Bulgaria, and UNESCO listed it in 1983.

  5. 5. Rila Monastery Complex

    Rila Monastery sits at around eleven hundred metres in a forested valley of the Rila mountains, the highest range in the Balkans, where Musala at nearly three thousand metres stands above all the peaks of south-eastern Europe. The monastery lies at the end of a long road that follows the Rilska river upstream through beech and spruce, and the approach feels like going back in time. Mist hangs in the trees, the air smells of resin, and the river noise grows louder as you climb.
