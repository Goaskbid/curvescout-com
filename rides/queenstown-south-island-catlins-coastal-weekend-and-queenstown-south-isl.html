Why this ride

812 km from Queenstown to Cromwell, about 17 h 04 of riding. On the way it passes Scenic stretch near Queenstown, near Cromwell and Catlins Coastal. The climbs: near Queenstown and near Kingston. It tops out at about 1,300 m. Long stretches are under trees.

Scenic road chapters

Crown Range Road

Slow enough to look. The surface follows the shoreline, so the corners arrive where the water decided, not where an engineer did. Water arrives beside the road and stays for 78 km. The line follows the shore because there is nowhere else for it to go.

  6. 6. Near Cromwell, on the way back

    Cromwell sits in the heart of Central Otago, on the shores of Lake Dunstan, in a dry, sunny basin of tawny hills, schist rocks and orchards. It is a town of around five thousand people, known for its fruit, its wine and its huge sculpture of fruit at the entrance, and it is a good place to stop on a hot day in summer.

  7. 7. Food stop near Cromwell

    Cromwell sits where two of Central Otago's big rivers used to meet, and these days the meeting happens under water. When the Clyde Dam was finished in the early 1990s, the rising Lake Dunstan swallowed the old town centre and much of the gorge, and what survived of the gold-era main street was rebuilt as a heritage precinct on the new lakeshore. It is worth the short detour: schist cottages, a bakery or two, and the eerie knowledge that the original riverbank is somewhere below the boats.

  16. 16. Back to Central

    The land is dramatic in an unfamiliar way: bare tawny slopes, tumbled grey rocks, thyme and briar on the dry banks, and a sky so clear that the light seems brittle. Mountains of the Southern Alps stand blue and white behind the nearer ranges, and the contrast between the dry foreground and the snow behind is one of the great views of the South Island.
