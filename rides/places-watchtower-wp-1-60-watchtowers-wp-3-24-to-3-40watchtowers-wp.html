Why this ride

160 km round trip from Höhr-Grenzhausen, about 2 h 56 of riding. It runs out through Watchtowers WP 2/7 and 2/13 including fort, Fortlets and site of the fort and civil se and Watchtowers WP 3/24 to 3/40Watchtowers WP and comes back by another road.

The roads

The stops

  1. 1. Watchtower WP 1/60

    Start here.

    Above Koblenz, on the edge of the Westerwald, the potters of Höhr-Grenzhausen have been making grey, salt-glazed stoneware with cobalt-blue decoration for centuries, giving this corner of Germany its nickname, the Kannenbäckerland, the land of jug bakers. In the forest nearby, the site of Roman watchtower WP 1/60 on the Upper German-Raetian Limes is part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire World Heritage Site. The tower was one of hundreds along the frontier, a wooden or stone structure from which soldiers watched the land beyond and signalled to their neighbours.

    Read the complete Watchtower WP 1/60 write-up.

  2. 2. Watchtowers WP 2/7 and 2/13 including fort

    31 km in.

    Between the Lahn and the Rhine, the Roman frontier crossed a high, wooded plateau known locally as the Einrich, and the watchtowers from 2/7 to 2/13 stood on this stretch, near the village of Dachsenhausen. In the forest, the earth bank and ditch can still be followed, and small forts, built to house the soldiers who manned the towers, have left their outlines in the ground. Here, as elsewhere in Germany, the Limes belongs to the frontiers of the Roman Empire on the World Heritage list, but the forest is quiet, and you may walk the line for an hour without meeting anyone.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 2/7 and 2/13 including fort write-up.

  3. 3. Fortlets and site of the fort and civil se

    61 km in.

    The western Taunus is wonderfully empty. Away from the commuter belt around Frankfurt and Wiesbaden, the hills north of Bad Schwalbach turn into a quiet patchwork of forest, small meadows and villages in the Heidenrod municipality, and right through the middle runs the Limes. Today you find grassy outlines, a few markers and the bank and ditch disappearing into the beech woods.

    Read the complete Fortlets and site of the fort and civil se write-up.

  4. 4. Watchtowers WP 3/24 to 3/40Watchtowers WP

    92 km in.

    On the western side of the Taunus, the hills are lower and the forest gives way more often to farmland, with villages in the folds of the valleys. Idstein, a small town with a remarkable old centre, sits on this side, with Waldems and Glashütten nearby. Through the woods above them ran the Roman frontier, and the stretch of watchtowers numbered WP 3/24 to 3/40 followed the ridges here, part of the Upper Germanic Limes and of the UNESCO Frontiers of the Roman Empire site.
