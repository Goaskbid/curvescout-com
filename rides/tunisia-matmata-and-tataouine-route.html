Why this ride

A one-way ride of 330 km, Gabès to Tataouine - roughly 4 h 38 in the saddle. On the way it passes Matmata, Ksar Ghilane and Chenini. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Gabes

    Gabès is a port town on the south-eastern coast of Tunisia, and it has a trick up its sleeve: a palm oasis that runs right down to the sea. People say it is the only coastal oasis in the Mediterranean, and when you stand under the date palms with pomegranates and bananas growing beneath and the sound of irrigation water in the channels, you can believe it. The oasis is also the source of henna, which has been grown and sold here for a very long time, and the stalls of the souq are packed with the dried leaves, powdered and bagged.

  2. 2. Matmata

    Matmata is a village you have to look down into. The houses of this Berber settlement in southern Tunisia are dug into the earth, around deep circular pits with rooms cut into the walls around them, so that from the road you see only a scatter of craters in the stony ground and a few chimneys. The people built them like this to escape the extreme heat and cold, and the clay and limestone keep temperatures steady through the year. Walking into one, down a ramp or tunnel, you pass from glaring sunlight into a cool, whitewashed courtyard where goats and chickens wander.

  3. 3. Ksar Ghilane

    Ksar Ghilane is where southern Tunisia stops pretending to be a country and becomes the Sahara. It lies on the edge of the Grand Erg Oriental, the great sea of sand dunes that rolls across the border toward Algeria and Libya, and the oasis is a small patch of date palms around a warm spring that bubbles up in the sand. The spring water is hot enough to be a pleasure on a cool evening, and travellers have swum there for generations, climbing out to watch the stars come out over the dunes.

  4. 4. Chenini

    Chenini clings to a ridge in the Ksour hills south of Tataouine, a Berber village of stone and plaster whose houses seem to grow out of the hillside. Rooms are carved into the rock, some of them below ground, with whitewashed walls and vaulted ceilings, and above them stands a small mosque with a white dome. From a distance, the village is the same colour as the hill and almost vanishes in the afternoon haze, and only a few pale domes give it away.

  5. 5. Tataouine

    Tataouine is where the Sahara starts to feel close. The town of some sixty thousand people sits on a dry plain south of the Dahar hills, in the middle of the southern Tunisian steppe, and beyond it to the south and west the dunes of the Grand Erg Oriental stretch toward Algeria and Libya. Its name will already be familiar to anyone who has watched Star Wars, since George Lucas borrowed it, with a small spelling change, for Luke Skywalker's desert home. The film crews shot around here, and the fans have kept coming.
