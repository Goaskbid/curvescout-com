  1. 1. Loja

    Loja is the quiet intellectual of Ecuador. Tucked into a green valley at about 2,100 metres in the far south, it has a reputation as the country's capital of music and the arts, with a conservatory and a long tradition of poets and composers. The city was founded in 1548 and is home to a respected university, so the streets have a youthful, studious feel. It has a calm centre of arcades and plazas, and a castle-like gate at the northern entrance to the city, the Puerta de la Ciudad, which is the favourite photo stop.

  2. 2. Vilcabamba

    Vilcabamba has a reputation that is bigger than it is. The small town in a sheltered valley south of Loja, at about 1,500 metres, was once marketed as the Valley of Longevity, with stories of residents living to well over a hundred, though researchers who checked the records found such claims did not stand up. What is true is that the climate is hard to fault: mild, sunny and dry for most of the year, with cool nights, which is why the valley has drawn a steady stream of foreign settlers and a laid-back community of artists, retirees and hikers.
