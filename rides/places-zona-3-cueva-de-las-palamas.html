Why this ride

From Nerpio it runs 169 km to Nerpio; allow about 2 h 55 of riding. The stops in between: Cañaica del Calar l, Benizar V, Cueva de las Palamas and back through Benizar V. The highest point is about 1,400 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Zona 3

    Nerpio sits where three provinces and two regions almost meet, and the roads out of it run in all directions to places that feel quite different. To the south and east the land falls toward Murcia and the dry plains, to the south-west the sierras of Jaén and Granada rise, and to the north the high farmland of Albacete rolls away toward La Mancha. Zona 3, about seven kilometres from the village, is one of the numbered areas used by archaeologists to catalogue the rock shelters of the district, and it lies on the high ground above the Taibilla valley.

  2. 2. Cañaica del Calar l

    Very few people get here. The Cañaica del Calar lies in the far north-west of the region of Murcia, near the border with Albacete and Andalusia, in a wild landscape of limestone plateaus, pine forests and deep valleys around Moratalla and Nerpio. The calar is the local word for a limestone plateau, and the rock shelters on its edges hold prehistoric paintings in the Levantine style, part of the World Heritage rock art of the Mediterranean. The area around Nerpio is one of the richest in Spain for rock art.

  3. 3. Benizar V

    Benizar is one of those places that only a handful of maps bother to name. It is a hamlet belonging to Moratalla, in the hilly north-west of the Murcia region, close to where the province meets Albacete and the first ridges of the Sierra de Segura begin. Around it, in the rock faces and gullies, archaeologists have recorded a cluster of painted shelters, and Benizar V is one in this series: a stone overhang with faint prehistoric figures, part of the Levantine rock art that runs along Spain's eastern mountains.

  4. 4. Cueva de las Palamas

    Cehegín, in the north-west of the Murcia region, is a town of two faces: a modern lower town, and an old town on a hill, with steep streets, noble houses and churches, declared a historic site. The Cueva de las Palomas, the cave of the doves, lies on the edge of the town, less than a kilometre from the centre, with the larger town of Caravaca de la Cruz about five kilometres away.
