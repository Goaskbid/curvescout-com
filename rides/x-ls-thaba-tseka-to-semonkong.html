  1. 1. Thaba-Tseka

    Thaba-Tseka is the remote administrative town of the central highlands of Lesotho, a place that was founded in the 1970s to give government a foothold in the mountains and has the feel of a frontier outpost. The name means black mountain, and the dark ridge above town fits it. About five thousand people live here, in a scatter of government buildings, schools, shops and houses on a plateau more than two thousand metres up, where the air is thin and the nights are cold.

  2. 2. Semonkong

    Semonkong means place of smoke, and the name comes from the plume of spray thrown up by the Maletsunyane Falls, which drop nearly two hundred metres in a single leap into a gorge a short walk from the village. It is a small settlement in the mountains of central Lesotho, a place of stone houses, sheep pens and a handful of lodges, with a reputation among adventurous travellers that far outweighs its size.
