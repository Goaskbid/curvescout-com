Why this ride

149 km from Andernach to Andernach, about 2 h 45 of riding. On the way it passes Fort site and civil settlement of Niederbe, Fort site and civil settlement of Marienfe, Watchtowers WP 2/50 to 2/55 including Just and back through Fort site and civil settlement of Marienfe. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Watchtowers WP 1/23 to 1/24

    Start here.

    The Upper German-Raetian Limes began at the Rhine, at Rheinbrohl, and climbed into the hills on the east bank, and towers WP 1/23 and 1/24, near the wine village of Leutesdorf, are among the first along its route. They are now marked by foundations or mounds in the forest above the river, and they are part of the World Heritage listing. From here the frontier ran for more than five hundred kilometres to the Danube, and it is fascinating to stand at the very beginning of such an enormous undertaking.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 1/23 to 1/24 write-up.

  2. 2. Fort site and civil settlement of Niederbe

    22 km in.

    On the heights above the right bank of the Rhine, opposite Koblenz, the suburb of Niederberg sits on top of a Roman fort. It is one of the forts of the Upper Germanic Limes, which ran through the hills here a few kilometres back from the river, and the label refers to the fort site and the civilian settlement that grew up around it, part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire World Heritage site. Not much is visible today, as the area is built over, but the location explains a great deal.

    Read the complete Fort site and civil settlement of Niederbe write-up.

  3. 3. Fort site and civil settlement of Marienfe

    50 km in.

    Marienfels is a small village in the hills of the western Taunus, between the Rhine and the Lahn, and it sits on the site of a Roman fort and its civilian settlement, part of the Upper Germanic-Raetian Limes World Heritage listing. The frontier wall ran through these hills, and the fort housed a garrison that guarded the frontier and the roads that crossed it. Little remains above ground, but excavations have revealed the outline of the fort and the settlement, with houses, baths and workshops.

    Read the complete Fort site and civil settlement of Marienfe write-up.

  4. 4. Watchtowers WP 2/50 to 2/55 including Just

    75 km in.

    Bad Schwalbach is a spa town that never quite lost its nineteenth-century manners: a long, leafy valley, a Kurpark with pavilions, and iron-rich springs that once drew the fashionable from across Europe to take the waters. Just above it, on the high ground of the Taunus, the Roman frontier ran through the forests, and the stretch catalogued as watchtowers WP 2/50 to 2/55 passes within a few kilometres of the town. Here the Upper German Limes followed the ridge, with wooden and later stone towers set at intervals within sight of one another, a palisade in front, and a ditch and bank added later. In the beech woods you can still trace the low line of the bank in places, a quiet scar running through the trees for kilometre after kilometre.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 2/50 to 2/55 including Just write-up.
