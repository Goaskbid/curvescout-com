Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Xiacaopu Iron Production Site of Qingyang

    In the green hills of Anxi county, inland from the port city of Quanzhou in Fujian, the Xiacaopu site preserves the remains of an iron industry that flourished during the Song and Yuan dynasties, roughly from the tenth to the fourteenth centuries. Archaeologists found furnaces, slag heaps, ore-roasting pits and workshops on a hillside, evidence of large-scale production. The iron produced here was shipped down the rivers to Quanzhou and exported to Southeast Asia and beyond, and that is why the site is part of the World Heritage listing of Quanzhou, Emporium of the World in Song-Yuan China, inscribed in 2021. At that time, Quanzhou was one of the largest ports in the world, visited by Arab, Persian and Indian merchants, and described by Marco Polo and Ibn Battuta.

  2. 2. Fujian Tulou

    Picture a giant ring of rammed earth, three or four storeys high, with almost no windows on the outside and a single heavy gate. Inside, galleries of wooden rooms face a courtyard with an ancestral hall at its heart, and up to several hundred members of one extended family once lived within those walls. That is a tulou, and the hills of south-western Fujian are dotted with them, round and square, some centuries old, many still inhabited.

  3. 3. Huaiyuan Lou

    Huaiyuan Lou is a tulou in Nanjing County, the name meaning something like a house that cherishes distant things, and it sits in a valley of tea terraces and bamboo in the same group of earthen buildings as its better-known neighbours. The lou form, a tall rectangular or square block of several storeys, differs from the round tulou, and the dwellings of this type are often built on a regular grid with the main hall facing south, as the principles of fengshui required. The orientation, the position of the gate and the placement of the well and the ancestral hall were all weighed by a geomancer before the first wall was raised.

  4. 4. Yanxiang Lou

    Yanxiang Lou belongs to the group of Fujian earthen houses recognised by UNESCO, and what distinguishes it, in a region full of astonishing buildings, is its history as the home of a single prosperous family. The word lou simply means a building of several storeys, and it is used for many of the larger tulou.

  5. 5. Hegui Lou

    Hegui Lou is the tulou with a famous tilt. It is a five-storey earthen building in Nanjing County that has leaned to one side for decades, and it has been compared to the leaning tower of Pisa, though the cause is more modest: the foundations on one side settled, probably through water and the work of termites, and the rammed earth walls have shifted to a noticeable angle. Despite the lean, the house has stood, and the locals show it off with a mixture of pride and humour. The upper floors have been propped with timber, and the lower ones remain in use.
