Why this ride

From Hengstdal it runs 71 km to Hengstdal; allow about 1 h 13 of riding. On the way it passes Kleve-Keeken, Till and back through Kleve-Keeken. It crosses from Netherlands into Germany - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Berg en Dal-aqueduct | Mariënboom

    The hills south-east of Nijmegen have a second history that has nothing to do with Rome. On Sunday 17 September 1944, thousands of American paratroopers of the 82nd Airborne Division landed on the heath and fields around Groesbeek, as part of Operation Market Garden, to seize the high ground and the bridges over the Maas and Waal. They succeeded in many things, but the push to Arnhem failed, and the front line stayed near here through a bitter winter. Nijmegen was liberated only partially, and fighting went on across the Reichswald forest just over the German border until the spring of 1945. The National Liberation Museum in Groesbeek tells the story, and the Canadian War Cemetery, with over two thousand graves, set among beeches, is one of the most moving places in the area.

  2. 2. Kleve-Keeken

    Here the Rhine stops being a German river. Just downstream of the village of Keeken, the great river crosses the border into the Netherlands, and a few kilometres further on it divides, with the Waal carrying most of the water west and the Pannerdens Canal leading to the Nederrijn and the IJssel. In Roman times, this was the northern frontier of the empire, the Lower German Limes, and Keeken is one of the sites listed as part of the UNESCO frontier, with traces of Roman military installations along the old river course.

  3. 3. Till

    The Lower Rhine around Kalkar and Kleve is wide, flat country: green polders, willows along drainage ditches, brick farmhouses and church spires visible for miles. Till is a small village here, part of Bedburg-Hau, just off the Rhine embankments between Kalkar and Kleve, with Emmerich across the river. It lies close to the line of the Roman frontier, the Lower German Limes, which followed the Rhine to the North Sea.
