Why this ride

317 km from Lindau to Schlehdorf, about 4 h 21 of riding. The stops in between: Oberstaufen, Oberjoch and Garmisch Partenkirchen. The highest point is about 1,550 m. Oberjoch is subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. It crosses from Germany into Austria - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Lindau

    Lindau is an island that behaves like a postcard, and the amusing thing is that it knows it. The old town sits on a small island in the eastern end of Lake Constance, the Bodensee, linked to the mainland by a causeway and a railway embankment, and the harbour at its south side is guarded by two monuments: a tall lighthouse and a stone lion of Bavaria on a pillar. The lighthouse was built in the nineteenth century, and from its top you see the whole lake, with the Austrian and Swiss Alps behind. About twenty-four thousand people live in the municipality, and most of them are on the mainland.

  2. 2. Oberstaufen

    Oberstaufen is a spa town with a taste for fasting. Its fame comes from the Schroth cure, a regime of dry days, wet days, wraps and very little food that visitors have been putting up with here for generations, and the place wears its wellness reputation with a certain Bavarian shrug. You see it in the clean flower boxes, the Kneipp-style water basins in the park, and the clinic-like hotels. It is also a proper Allgäu town, and a pleasant one, with about seven thousand people, cow bells in the background, and a brewing tradition that makes the fasting look like a very optional part of life.

  3. 3. Oberjoch

    Oberjoch is a village that sits on a saddle, and the saddle belongs to two countries. At 1,178 metres, the pass on the German Alpine Road marks the boundary between the Allgäu in Bavaria and the Tannheimer Valley in Tyrol, and the road crosses it from the town of Hindelang with a steep climb. The pass has a ski resort, a clutch of hotels and a few tidy farmhouses, and it is among the highest villages in Germany.

  4. 4. Garmisch Partenkirchen

    Garmisch-Partenkirchen lies in a wide Alpine valley in Bavaria, close to the Austrian border, with the Wetterstein range rising behind it and the Zugspitze, at 2,962 metres the highest mountain in Germany, standing at the end of the valley. The town is really two, Garmisch and Partenkirchen, joined together for the 1936 Winter Olympics, and each still has its own character. Partenkirchen, the older part, has a Roman history, as it stood on the road across the Alps, and its main street, with painted houses, is the more charming.

  5. 5. Walchensee

    Walchensee is one of the deepest and clearest alpine lakes in Germany, lying at about eight hundred metres in the Bavarian Pre-Alps, with a surface the colour of turquoise glass on a good day and dark green on a stormy one. The lake is surrounded by forested mountains, with the Herzogstand and the Heimgarten rising to the north, and it has a calm, almost Scandinavian look in the early morning.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Lindau to Oberstaufen

Lindau → Oberstaufen is 32 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Oberstaufen to Oberjoch

Oberstaufen → Oberjoch climbs about 1178 m in 39 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Oberjoch to Garmisch Partenkirchen

Oberjoch → Garmisch Partenkirchen drops about 1178 m over 62 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Garmisch Partenkirchen to Walchensee

Garmisch Partenkirchen → Walchensee is 25 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
