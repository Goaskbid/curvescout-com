Why this ride

A 265 km round trip from Alfafara - roughly 4 h 34 in the saddle. The stops in between: Cueva de la Araña- Abrigo I, Barranco de las Colochas- Abrigo I and back through Cueva de la Araña- Abrigo I. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Barranc d'Alpadull- Abric II

    Bocairent is a strange and beautiful town, and the hills around it are among the lesser-known gems of Valencian inland. It sits on a rocky spur above a gorge, with a medieval quarter of steep, narrow streets and a baroque church, and in the cliffs below the town are cave dwellings called the Covetes dels Moros, hollows cut into the rock that have been used as homes, storehouses and shelters for centuries. Beyond the town, in the Serra de Mariola, there are also old stone ice-houses, the neveres, in which snow was packed in winter and sold in summer. The Barranc d'Alpadull lies only three and a half kilometres away.

  2. 2. Cueva de la Araña- Abrigo I

    Bicorp is a small village in the folds of the Valencian interior, west of Xàtiva, in a country of pine, scrub and deep ravines that falls towards the Júcar and the Sierra de Martés. The Cueva de la Araña, the Spider Cave, is one of the most famous rock art sites in Spain, and the main reason to come. In its shelter is a painted scene of a figure climbing a rope or ladder of plant fibre to collect honey from a wild hive, with bees flying around. It is thought to be several thousand years old, and it is one of the finest and most human images of the Levantine rock art, listed by UNESCO in 1998.

  3. 3. Barranco de las Colochas- Abrigo I

    West of Valencia, the river Turia leaves the mountains through a series of deep gorges, and around the villages of Gestalgar, Sot de Chera and Chulilla, the landscape turns dramatic: limestone cliffs, pine-covered slopes and the river winding green below. In a side ravine near Gestalgar, the Barranco de las Colochas shelters a handful of painted panels that UNESCO counts among the Mediterranean rock art of Iberia. The figures are faint, but the setting is worth the effort on its own.
