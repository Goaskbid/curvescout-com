Why this ride

A 34 km round trip from Medford by way of Arlington - roughly 39 min in the saddle. The stops in between: Middlesex Fells and Winchester. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

  2. 2. Middlesex Fells

    The Middlesex Fells is a surprise: a large block of rocky, wooded hill, a couple of thousand acres of it, a few kilometres north of Boston, and almost entirely undeveloped. The name comes from an old word for rocky upland, and the land is made of ancient granite and gneiss, scraped by glaciers and left with outcrops, boulders and thin soils that never made good farmland. When the surrounding villages began to grow into suburbs in the nineteenth century, a group of citizens fought to protect it, and in the 1890s the state created one of the first metropolitan park systems in the country, with the Fells at its heart.

  3. 3. Winchester

    Winchester is a commuter suburb with a conscience. It lies about ten miles north-west of Boston, around a small, pretty centre at the head of the Mystic Lakes, and the town has had its own identity since 1850, when it was carved out of Woburn, Medford and Arlington. The commuter rail line runs through the middle, and a station and a row of old brick shops give the centre a Victorian character.
