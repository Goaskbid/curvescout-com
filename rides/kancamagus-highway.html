Why this ride

Out of Woodstock, one way: 95 km, roughly 1 h 36 in the saddle. The stops in between: Kancamagus Pass and Sabbaday Falls. The highest point is about 850 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Kancamagus Highway

The ground tilts up and stays tilted for 29 km. 863 m of it, taken steadily rather than in one wall. A steady rhythm suits this better than bursts. The height arrives whether you hurry or not.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Lincoln New Hampshire

    Lincoln is a mountain town that plays the role of ski village in winter and gateway in summer. It sits on the Pemigewasset River, in a bowl of the White Mountains, with Loon Mountain's slopes rising behind and the dramatic cut of Franconia Notch just to the north. On the east side of town, the road named after a seventeenth-century Penacook leader, Kancamagus, starts its climb into the national forest.

  2. 2. Kancamagus Pass

    Locals call it the Kanc, and every autumn it carries a procession of cars, bikes and cameras through the White Mountains of New Hampshire to see the leaves. The highway, New Hampshire 112, runs thirty-four miles between Conway and Lincoln, and the high point, Kancamagus Pass at 2,855 feet, sits in the middle of the national forest, with spruce and birch forest around it and an overlook looking out over the ridges. It is not a dramatic height, but the effect, rolling green mountains stacked to the horizon, is one of the finest in the eastern United States.

  3. 3. Sabbaday Falls

    Sabbaday Falls is the waterfall you can reach in your riding boots. A short, well-made path leads from the roadside car park through hemlock and birch woods for about a third of a mile to a three-step cascade where the Sabbaday Brook squeezes through a narrow granite flume, drops in a series of pools and finishes in a green basin. The stone is smooth and pale, the water is clear and cold, and a bridge and platforms let you look down into the cleft without climbing over wet rock.

  4. 4. Conway

    Conway is the front door to the Kancamagus, and the road starts quietly, with a few motels and a gas station, and then plunges into the White Mountain National Forest. The Kancamagus Highway, New Hampshire Route 112, runs about thirty-four miles west to Lincoln, following the Swift River up a narrow valley and then climbing over a pass at nearly 2,900 feet, with forest on both sides and almost no buildings. The name comes from Kancamagus, a sachem of the Pennacook people in the late seventeenth century, who tried to keep the peace with English settlers before leaving the region.

Lincoln New Hampshire to Kancamagus Pass

Lincoln New Hampshire → Kancamagus Pass climbs about 863 m in 17 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Kancamagus Pass → Sabbaday Falls drops about 863 m over 12 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Sabbaday Falls to Conway

Sabbaday Falls → Conway is 27 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
