Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Orşova

    The Orșova you ride into is not the old one. The original town lies under the Danube, drowned in the early 1970s when the Iron Gates I dam downstream raised the river by tens of metres, and the present town was rebuilt on the hillside above the new shoreline, with concrete blocks, a modern Catholic church shaped like a ship's prow, and a long waterfront promenade. The same flood swallowed Ada Kaleh, a small island just downstream that had been home to a Turkish community with a mosque, a bazaar and a reputation for coffee, figs and Turkish delight. People of a certain age in this part of Romania still talk about it with real sadness.

  2. 2. Dubova

    At Dubova the Danube does something remarkable: it narrows from a wide lake into a channel only a couple of hundred metres across, squeezed between limestone cliffs that rise straight out of the water. These are the Cazane, the Cauldrons, the most dramatic part of the Iron Gates gorge, and the small village of Dubova sits on a bay between the Big Cauldron and the Small Cauldron. Before the dams were built the river here was a mass of rapids and whirlpools, one of the most dangerous stretches of water on the whole river, and boatmen had to navigate it with great care.

  3. 3. Moldova Nouă

    Moldova Nouă is the western gateway to the Romanian side of the Iron Gates, a former mining town on the Danube where the river is still wide and calm before it enters the gorges further east. The town grew around copper mining in the hills behind, and the mines and their waste have left their mark: a large tailings pond near the river has been a long-running environmental problem, sending dust over the area on windy days. It is not the most beautiful town on the Danube, but it has a working, unpolished honesty and it is a useful stop on the long riverside road.
