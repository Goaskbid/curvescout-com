Why this ride

From Sakurai it runs 70 km to Sakurai; allow about 1 h 12 of riding. On the way it passes Yoshino and Ômine - Yoshinoyama, Yoshino and Ômine - Ôminesan-ji and back through Yoshino and Ômine - Yoshinoyama. The highest point is about 800 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ishibutai Mounded Tomb

    Ishibutai is the skeleton of a tomb. The massive granite blocks of its burial chamber, now exposed to the open sky, form one of the most astonishing sights in Asuka, a giant stone room that was once buried under an earth mound, and it is generally thought to be the tomb of Soga no Umako, the minister who built Asuka-dera and dominated the court until his death in 626. The mound has long since been stripped away, probably by looters and by the passage of centuries, leaving the stones standing in a grassy square like the bones of some enormous animal.

  2. 2. Yoshino and Ômine - Yoshinoyama

    Cherry trees are the whole point of Yoshino, and they are planted by the thousand up the side of a ridge in the mountains of Nara Prefecture, about five kilometres from the little town of Yoshino and an hour or two from Osaka. Over some three hundred years, pilgrims planted the sacred shidarezakura and the mountain cherry as offerings to the god of the mountain, and in the first half of April the slopes turn pink, white and green in four layers from the bottom to the top, a wave of flowering that rises up the hillside over about a fortnight.

  3. 3. Yoshino and Ômine - Ôminesan-ji

    Ōminesan-ji is a temple on the summit of Mount Sanjō, one of the peaks of the Ōmine range in the heart of the Kii peninsula, and it holds a special place in Japanese religion. It is a centre of Shugendō, the mountain ascetic tradition that blends Buddhism, Shinto and older mountain worship, and its practitioners, the yamabushi, undergo arduous training on the ridges. The temple is famous for another reason too: the mountain is still closed to women by tradition, one of the last such places in Japan, which has become a subject of ongoing debate.
