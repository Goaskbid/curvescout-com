Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Nizwá

    Nizwá was the capital of Oman for centuries, and it still feels like the heart of the interior. About seventy thousand people live in this oasis city, at the foot of the Hajar Mountains, among date palms watered by falaj channels, the ancient irrigation systems that carry water from the hills by gravity, some of which have been in use for more than a thousand years. UNESCO has recognised the aflaj of Oman for their importance.

  2. 2. Near Izkī

    Birkat al Mawz sits at the foot of the Jebel Akhdar, where the mountains of the Hajar range rise abruptly from the gravel plains of interior Oman, and it is a classic Omani oasis: date palm groves watered by a falaj, an ancient irrigation channel, mud-brick houses, a fort, and the bare, folded mountains rising behind. The name means pool of bananas, and bananas do grow in the gardens, along with dates, limes and mangoes, all watered by the channel that brings water from the mountains.

  3. 3. Near Izkī

    The road up the Jebel Akhdar is one of the steepest paved roads in the Arabian peninsula, and it does not ease you in. From the police checkpoint near Birkat al Mawz, it climbs relentlessly through a gorge and up the bare, rocky face of the mountain, gaining over a thousand metres in a short distance, with gradients that test brakes on the way down and engines on the way up. Escape lanes filled with gravel line the descent, for trucks and cars whose brakes have failed, and they tell you everything about the gradient.

  4. 4. Oman Jebel Akhdar Road point 4

    On top of the Jebel Akhdar, around two thousand metres up, the air is cool and the landscape changes completely. The Saiq plateau is a high, rocky tableland cut by deep gorges, and in the villages that cling to the edges of these gorges, terraces of fruit trees and roses step down the cliffs, watered by aflaj channels. The name Jebel Akhdar means Green Mountain, and after the bare rock of the climb, the green of the terraces is a shock. In winter it can be cold enough for frost, and occasionally snow, which draws excited crowds from the lowlands.
