Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Coimbra

    Coimbra is a hill town that happens to hold one of the oldest universities in Europe. The old city climbs up from the Mondego river in a tangle of steep, cobbled lanes, with the university buildings at the summit, and in the evenings the sound of fado drifts down from taverns and small squares. The Coimbra version of fado is different from the Lisbon one, sung traditionally by men, often students in black capes, and tied to serenades beneath windows.

  2. 2. Penacova

    Penacova perches on a ridge high above the Mondego, and the first thing you notice is the drop. The town looks down into a deep green valley where the river runs through a wooded gorge on its way from the mountains to the sea at Figueira da Foz. A viewpoint at the edge of the town gives a wide look over the river bend, with forested hills on either side. It is a calm spot for a break, and the air feels cooler and fresher than the coast.

  3. 3. Luso

    Luso is where Portuguese mineral water comes from, and it is also where one of the great forests in the country begins. The little spa town sits at the foot of the Buçaco hills, and for generations visitors came to take the waters. The water is still bottled here and sold across the country under the name of the town, and the spa buildings and old hotels give Luso a gentle, slightly faded charm.
