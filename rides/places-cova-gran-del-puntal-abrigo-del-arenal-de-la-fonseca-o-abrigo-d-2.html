Why this ride

A 522 km round trip from Albocàsser - roughly 11 h in the saddle. The stops in between: Racó de Nando, Castell de Villafamés, Cova de Gargán and Abrigo del Arenal de la fonseca ó Abrigo d. It tops out around 1,350 m. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Cova Gran del Puntal

    Start here.

    A cova gran is a big cave, and in the limestone of the Alt Maestrat that is an easy thing to find. This whole upland is karst, pale rock that rain has been dissolving for millions of years, leaving ravines, sinkholes, springs that vanish underground and a good number of overhangs deep enough to sleep under. The Cova Gran del Puntal sits about six kilometres from Albocàsser in the ravine that the rides call the Barranco Hondo, the deep one, and it belongs to the family of Levantine rock-art shelters that UNESCO has listed across eastern Spain. Paintings of this kind sit in shallow recesses rather than in the dark depths of a cave, so do not expect Altamira. Expect a modest rock wall, a lot of faded red, and a very good view down the gully.

  2. 2. Racó de Nando

    36 km in.

    Racó means corner in Valencian, and this really is a corner: a fold in the limestone hills between Benassal, Culla and Vilafranca, in the interior of Castellón where the coast's crowds feel a continent away. The rock shelter here is one of the many small sites of Levantine prehistoric painting that together make up a UNESCO World Heritage listing, and like most of them it is modest, fenced and best seen with a local guide. What lingers is less the painting than the idea of hunters choosing this exact overhang, with water close by and a view down the valley, and leaving their mark on it.

    Read the complete Racó de Nando write-up.

  3. 3. Castell de Villafamés

    83 km in.

    Villafamés announces itself from a long way off: a village of white and ochre houses stacked up a hill of reddish rock, with the remains of a castle crowning the top. It stands a little inland from the coast of Castellón, roughly between Borriol, Cabanes and the beach town of Benicàssim, close enough to the sea that you can smell salt on a westerly breeze turning, yet already in that rumpled hill country that leads up into the Maestrat.

    Read the complete Castell de Villafamés write-up.

  4. 4. Cova de Gargán

    127 km in.

    High in the interior of Castellón, where the coastal orange groves are long forgotten and the land climbs toward the Penyagolosa massif, the shelter known as Cova de Gargán sits in a country of limestone ledges, holm oak and dry-stone terraces. Vistabella del Maestrazgo is the nearest village, a handful of kilometres away on its high plateau, with Villahermosa del Río down in its valley to the other side and Lucena del Cid a little further south. It is quiet up here in a way that the coast never is.

    Read the complete Cova de Gargán write-up.

  5. 5. Abrigo del Arenal de la fonseca ó Abrigo d

    262 km in.

    The Guadalope river cuts a deep canyon through the limestone of the Maestrazgo in eastern Teruel, and the Abrigo del Arenal de la Fonseca lies in this wild country near the hamlet of Ladruñán, between the villages of Castellote and Cantavieja. It is one of the important rock-art shelters of Aragon, part of the UNESCO listing of Mediterranean prehistoric art, with Levantine paintings of animals and human figures in red pigment on the rock. The setting is spectacular: a narrow gorge with towering cliffs, the river flowing below, and pine and holm oak clinging to the slopes. Access involves a walk, and the paintings are protected by a fence.
