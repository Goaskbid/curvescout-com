Why this ride

170 km from Pushkin to Pushkin, about 3 h 15 of riding. It goes by way of Historical Centre of the Town of Gatchina, Izhorsky Bench, The Palace and Park Ensemble «Sobstvennaya and back through Izhorsky Bench. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

The 28 km between Izhorsky Bench and The Palace and Park Ensemble «Sobstvennaya on Ропшинское шоссе. Fairly level - 20 to 75 m end to end. Best in the early light, before the traffic.

The stops

  1. 1. The Babolovsky Park

    Start here.

    Inside a ruined palace in a quiet park south of Saint Petersburg sits one of the strangest objects in Russia: a gigantic bath carved from a single block of granite, more than five metres across, made in the early nineteenth century by the stonemason Samson Sukhanov for the Babolovsky palace. The palace, a small Gothic style residence built for Catherine the Great's favourite Prince Potemkin, was later rebuilt and then ruined during the Second World War, but the bath was too heavy to move and remains inside the shell of the building.

    Read the complete The Babolovsky Park write-up.

  2. 2. Historical Centre of the Town of Gatchina

    26 km in.

    Gatchina is the kind of town you reach by flat, straight Ingrian roads, and the first impression is of birch, pine and wide skies rather than anything dramatic. Forty-odd kilometres south-west of St Petersburg, it sits on land that was once swampy and sparsely settled, until the eighteenth century decided otherwise. Catherine the Great gave the estate to her favourite Grigory Orlov, and the architect Antonio Rinaldi built him a severe, castle-like palace of local limestone beside a lake. After Orlov died it passed to her son Paul, who made it his private kingdom, drilled his soldiers on the parade ground and added the odd fortress-style touches that suited his nature.

    Read the complete Historical Centre of the Town of Gatchina write-up.

  3. 3. Izhorsky Bench

    55 km in.

    South of St Petersburg, the flat marshy plain of the Neva lowland suddenly steps up onto a limestone plateau. Geologists call the edge a glint or bench, and here, near Russko-Vysotskoye and Kipen, it forms a low but noticeable escarpment, the northern edge of the Izhora plateau. It is one of the natural features included in the wide UNESCO listing of St Petersburg and its surroundings, which recognises not only palaces and canals but the landscape the city was built against.

    Read the complete Izhorsky Bench write-up.

  4. 4. The Palace and Park Ensemble «Sobstvennaya

    84 km in.

    Peterhof is the one everyone has seen in photographs, with the golden statue of Samson tearing open a lion's jaws and the long canal running down to the Gulf of Finland. The Sobstvennaya Dacha, the Tsar's Own Dacha, is a quieter corner of the same ensemble, tucked into the parkland on the southern shore of the gulf. The ground here is the Izhorsky bench, a limestone shelf running east to west with a sudden step down toward the water.
