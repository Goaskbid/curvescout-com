Why this ride

From Siracusa it runs 398 km to Siracusa; allow about 6 h 52 of riding. It goes by way of Necropolis of Pantalica, Militello Val di Catania, Caltagirone and Mount Etna. The highest point is about 1,200 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Syracuse and the Rocky Necropolis of Panta

    Syracuse is far more than the island of Ortygia. The mainland district of Neapolis holds the Greek theatre, carved into the hillside and still used for classical drama in early summer, and the Latomie, the huge quarries from which the Greeks cut their stone, now filled with citrus trees and overhung with cliffs. The most famous cavity is the Ear of Dionysius, a tall curved cave with remarkable acoustics, which Caravaggio is said to have named when he visited in 1608. The same painter's Burial of Saint Lucy hangs in Syracuse too, dark and physical, with two gravediggers in the foreground dwarfing the saint.

  2. 2. Necropolis of Pantalica

    Thousands of small square doorways riddle the limestone cliffs above the Anapo and Calcinara valleys, like the windows of a vast abandoned apartment block. These are rock-cut tombs, some five thousand of them, carved by the people who lived at Pantalica between roughly the thirteenth and seventh centuries before Christ. Together with Syracuse, the necropolis is a UNESCO World Heritage site, and it is one of the most evocative places in Sicily, in a gorge of the Hyblaean Mountains west of Syracuse, between the towns of Sortino and Ferla.

  3. 3. Militello Val di Catania

    Militello in Val di Catania is a small town in the hills south-west of Catania, in eastern Sicily, surrounded by orange groves and olive trees, with Mount Etna visible on the horizon. Like many towns in this part of Sicily, it was devastated by the earthquake of 1693, which destroyed much of the south-east of the island, and was rebuilt in the Baroque style. Its churches and palaces are part of the UNESCO-listed Late Baroque Towns of the Val di Noto, inscribed in 2002, together with Noto, Ragusa, Modica, Caltagirone and others.

  4. 4. Caltagirone

    Caltagirone is a city built on ceramics. Perched on a ridge in the hills of south-east Sicily, it has been making pottery since ancient times, and the tradition is everywhere: majolica tiles on balconies, bridges and church domes, pottery shops lining the streets and a museum devoted to the craft. Its most famous sight is the Scalinata di Santa Maria del Monte, a staircase of well over a hundred steps climbing from the lower town to a church at the top, each riser decorated with hand-painted tiles in different patterns and colours. In late July, for the feast of Saint James, the staircase is lit with thousands of oil lamps arranged in designs, a magical sight.

  5. 5. Mount Etna

    Etna is not so much a mountain as a living presence that dominates eastern Sicily. Europe's highest active volcano rises over 3,300 metres above Catania, its height changing as eruptions add and remove material, and it smokes, rumbles and occasionally spits fire with a regularity that the locals regard with a mix of reverence and resignation.
