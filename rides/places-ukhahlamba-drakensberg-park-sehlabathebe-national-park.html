Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. uKhahlamba Drakensberg Park

    The Zulu name uKhahlamba means barrier of spears, and the first sight of the Drakensberg escarpment from the plain makes it clear why. For about two hundred kilometers along the border between KwaZulu-Natal and Lesotho, a wall of basalt cliffs rises to more than three thousand meters, broken by jagged buttresses, towers and deep gorges. The park protects much of it, and UNESCO gave it dual listing for both nature and culture in 2000, reflecting the richness of the wildlife and the astonishing rock art.

  2. 2. Maloti-Drakensberg Park

    The Drakensberg is South Africa's great wall, a basalt escarpment that rises along the border with Lesotho for hundreds of kilometres and reaches over three thousand metres. The Zulus called it uKhahlamba, the barrier of spears, and the Afrikaners called it the Dragon Mountain. The uKhahlamba Drakensberg Park on the South African side and Sehlabathebe National Park in Lesotho together form the Maloti-Drakensberg Park, a World Heritage site recognised both for its landscape and for its remarkable collection of San rock paintings, some made by hunter-gatherers over several thousand years.

  3. 3. Sehlabathebe National Park

    Sehlabathebe is the kind of place that makes you feel you have gone off the edge of the map. It sits in the south-east of Lesotho at around 2,400 metres, on a plateau of sandstone and basalt that rolls up to the Drakensberg escarpment, and it was the country's first national park, set up in 1969. It adjoins the uKhahlamba-Drakensberg Park in South Africa, and together they form the Maloti-Drakensberg Transfrontier World Heritage Site, a high, wild mountain landscape with grassy uplands, rock pools and sandstone arches carved by wind and water.
