Why this ride

It goes by way of Upper Tsagaan Gol, Aral Tolgoi and back through Upper Tsagaan Gol. The highest point is about 2,950 m. It crosses from Russia into Mongolia - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. Open plateau country, wide and exposed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ukok Quiet Zone on the Ukok Plateau

    The Ukok Plateau is where Russia runs out of room. At around two thousand meters, in the far south of the Altai Republic, a wide cold steppe lies pinned between the borders of Kazakhstan, China and Mongolia, with snow peaks on the horizon and permafrost under the grass. It is part of the Golden Mountains of Altai, a UNESCO site listed in 1998, and the Russian authorities named it a Quiet Zone to protect the landscape and the traditions of the people who live and herd animals here.

  2. 2. Upper Tsagaan Gol

    Far out in Mongolia's westernmost corner, where the country wedges itself between Russia, China and Kazakhstan, the Altai mountains rise to the glaciated crown of Tavan Bogd. Below them the Tsagaan Gol, the White River, runs through valleys of larch and bare rock, and on the walls above the water prehistoric hunters carved some of the finest petroglyphs in Central Asia. They are part of the Petroglyphic Complexes of the Mongolian Altai, a UNESCO listing that spans thousands of years, from around the end of the Ice Age through the Bronze Age to the early Turkic era. The images show ibex, aurochs, deer, camels, hunters with bows, and in the later layers horsemen and chariots.

  3. 3. Aral Tolgoi

    Aral Tolgoi sits on a bend of open ground in the Altai of western Mongolia, in the province of Bayan-Ulgii, where the land climbs steadily toward the borders with China and Russia. The name has the sound of a place: tolgoi means a hill or head, and aral an island, so think of a rise standing out in flat, braided ground, perhaps once surrounded by channels of the river nearby. It is part of the same ancient story as the petroglyph sites that lie along these valleys.
