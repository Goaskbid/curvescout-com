Why this ride

From Poceirão it runs 757 km to Samora Correia; allow about 14 h 08 of riding. The stops in between: Óbidos, São Pedro da Cadeira, Monte Estoril and Charneca de Caparica. The climbs: Comporta and Arrábida and Setúbal.

The roads

Atlantic Coast N247

52 km from Near Silveira, on the way back to Colares, mostly on EN 9. Fairly level - 27 to 206 m end to end. Water alongside for much of it - pick one good pull-out and stop.

The stops

  1. 1. Fuel stop near Poceirão

    Start here.

  2. 2. Near Óbidos, on the way back

    111 km in.

    Óbidos is a walled village so well preserved that it looks arranged for the camera, which does not make it any less lovely. White houses trimmed with blue and yellow climb a hill inside the medieval walls, and a castle on the summit has been turned into a hotel. The story goes that in the thirteenth century King Dinis gave the town to his queen Isabel as a wedding present, and for centuries afterwards it belonged to the queens of Portugal. The tradition made it a place of royal favour, and it kept its walls and its charm through the centuries.

    Read the complete Lisbon / Portugal write-up.

  3. 3. São Pedro da Cadeira

    169 km in.

    Out in the rolling country of the Oeste, north of Lisbon, São Pedro da Cadeira is a farming parish in the municipality of Torres Vedras, a scatter of villages, vineyards, orchards and greenhouses on the hills that run down to the Atlantic. It is not a destination in its own right. It is a place where people work the land, and that is exactly why it makes a good, honest lunch stop on the way north to the mountains.

    Read the complete São Pedro da Cadeira write-up.

  4. 4. Monte Estoril

    220 km in.

    Faded grandeur and Atlantic light define Monte Estoril. The district rises from the coast between Estoril and Cascais, west of Lisbon, with villas, gardens and old hotels on the slopes above the sea, and a long promenade along the beach below. This was once one of Europe's most fashionable resorts, and some of that elegance survives in the palm-lined avenues and the houses with their tiled facades.

    Read the complete Monte Estoril write-up.

  5. 5. Charneca de Caparica

    258 km in.

    Charneca de Caparica sits on the high, sandy plateau behind the beaches of the Costa da Caparica, on the south side of the Tagus across from Lisbon. It is part of Almada and mostly residential, a sprawl of villas, small streets and umbrella pines spread over land that was once heath and scrub, which is what charneca means. To the west the plateau ends abruptly at a line of tall, golden cliffs, and below them lies one of the longest unbroken beaches near any European capital.

    Read the complete Charneca de Caparica write-up.

  6. 6. Comporta

    319 km in. Poceirão · Óbidos and São Pedro da Cadeira Loop high point is the summit of this stretch.

    Rice is not what most people expect in southern Portugal, but here at Comporta the land flattens into wide green paddies laid out along the Sado estuary, with herons and white storks standing in the water and the smell of mud and salt on the wind. Comporta is a small village of whitewashed houses and reed-thatched cottages on the edge of these fields, set between the estuary to the east and a huge Atlantic beach to the west, on the long sandy peninsula that runs south from Tróia.

    Read the complete Comporta write-up.

  7. 7. Scenic stretch near Setúbal

    330 km in.

    Setúbal is a working port city at the mouth of the Sado estuary, the sort of place that tourists tend to skip and locals quietly love. Fishing boats, ferries and cargo ships share the water, and across the estuary the long, flat sand spit of Tróia stretches out. Out in the water lives a small resident community of bottlenose dolphins, which you can sometimes see from boat trips leaving the harbour. Behind the city rises the Serra da Arrábida, a steep ridge of limestone covered in Mediterranean scrub.

    Read the complete Scenic stretch near Setúbal write-up.

  8. 8. Back to Poceirão

    385 km in.

    There is nothing glamorous about this spot, and that is its charm. It lies on the flat lands south of the Tagus, in the region between Alcochete and the town of Poceirão, a country of cork oaks, pine plantations, sandy soil and straight roads. After a long day in the mountains of the Serra da Estrela, this is where the Lisbon area starts to wrap itself around you again.

    Read the complete Poceirão write-up.

    428 km in.

  10. 10. Near Silveira, on the way back

    492 km in.

    In 1810, the Duke of Wellington, defending Portugal against Napoleon's army, built a series of forts and earthworks across the peninsula north of Lisbon, from the Atlantic to the Tagus. The Lines of Torres Vedras were so strong that the French, arriving in front of them, simply stopped, and after months of hunger they retreated. Forts and redoubts survive on the hilltops around Torres Vedras, and some have been restored.

    Read the complete Near Silveira, on the way back write-up.

  11. 11. Colares

    543 km in.

    In Colares, the vines grow in sand. The vineyards, on the dunes near the Atlantic, are dug deep into the sand to reach the clay beneath, and the vines, low and sprawling, are protected from the wind by fences of cane. Because phylloxera, the insect that destroyed Europe's vineyards in the nineteenth century, cannot survive in sand, the Ramisco vines of Colares were never grafted, and some are very old. The wine is rare, tannic and long-lived, a curiosity of Portuguese wine.

    Read the complete Colares write-up.

  12. 12. Sesimbra

    610 km in.

    Sesimbra is a fishing town that has become a beach resort without quite forgetting what it was. In the morning the boats come in and the catch is sold at the harbour, and by lunchtime the grill smoke is already rising from the waterfront restaurants. The black scabbardfish, the espada, and fresh sardines in season are the local specialties, along with swordfish. Above the town stand the walls of a Moorish castle, with a view down over rooftops to the bay, and a smaller sea fort guards the waterfront.

    Read the complete Sesimbra write-up.

  13. 13. Near Charneca de Caparica

    638 km in.

    Start and finish here and you are on the south bank of the Tagus, in the pine-scented suburbs of Almada between Charneca de Caparica, Corroios and Sobreda. It is a residential area of houses, schools and supermarkets, without much to look at in itself, but its position is very good: close to Lisbon across the river, and close to the open roads that lead south to the Arrábida hills and the city of Setúbal.

    Read the complete Near Charneca de Caparica write-up.

  14. 14. Setubal

    680 km in. The high point of this section.

    Palmela's castle crowns a hill above the Setúbal plain, and from this high point you see everything: the Sado estuary to the south, the Tagus and Lisbon to the north, the Arrábida range filling the horizon, and on a clear day a slice of the Atlantic. The Moors held the castle until it fell to the first king of Portugal in the twelfth century, and it was later the headquarters of the Order of Santiago, a military order whose knights helped defend the frontier. The fortress, now partly a hotel, has a bastion walk that is worth the climb.

    Read the complete Setubal write-up.

  16. 16. Back to Arrábida

    The end: 757 km.

    The Arrábida itself lies further south, beyond Palmela, but the estuary is worth a paragraph of its own because it shapes everything about this part of Portugal.
