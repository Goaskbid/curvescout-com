  1. 1. Amsterdam

    Amsterdam is built on water and wood. The city was founded in the thirteenth century as a fishing village on a dam across the Amstel, and in the seventeenth century it grew into the richest trading city in the world, and the canals that circle the centre in concentric arcs were dug in that Golden Age. Merchants built tall, narrow houses along them, with gabled fronts and hoisting beams so that goods could be lifted to the upper floors. The canal ring is on the UNESCO list. The houses lean forward slightly, partly by design, and sink on their wooden piles, so that some visibly tilt.

  2. 2. Fort near Heemstede

    Between Haarlem and the bulb fields of the Dutch coast, near the town of Heemstede and the hamlet of De Glip, lies a squat, grassy fort surrounded by a moat. This is the Fort near Heemstede, built around the turn of the twentieth century as part of the Defence Line of Amsterdam, a ring of forts, dykes and flooding zones around the city, and now part of the UNESCO-listed Dutch Water Defence Lines.

  3. 3. Droogmakerij de Beemster

    In 1612 a lake north of Amsterdam disappeared. Wealthy merchants, flush with money from the Dutch trading boom, financed the draining of the Beemster, and the engineer Jan Adriaenszoon Leeghwater used dozens of windmills to pump the water out into a ring canal. What remained was a polder several metres below sea level, laid out in a perfectly regular grid of roads, canals and fields based on Renaissance ideas of order and proportion. The Beemster was inscribed by UNESCO in 1999 as a masterpiece of creative planning, and from above it looks like a ruled sheet of paper.

  5. 5. Fort Werk IV

    The Gooi, the area east of Amsterdam around Bussum, Naarden and Hilversum, is one of the few places in the western Netherlands with hills, if you can call low sandy ridges hills. It is a region of heathland, woods and wealthy villages. On the edge of Bussum, close to the fortified town of Naarden, stands Fort Werk IV, a small fortification that was part of the New Dutch Waterline and the Defence Line of Amsterdam, and is now part of the UNESCO-listed Dutch Water Defence Lines.
