Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Archaeological Ensemble of Mérida

    Mérida is one of those places where you can trip over two thousand years of history on the way to buy bread. The Archaeological Ensemble is the UNESCO name, dating from 1993, for a cluster of Roman monuments spread through the modern town: a theatre, an amphitheatre, a circus, bridges, aqueducts, temples and a forum, all preserved to a degree that is rare outside Italy. The Guadiana flows past below, wide and slow in summer, and the town sits on its north bank among olive groves and plains of wheat.

  2. 2. Old Town of Cáceres

    Cáceres has an old town so complete that film crews treat it as a ready-made set, and walking its streets at dusk, when the stone glows amber and the crowds have gone, you can see why. Inside the walls, built largely by the Almohads in the twelfth century, are dozens of fortified mansions, towers, churches and palaces, many built by noble families who made their fortunes in the conquest of the Americas. The towers once had battlements, but Queen Isabella ordered most of them cut down to curb the nobles' power. UNESCO listed the old town in 1986.
