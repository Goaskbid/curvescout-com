Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Litomyšl Castle

    Bedřich Smetana, the composer of Má vlast and The Bartered Bride, was born in 1824 in the brewery of Litomyšl Castle, where his father was the brewer, and the town has never forgotten it. Every summer, the castle courtyard hosts an opera festival named after him, Smetanova Litomyšl, and his birthplace in the brewery building is a small museum. The castle itself, an elegant Renaissance building with arcaded courtyards, was built in the sixteenth century for the Pernštejn family and is famous for its sgraffito decoration: the facades are covered with thousands of rectangular panels, each with a slightly different pattern. It was listed by UNESCO in 1999.

  2. 2. Landscape for Breeding and Training of Cer

    Kladruby nad Labem is a horse village in the flat country of the Elbe valley in eastern Bohemia, and its stud farm is among the oldest in the world. It was founded in the sixteenth century to breed carriage horses for the Habsburg court, and the Kladruber, a large, elegant horse that is usually white or black, is still bred here. The landscape, with its stables, riding grounds, pastures, avenues of trees and farm buildings laid out as a single working system, was added to the UNESCO list in 2019 as a cultural landscape.

  3. 3. Jizera Mountains

    The Jizera Mountains sit in the far north of Bohemia, right where the Czech Republic meets Poland and Germany, and they have a stern, quiet character that sets them apart from the better-known Krkonoše to the east. Hejnice, at their foot, is a small town in the valley of the Smědá river, famous for a large Baroque pilgrimage church with a basilica and a Franciscan monastery. Above it, the ridges rise to forested plateaus and open peat bogs, with roads that thread between spruce and beech.
