Why this ride

From Johannesburg it runs 347 km to Johannesburg; allow about 5 h 59 of riding. It goes by way of Sharpeville Memorial garden, Sharpeville Massacre Site: police station, The stromatolite/basal fault plane site and Vredefort Dome. The highest point is about 1,650 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

  1. 1. Johannesburg

    Johannesburg began with a rumour, and the rumour was gold. In 1886, a reef of it was found on the Witwatersrand, and within a decade a tented camp had become a city of mines, offices and shanties, the biggest in southern Africa. It sits on the Highveld at nearly 1,800 metres, which gives it a bright dry climate, cold winter nights and violent summer thunderstorms. Today it is a sprawling metropolis of over two million, with a lot of history to confront.

  2. 2. Sharpeville Memorial garden

    On 21 March 1960, a crowd of several thousand people gathered outside the police station in Sharpeville, a township near Vereeniging in the south of Gauteng, to protest against the pass laws, which required black South Africans to carry documents restricting where they could live and work. The police opened fire, killing sixty nine people and wounding many more, most of them shot in the back as they fled. The massacre shocked the world and marked a turning point in the struggle against apartheid, leading to the banning of the liberation movements and the start of armed resistance. The date is now commemorated in South Africa as Human Rights Day, and internationally as the day for the elimination of racial discrimination.

    Sharpeville is a heavy place, and it should be approached quietly. On 21 March 1960, thousands of people gathered outside the police station to protest against the pass laws, which forced Black South Africans to carry documents controlling where they could live and work. The police opened fire on the crowd, and sixty-nine people were killed, many shot in the back as they fled. The massacre shocked the world, led to the banning of liberation movements in South Africa and hardened international opposition to apartheid.

  4. 4. The stromatolite/basal fault plane site

    Microbes made this. That is the surprising thing about stromatolites: the layered, domed structures that look like cabbages turned to stone are the fossil remains of mats of bacteria that lived in shallow water and trapped sediment, growing layer on layer. They are among the oldest evidence of life on the planet, and the rocks around the Vredefort Dome in South Africa carry some very ancient examples.

  5. 5. Vredefort Dome

    A hundred and fifty kilometres from Johannesburg, the ground rises into a ring of low hills about seventy kilometres across, and that ring is the surviving core of the largest meteorite impact the Earth has kept a record of. Vredefort Dome formed about two billion years ago, when an asteroid several kilometres wide hit what was then a very different landscape. The structure was once perhaps three hundred kilometres across, and erosion since has scraped most of it away, leaving the central uplift visible. UNESCO listed it in 2005.
