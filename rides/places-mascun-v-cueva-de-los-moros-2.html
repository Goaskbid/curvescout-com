Why this ride

554 km from Bárcabo to Bárcabo, about 8 h 08 of riding. On the way it passes Pyrénées - Mont Perdu, Cueva de los moros. and back through Pyrénées - Mont Perdu. It tops out around 1,950 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Pyrenees N260 Route

The 134 km between Pyrénées - Mont Perdu and Cueva de los moros. on Eje Pirenaico. Over the top at 1,313 m - roughly 440 m up and 1080 m down. A long one - plan a coffee halfway.

The stops

  1. 1. Mascún V

    Start here.

    The Barranco de Mascún is one of the great canyons of the Sierra de Guara, a limestone range in the pre-Pyrenees of Huesca, where rivers have carved deep, narrow gorges, caves and towers into the rock. The canyon, near the village of Rodellar, is famous among climbers for its overhanging walls and among canyoners for its pools and waterfalls, and it also holds traces of prehistoric painting in its shelters. Mascún V is one of these sites, part of the group of Mediterranean rock art sites listed by UNESCO, and the setting, with sheer orange and grey walls rising above the river, is extraordinary.

    Read the complete Mascún V write-up.

  2. 2. Pyrénées - Mont Perdu

    152 km in. The ride passes about 6 km from it.

    Monte Perdido, the lost mountain, at 3,355 metres, is the highest limestone massif in Europe and the centrepiece of a cross-border World Heritage site shared by Spain and France. On the Spanish side, it gives its name to the Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park, with deep canyons radiating out from the summit; on the French side, it looks down on the great cirques of Gavarnie and Troumouse.

    Read the complete Pyrénées - Mont Perdu write-up.

  3. 3. Cueva de los moros.

    287 km in.

    There is a Cueva de los Moros in half the valleys of Spain. Every unexplained cave, ruin or rock shelter used to be credited to the Moors, and this one, in the hills of the western Aragonese Pyrenees between the valleys of Hecho and Ansó, is a rock shelter with ancient traces. You should approach it as an excuse to spend time in two of the most beautiful and least spoiled valleys of the Pyrenees, rather than as a monument.
