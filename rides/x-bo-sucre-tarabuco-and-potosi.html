  1. 1. Sucre

    Sucre is the city you stay longer in than you meant to. It is Bolivia's constitutional capital, tucked into a mild valley at roughly 2,800 metres, and its centre is a calm grid of whitewashed colonial buildings with red-tiled roofs that earned it a place on the UNESCO list. The altitude is gentler than La Paz or Potosí, the air is warmer and the pace slower, which is why overlanders tend to rest here after weeks on the plateau.

  2. 2. Tarabuco

    Come on a Sunday or do not bother, is the local wisdom, because Tarabuco is a small highland town with a very large market once a week. It sits in the hills east of Sucre at around 3,300 metres, roughly forty kilometres from the city, and it belongs to the Yampara people, whose weaving traditions are among the finest in Bolivia. The streets are quiet most days and then fill on Sunday morning with farmers, weavers and buyers from the surrounding valleys.

  3. 3. Potosi

    Potosí is the highest large city in the world, near 4,000 metres, and it is a place of enormous history. Above it stands the Cerro Rico, the Rich Hill, a reddish cone that gave the Spanish crown so much silver after 1545 that it helped shape the economy of the whole of Europe. For a time Potosí was one of the biggest cities in the world, and the phrase worth a Potosí became a Spanish byword for great wealth. Today the mountain is hollowed out by centuries of tunnels, and cooperative miners still work it, in conditions that leave a mark on any visitor.
