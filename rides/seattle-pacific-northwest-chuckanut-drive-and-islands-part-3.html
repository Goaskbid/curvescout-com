  1. 1. Seattle / Pacific Northwest

    Darrington is a logging town in the north Cascades foothills, about ten kilometres from this stop, and it sits at the confluence of the Sauk and Suiattle rivers, in a valley overshadowed by the cone of Whitehorse Mountain. To the east, the snowy volcano Glacier Peak rises to 3,213 metres, the most remote of the five Washington volcanoes, and on a clear day it can be seen from the roads around town. The country is wild, wet and steep, covered in dense forest, and the rivers run milky green with glacial silt.

  2. 2. Near Manchester

    Across Puget Sound from Seattle, on the eastern shore of the Kitsap Peninsula, the little community of Manchester looks back at the city skyline from a beach of pebbles and driftwood. On a clear evening the view is remarkable: the towers of downtown Seattle across the water, the Space Needle, ferries crossing in the dusk and, on a good day, Mount Rainier floating above it all. It is a quiet, unassuming place, with houses scattered along the shore and in the woods behind.

  3. 3. Back to Chuckanut Drive

    Black Diamond is a coal town that outlived its coal. In the 1880s, a coal company opened mines on this ridge in the foothills south-east of Seattle, and for decades the town sent coal by railway to ships in Seattle and San Francisco. Miners from Wales, Italy, Slovenia and many other countries lived in company houses, and a nearby ghost town, Franklin, was abandoned after a disaster in 1894. The mines closed in the early twentieth century, and today the town is a quiet place of old frame buildings, a railroad museum and a famous bakery.
