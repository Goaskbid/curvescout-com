Why this ride

A one-way ride of 194 km, Dawson City to the finish - roughly 2 h 40 in the saddle. The highest point is about 1,400 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Dawson City → Top of the World summit

The ground tilts up and stays tilted for 132 km. 1376 m of it, taken steadily rather than in one wall — a steady rhythm suits this better than bursts. The height arrives whether you hurry or not.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Dawson City

    Dawson City was built on a gamble. In August 1896, gold was found in a creek flowing into the Klondike river, and within two years, more than thirty thousand people had come to the swampy flat at the river's mouth, having crossed mountain passes with tons of supplies, floated down the Yukon, and spent everything they had. Most got no gold. A few got rich overnight, and the town rose, with saloons, theatres, hotels and dance halls, to become the largest city north of Seattle and west of Winnipeg.

  2. 2. Top of the World summit

    Somewhere past the last bend, the trees just give up. The Top of the World Highway, on the Yukon side, climbs out of the Klondike valley and follows a bare ridgeline for miles, with the sky enormous and the hills rolling away in every direction in tundra shades of rust, gold and grey. The listed height here is around 1,376 metres, which is not Alpine, but at this latitude it is well above the tree line and feels like the roof of the continent.

  3. 3. Poker Creek border

    Poker Creek is as far north as most riders will ever cross an international border by road, and it has the feel of a place at the end of the world. The tiny US customs post sits on a bare ridge on the Top of the World Highway, a road that follows the crest of the hills between Dawson City in the Yukon and Chicken in Alaska, with views that stretch for a hundred kilometres across a sea of rolling, treeless mountains. Here the boreal forest thins out into tundra, and the light in summer is long and golden.
