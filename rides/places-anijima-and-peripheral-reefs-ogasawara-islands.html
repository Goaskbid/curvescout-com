Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Anijima and peripheral reefs

    Anijima, whose name means elder brother island, lies a short boat ride off Chichijima in the Ogasawara or Bonin Islands, a chain of volcanic specks roughly a thousand kilometres south of Tokyo. Nobody gets here by accident. The only link to the mainland is the ferry that makes the journey of around twenty-four hours, running about once a week, and the whole group has never been joined to any continent. That isolation is why UNESCO listed it as a natural World Heritage Site in 2011, calling it an oceanic Galápagos.

  2. 2. Ogasawara Islands

    About a thousand kilometres south of Tokyo, in the Pacific, a scatter of small volcanic islands sits on the road to nowhere. These are the Ogasawara Islands, also called the Bonin Islands, and they have never been joined to any continent. That isolation is the whole point. Snails, plants and insects have evolved here in ways found nowhere else, which is why UNESCO listed the islands in 2011 and why some people call them the Galapagos of the East.
