Why this ride

138 km from La Cieneguita to La Cieneguita, about 2 h 23 of riding. On the way it passes Chapel of the former hacienda of Buenavist, Former hacienda of Chichimequillas and back through Chapel of the former hacienda of Buenavist. The highest point is about 2,250 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bridge of San Rafael

    The map gives this stop a name, the Bridge of San Rafael, and a spot on the edge of the valley of the Río Laja, west of San Miguel de Allende, close to the small settlement of La Cieneguita. What I can say is how a bridge like this fits the country. San Miguel stands on the slopes above the Laja, a river that wanders across the highland plateau of the Bajío, and for centuries the traffic of mule trains, wagons and pilgrims had to cross it somewhere. A stone bridge on a colonial road is a small, sturdy thing, of arches and cutwaters, and it is often older than anything else for miles.

  2. 2. Chapel of the former hacienda of Buenavist

    North of the city of Querétaro, the land rises into dry hills dotted with cactus, mesquite and the remains of old haciendas. These estates were the economic engines of colonial Mexico, producing grain, livestock and pulque for the cities and mines of the north. The chapel of the former Hacienda of Buenavista stands in this landscape near the village of the same name, close to Santa Rosa Jáuregui, and it is part of the UNESCO-listed Camino Real de Tierra Adentro.

  3. 3. Former hacienda of Chichimequillas

    On the high, dry plain northeast of Querétaro, the village of Chichimequillas grew up around an old hacienda, one of the many great estates that lined the Camino Real de Tierra Adentro, the Royal Inland Road that connected Mexico City with Santa Fe, in what is now New Mexico, for nearly three centuries. Silver, mercury, cloth, livestock, missionaries and soldiers travelled this route, and haciendas like this one supplied food, water and rest. The route was listed by UNESCO in 2010 as a series of sites, and the hacienda is one of them; its old buildings, chapel and walls hint at the wealth and power once concentrated here.
