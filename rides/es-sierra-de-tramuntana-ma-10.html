Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Andratx

    Andratx is the south-western gateway to Mallorca's Serra de Tramuntana, the mountain range that runs like a spine along the island's north-western coast, and the start of the Ma-10, the island's most famous road. The town is a quiet inland place of about twelve thousand people, with a church fortified against pirates, narrow lanes and a lively Wednesday market, and the sea is a few kilometres away at Port d'Andratx, a smart harbour lined with restaurants and yachts.

  2. 2. Valldemossa

    The Ma-10 is the road that made Mallorca's north-west famous with riders, and Valldemossa is its most photographed village. It lies in a fold of the Serra de Tramuntana, a long limestone ridge that runs down the island's western flank and earned UNESCO World Heritage status for its terraced hillsides and centuries-old water systems. Cobbled lanes, honey-coloured stone and green shutters crowd the village, and the hills behind are dark with holm oak and pine.

  3. 3. Sóller

    Sóller lies in a basin of orange and lemon trees, enclosed by the highest peaks of the Serra de Tramuntana, and it is the nearest thing in Mallorca to a green bowl. For centuries the only way in or out was a mountain pass or a boat, and the isolation made the valley rich and strange. Local merchants sold oranges to France and returned with money and ideas, and some of the town's buildings show it, including a modernista church front and a bank in a style borrowed from Barcelona. The old wooden train from Palma, which opened in 1912, still runs through tunnels and viaducts to the station in the middle of town, and a small tram carries passengers down to the harbour at Port de Sóller.

  4. 4. Pollença

    Pollença sits in a shallow bowl at the foot of the Serra de Tramuntana, the limestone mountain range that runs down Mallorca's northwest coast, and it is one of the island's best-looking towns. Pale stone houses, green shutters and plane-lined squares fill the old center, and above it rises the Puig de Maria, a hill topped with a small monastery. The town has the confident, slightly arty air of a place that painters have loved for a century, and its main square, the Plaça Major, fills with a market on Sundays.
