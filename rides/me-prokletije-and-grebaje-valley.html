Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Plav

    Plav lies in the far east of Montenegro, in a high green basin beside a glacial lake, with the jagged limestone peaks of the Prokletije, the Accursed Mountains, rising to the south and west. The lake is calm and cold, edged with willows and reed, and the mountains behind seem to hang above it, their summits often streaked with snow until early summer. Zla Kolata, near the Albanian border, is the highest peak in Montenegro, at over twenty-five hundred metres, and the whole range is a wilderness of cirques, screes and deep valleys.

  2. 2. Gusinje

    Gusinje is a mountain town at the foot of the Prokletije, the range whose name means Accursed Mountains, and from the main street you can look up at limestone peaks that crowd the border with Albania and Kosovo. It lies in a high valley of the Lim headwaters near the lake of Plav, a place of green meadows, old stone houses and a population that is largely Bosniak and Albanian. The town's character is more Ottoman than Slavic, with mosques, small shops and family-run cafés, and the pace of life is slow.
