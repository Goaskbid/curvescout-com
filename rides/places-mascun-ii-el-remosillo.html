Why this ride

99 km from Bárcabo to Bárcabo, about 1 h 42 of riding. It goes by way of Malforá I, El Remosillo and back through Malforá I. The highest point is about 1,050 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mascún II

    Boltaña is a small town of nine hundred people in the Sobrarbe, the valley country at the foot of the high Pyrenees, and from its edge the view north is dominated by the great limestone massifs of the Monte Perdido range, often white with snow into early summer. The shelter at Mascún II lies about twenty kilometres away, in the quieter, lower hills of the Sierra de Guara. It carries painting in the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain, and the ride links it with El Remosillo, another site in the canyons.

  2. 2. Malforá I

    The Sierra de Guara is where the Pyrenean foothills turn to stone. Limestone ridges, deep canyons and rocky crags form a country that is famous among canyoners, and the Vero river runs through the middle in a gorge of pale, polished rock. Alquézar, a village of only about 300 people, perches on a rocky spur over the river, with a collegiate church and a castle on top, and its narrow lanes are among the most photographed in Aragon. It was once a Moorish stronghold, and in the eleventh century it fell to the Christian king Sancho Ramírez, an event that marks the early Reconquista in this region.

  3. 3. El Remosillo

    If you ride north from Barbastro toward Graus, the Ésera river eventually forces its way through a ridge of limestone, and the result is the Congosto de Olvena, a short, deep gorge with the road squeezed between rock and water. Up on the cliffs around here are rock shelters with prehistoric paintings, and El Remosillo is one of the best known in this corner of Aragón, between Estadilla, Graus and Fonz.
