Why this ride

From la Salut it runs 272 km to la Salut; allow about 4 h 41 of riding. On the way it passes Roca Roja, Abric de la Serra de la mussara and back through Roca Roja. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Works of Antoni Gaudí

    Antoni Gaudí died on 10 June 1926, after being struck by a tram on the Gran Via, and since this year marks the centenary of his death, Barcelona has been in a mood of homage. The seven works of his that UNESCO lists as a group, among them the crypt of the Colònia Güell, a few kilometres outside the city, form one of the most distinctive bodies of architecture in Europe. They include the Sagrada Família, which he began to direct in 1883 and which is still being completed, the Casa Batlló and Casa Milà with their rippling façades, the Palau Güell on a narrow street off the Rambla, Casa Vicens in Gràcia, and Park Güell.

  2. 2. Roca Roja

    Roca Roja, the red rock, is a fitting name for this corner of inland Catalonia, where sandstone and conglomerate cliffs glow rust-coloured in the evening light above pine forest and small terraced fields. The point lies in the hills between Querol and Mediona, where the comarques of Alt Camp, Alt Penedès and Anoia meet, a rugged, thinly populated upland that most people speed past on the motorway between Barcelona and Lleida without realising it exists.

  3. 3. Abric de la Serra de la mussara

    Climb out of Reus on a clear morning and the Serra de la Mussara rises ahead like a wall, a limestone escarpment that holds up the edge of the Prades plateau and drops steeply to the plain of the Camp de Tarragona. The rock shelters along this cliff line, close to the village of Vilaplana, are part of the prehistoric painted landscape of eastern Spain. The best reason to stop, though, is the view: on a good day you look straight down over hazelnut orchards and olive groves to Reus, Tarragona and the sea.
