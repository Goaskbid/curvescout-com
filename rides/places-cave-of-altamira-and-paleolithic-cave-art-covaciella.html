Why this ride

185 km from Santillana to Santillana, about 2 h 51 of riding. On the way it passes Chemin de la Liébana, Llonín, Covaciella and back through Llonín. It tops out around 1,100 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Picos de Europa Loop

The 29 km between Llonín and Covaciella on Carretera Cangas de Onís - Panes. Fairly level - 92 to 388 m end to end.

The stops

  1. 1. Cave of Altamira and Paleolithic Cave Art

    Start here.

    In 1879 a little girl lost interest in her father's digging and wandered further into a cave in Cantabria. She looked up and saw bulls on the ceiling. The father, Marcelino Sanz de Sautuola, a local landowner and amateur archaeologist, was digging in the floor of Altamira, a cave a hunter had stumbled on some years earlier. When he announced that the paintings were of the Ice Age, the experts of Europe laughed at him, and it was not until 1902, after similar paintings turned up in France, that the leading sceptic published a famous apology. Sautuola died long before that.

    Read the complete Cave of Altamira and Paleolithic Cave Art write-up.

  2. 2. Chemin de la Liébana

    37 km in.

    San Vicente de la Barquera is where the Camino Lebaniego begins, the old pilgrim route that leaves the Cantabrian coast and climbs south into the mountains of Liébana to the monastery of Santo Toribio. The town sits on a pair of estuaries, with a long medieval bridge carrying the road across the water, a castle and the Gothic church of Santa María above the harbour, and on clear days the snowy peaks of the Picos de Europa rising behind the marshes like a stage set.

    Read the complete Chemin de la Liébana write-up.

  3. 3. Llonín

    66 km in.

    The cave of Llonín is not one you can walk into. It sits in the valley of the river Cares in Peñamellera Alta, in the far east of Asturias, and it is one of the decorated Palaeolithic caves of northern Spain listed by UNESCO alongside Altamira. Its walls hold engravings and paintings of deer, goats and other animals, and the cave has been studied by archaeologists for decades, but access is restricted to protect the art. What you get instead is the landscape that sheltered those artists, and that is very much worth the trip.

    Read the complete Llonín write-up.

  4. 4. Covaciella

    95 km in.

    Between the coastal town of Llanes and the high limestone walls of the Picos de Europa, a small cave near Carreña de Cabrales holds images that have been in the dark for something like fifteen thousand years. Covaciella was only found in the 1990s, and its paintings of bison, horses and deer, in black and red, are among the finest examples of Palaeolithic art in Asturias. It was added to the UNESCO listing of the Cave of Altamira and Paleolithic Cave Art of Northern Spain in 2008. The cave is closed to the public to protect the paintings, but the nearby interpretation centre explains what is inside and why it matters.

    Read the complete Covaciella write-up.
