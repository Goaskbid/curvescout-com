Why this ride

Out of London and back again: 541 km, roughly 9 h 50 in the saddle. The stops in between: Weedon Beck and Stone.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. London

    London is the one place on the map where the best advice is to leave the bike at the edge. The centre around Charing Cross and Trafalgar Square is a maze of one-way streets, bus lanes, taxis and tourists, and parking is limited and expensive. The city also has a congestion charge and a low-emission zone, whose rules for motorcycles have changed over the years, so check the current conditions for your own machine before you ride in. For a start and finish, a quiet side street or a secure garage on the outskirts is a better plan.

    Read the complete London write-up.

  2. 2. Weedon Beck

    Roads, canals and railways all squeeze through the same gap at Weedon Bec, and that is the key to its story. The village lies in the gentle Northamptonshire countryside near Daventry, where the old Roman road of Watling Street, now the A5, the Grand Union Canal and the West Coast Main Line run side by side through a shallow valley. For centuries this has been a natural corridor between London and the northwest.

    Read the complete Weedon Beck write-up.

  3. 3. Stone

    Narrowboats, breweries and a Georgian high street make Stone one of the more likeable market towns in Staffordshire. It stands on the River Trent and the Trent and Mersey Canal, which brought prosperity to the town when it opened in the late eighteenth century, with boatyards, warehouses and locks still lining the waterway today. The high street is lined with old coaching inns, independent shops and cafés, and the town has a relaxed, welcoming feel.

    Read the complete Stone write-up.
