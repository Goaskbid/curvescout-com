  1. 1. Underberg

    Underberg is a small farming town in the foothills of the Southern Drakensberg, in the KwaZulu-Natal Midlands, and it is the last place of any size before the mountains. The little main street has a handful of shops, a pub, a few guesthouses and the scent of woodsmoke in the cool air, and above it all the Drakensberg escarpment rises in a wall of dark basalt. The district grows potatoes, apples and cattle, and the town has long served as a base for walkers and trout fishers.

  2. 2. Sani Pass top

    At the top of Sani Pass you are standing on the roof of the Drakensberg, close to 2,900 metres up, on the border between South Africa and the Kingdom of Lesotho. A small border post, a few huts, a stone chalet that claims to be the highest pub in Africa, and a view down the escarpment that makes your stomach lurch, are about all there is. On a clear day the plateau of Lesotho rolls away to the north, brown and treeless, while behind you the cliffs drop away into a haze of valleys.
