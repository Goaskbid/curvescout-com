Why this ride

A one-way ride of 211 km, Skopje to Debar - roughly 2 h 50 in the saddle. It goes by way of Mavrovo and Galicnik. The highest point is about 1,450 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Skopje

    Skopje lies in the valley of the Vardar river, ringed by mountains, and it is a city that was knocked down and rebuilt in living memory. In July 1963, a powerful earthquake destroyed most of it, killing over a thousand people, and the international response was large; Kenzo Tange, the Japanese architect, drew up a reconstruction plan. The result is a strange mix of Ottoman old town, brutalist concrete and, in recent years, an expensive programme of neoclassical buildings and statues in the centre. Nearly half a million people live in the city, so fuel and services are plentiful.

  2. 2. Mavrovo

    Mavrovo is the name of a lake, a village, and a very large national park, and the three overlap in a way that confuses everybody for the first ten minutes. The park is the biggest in North Macedonia, a block of high ranges along the Albanian border including Korab, the country's top peak, and the Radika river collects most of its water. The reservoir was built in the 1950s to feed a hydropower scheme, and it covered a church. The bell tower of St Nikola still stands in the lake when the water is low, a little stone drowned thing that has become the emblem of the place, and it is the picture you will see on every postcard in the region.

  3. 3. Galicnik

    Galičnik hangs on a mountain shoulder at around 1,400 metres, high above the Radika valley, and the road up to it is a winding, narrow affair that keeps you honest. The surface is tarmac but not always smooth, there are tight bends where two cars have to negotiate, and in places the drop is serious. Rostuša, in the valley below, is the nearest town to speak of, and once you commit to the climb there is no turning back for coffee. Most of the village is stone houses with wooden balconies and slate or stone roofs, cut into the steep slope and facing out over a big, wild view.

  4. 4. Debar

    Debar sits in the far west of North Macedonia, close to the Albanian border, in a bowl of hills at the northern end of an artificial lake. It is a town of eighteen thousand people with a mixed population, mostly Albanian and Macedonian, a long history of trade and a reputation for craft: woodcarving, silverwork and weaving. Its old bazaar, its mosques and churches and a cluster of small spas on the outskirts give it a layered feel, and the pace of life is easy. The lake was created in the 1960s by the Spilje dam on the Black Drin.
