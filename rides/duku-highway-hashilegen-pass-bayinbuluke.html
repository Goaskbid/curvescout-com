Why this ride

The highest point is about 3,400 m. Hashilegen Pass is open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Hashilegen Pass → Nalati

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hashilegen Pass

    Hashilegen Pass lies high in the central Tian Shan at roughly 3,400 metres, where the Duku Highway climbs to the divide and the world turns to rock, snow and sky. The scenery is wide and austere, with glaciers on the high peaks, meltwater streams across the alpine meadows and the occasional herder's felt tent.

  2. 2. Nalati

    Nalati, known in Kazakh as Narat, is a high grassland on the northern slopes of the Tian Shan in Xinjiang, in the far west of China, and it is one of those landscapes that looks like a painter's idea of mountain summer pasture. Rolling green meadows spread beneath snowy peaks, dotted with spruce forest and herders' yurts, and the air is cool and thin at around two thousand metres. The Kazakh herders who move their sheep, horses and cattle up here every summer are the people who made the grasslands what they are.

  3. 3. Bayinbuluke

    Bayinbuluke is a vast, high grassland basin in the Tian Shan of Xinjiang, around two and a half thousand metres up, and the second largest grassland in China. The Kaidu River winds across it in great loops, and in evening light it looks like a silver snake draped over an emerald carpet. The Torghut Mongols graze their sheep and horses here, and herds of yaks and cattle dot the slopes.

Hashilegen Pass to Nalati

Hashilegen Pass → Nalati drops about 3400 m over 50 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Nalati to Bayinbuluke

Nalati → Bayinbuluke is 58 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
