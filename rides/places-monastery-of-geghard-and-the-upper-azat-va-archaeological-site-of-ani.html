Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Monastery of Geghard and the Upper Azat Va

    Geghard is cut into a cliff. Some of its chapels are built out of the mountain, others hollowed from it, and the effect is of a monastery that grew out of the rock instead of being placed on it. It sits at the head of the Azat gorge, about forty kilometres east of Yerevan, in a narrow valley where the walls rise vertically on three sides and the stream chatters below. The name means the spear, from the relic of the lance that pierced Christ, brought here by the apostle Thaddeus according to tradition and kept for centuries before being moved to Echmiatsin. The monastery was founded in the early Middle Ages, but most of what you see dates to the thirteenth century, when the princely Proshyan family built the rock-cut chapels. Together with the upper Azat valley it is a UNESCO site.

  2. 2. Archaeological site of Zvartnots with ruin

    Zvartnots is a ruin with a great deal of ghost left in it. The name means roughly the temple of the celestial angels, and it was built in the seventh century by the Armenian Catholicos Nerses III, who wanted a church of astonishing size and shape: a round, three-storey building rising from a cross-shaped plan, with a high central dome and a ring of columns. For a few centuries it dominated this plain between the Hrazdan river and the old capital of Vagharshapat. Then it collapsed, probably in an earthquake some time before the tenth century, and the stones lay forgotten under a mound until archaeologists dug them out in the early twentieth century.

  3. 3. Archaeological Site of Ani

    The first thing to understand about Ani is that you stand in one country and look into another. The ruined city lies on a promontory above the Akhurian River, and that river is the border between Armenia and Turkey, shut since 1993. The ruins themselves are on the Turkish bank, while the nearest Armenian towns, Maralik and the villages around it, are twenty kilometres or more away across open plateau. A rider coming from the Armenian side can get close enough to see the walls and the cathedral dome across the gorge, and that is a strange, quiet, rather moving experience in its own right.
