Why this ride

112 km round trip from Haltwhistle, about 1 h 27 of riding. It runs out through Hadrian's Wall & vallum between field boun and Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 7 and comes back by another road.

The stops

  1. 1. Sunny Rigg 2 Roman temporary camp

    Start here.

    Haltwhistle likes to call itself the centre of Britain, and whether or not you believe the measurements, it sits at the very heart of Hadrian's Wall country. Just outside the town, on a gentle slope called Sunny Rigg, the outlines of two Roman temporary camps have been traced, rectangular enclosures with rounded corners where units on the march, or building the wall, pitched their leather tents behind a bank and a ditch for a night or a season. They are part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire World Heritage Site, though on the ground you will see only faint lines in the grass, best appreciated from the air or in low evening light.

    Read the complete Sunny Rigg 2 Roman temporary camp write-up.

  2. 2. Hadrian's Wall & vallum between field boun

    27 km in.

    Hadrian's Wall runs for about seventy-three miles across the north of England, from the Solway Firth in the west to the Tyne in the east, built on the orders of the Emperor Hadrian after his visit to Britain in 122 AD.

    Read the complete Hadrian's Wall & vallum between field boun write-up.

  3. 3. Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 7

    55 km in.

    It takes a little imagination to find the Roman frontier in Benwell, a suburb on the western hill of Newcastle, but it is there, tucked between semi-detached houses and bus stops. Wall mile 7 counts the distance from the Wall's eastern end at Wallsend, and in this part of the city the Romans built a fort called Condercum on the high ground above the Tyne, with fine views up and down the river. Most of it now lies under roads and housing, yet two small survivals make the detour worthwhile.
