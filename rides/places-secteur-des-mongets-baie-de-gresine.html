Why this ride

From Saint-Jorioz it runs 241 km to Saint-Jorioz; allow about 4 h 37 of riding. It goes by way of Baie de Châtillon, Littoral de Tresserve, Lac d’Aiguebelette and Baie de Grésine.

The roads

Littoral de Tresserve → Lac d’Aiguebelette

It is 28 km from Littoral de Tresserve to Lac d’Aiguebelette, mostly on A 43. Fairly level - 231 to 456 m end to end. Water alongside for much of it - pick one good pull-out and stop.

The stops

  1. 1. Secteur des Mongets

    Start here.

    Sevrier, on the western shore of Lake Annecy, is home to an unusual industry: bells. The Paccard foundry, which has been casting bells since the late eighteenth century and moved to Sevrier in the 1980s, has made bells for churches and carillons all over the world, and its museum explains how they are designed, cast and tuned. It is a curious contrast to the quiet lakeshore just below the village, where the Secteur des Mongets marks the remains of a prehistoric village built on the edge of the lake, now under water. It is one of the sites included in the World Heritage listing of the Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps, and divers have found wooden piles, pottery and tools in the lake bed.

    Read the complete Secteur des Mongets write-up.

  2. 2. Baie de Châtillon

    42 km in.

    At the northern tip of the Lac du Bourget, the largest natural lake entirely in France, the shore curves into a shallow bay below the village of Châtillon and its castle. In the shallows, divers and archaeologists have found the remains of prehistoric lake villages, and the Baie de Châtillon is one of the French sites included in the UNESCO listing of prehistoric pile dwellings around the Alps. The castle, perched on a rocky hill above the bay, was the seat of local lords in the Middle Ages and has a commanding view over the lake.

    Read the complete Baie de Châtillon write-up.

  3. 3. Littoral de Tresserve

    57 km in.

    The Lac du Bourget is the largest natural lake entirely in France, a long blue trough in the Savoie Prealps, and the shore at Tresserve, just south of Aix-les-Bains, is one of the quiet places to see it. Beneath the water here lie the remains of prehistoric lake dwellings, villages of timber houses built on piles along the shore thousands of years ago. The site is part of the UNESCO-listed Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps, a series of more than a hundred settlements in six countries. There is nothing to see above the surface, but knowing it is there changes the way you look at the reeds and shallows.

    Read the complete Littoral de Tresserve write-up.

  4. 4. Lac d’Aiguebelette

    85 km in.

    Motorboats are banned on the Lac d'Aiguebelette, and on a calm summer morning you notice the difference straight away: rowers sliding across water so still it mirrors the wooded slopes above, swimmers heading out from small beaches, and no engine noise beyond the occasional car on the shore road. The lake, in Savoie just west of Chambéry, is one of the largest natural lakes in France, and its clean water is among the warmest of the alpine lakes in summer. It hosted the World Rowing Championships in 2015.

    Read the complete Lac d’Aiguebelette write-up.

  5. 5. Baie de Grésine

    120 km in.

    Lac du Bourget is the largest natural lake in France, a long, deep lake in Savoie between the Jura and the Alps, and the poet Alphonse de Lamartine made it famous with his poem Le Lac, written after a love affair that began on its shores. The Baie de Grésine, near Brison-Saint-Innocent on the eastern shore, is one of the prehistoric pile-dwelling sites of the lake, part of the Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps listed by UNESCO in 2011. As with other such sites, the remains lie underwater, but the bay and its surroundings are a beautiful place to stop.

    Read the complete Baie de Grésine write-up.
