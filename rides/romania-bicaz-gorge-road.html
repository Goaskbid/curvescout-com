Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Piatra Neamt

    Piatra Neamț is a proper Moldavian town of around a hundred thousand people sitting in a bowl of forested hills, and it makes a very good place to start a ride into the Eastern Carpathians. The Bistrița river runs just to the south, the Ceahlău massif stands on the western horizon, and on a clear morning the whole thing looks like a model of itself. Fuel, supermarkets, cash machines and big breakfasts are all easy here, so do whatever errands you have before you go.

  2. 2. Bicaz

    Bicaz is a town of some eight thousand people at the eastern end of the Bicaz Gorge, in the Moldavian foothills of the Carpathians, and it is known for two big things, a dam and a cement works. The dam, completed in 1960 on the Bistrița river, holds back Izvorul Muntelui, the largest reservoir in Romania by volume, a long arm of water that fills the valley and reaches far into the hills. The cement works, a concrete reminder of the communist industrial plan, still dominates one side of town. It sounds grim, but the lake is beautiful and the hills around it are green.

  3. 3. Bicaz Gorge

    The Bicaz Gorge is a crack in the Carpathians, and the road goes straight through it. For several kilometres the limestone walls of the Hășmaș mountains close in on both sides, rising some three hundred metres in places, so narrow that the road and the stream beside it share the floor and the sky shrinks to a ribbon. Bends are tight, the walls overhang, and the light changes from gold to deep shadow as you go. It is one of the great short rides of Eastern Europe, and it ends too soon.

  4. 4. Red Lake

    Red Lake is a lake that should not exist, and the dead trees sticking out of the water tell you so. In the summer of 1837, a landslide off the flank of Mount Suhard dammed the Bicaz stream and the valley slowly filled behind it. The drowned spruces stayed standing and their pale trunks still rise from the water like a stand of masts. The name comes from the reddish clay that the stream washes down, though in practice the lake usually looks green, dark brown or blue-grey, depending on the weather and your angle.
