Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Jewish Quarter and St Procopius' Basilica

    The basilica of Saint Procopius in Třebíč looks stern from outside, a long and plain Romanesque and Gothic church with massive walls and a squat tower, and it was built in the early thirteenth century as part of a Benedictine monastery founded in 1101. The monks chose a site by the Jihlava river, in the rolling country of the Bohemian-Moravian Highlands, and over the centuries the monastery became rich and powerful before being closed in the eighteenth century. The church survives as a rare and fine example of a style that blended the round arches of Romanesque with the pointed arches of Gothic, with a crypt that is older than the rest.

  2. 2. Historic Centre of Telč

    Ride into the main square of Telč for the first time and you may laugh out loud, because it looks too perfect to be real. A long, tapering square is lined on both sides by Renaissance and Baroque houses, each with a different gable, painted in soft pastels, cream, pink, green and yellow, with arcades running along the ground floor. After a fire in the sixteenth century, the town was rebuilt under its lord, Zachariáš of Hradec, who had travelled in Italy and brought back a taste for Renaissance style. His château, at the end of the square, has richly decorated halls and a garden. The historic centre was listed by UNESCO in 1992.

  3. 3. Pilgrimage Church of St John of Nepomuk at

    On a low wooded rise called Zelená hora, Green Mountain, just outside Žďár nad Sázavou, stands one of the strangest and most beautiful churches in Central Europe. The architect was Jan Blažej Santini-Aichel, a Prague builder of Italian descent who loved to mix Gothic forms with Baroque drama, and here he built a pilgrimage church to St John of Nepomuk in the 1720s, shortly before the saint was even canonised. Everything in it plays on the number five: a star-shaped ground plan, five gates, five altars, five-pointed geometry repeated in the cloister around it. The reason is the legend that five stars appeared over the Vltava when John's body was thrown into the river in Prague, and the saint's tongue, said to have been found uncorrupted, is the other motif you will spot once you know to look for it. UNESCO listed it in 1994.

  4. 4. Litomyšl Castle

    Bedřich Smetana, the composer of Má vlast and The Bartered Bride, was born in 1824 in the brewery of Litomyšl Castle, where his father was the brewer, and the town has never forgotten it. Every summer, the castle courtyard hosts an opera festival named after him, Smetanova Litomyšl, and his birthplace in the brewery building is a small museum. The castle itself, an elegant Renaissance building with arcaded courtyards, was built in the sixteenth century for the Pernštejn family and is famous for its sgraffito decoration: the facades are covered with thousands of rectangular panels, each with a slightly different pattern. It was listed by UNESCO in 1999.
