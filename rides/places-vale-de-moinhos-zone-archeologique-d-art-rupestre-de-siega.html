Why this ride

From Vila Nova de Foz Côa it runs 384 km to Vila Nova de Foz Côa; allow about 6 h 37 of riding. The stops in between: Prehistoric Rock Art Sites in the Côa Vall, Faia - Vale Afonsinho, Zone archéologique d’art rupestre de Siega and back through Faia - Vale Afonsinho. It crosses from Portugal into Spain - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Vale de Moinhos

    Vale de Moinhos means valley of the mills, and the name is a good clue to what this ground was once for. Small streams run down from the plateau toward the Côa, and wherever the water ran fast enough, farmers built stone mills to grind their grain, many of them now roofless and swallowed by rockrose and broom. It is a quiet fold of schist hills a few kilometres south of Vila Nova de Foz Côa, in the north-east of Portugal, with the big Douro country just beyond.

  2. 2. Prehistoric Rock Art Sites in the Côa Vall

    On the Spanish side of the border with Portugal, the river Águeda cuts through granite and schist on its way to the Douro, and on the rock faces along its banks, at Siega Verde, Palaeolithic people engraved horses, aurochs, deer and goats more than ten thousand years ago. In 2010 Siega Verde was added to the Prehistoric Rock Art Sites in the Côa Valley, the Portuguese World Heritage Site just across the border, recognising that these open-air engravings form one of the largest collections of Ice Age art in the world.

  3. 3. Faia - Vale Afonsinho

    Thirty thousand years ago, give or take, people in this valley scratched horses, aurochs and ibex into the schist rocks along the River Côa, and their drawings would almost certainly have been drowned if a dam had been built here in the 1990s. Instead, after a fierce campaign, the dam was abandoned and the Côa Valley rock art became one of the most important open-air Palaeolithic art sites in the world, listed by UNESCO in 1998 together with Siega Verde across the border in Spain. Faia, in the side valley near Vale Afonsinho, is one of its core sites, with engravings and some rare painted figures on the rock faces above the river.

  4. 4. Zone archéologique d’art rupestre de Siega

    Eighteen thousand years is a conservative guess for some of the engravings on the rocks at Siega Verde, and the animals they show are still recognisable: horses, aurochs, deer, ibex and the occasional bison, scratched with flint into the flat grey-brown schist slabs that line the river Agueda. There are hundreds of them, and the open-air gallery runs for about a kilometre along the water. They were made by hunter-gatherers of the Upper Palaeolithic, and they are all outdoors, unprotected by caves, which makes the survival of so much of it remarkable. The site was found only in 1988 by a pair of archaeologists, and it was added to the World Heritage list in 2010 as an extension of the Coa Valley in Portugal, which has a related collection less than a hundred kilometres away.
