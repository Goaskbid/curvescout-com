Why this ride

A 107 km round trip from Umm el Faḥm - roughly 1 h 45 in the saddle. On the way it passes Necropolis of Bet She’arim: A Landmark of, Sites of Human Evolution at Mount Carmel: and back through Necropolis of Bet She’arim: A Landmark of. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Tel Megiddo

    Start here.

    From the top of Tel Megiddo, the whole of the Jezreel Valley spreads out below you, a green and gold checkerboard of fields stretching towards Mount Tabor and the hills of Galilee. You can see at once why so many armies fought here. The mound guarded the narrow pass where the ancient coastal road, the Via Maris, crossed from the Mediterranean plain into the valley, and anyone who held it controlled one of the great trade and military routes of the ancient world.

    Read the complete Tel Megiddo write-up.

  2. 2. Necropolis of Bet She’arim: A Landmark of

    18 km in.

    In the second century AD, after the failed revolts against Rome, the centre of Jewish life moved to Galilee, and the Sanhedrin, the supreme rabbinical council, settled for a time at Bet She'arim, on a hill at the western edge of the Jezreel valley. Rabbi Judah ha-Nasi, the patriarch who compiled the Mishnah, the first great codification of Jewish law, lived here, and when he was buried in the necropolis, Jews from all over the diaspora wanted to be buried near him. The result is a vast network of catacombs carved into the soft limestone, listed by UNESCO in 2015.

    Read the complete Necropolis of Bet She’arim: A Landmark of write-up.

  3. 3. Sites of Human Evolution at Mount Carmel:

    53 km in.

    In the caves of Nahal Me'arot, on the western slope of Mount Carmel near the coast south of Haifa, people lived on and off for half a million years, and both Neanderthals and early modern humans left their bones here. The four caves, Tabun, Jamal, el-Wad and Skhul, contain one of the longest records of human evolution anywhere, and they were listed as a World Heritage Site in 2012. Tabun alone has layers spanning hundreds of thousands of years. The Natufian culture, among the first people to settle in villages before farming, also left traces in el-Wad.

    Read the complete Sites of Human Evolution at Mount Carmel: write-up.
