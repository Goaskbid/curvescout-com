Why this ride

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hatazuka Kofun

    Bicycles are the unofficial emblem of Sakai, and that makes Hatazuka a nicely ironic place to begin a motorcycle ride. The city's most famous modern company, Shimano, which makes gears and brakes for cyclists around the world, was founded here in the 1920s, and on the quiet streets around the old tombs you will see a great many commuters, children and elderly shoppers on two wheels. Hatazuka itself is one of the smaller mounded tombs in the cluster of fifth-century monuments that the city is proud of, its earthen slopes now wooded and its moat quiet, an easy place to pause and look at before the day's engine noise begins.

  2. 2. Nakayamazuka Kofun

    From the road you would never know it was a tomb. Nakayamazuka is a keyhole-shaped burial mound in the Furuichi cluster of the great Mozu-Furuichi tomb group in southern Osaka Prefecture, a landscape of ancient mounds built between about the fourth and sixth centuries AD, when the Yamato rulers were consolidating power in Japan. UNESCO listed forty-nine of the tombs in 2019. Each is a hill of earth, once faced with stones and lined with clay figures called haniwa, surrounded by a moat, and many have a shape that can only be recognised from above: a round mound joined to a trapezoid, like the head and body of a keyhole.

  3. 3. Horyu-ji Area

    The buildings at Hōryū-ji are around thirteen hundred years old, and some of them are among the oldest wooden structures anywhere in the world. Founded in the early seventh century by Prince Shōtoku, a regent remembered as a champion of Buddhism and a founding figure of the Japanese state, the temple was rebuilt after a fire later in the same century, and parts of that rebuilding still stand. In 1993 it became one of the first sites in Japan to be listed by UNESCO, as the Buddhist Monuments in the Hōryū-ji Area.
