Why this ride

curated scenic road. high Andes, canyon, volcanoes. 129 km from Arequipa to Cabanaconde, about 2 h 21 of riding. The stops in between: Huarancante, Chivay and Tapay. The highest point is about 4,900 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Arequipa

    Arequipa announces itself from a distance with a volcano. El Misti, a near-perfect cone of about 5,800 metres, stands behind the city, flanked by the broader mass of Chachani and the older, eroded Pichu Pichu, and on a clear morning the three seem to hang above the rooftops. Arequipa is Peru's second city, home to well over eight hundred thousand people, sitting in a dry valley at roughly 2,300 metres, so you arrive here after a long climb from the coast with your lungs and your engine both a little short of breath.

  3. 3. Chivay

    You will feel Chivay before you see it, in the lungs. The town stands at about 3,600 metres in the Colca Valley, and anyone who rode up from Arequipa at near sea level on the same day tends to arrive a little dizzy, thick-headed and surprisingly hungry. The road does it to you in stages: out of the desert suburbs, up past the cones of Misti and Chachani, across a bleak plateau of alpaca pasture and vicuña at something close to 4,900 metres, and then down again into a green valley that looks like another country entirely.

  5. 5. Cabanaconde

    Cabanaconde sits right on the lip of the Colca Canyon, at a height of about 3,300 metres, and at the end of the road you simply look down into one of the deepest valleys on the planet. The canyon plunges more than three thousand metres in places, though people argue about the measurement, and the Colca River is a thin silver thread at the bottom. Terraces carved by pre-Inca peoples climb the walls in neat steps, and are still farmed with maize and potatoes, as they have been for centuries.
