Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Tel Aviv

    Tel Aviv is a city that is barely more than a century old. It was founded in 1909 on the sand dunes north of the old port of Jaffa by a group of Jewish families, and it grew with astonishing speed. In the 1930s, architects who had trained in Germany and in the Bauhaus tradition built thousands of modernist apartment blocks, with white walls, curved balconies and small windows to keep out the sun, and the White City is now listed by UNESCO as a remarkable example of that style. Rothschild Boulevard, lined with trees and cafes, is the best place to see them.

  2. 2. Sebastia

    Sebastia, Sabastiya in Arabic, is a Palestinian village on a hill north-west of Nablus, in the northern West Bank, surrounded by olive groves and terraced hillsides. It stands on the site of Samaria, the capital of the northern Kingdom of Israel in the ninth and eighth centuries BC, later rebuilt by Herod the Great, who named it Sebaste in honour of the Emperor Augustus. The ruins on the hilltop above the village span a thousand years of history.

  4. 4. Area 1

    The hills south of Jerusalem and Bethlehem are older than most of the countries now claiming them, and the terraced slopes around Battir are among the best evidence. For centuries, perhaps for two thousand years, farmers here have built dry-stone terraces down the steep valley sides to grow olives, grapes, figs and vegetables, and the water that feeds them comes from springs and is shared out through channels according to a rota among local families. The system was recognised as a World Heritage landscape in 2014, under the title of the land of olives and vines in southern Jerusalem.

  5. 5. Palestine: Land of Olives and Vines - Cult

    Battir is the quiet village at the heart of this cultural landscape, in the hills southwest of Jerusalem and Bethlehem, where terraces of dry stone climb the slopes in layer after layer. They are planted with olives, vines, and the vegetables that Battir is famous for, especially a small, sweet kind of aubergine. UNESCO inscribed the area in 2014 as the Land of Olives and Vines, a cultural landscape of southern Jerusalem, because the terraces, the irrigation channels and the old watch-towers show an agricultural system that has worked for many centuries.
