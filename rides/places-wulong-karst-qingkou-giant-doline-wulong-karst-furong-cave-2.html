Why this ride

It goes by way of Wulong Karst - Three Natural Bridges, Wulong Karst - Furong Cave and back through Wulong Karst - Three Natural Bridges. The highest point is about 1,200 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Wulong Karst - Qingkou Giant Doline

    The ground drops away without warning. At Qingkou, in the hills of Wulong in the south of Chongqing, the forest opens onto a hole in the earth so large that you can hardly believe it: a giant doline, or sinkhole, hundreds of metres across and deep enough to swallow a skyscraper. Karst country is full of such things, because limestone dissolves in rainwater, and over millions of years, caves and tunnels have collapsed to create these enormous pits.

  2. 2. Wulong Karst - Three Natural Bridges

    In Wulong, in the mountains of Chongqing, the rock is the architecture. The karst here, a limestone landscape of caves, gorges and sinkholes, has been carved by water over millions of years into huge forms, among them three natural bridges, giant stone arches that span a river gorge. Tianlong, Qinglong and Heilong bridges, each one rising well over a hundred metres, with spans of tens of metres, are among the biggest natural bridges in the world, and the river runs beneath them, in forest so deep that the light is green.

  3. 3. Wulong Karst - Furong Cave

    Wulong lies in the folded mountains of southeastern Chongqing, where the Wu river has carved its way through limestone for millions of years, and the result is among the most dramatic karst landscapes in China. It forms part of the South China Karst, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the area includes Furong Cave, the Three Natural Bridges and the Qingkou Giant Doline, a sinkhole so large that it holds a forest inside it. The scale of everything is hard to take in until you stand at the edge.
