Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Springfield Illinois

    Abraham Lincoln lived in Springfield for seventeen years before he went to Washington, and the town still revolves around him. His home, a modest two-storey frame house, is kept as a national historic site on its original street, and the Old State Capitol, where he gave his 'House Divided' speech, stands on the main square. His tomb lies in Oak Ridge Cemetery on the north side of town, a granite obelisk and a bronze head whose nose is rubbed shiny by visitors for luck. The city has a quiet, civic air that suits its role as the state capital.

  2. 2. St Louis

    St. Louis is a river city with a very large arch in it. The Gateway Arch, designed by Eero Saarinen and finished in 1965, rises 630 feet on the west bank of the Mississippi, a catenary curve of stainless steel that is the tallest monument in the United States and one of the most recognisable. It marks the city's historic role as the gateway to the West, the starting point for settlers, trappers and explorers, including Lewis and Clark, who set out up the Missouri in 1804 from a camp not far north. At its feet, a museum tells the story of westward expansion, and a tram carries visitors to the top.

  3. 3. Cuba Missouri

    Cuba, Missouri, is a small town of about three and a half thousand people in the Ozark foothills, and it has turned itself into the Mural City of Route 66. More than a dozen big paintings cover the walls of old buildings, showing the history of the town, the railroad, the highway and the people, and the effect is cheerful and slightly surreal, like a gallery laid out along a main street. The town is said to take its name from the island of Cuba.
