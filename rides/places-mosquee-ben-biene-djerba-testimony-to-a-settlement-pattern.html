Why this ride

A 77 km round trip from Erriadh - roughly 1 h 20 in the saddle. On the way it passes Mosquée Sidi Yeti, Djerba: Testimony to a settlement pattern and back through Mosquée Sidi Yeti. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Mosquée Ben Biene

    In the south-west of Djerba, between Erriadh and the pottery village of Guellala, the island's farmland is at its most traditional, and the mosque of Ben Biene sits among it. This is the part of the island where the compounds stand furthest apart, each one an island of white wall and green trees in the middle of a field, and where the lanes are quiet enough for a rider to hear the pigeons and the wind in the palms.

  2. 2. Mosquée Sidi Yeti

    Djerba is the sort of island where you can ride for an hour and never see anything higher than a date palm. The mosque of Sidi Yati sits on the flat interior, a short run from Erriadh, which is the nearest proper town, with Midoun and the capital Houmt Souk a little further out. Everything here is low, white and thick-walled, and the mosques follow the same rule: buttressed, squat, almost fortress-like, with small windows and none of the soaring minarets you might expect from the mainland.

  3. 3. Djerba: Testimony to a settlement pattern

    In 2023 UNESCO added Djerba to its World Heritage list under a title that sounds more like a university thesis than a holiday island: Djerba, Testimony to a Settlement Pattern in an Island Territory. What it means in practice is that the island has kept the way people have lived on it for centuries. Instead of one big town, families lived in scattered farms called menzels, each surrounded by its own fields, and villages grew around mosques, markets and cisterns. Rain is scarce here, so water was collected from the roofs and stored in underground tanks, and the layout of the land reflects that.
