Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. George SA

    George is the capital of the Garden Route, a town of oak-lined streets and sleepy suburbs on a coastal shelf under the Outeniqua Mountains, and it feels more like a market town in the English countryside than something in Africa. It was founded in 1811 and named after King George III, which also explains the unusual number of old English oaks planted along its avenues. The sea is only ten minutes away, but the mountains are what define the place: a long, green, cloud-catching wall rising behind the town.

  2. 2. Avontuur

    Prince Alfred's Pass runs north from the forests of Knysna over the Outeniqua range to the dry valley called the Langkloof, and Avontuur sits at its far end, a small farming outpost where the green of the coast gives way to orchards and rock. The name means adventure in Afrikaans, and the pass, opened in 1867 and designed by the road engineer Thomas Bain, is among the finest of his works, climbing in great bends through indigenous forest, across a high stretch of mountain fynbos, and down the northern slope.

  3. 3. Knysna

    Knysna lies around a tidal lagoon on the Garden Route, with a pair of sandstone cliffs known as the Heads guarding the narrow channel to the sea. Forest runs down to the water, and the whole town has a green, relaxed look, with a population around sixty-nine thousand and a waterfront full of small shops and restaurants. The lagoon is an estuary, home to rare seahorses and a rich oyster industry, and every year there is an oyster festival in the winter that fills the town.
