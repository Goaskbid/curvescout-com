Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Canazei

    Canazei is a small Ladin town at the head of the Val di Fassa, and the first impression is of towering rock. The Marmolada, the highest peak in the Dolomites at 3,343 metres, rises to the south-east with its glacier, while the cliffs of the Sella group fill the northern horizon, huge walls of pale dolomite that glow pink and orange at sunrise and sunset. The Dolomites were declared a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2009, and no one who stands here is likely to wonder why.

  2. 2. Near Canazei

    Passo Pordoi, at 2,239 metres, is the highest point of the Great Dolomites Road, which runs from Bolzano to Cortina d'Ampezzo through the heart of the Dolomites. The pass sits between the Sella group, a massive fortress of rock with sheer walls, and the Marmolada, the highest mountain in the Dolomites, with its glacier. From the top, a cable car climbs to the Sass Pordoi, nearly three thousand metres high, with a view over the whole range.

  6. 6. Near Corvara in Badia

    Passo Campolongo, at 1,875 metres, is the gentlest of the four Sella passes, a short, easy climb through meadows and larch forests between Corvara in Alta Badia and Arabba in Livinallongo. It is the link that completes the Sella Ronda, and its broad, grassy saddle is a place to pause and look at the peaks around: the Sassongher, a pyramid of rock above Corvara, and the massive walls of the Sella group to the west.
