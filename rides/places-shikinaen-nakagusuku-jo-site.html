Why this ride

A 73 km round trip from Tomigusuku - roughly 1 h 16 in the saddle. The stops in between: Sêfa-utaki, Nakagusuku-jô site and back through Sêfa-utaki. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Shikinaen

    Shikinaen is a villa garden on the southern edge of Naha, built at the end of the eighteenth century as a second residence for the Ryukyu royal family. It was also used to host envoys sent from China, who came to invest each new king, and who were treated to feasts, music and walks through the grounds, in a ritual of diplomacy that kept the small kingdom on good terms with its powerful neighbour. The design blends Chinese and Japanese ideas with local ones, with a pond at the centre, a stone bridge, a pavilion and walkways laid out among trees.

  2. 2. Sêfa-utaki

    For the people of the old Ryukyu Kingdom, there was no holier place than Sēfa-utaki, a grove of forest and rock on the southeastern tip of Okinawa island. An utaki is a sacred site in the Ryukyuan religion, often a grove, a rock or a spring, where gods are believed to descend, and this one was the most important of all. The kingdom's highest priestess, the Kikoe-ōkimi, usually a woman of the royal family, was inaugurated here, and for centuries men were not allowed to enter.

  3. 3. Nakagusuku-jô site

    On the ridge above Nakagusuku Bay the walls of an old castle follow the curve of the hill in a long ribbon of pale limestone, and the shapes are surprisingly sophisticated. Nakagusuku was built, probably in the fourteenth century and extended in the fifteenth, by the lord Gosamaru, who was respected as a master builder, and its walls use a technique of fitted, curved stones with no mortar, in a pattern the Okinawans call nunozumi, cloth-laying, and a more rugged style called aikatazumi, which looks like a jigsaw. Six enclosures step up along the ridge, with gateways, a well and the foundations of halls.
