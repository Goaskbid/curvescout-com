Why this ride

A 62 km round trip from Santander - roughly 1 h 04 in the saddle. It goes by way of Monte Castillo - El Castillo, Monte Castillo - Las Chimeneas, Monte Castillo - La Pasiega and Monte Castillo - Las Monedas.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Santander

    Santander is a seaside capital that happens to have a mountain range at its back. The city wraps around a broad, sheltered bay, with long beaches at El Sardinero on the Atlantic side and a working harbour facing the old town, and on a clear day the Picos de Europa show as a faint white line far to the west. A great fire in 1941 destroyed much of the historic centre, so the architecture is mostly post-war, but the waterfront has been given a bright new face, with the glass and steel Centro Botín by Renzo Piano projecting out over the water. For riders arriving from England, the ferries from Plymouth and Portsmouth land here, which gives it a slightly salty, expectant air.

  2. 2. Monte Castillo - El Castillo

    Some of the oldest paintings in Europe sit in a hill above a village best known for its hot springs. The cave of El Castillo opens in the flank of Monte Castillo, right above Puente Viesgo in the Pas valley of Cantabria, and inside it are hand stencils and a red disc that scientists dated, in 2012, to more than forty thousand years ago. That makes them contenders for the oldest cave art known on the continent, and possibly older than any made by modern humans, since Neanderthals were around at the time. UNESCO lists the Cantabrian caves, and a visit means a guided group entering in small numbers, so book ahead and expect limits on the day.

  3. 3. Monte Castillo - Las Chimeneas

    Everyone talks about Altamira, but Monte Castillo has a better claim to the title of Europe's oldest gallery. El Castillo, the cave that gives the hill its name, contains a red disc that was dated in 2012 to more than forty thousand years ago, which makes it among the oldest known cave paintings anywhere. Las Chimeneas, the cave marked here, is one of its neighbours inside the same hill, and the whole mountain is a kind of honeycomb of Ice Age art.

  4. 4. Monte Castillo - La Pasiega

    Puente Viesgo has the strange distinction of being a Cantabrian village of about two and a half thousand people that sits under one of the most important prehistoric hills in Europe. Monte Castillo is a steep, cone-shaped limestone mountain beside the River Pas, and it is riddled with caves. La Pasiega is one of four that have been explored and painted on a grand scale, the others being El Castillo, Las Chimeneas and Las Monedas. Together they form a small campus of Ice Age art, and people travel from across the world to look at them.

  5. 5. Monte Castillo - Las Monedas

    Las Monedas is the cave that took its name from a handful of small coins, and the story says a lot about the place. Monte Castillo, at Puente Viesgo in Cantabria, has been known as a prehistoric hill since the early twentieth century, and visitors have been coming here for centuries, leaving behind bits of themselves. The coins were found in the cave, and the name stuck.
