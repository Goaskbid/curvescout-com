Why this ride

From Vlorë it runs 185 km to Sarandë; allow about 2 h 29 of riding. The stops in between: Llogara Pass, Dhermi, Himare and Porto Palermo. The highest point is about 1,050 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Vlore → Llogara Pass

The ground tilts up and stays tilted for 70 km. 1027 m of it, taken steadily rather than in one wall. A steady rhythm suits this better than bursts. The height arrives whether you hurry or not.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Vlore

    Vlorë is where the Adriatic and the Ionian Sea are said to meet, and that is the first thing a local will tell you, with some pride, even if the geographers are a little vague about exactly where the line falls. The town sits at the head of a wide bay, closed off to the west by the long mountainous arm of the Karaburun peninsula and the island of Sazan, and behind it the Llogara range stands up to well over two thousand metres. It is a big, busy, slightly chaotic place of nearly ninety thousand people, with a long seafront promenade of palms and cafes.

  2. 2. Llogara Pass

    At 1,027 metres, the Llogara Pass is the high point of the Albanian Riviera road, the SH8, and it is a place where the mountain suddenly lets go of the coast. From Vlorë the road climbs through the Ceraunian range, which the ancients called the thunder mountains, through forest of black pine and fir, and then at the saddle the whole Ionian Sea opens out below, with Corfu a smudge on the horizon on a clear day. The first time is hard to forget.

  3. 3. Dhermi

    Below the pass, the road drops to the sea at Dhërmi, a village that spreads down a green slope from a ridge of old stone houses to a long, pale beach. The village was historically mostly Greek-speaking and Orthodox, part of the Himara region where Greek communities have lived for centuries, and its churches, notably the one called Panagia and a monastery up the hill, have fine old frescoes. The old quarter, with its steep lanes and walled gardens, is worth a walk before the day gets hot.

  4. 4. Himare

    Himarë is the heart of the Albanian Riviera, a small town on the Ionian coast where the mountains stand so close to the sea that the road has to squeeze between them. The coast road, the SH8, runs for about a hundred and fifty kilometres from Vlorë to Sarandë, and it is one of the best rides in the Balkans, though it is still a road that demands attention. The town itself has a population of a few thousand, an old hilltop quarter with a Byzantine castle, and a long modern waterfront with cafes, hotels and a gravel beach.

  5. 5. Porto Palermo

    Porto Palermo is a bay on the Albanian Riviera that looks like a film set, with a small castle on a rocky peninsula that pokes into clear blue water. The castle, a compact triangular fortress, was built in the early nineteenth century by Ali Pasha of Tepelena, the Ottoman governor who ruled much of southern Albania and northern Greece with his own mix of ambition and cruelty. The walls are thick, and the stone is the colour of honey, and from the causeway the fortress seems to float in the sea. Inside, the courtyard is bare but atmospheric.

  6. 6. Sarande

    Sarandë sits at the bottom of Albania's long coast, in a curving bay facing the Greek island of Corfu, which on a clear evening looks close enough to swim to. It is a town of about fifteen thousand, built in tiers up a steep hillside, with a promenade running the length of the waterfront and a view that makes the end of the SH8 feel like a reward. The road you have just finished, the coastal highway from Vlorë over the Llogara Pass, is among the most dramatic in the Balkans.
