Why this ride

curated scenic road. white villages, mountains, gorges. From Arcos de la Frontera it runs 81 km to Ronda; allow about 1 h 28 of riding. On the way it passes Grazalema, Zahara and Setenil de las Bodegas. The highest point is about 850 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Arcos de la Frontera

    Arcos sits on top of a limestone ridge with a sheer drop on one side and the river Guadalete curling around the bottom of it, which is exactly how you want your first white village to look. It is the gateway to the Pueblos Blancos of Cádiz province, and you can see the whole town from a long way off as a tumble of whitewashed houses heaped along the crest, with the church tower and the old castle holding the high end. Jerez and its sherry cellars are about half an hour to the south-west, which tells you what the surrounding country smells like in late summer: dry earth, sunflowers gone to seed, and a little wine on the wind.

  2. 2. Grazalema

    In Spain, the wettest place is in the south. That surprises people who think of Andalusia as a land of endless sun, but the Sierra de Grazalema catches the Atlantic weather as it comes in from the Gulf of Cádiz and forces it upwards, and the result is the highest rainfall in the country, with well over two metres in an ordinary year. The village of Grazalema sits in the middle of this, at the foot of a limestone crag, a cluster of white houses with red-tiled roofs, balconies with geraniums, and a small square with a church. The surrounding natural park protects one of the last forests of Spanish fir, the pinsapo, an ancient tree that survived the ice ages in these damp mountains and nowhere else.

  3. 3. Zahara

    Zahara de la Sierra is the village you see first from the road, long before you reach it. A sharp rock rises above a turquoise reservoir, with a ruined Moorish castle on top and the white houses of the village tumbling down the slope beneath. In the right light, with the water glittering below and the grey Sierra de Grazalema behind, it is among the most striking sights in all of Andalusia.

  4. 4. Setenil de las Bodegas

    Most villages are built beside a river. Setenil is built inside it. The Guadalporcún river has cut a narrow gorge through the soft rock, and over the centuries the people of the village found it easier to roof their houses with the overhanging cliff than to build their own. The result is a lane where white-painted houses stand under a huge natural ceiling of grey rock, with terraces and bars tucked into the gaps and a line of sky visible only as a thin ribbon overhead. The most famous streets are called Cuevas del Sol and Cuevas de la Sombra, the Caves of the Sun and the Caves of the Shade, and in August the shaded side is the one to sit on.

  5. 5. Ronda

    Ronda is built on both sides of a gorge, and nobody quite believes it until they stand on the bridge. The Tajo, a cleft some hundred metres deep, cuts the town in two, and the Guadalevín river runs along its bottom. The Puente Nuevo, a tall stone bridge finished in the late eighteenth century after earlier collapses, joins the old Moorish quarter to the newer town on the other side, and the views from the viewpoints around it, down the cliff and out over the plain, are among the best in Andalusia.
