Why this ride

From Kalampáka it runs 159 km to Ioánnina; allow about 2 h 10 of riding. On the way it passes Meteora, Metsovo and Katara Pass. The highest point is about 1,700 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Greece Meteora and Pindus Route

Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it. The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 1705 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kalambaka

    Kalambaka sits at the foot of a cliff that does not look real. Behind the town the Thessalian plain simply ends, and a wall of grey-brown rock towers up in columns and domes, with the monasteries of Meteora perched on the summits. The nearest sizeable settlement is Kalambaka itself, with around eight thousand people, and the village of Kastraki hugs the rocks just 1.6 km away. For a rider, this is the natural gate to the Pindus mountains as well: the road to Metsovo and the Katara Pass climbs out of the plain from here.

  2. 2. Meteora

    Meteora is the kind of place you assume is a trick of photography until you round the bend. Enormous grey pillars of rock rise out of the Thessaly plain, some more than 300 metres high, and on top of several of them sit monasteries, walls and windows flush with the cliff edge. The name means suspended in the air, and the sandstone and conglomerate towers were shaped over millions of years by water, wind and earthquakes, long before anyone thought to build on them.

  3. 3. Metsovo

    Metsovo is a stone mountain town in the Pindus, about a thousand metres up, and it has a character of its own, thanks largely to its people. Many of the inhabitants are Vlachs, also called Aromanians, a community with its own Latin-derived language and a history as shepherds, traders and craftsmen who moved with their flocks across the Balkans. The town has long been prosperous, thanks to the patronage of wealthy benefactors, and the stone mansions with their wooden balconies and slate roofs speak of confidence. On weekends, the older residents still wear traditional dress, and you may hear Vlach spoken in the street.

  4. 4. Katara Pass

    The Katara Pass, at 1,705 metres, is the highest paved road pass in Greece, and it carries the old main road between Ioannina and Trikala over the spine of the Pindus. The name means curse, which is generally supposed to refer to the many travellers who crossed it in snow and wind and regretted the day they set out. For centuries it was the only route between Epirus and Thessaly, and trade caravans and armies crossed it along stone paths, long before the road was paved. Since the new motorway tunnels were opened, most of the traffic bypasses it, leaving the pass quiet.

  5. 5. Ioannina

    Ioannina wraps itself around a lake, and the lake is the first thing to understand about it. Lake Pamvotida lies flat and grey-green beside the city, with a small wooded island in the middle that holds a cluster of monasteries and a village, reached by a short boat from the waterfront. Behind the city rise the limestone ranges of Epirus, the Pindus mountains, which isolated this part of north-west Greece for centuries and gave it a character quite unlike the islands and the south.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Meteora to Metsovo

Meteora → Metsovo is 48 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Metsovo to Katara Pass

Metsovo → Katara Pass climbs about 1705 m in 9 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Katara Pass to Ioannina

Katara Pass → Ioannina drops about 1705 m over 32 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
