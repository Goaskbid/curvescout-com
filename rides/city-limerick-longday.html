Why this ride

A 356 km round trip from Luimneach - roughly 5 h 30 in the saddle. It goes by way of Sceilg Mhichíl and Limerick. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

The stops

  1. 1. Limerick

    Start here.

    Limerick sits on the Shannon, Ireland's longest river, at the point where it widens into a broad tidal estuary, and for a thousand years that position made it a prize. Vikings founded a settlement on King's Island in the tenth century, and the Normans built King John's Castle there in the early thirteenth, a massive walled fortress with round towers that still looms over the river. In 1691 the city withstood a long siege by William of Orange's army, and the Treaty of Limerick that ended the war is commemorated by the Treaty Stone, a lump of limestone beside Thomond Bridge.

  2. 2. Sceilg Mhichíl

    178 km in. The ride passes about 14 km from it.

    Sceilg Mhichíl, in English Skellig Michael, is a pinnacle of rock rising out of the Atlantic about twelve kilometres off the Kerry coast, and it is one of those places that makes you wonder what kind of person would choose to live there. Monks did, starting somewhere around the sixth to eighth century, building a monastery of beehive-shaped stone huts, terraces and oratories on a ledge some two hundred metres above the waves, reached by roughly six hundred stone steps. They grew what they could, caught fish and birds, collected rainwater, and held services in some of the wildest conditions in Europe, until the community moved away in the thirteenth century. UNESCO listed the island in 1996.
