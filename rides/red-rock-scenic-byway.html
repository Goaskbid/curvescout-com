Why this ride

A one-way ride of 24 km, Big Park to Sedona - roughly 25 min in the saddle. It goes by way of Bell Rock and Cathedral Rock junction. The highest point is about 1,950 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Village of Oak Creek

    The Village of Oak Creek is the southern gateway to Sedona, and it announces the red rock country with a flourish. Bell Rock and Courthouse Butte stand right beside the road, glowing orange and rust against the blue sky, their layered sandstone forming tilted sheets of colour. The village is a collection of resorts, golf courses, galleries and shops at around thirty-nine hundred feet, with the dry, bright air of the high desert and the green line of Oak Creek running through it.

  2. 2. Bell Rock

    Bell Rock is the first great butte you meet when you come north from the interstate into Sedona, and it looks every inch the part: a symmetrical, flaring cone of red sandstone, banded in cream and rust, rising from a flat of juniper and prickly pear beside State Route 179. Its shape is a gift to photographers, and on a still evening, when the sun sets behind it, the rock seems to glow from inside. Courthouse Butte stands next to it like a patient companion.

  3. 3. Cathedral Rock junction

    There is a side road off State Route 179 that leads, in a mile or so, to the most photographed rock in Sedona. Cathedral Rock rises behind the junction as a ragged crown of red towers and buttresses, lit gold at sunset, and the sight of it reflected in the shallows of Oak Creek below is the image that sells half the postcards in town. The junction is the turn for Back O' Beyond Road, which leads to the trailhead and the creek crossing, and the traffic here, on a busy day, is as dense as the foliage.

  4. 4. Sedona

    Sedona's red rocks look so unreal that visitors often assume they have been lit for effect. Cathedral Rock, Bell Rock, Courthouse Butte and a ring of cliffs the colour of rust and terracotta rise from the juniper and cypress woodland around the town, and in the early morning and at sunset, the light catches the sandstone and turns it fiery. The colour comes from iron oxide in the layers of rock, deposited over hundreds of millions of years, and then carved by wind and water into spires and buttes. About ten thousand people live in the town itself, at around 4,350 feet, in a place that has grown from a few orchards and a small post office in the early twentieth century, when it was named after Sedona Schnebly, the wife of an early settler.
