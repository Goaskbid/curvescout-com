Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ampitheatre

    On a hillside on the eastern edge of Trier, a great oval bowl cut into the slope was once the place where the Roman city went to watch gladiators and wild animals. The amphitheatre was built around the end of the first century or the early second century AD, and could hold roughly twenty thousand spectators, which was probably more than the population of the town at the time. Its arena is about seventy metres long, and beneath the sand floor ran a cellar with machinery and cages used to raise animals and scenery into view.

  2. 2. City of Luxembourg: its Old Quarters and F

    Luxembourg City is built on a rocky plateau above the deep, green valleys of the Alzette and Pétrusse rivers, and for centuries it was one of the most powerful fortresses in Europe, nicknamed the Gibraltar of the North. Spanish, French, Austrian and Prussian armies all held it, and each added to its walls, bastions and tunnels. In 1867, after an international treaty, most of the fortifications were dismantled, but enough remains to make the old quarters and fortifications a UNESCO World Heritage site, inscribed in 1994.

  3. 3. La place forte de Longwy

    In 1678, after Louis XIV won Longwy from the Spanish Netherlands in the Treaty of Nijmegen, he sent his great military engineer Vauban to make sure nobody took it back. Vauban did not just fortify the old town; he built a new one, Longwy-Haut, on the plateau, designing a star-shaped stronghold with bastions, ditches and a geometric grid of streets around a central square with a well and church. It is one of the twelve sites grouped as the Fortifications of Vauban on the World Heritage list, and walking the ramparts you can still read the logic of seventeenth-century siege warfare in the earth and stone.
