Why this ride

A one-way ride of 190 km, Manāli to the finish - roughly 2 h 45 in the saddle. It tops out around 5,100 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

The roads

Leh–Manali Highway

Keylong to Sarchu: 120 km. Over the top at 4,867 m - roughly 1920 m up and 780 m down. Cold up there even in July - pack a warm layer and check it is open.

Rider note

Check the pass is open before you commit to it.

The stops

  1. 1. Manāli

    Start here.

    Manāli is where the plains give up and the Himalaya begins in earnest. The town sits in the Kullu valley in Himachal Pradesh, on the banks of the Beas, among deodar forests and apple orchards, with snow peaks rising behind. Old Manali, across the river, is the quieter half, a tangle of lanes, wooden houses and cafes, and in the cedar woods above the town stands the Hadimba temple, a pagoda-like wooden shrine built in the sixteenth century and dedicated to a figure from the Mahabharata. The valley has long been a stopping place for traders, travellers and, more recently, backpackers and motorcyclists.

    Read the complete Manali write-up.

  2. 2. Keylong

    70 km in.

    Keylong is the main town of Lahaul, a high valley beyond the Rohtang Pass, and it sits at roughly three thousand metres above the Chandra and Bhaga rivers. For anyone riding north from Manali, this is the first real settlement after the climb, and the place where the road finally leaves the green side of the Himalaya and enters a drier, starker landscape. The mountains here are bare and brown, topped with snow, and the air is thin and clear.

    Read the complete Keylong write-up.

  3. 3. Sarchu

    The end: 190 km.

    Sarchu is less a town than a seasonal camp, a flat, windswept plain at about four thousand metres on the border between Himachal Pradesh and Ladakh. There are no permanent villages here, only a scattering of tented camps and a few simple dhabas that open in the summer months for travellers on the Manali to Leh road. In the middle of the Himalaya it feels oddly open, ringed by pale mountains and crossed by a stream that comes down from the snow.

    Read the complete Sarchu write-up.
