Why this ride

56 km round trip from Beffroi de l’Hôtel de Ville de Douai, about 1 h 18 of riding. It runs out through Monument à Madame Declercq and Grands Bureaux de la Société des Mines de and comes back by another road.

The stops

  1. 1. Beffroi de l’Hôtel de Ville de Douai

    Start here.

    The belfry of Douai rises above the town hall like a stone exclamation mark, a tall, square tower with a corner turret and a gilded lion at the top. Belfries were the symbols of medieval towns in Flanders and northern France, built to show that a town had won its freedoms, and to hold the bell that called citizens to meet, fight or put out fires. Douai's was begun in the fourteenth century and finished in the fifteenth, and it is one of the group of belfries of Belgium and France that UNESCO lists together.

    Read the complete Beffroi de l’Hôtel de Ville de Douai write-up.

  2. 2. Monument à Madame Declercq

    18 km in.

    Oignies is a small town in the Pas-de-Calais, at the edge of the old coal basin near the border with the Nord, with a monument at its heart to the people who lived and died by the pits.

    Read the complete Monument à Madame Declercq write-up.

  3. 3. Grands Bureaux de la Société des Mines de

    32 km in.

    Every mining company in the north of France needed an office, and the one that held the pay books, maps and plans of the Lens pits needed a large one. The Grands Bureaux of the Société des Mines de Lens stand in the middle of the town as a monument to an organisation that employed tens of thousands of men and controlled whole districts: the housing, the schools, the shops, the churches and the clinics were all in the company's gift. The building was part of the reconstruction after the First World War, when the town had been reduced to rubble and the company set about rebuilding its pits and its headquarters in brick and stone.
