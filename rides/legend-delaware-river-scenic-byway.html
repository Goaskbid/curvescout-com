Why this ride

National Scenic Byway. Delaware River, historic towns, canal. A one-way ride of 50 km, Trenton to Milford - roughly 55 min in the saddle. The stops in between: Lambertville and Frenchtown. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Trenton

    Trenton does not show up on many dream lists, but the start of a river ride does not have to be pretty, and this one has more history than most. New Jersey's capital sits on the Delaware at the head of navigation, the place where the river stops being tidal and starts being a proper river, and where the falls once stopped boats from going farther. That made it a mill town, and the bridge carries a famous sign in big white letters: Trenton Makes, The World Takes. It is the old industrial pride of a city that once made pottery, wire rope and steel.

  2. 2. Lambertville

    Two towns share the Delaware at Lambertville, and they have been admiring each other across the water for generations. On the New Jersey bank stands Lambertville, all brick Victorian rows climbing the slope from the river; on the Pennsylvania side lies New Hope, linked by an old bridge you can walk or ride across in a minute. Either could be the stop on its own. Together, they make the best place on the Delaware River Scenic Byway for a long, unhurried lunch.

  3. 3. Frenchtown

    Frenchtown has the good luck to be small, handsome, and on a river that has not been spoiled. It sits on the New Jersey bank of the Delaware, in Hunterdon County, with a main street of well-kept brick buildings, a handful of cafes and galleries, and a bridge across the water to Pennsylvania. The name comes from a Swiss-French settler who bought the land in the late eighteenth century and set up a ferry, and the town grew around that crossing.
