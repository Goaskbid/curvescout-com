Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ottawa

    Ottawa is a capital that still has the feel of a lumber town. It began in the 1820s as Bytown, named for Colonel John By, the army engineer who was sent to build a canal to link the Ottawa River to Lake Ontario, and the Rideau Canal, finished in 1832, still runs through the middle of the city. In winter it freezes and becomes one of the longest skating rinks in the world, with commuters gliding to work in scarves and hats, and in summer boats pass through the locks beside the grand Château Laurier hotel. The canal is a UNESCO site, and the whole waterway to Kingston is a good place to follow by bike.

  2. 2. Fort Henry

    Fort Henry stands on a limestone headland above the point where Lake Ontario narrows into the St. Lawrence River, and its job was to protect the eastern end of the Great Lakes. A first fort was thrown up here during the War of 1812, and the big stone fortress you see today was built between 1832 and 1837, after the British worried that the Americans might one day try again. It never fired a shot in anger, which is the usual fate of well-built forts, and it has been restored as a museum, where the Fort Henry Guard in red coats and white gaiters stage drills, marches and musket volleys in summer.

  3. 3. Cathcart Tower

    Cathcart Tower stands on Cedar Island, a low, rocky speck in Lake Ontario just off Kingston's harbour, and it is one of a ring of stone towers the British built in the 1840s to guard the city. They are called Martello towers, after a design the British had copied from a tower on Corsica, and they were meant to be squat, thick-walled gun platforms that were hard to knock down. Kingston has four, built in a time of tension with the United States over the Oregon boundary. None was ever tested in battle.

  4. 4. Fort Frederick

    Fort Frederick occupies Point Frederick, a peninsula across the harbour from the old city, and the first thing to understand is that it is now part of a military college. The Royal Military College of Canada was founded here in 1876, and cadets in scarlet or green still march across the parade square and jog around the campus. The point has been a naval and military site for more than two centuries, which gives it an unusual density of history.
