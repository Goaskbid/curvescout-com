Why this ride

132 km from Norrent-Fontes to Norrent-Fontes, about 2 h 41 of riding. It goes by way of Chevalement du Vieux-Deux, Paysage et ensemble miniers de Noeux-les, Cité n°2 62 Pas-de-Calais and Ancienne Fosse n°2 et mine-image.

The stops

  1. 1. Terril 34

    Start here.

    A terril is a slag heap, a mountain of waste rock dumped beside a coal mine, and in the flat land of northern France these man-made hills are the closest thing to relief for miles around. Terril 34 is one of hundreds that dot the Nord-Pas-de-Calais Mining Basin, a UNESCO site since 2012, which recognises not only the heaps but also the pithead frames, miners' housing estates, schools, churches and railway lines that made up the landscape of coal mining in the region for nearly three centuries. This one sits near Norrent-Fontes and Lillers, at the western end of the basin, where it meets the chalk hills of Artois.

    Read the complete Terril 34 write-up.

  2. 2. Chevalement du Vieux-Deux

    16 km in.

    Marles-les-Mines is a small town of about six thousand people west of Béthune, in the hilly western fringe of the coalfield, and the headframe known as the Vieux-Deux, the old Number Two, stands among its houses as a stubborn monument. The Compagnie des mines de Marles sank its shafts here in the nineteenth century, and the pit worked until the twentieth. The tall frame has been preserved, and together with the nearby terril and the cités around it, forms part of the Nord-Pas-de-Calais Mining Basin, the World Heritage Site listed in 2012.

    Read the complete Chevalement du Vieux-Deux write-up.

  3. 3. Paysage et ensemble miniers de Noeux-les-

    32 km in.

    Noeux-les-Mines stands between Béthune and Lens, and its skyline is dominated by two things: tall slag heaps, the terrils, and the old pithead structures that survive from the coal years. It is the surviving heart of a mine, with its headframe, winding house, baths and offices, plus the housing, school and church that went with it. The UNESCO listing of 2012 includes the Noeux-les-Mines area among its many components, for the sake of the whole landscape rather than any one building.

    Read the complete Paysage et ensemble miniers de Noeux-les- write-up.

  4. 4. Cité n°2 62 Pas-de-Calais

    49 km in.

    The mining basin of Nord-Pas-de-Calais is flat country made hilly by hand. Around Lens, the heaps of slag left by two centuries of coal digging rise like black pyramids above the fields, and the red-brick miners' houses stand in long, tidy terraces, row after row, called corons. This particular stop lies in the town of Lens, in a cité, the name for a company-built miners' estate, and the numbers in the label come from the way the mining companies numbered their pits and estates.

    Read the complete Cité n°2 62 Pas-de-Calais write-up.

  5. 5. Ancienne Fosse n°2 et mine-image

    63 km in.

    Oignies holds a special place in the history of northern France: it is where coal mining in the Pas-de-Calais began and where it ended. In 1842, coal was found here during a search for water on the estate of a local landowner, Henriette de Clercq, starting a rush that transformed the region. And on 21 December 1990, the last tonne of coal in the Nord-Pas de Calais basin was brought up from the 9-9bis pit in Oignies, ending more than two and a half centuries of mining in the region. The old Fosse n°2 and its mine-image, a training gallery where young miners learned their trade above ground, are part of the World Heritage site.

    Read the complete Ancienne Fosse n°2 et mine-image write-up.
