Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Jesuit Mission of Jesús de Tavarangue

    The most striking thing about Jesus de Tavarangue is what is missing. The church tower is a rectangular stone shaft rising into the air with nothing attached; the great church it was meant for was never finished. In 1767 the Spanish crown expelled the Jesuits from its territories, and the mission, which was in the middle of its final, grandest phase of construction, was abandoned. What remains is a handsome ruin of red sandstone, with arches, columns and carved details, set in a grassy plaza under the subtropical sky, silent and a little strange.

  2. 2. San Ignacio Mini

    Red sandstone, carved with angels, vines and saints, rises out of the green jungle of northeastern Argentina at San Ignacio Miní, the best preserved of the Jesuit missions in the province of Misiones. Founded in the early seventeenth century and moved to its present site in the 1690s, the mission was a town for several thousand Guaraní people, organised by Jesuit priests, with a great church, workshops, houses, a school and a plaza. The church's facade, in a style often called Guaraní Baroque, combines European forms with local motifs carved by Guaraní craftsmen. The mission, with others in Argentina and Brazil, became a World Heritage Site in 1983 and 1984.

  3. 3. Jesuit Mission of La Santísima Trinidad de

    In the south-east of Paraguay, near the Parana river and the Argentine border, a field of red earth, tall trees and golden sandstone walls marks the site of La Santisima Trinidad de Parana, one of the largest of the Jesuit missions built among the Guarani people in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. The ruins are about nine kilometres from Hohenau, a small town of just over five thousand people, and not far from Trinidad village and Jesus, where another mission stands.

  4. 4. Nuestra Señora de Loreto

    The ruins of Loreto lie in a clearing in the forest of Misiones, in the north-east tip of Argentina, and the forest is slowly winning. Strangler figs and giant roots creep over the red sandstone walls of what was once a church, and the grass grows over the plaza where thousands of Guarani people once lived. Loreto was one of the Jesuit missions, the reducciones, that the Society of Jesus founded in the early seventeenth century in the lands of the Guarani, in what is now Argentina, Paraguay and Brazil. This one was moved to its present site in 1632 after raids by slave hunters from Brazil.
