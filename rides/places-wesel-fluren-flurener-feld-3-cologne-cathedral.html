The stops

  1. 1. Wesel-Flüren | Flürener Feld 3

    Start here.

    Hamminkeln gives this stretch of the Lower Rhine its odd claim to a place in military history. On the morning of 24 March 1945, as part of the Allied operation to cross the Rhine, thousands of British and American airborne troops landed around Hamminkeln and Wesel by parachute and glider, in daylight, into a countryside of farms and woods, and the fighting was fierce. Many gliders were shot down, and the losses were heavy. The area has quiet memorials, and it is worth knowing that the fields you ride through carry that history as well as the older, Roman one.

  2. 2. Moers-Asberg

    38 km in.

    Asberg is a quiet district on the southern edge of Moers, on the left bank of the Lower Rhine, and beneath its houses and fields lies the Roman fort of Asciburgium, one of the earliest military bases on the Rhine frontier. It was founded around the time of the emperor Augustus or soon after, garrisoned by cavalry and auxiliary troops, and abandoned toward the end of the first century, when the Rhine shifted its course and moved away from the fort. The site is now part of the Lower German Limes UNESCO site, and although there is little to see above ground, information boards and outlines help you imagine the scene.

    Read the complete Moers-Asberg write-up.

  3. 3. Neuss-Reckberg | Wachtturm

    77 km in.

    Neuss has been a garrison town for two thousand years, and it still carries the Roman name in a shortened form: Novaesium, the legionary fortress that stood on the Rhine near the mouth of the Erft, was one of the oldest Roman military sites in Germany, founded in the days of Augustus. The Rhine was the frontier here, the Lower Germanic Limes, with no wall but a river, fortresses and watchtowers like the one at Reckberg, and the whole line was added to the UNESCO list in 2021.

    Read the complete Neuss-Reckberg | Wachtturm write-up.

  4. 4. Cologne Cathedral

    113 km in.

    Six hundred and thirty-two years. That is how long it took to finish Cologne Cathedral, from the laying of the foundation stone in 1248 to the completion of the spires in 1880, and when you stand at its foot by the main station it feels as though the building is still growing. The twin towers climb to around 157 metres, black with soot and weather, and for a few years after completion they made it the tallest building in the world.

    Read the complete Cologne Cathedral write-up.

  5. 5. Zollverein Coal Mine Industrial Complex in

    192 km in.

    The headframe of Shaft XII at Zollverein, with its red steel lattice and double wheels, is the Eiffel Tower of the Ruhr, and it is easy to see why. Designed by the architects Fritz Schupp and Martin Kremmer and opened in 1932, the shaft and its surrounding buildings were built in a clean, functional style influenced by the Bauhaus, and it was hailed as the most beautiful coal mine in the world. At its peak, Zollverein was one of the largest and most productive mines in Europe, and the adjacent coking plant processed coal into coke for the steel industry. The mine closed in 1986, the coking plant in 1993, and UNESCO listed the complex in 2001.
