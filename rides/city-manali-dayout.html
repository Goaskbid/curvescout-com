Why this ride

From Manāli it runs 198 km to Manāli; allow about 3 h 39 of riding. On the way it passes Great Himalayan National Park Conservation and back through Great Himalayan National Park Conservation. It tops out around 4,700 m.

The stops

  1. 1. Manāli

    Start here.

    Manāli is where the plains give up and the Himalaya begins in earnest. The town sits in the Kullu valley in Himachal Pradesh, on the banks of the Beas, among deodar forests and apple orchards, with snow peaks rising behind. Old Manali, across the river, is the quieter half, a tangle of lanes, wooden houses and cafes, and in the cedar woods above the town stands the Hadimba temple, a pagoda-like wooden shrine built in the sixteenth century and dedicated to a figure from the Mahabharata. The valley has long been a stopping place for traders, travellers and, more recently, backpackers and motorcyclists.

    Read the complete Manali write-up.

  2. 2. Great Himalayan National Park Conservation

    99 km in. The ride passes about 19 km from it.

    The Great Himalayan National Park is a long name for a wild place. It lies in the Kullu district of Himachal Pradesh, at the meeting of several river valleys, the Tirthan, the Sainj and the Jiwa Nal, and it protects a steep forested wilderness that climbs from about fifteen hundred metres to over six thousand. It was established in the 1980s and listed by UNESCO in 2014, for its extraordinary biodiversity. Snow leopards, Himalayan tahr, brown bears and musk deer live here, and the forests are home to the western tragopan, a pheasant with a brilliant red and spotted plumage, and the monal, whose feathers shine in the sun like metal.
