  1. 1. Montpellier

    Montpellier is a young city in an old shell. More than a quarter of its population is made up of students, and its university, whose medical faculty dates back to the thirteenth century, is among the oldest in Europe. The centre is the Place de la Comédie, a huge oval square paved in stone, with the opera house at one end and the fountain of the Three Graces in the middle, and from there the old town of tight, shaded lanes spreads out, with elegant stone houses and hidden courtyards.

  2. 2. Arles

    Arles is where the Rhône splits in two and the Camargue begins, and for more than two thousand years it has been a city of power, art and sun. The Romans made it one of the great cities of Gaul, building an amphitheatre that still hosts events, a theatre, baths and an underground gallery, the cryptoportiques, and the Roman and Romanesque monuments of Arles are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Romanesque church of Saint-Trophime, with its carved portal and cloister, is one of the finest in Provence.

  4. 4. Pont du Gard

    Built without mortar, three tiers of arches carry a water channel across the gorge of the Gardon nearly fifty metres above the river, and the stones still fit together as if they had been placed last week. The Pont du Gard was one section of an aqueduct some fifty kilometres long that brought spring water from near Uzès to Roman Nîmes in the first century, with a total drop of only a few metres over the whole distance. It is a masterpiece of surveying as much as building, and it has stood through floods that have destroyed far younger bridges.
