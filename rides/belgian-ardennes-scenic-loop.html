Why this ride

A 146 km loop that starts and ends near Liège, about 2 h 21 of riding. The stops in between: Spa, Signal de Botrange, La Roche en Ardenne and Durbuy. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Liege

    Liège is a hard-working Walloon city on the Meuse, where steelworks once glowed along the river and the people have a reputation for warmth and bluntness. It has none of the polished prettiness of Bruges, and that is part of its appeal. The old centre is a tangle of lanes and courtyards under the hill called the Citadelle, with the grand Palace of the Prince-Bishops standing on a vast square, a reminder that this was an independent church state for nearly eight centuries.

  2. 2. Spa

    The word spa comes from this town, and that is the kind of claim that most places make and few can prove. Here it happens to be true: Spa, in the Ardennes foothills of eastern Belgium, has been a place for taking the waters since the Romans, who are said to have noted its iron-rich springs, and from the sixteenth century it was a fashionable resort. Visitors have included the Russian emperor Peter the Great, who came in 1717 to cure his stomach, and the town's central spring, the Pouhon Pierre-le-Grand, is named after him. The bottled water that bears the town's name is sold worldwide, and the little town has a quiet, slightly faded charm, with grand hotels, a thermal baths complex on the hill and a casino.

  3. 3. Signal de Botrange

    Belgium's highest point is a gentle bump in a moor, and the country is rather proud of it. Signal de Botrange stands at 694 metres in the Hautes Fagnes, a high plateau of peat bog in the east of Wallonia near the German border, and it is so modest that a short stone staircase was built on top to give it a little extra height. Climb the steps and you reach a round 700 metres, which is, technically speaking, as high as anyone in the kingdom can stand, and the view across the heather and the fir trees is an unexpectedly open one.

  4. 4. La Roche en Ardenne

    La Roche-en-Ardenne is a town of about four thousand folded into a tight loop of the Ourthe river, with the ruins of a medieval castle on a rock above the rooftops. The Ardennes here are not high, but they are steep: wooded ridges cut by deep, winding valleys, with fast clear rivers, slate roofs and stone farmhouses. The town was a tourist favourite long before the motorcycle, and it fills on summer weekends with riders from Belgium, the Netherlands and Germany, who come for the roads.

  5. 5. Durbuy

    Durbuy bills itself as the smallest city in the world, a claim based on a charter granted in 1331, and while the title is more marketing than fact, the little town on the Ourthe river in the Belgian Ardennes is a delight. Its old centre, packed into a bend of the river beneath a limestone cliff, is a tangle of cobbled lanes, stone houses and small squares, with a castle above that has belonged to the same noble family for centuries.

Liege to Spa

Liege → Spa is 32 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Spa to Signal de Botrange

Signal de Botrange → La Roche en Ardenne drops about 694 m over 64 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.
