Aqaba Marine Reserve and Petra

Why this ride

A 343 km round trip from Tala Bay - roughly 5 h 55 in the saddle. On the way it passes Wadi Rum Protected Area, Petra and back through Wadi Rum Protected Area. The highest point is about 1,150 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Aqaba Marine Reserve

    The Gulf of Aqaba is a narrow arm of the Red Sea, a deep trench between Jordan and Saudi Arabia on one side and Sinai on the other, and its clear water holds some of the northernmost coral reefs in the world. Jordan's marine reserve runs along a short coastal strip south of Aqaba, where the mountains of the desert drop to a thin ribbon of beach and the water goes from beige to turquoise to deep blue within a hundred metres.

  2. 2. Wadi Rum Protected Area

    Wadi Rum looks like the moon dressed up in rust. Great walls of sandstone and granite rise sheer from a flat desert floor of red sand, cut with canyons and carved with arches, and the whole protected area covers hundreds of square kilometres south of Jordan's King's Highway and east of the Desert Highway. The nearest sizeable town is Quweira, about twenty kilometres away, and Aqaba lies roughly an hour south.

  3. 3. Petra

    Petra is the most famous ruin in Jordan, and it earns the fame. The Nabataeans, an Arab people who grew rich on the incense and spice trade, carved their capital into the rose-coloured sandstone cliffs of a hidden valley more than two thousand years ago. You approach through the Siq, a narrow gorge about a kilometre long, with walls rising on either side until, at the end, the Treasury appears in a slice of light, a monumental facade cut straight out of the rock.
