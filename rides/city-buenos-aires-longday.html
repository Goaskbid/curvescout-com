Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Buenos Aires

    Buenos Aires is the capital that looks across a river wide enough to be mistaken for the sea. The Río de la Plata is brown, shallow and enormous, and the city that sits on its western shore has always seemed to face outward, toward Europe. Pedro de Mendoza first tried to found a settlement in 1536 and failed, and Juan de Garay refounded it in 1580. Since then the port has grown into one of the great cities of the southern hemisphere, with about thirteen million people in its wider area, wide avenues designed on a Parisian scale, and cafés that keep a table for you all afternoon.

  2. 2. Fray Bentos Industrial Landscape

    Fray Bentos is a small city on the east bank of the Río Uruguay, and its fame comes from a corned-beef tin. In 1865, a company built a factory here to make Liebig's Extract of Meat, a concentrate of beef boiled down from the enormous herds of the pampas, and the plant grew into a giant that shipped meat products around the world. Later a British-owned company took it over, and the brand Fray Bentos, still sold on supermarket shelves as tinned pies in Britain, was born. The factory closed in the late 1970s, and today the whole complex, with its brick sheds, chimney and workers' houses, is a museum, listed by UNESCO in 2015 as an industrial landscape.
