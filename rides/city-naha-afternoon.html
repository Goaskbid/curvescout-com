Why this ride

A 243 km round trip from Naha - roughly 4 h 11 in the saddle. It goes by way of Nakagusuku-jô site, Zakimi-jô site, Katsuren-jô site and Nakijin-jô site.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Naha

    Naha is the capital of Okinawa, a low, bright, warm city at the southern end of a long, thin island that for centuries was the Ryukyu Kingdom, independent and rich from trade between China, Japan and Southeast Asia. Its royal seat was Shuri Castle, on a hill above the city, with red lacquered halls in a style that mixes Chinese and Japanese ideas. A fire destroyed the main buildings in October 2019, and reconstruction is under way, though the walls and gates and the view over the city remain. The castle site belongs to the Gusuku Sites and Related Properties of the Kingdom of Ryukyu, listed by UNESCO in 2000.

  2. 2. Nakagusuku-jô site

    On the ridge above Nakagusuku Bay the walls of an old castle follow the curve of the hill in a long ribbon of pale limestone, and the shapes are surprisingly sophisticated. Nakagusuku was built, probably in the fourteenth century and extended in the fifteenth, by the lord Gosamaru, who was respected as a master builder, and its walls use a technique of fitted, curved stones with no mortar, in a pattern the Okinawans call nunozumi, cloth-laying, and a more rugged style called aikatazumi, which looks like a jigsaw. Six enclosures step up along the ridge, with gateways, a well and the foundations of halls.

  3. 3. Zakimi-jô site

    The walls of Zakimi gusuku curve across the top of a hill in Yomitan like a ribbon of pale limestone, following the contour of the land in graceful arcs rather than straight lines. Built in the early fifteenth century by Gosamaru, a celebrated castle builder of the Ryukyu Kingdom, the fortress is small but beautifully made. Its arched stone gateways, held in place by a wedge-shaped keystone, are among the oldest stone arches in Okinawa. Zakimi is part of the UNESCO site of the Gusuku Sites and Related Properties of the Kingdom of Ryukyu, listed in 2000.

  4. 4. Katsuren-jô site

    Katsuren castle stands on a limestone hill on the east coast of Okinawa, near the base of the Katsuren peninsula, and its curving walls step up the slope in a series of enclosures to a summit with sweeping views across the sea. It is one of the gusuku, the castles of the old Ryukyu kingdom and the warring lords who came before it, and it was inscribed in 2000 together with Nakijin, Zakimi, Nakagusuku, Shuri and several sacred groves.

  5. 5. Nakijin-jô site

    On a hill on the Motobu peninsula in northern Okinawa, the long, curving walls of Nakijin castle follow the contours of the limestone like a stone dragon. In the fourteenth century, Okinawa was divided into three kingdoms, and Nakijin was the seat of the northern kingdom, Hokuzan, until it was conquered by the Chuzan kingdom in the early fifteenth century. The site is part of the UNESCO-listed Gusuku Sites and Related Properties of the Kingdom of Ryukyu, which celebrates the unique culture of the Ryukyu islands, shaped by trade with China, Japan and Southeast Asia.
