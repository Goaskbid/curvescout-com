Why this ride

National Scenic Byway. Sierra Nevada, lakes, narrow mountain road. A one-way ride of 74 km, Arnold to the finish - roughly 1 h 21 in the saddle. The highest point is about 2,650 m. Ebbetts Pass is open in summer only - check it is open before you set off. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Scenic road chapters

Ebbetts Pass Scenic Byway

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 18 km this is Ebbetts Pass Scenic Byway, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Arnold

    Arnold is a pine-shaded mountain town strung out along Highway 4 at around 1,200 metres, high enough to have left the oak and grass of the Sierra foothills behind and low enough to stay open all year. It is the last proper town on the climb toward Ebbetts Pass, and the sensible place to fill the tank and eat something before the road turns serious.

  2. 2. Ebbetts Pass Scenic Byway point 2

    Past Lake Alpine, Highway 4 stops pretending to be a normal road. The centre line disappears, the tarmac narrows to something barely wider than a car and a half, and the route starts to wriggle over granite, between ridges and around small lakes, at around 2,300 metres.

  3. 3. Ebbetts Pass Scenic Byway point 3

    At 2,663 metres, Ebbetts Pass is the high point of Highway 4 and one of the quieter crossings of the Sierra Nevada. There is no lodge, no café and no souvenir stand, just a sign, a small parking area and the Pacific Crest Trail crossing the road, with thru-hikers sometimes resting in the shade on their long walk from Mexico to Canada. Ask one how far they have come and you will feel very lazy on your motorcycle.

  4. 4. Ebbetts Pass Scenic Byway point 4

    Coming down off Ebbetts, the road follows the East Fork of the Carson River out of the mountains toward Markleeville, the county seat of Alpine County. Alpine is the least populated county in California, home to only around a thousand people, and Markleeville is so small that its courthouse, an old general store and a handful of houses make up most of the town. It is a pleasant surprise after the wild high country, and its main street feels like it belongs to another century.
