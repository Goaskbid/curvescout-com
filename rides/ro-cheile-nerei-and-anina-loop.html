  1. 1. Oraviţa

    Oravița lies at the south-western edge of Romania, in the Banat hills where the land begins to fold up toward the Anina Mountains. It is a small town, twelve thousand people or so, and yet it holds a handful of distinctions that would suit a city ten times its size. The little theatre here, opened in 1817, is regarded as the oldest in Romania, a neat neoclassical hall with a horseshoe auditorium that has been restored and still hosts performances.

  2. 2. Anina

    Anina is a coal town high in the Banat mountains, and for a long time it was a place people left rather than visited. The mines here were opened in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries by the Austrian authorities and later by a railway company, and the town grew around them, with miners brought in from Austria, Bohemia, Slovakia and elsewhere. The neighbouring settlement of Steierdorf was named after the Styrian settlers who came to work the forests and the coal. The last mine closed after a deadly explosion in the 2000s, and the town has struggled since, with abandoned buildings and an ageing population.
