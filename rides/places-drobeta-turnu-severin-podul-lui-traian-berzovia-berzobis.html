Why this ride

From Drobeta-Turnu Severin it runs 394 km to Drobeta-Turnu Severin; allow about 6 h 48 of riding. The stops in between: Domogled - Valea Cernei - Domogled-Coronin, Teregova - La Hideg, Iaz Tibiscum Dâmb and Berzovia - Berzobis. The highest point is about 900 m. It crosses from Serbia into Romania - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Drobeta-Turnu Severin -Podul lui Traian

    When the emperor Trajan wanted to invade Dacia for the second time, he told his engineer to build a bridge across the Danube, and the engineer, Apollodorus of Damascus, made it happen. The bridge at Drobeta, completed in 105 AD, was a marvel, with twenty stone piers carrying timber arches over a river that is more than a kilometre wide at this point, and for a thousand years it was the longest arch bridge in the world. It was built to move the legions across the river in one season, and it did its job. The wars that followed ended with the conquest of Dacia in 106 and the creation of a new Roman province.

  2. 2. Domogled - Valea Cernei - Domogled-Coronin

    The river Cerna runs through one of the deepest and wildest valleys of the Romanian Carpathians, a narrow gorge between limestone cliffs, before emerging at Băile Herculane, one of the oldest spa towns in Europe. The Romans bathed in its hot springs nearly two thousand years ago and named the place after Hercules. In the nineteenth century, the Habsburgs built grand bathhouses, hotels and casinos in an ornate imperial style, and Emperor Franz Joseph and Empress Sisi came to take the waters. Many of these buildings now stand empty and decaying, a haunting sight, though restoration efforts are under way. The surrounding mountains form the Domogled-Valea Cernei National Park, and parts of its old beech forests are included in the UNESCO site of Ancient and Primeval Beech Forests.

  3. 3. Teregova - La Hideg

    Teregova is a village in the narrow corridor between the Banat mountains and the Țarcu range in western Romania, where the Timiș river flows north toward the plain and a low pass leads south to the Cerna and the Danube. This corridor has been a route since ancient times: the Roman road from Dierna, on the Danube, to Tibiscum, near Caransebeș, ran through here, linking the river crossing with the heart of the province of Dacia. La Hideg is a local place name near the village, and the point is listed among the sites of the Roman frontier of Dacia, the UNESCO site recognised in 2024. As with many such sites, the remains may be hard to spot, but the logic of the location is clear.

  4. 4. Iaz Tibiscum Dâmb

    Tibiscum was a Roman town at the meeting point of two rivers in the Banat, in the west of today's Romania, and the Iaz Dâmb is one of its pieces: a low rise where archaeologists have traced buildings, defences and the signs of a busy settlement. The Romans founded a fort here after the conquest of Dacia in the early second century, and a civilian town grew around it, with workshops, houses, a bath and a temple. It stood on the route that linked the Danube with the heart of the new province, and it was important enough to be given the rank of a municipium.

  5. 5. Berzovia - Berzobis

    Only a handful of sentences survive from the Emperor Trajan's own account of his wars against the Dacians, and one of them mentions this place. Quoted by a later grammarian, it reads, in Latin, that from there we advanced to Berzobis, then to Aizi. Berzobis is the village now called Berzovia, in the Banat hills of south-western Romania, and the site of the Roman fort here, where the Fourth Legion Flavia Felix was stationed after the conquest, is part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire listing in Romania inscribed in 2024.
