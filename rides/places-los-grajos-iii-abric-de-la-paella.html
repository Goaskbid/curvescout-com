Los Grajos III and Abric de la Paella

Why this ride

328 km from Cieza to Cieza, about 5 h 39 of riding. On the way it passes Cueva del Peliciego, Olula, Abric de Gontrán and Abric de la Paella. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Los Grajos III

    Cieza is a big town for this part of Murcia, with over thirty-five thousand people on the banks of the Segura, and its fame in Spain rests on fruit. In February, the peach and apricot orchards of the river valley burst into pink and white blossom, and visitors come from all over the region to see the hills turn soft with it. The name of the shelter, Los Grajos, means the jackdaws, or rooks, the black birds that nest in the cliffs, and the ride ties it to Maina and to the Abric de la Paella. The paintings belong to the Levantine tradition protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain, and Cieza has some of the most important sites in this part of the country.

  2. 2. Cueva del Peliciego

    Jumilla is wine. This town in the north of Murcia sits in a high, dry valley surrounded by bare mountains, and its vineyards, planted with the Monastrell grape, produce powerful red wines under the Jumilla designation of origin, one of the oldest in Spain. The Cueva del Peliciego, a rock shelter in the hills near the town, holds prehistoric paintings in the Levantine style, and it has been known since the early twentieth century, when it was one of the first rock art sites studied in the region.

  3. 3. Olula

    Caudete and Almansa sit in a corridor of land where Castile-La Mancha, Valencia and Murcia meet, and for centuries this was frontier country, where kingdoms and armies passed and fought. Olula is a point in the hills near Caudete, linked on the rides with rock-art shelters in the surrounding sierras, part of the extensive UNESCO listing of prehistoric art in Mediterranean Spain.

  4. 4. Abric de Gontrán

    Head south-west from Xàtiva along the old road to Castile and you climb gently into a corridor between two mountain ranges, the Serra Grossa on one side and the Serra de Enguera on the other. The town of Moixent sits where the corridor narrows, with Vallada just downstream and the vineyards of Fontanars dels Alforins on the plateau beyond. The Abric de Gontran lies in the hills just outside Moixent.

  5. 5. Abric de la Paella

    Muro de Alcoy and Cocentaina sit side by side in the valley of the Serpis river, in the Comtat, a district of northern Alicante surrounded by mountains. Cocentaina is crowned by a castle with a tall tower, and below it lies an old town with a palace of the Counts of Cocentaina and a Franciscan convent. The Abric de la Paella, named after the paella pan perhaps because of its shape, lies in the hills between the two towns.
