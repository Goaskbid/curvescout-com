Why this ride

185 km from Calasparra to Calasparra, about 3 h 11 of riding. It goes by way of Cejo Cortado I, Los Grajos, maina and back through Los Grajos. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Los Rumies

    The name Los Rumíes looks like the Arabic word rumi, which was once used across the Muslim world for Romans, then for Christians, and the Spanish landscape is full of names like it, left behind by eight centuries of Andalusi presence. The place lies in the dry hills north of the Segura valley, near Calasparra, and the rides that visit it also pass through the wider district of the Abrigo Grande de Minateda, one of the most famous painted shelters in the whole Levantine tradition. The rock art of this corner of Spain is substantial and varied.

  2. 2. Cejo Cortado I

    Mula is a town in the heart of the Murcia region, built on the slopes of a hill crowned by a sixteenth-century castle, with an old quarter of narrow streets, noble houses and churches. Around it, a dry landscape of hills, ravines and badlands opens out, with orchards and olive groves along the river. The Cejo Cortado, a name meaning the cut brow, refers to a rocky escarpment with prehistoric shelters about seven kilometres from the town, between Mula, Albudeite and Ricote.

  3. 3. Los Grajos

    Jackdaws gave this place its name, grajos in Spanish, and they still wheel and chatter around the limestone cliffs where prehistoric people once sheltered and painted. Los Grajos lies in the hills near Alhama de Murcia, in the valley of the Guadalentín, where dry mountains rise above a plain of orchards, greenhouses and towns. The rock shelters here hold paintings in the Levantine style, and access is generally restricted to protect them.

  4. 4. maina

    Maina is an obscure name on the map in the Murcian hills north-west of Totana, where the Sierra Espuña rises above the valley of the Guadalentín. It refers to a rock shelter or painted site in the dry limestone country, part of the cluster of prehistoric art sites around here included in the World Heritage listing of Levantine rock art. As with many such places, there is often little to see without a guide, but the landscape around it is worth the ride.
