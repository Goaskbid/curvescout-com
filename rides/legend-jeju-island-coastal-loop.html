Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Jeju City

    Jeju is a volcanic island off the southern tip of Korea, and Jeju City is its biggest town, the gateway for most visitors who arrive by air. Behind it stands Hallasan, a shield volcano reaching just under two thousand metres, and its slopes give the whole island a gentle, tilted shape. UNESCO has recognised the lava tubes and the volcanic landscape here, and a sense of dark basalt is everywhere, in the stone walls that divide the fields, in the statues called dol hareubang that stand guard in villages, and in the black rocks of the shoreline.

  2. 2. Jeju Island Coastal Loop point 2

    The eastern tip of Jeju is where the island's volcanic origins are most obvious. Seongsan Ilchulbong, the Sunrise Peak, rises straight from the sea here, a huge bowl-shaped tuff cone with steep green walls and a crater wide enough to hold a village. It formed when rising magma met seawater a few thousand years ago and blew itself apart into layers of ash, and the sea has been cutting at its sides ever since. It is part of the UNESCO World Heritage site that covers Jeju's volcanoes and lava tubes, and the climb to the rim, steep and stepped, takes about twenty minutes.

  3. 3. Seogwipo

    Seogwipo is the main town on the south coast of Jeju Island, with about a hundred and fifty thousand people, and it faces the open ocean with Mount Halla behind. Jeju is a volcanic island, and the southern coast has some of its finest scenery, with black basalt cliffs, waterfalls and small islands. The town itself is relaxed and pleasant, with a lively harbour, seafood restaurants and an old-fashioned market, the Maeil Olle, where you can buy tangerines, fish and snacks.

  4. 4. Near Seogwipo

    West of Seogwipo, the southern slopes of Hallasan run down to the sea in long, gentle ramps, and the land here is sheltered, warm and green. Hallasan, the volcano at the heart of the island, is South Korea's highest mountain at 1,947 metres, and on this side its summit often stands clear above the clouds that gather on the northern slopes. Below it are tangerine orchards, greenhouses, small villages and the resort district of Jungmun on the coast, with big hotels and a stretch of beach.

  5. 5. Near Gaigeturi

    The north-west coast of Jeju around Hallim faces the open sea and the prevailing wind, and it feels different from the sheltered south. The water off the beaches at Hyeopjae and Geumneung is turquoise over white sand, an unexpected colour against the black lava rocks, and the small island of Biyangdo sits just offshore, a low cone that looks like a hat dropped in the sea. Hallim itself is a working harbour town with fish markets, boats and a scattering of cafes, a little rough around the edges and all the better for it.
