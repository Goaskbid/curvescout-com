  1. 1. Jerusalem

    Jerusalem sits on a ridge about seven hundred and fifty metres above the sea, and the air is cooler and drier than on the coast, with a light that turns the pale limestone of the buildings to gold at the end of the day. The Old City, enclosed by walls built by the Ottoman sultan Suleiman in the sixteenth century, is a dense maze of lanes, markets and domes, divided traditionally into four quarters, and sacred to Jews, Christians and Muslims. The Dome of the Rock gleams above the Temple Mount, the Western Wall stands at its foot, and the Church of the Holy Sepulchre holds the places traditionally linked to the crucifixion and burial of Jesus.

  2. 2. Ancient Jericho/Tell es-Sultan

    Jericho is one of the oldest continuously inhabited towns on earth, and at Tell es-Sultan, on its northern edge, archaeologists have dug down through layers of settlement more than ten thousand years old. Among the finds is a great stone tower, built in the Neolithic, around eleven thousand years ago, long before writing, metal or the wheel, which is often called the oldest such tower in the world. The tell, with its trenches and mud brick walls, was listed as a World Heritage Site in 2023.

  4. 4. Hebron/Al-Khalil Old Town

    Hebron, al-Khalil in Arabic, is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the Middle East, set in the hills of the southern West Bank. Its heart is the old town, a warren of stone alleys, covered souqs and Mamluk-era houses, grouped around the great mosque built over the Cave of the Patriarchs, where tradition places the tombs of Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and their wives. The site is holy to Muslims, Jews and Christians, and its name in Arabic, al-Khalil, means the friend, a reference to Abraham as the friend of God.
