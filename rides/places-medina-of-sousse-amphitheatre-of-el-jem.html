Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Medina of Sousse

    Sousse is a city that has been busy for nearly three thousand years, under the Phoenicians, the Romans and the Arabs, and its old town, the medina, still has the crowded, enclosed feel of a place built behind walls. It stands on the Tunisian coast, in the region called the Sahel, a belt of olive groves and white towns between the sea and the dry interior. Sousse was Hadrumetum in Roman times, and later became an important port, and it was ringed by walls in the ninth century, parts of which still stand.

  2. 2. Kairouan

    Kairouan is the fourth holy city of Islam, after Mecca, Medina and Jerusalem, and that makes it unusual in Tunisia, which otherwise has a relaxed Mediterranean air. It was founded in 670 by the Arab general Uqba ibn Nafi as a military base in the heart of the plains, far from the coast and the Byzantine fleets, and it became the first Islamic capital of the Maghreb. The Great Mosque, also called the Mosque of Uqba, dates from that early period and was rebuilt in the ninth century. It has a huge courtyard surrounded by arcades, a three-storey minaret and a prayer hall with hundreds of columns, many taken from Roman and Byzantine ruins. The old city is a UNESCO site.

  3. 3. Amphitheatre of El Jem

    El Jem is a small Tunisian town with a very large building. Rising out of the flat plain of the Sahel, the amphitheatre of Thysdrus, as the Romans called the place, is one of the biggest ever built, a three-tiered oval of pale limestone more than a hundred and thirty metres long, and it was designed to hold some thirty-five thousand spectators. Construction began in the early third century, probably under the rule of the wealthy local landowners who grew rich on olive oil, and it was never fully finished, because the African governor who encouraged it was killed in a revolt in 238.
