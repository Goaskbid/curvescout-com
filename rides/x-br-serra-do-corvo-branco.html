Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Urubici

    Frost on the grass in a country famous for beaches is the first surprise of Urubici. The town lies on the high plateau of the Serra Catarinense in Santa Catarina, at roughly 900 metres, and it is regularly named as one of the coldest places in Brazil, where winter mornings bring ice on puddles and, once in a while, a dusting of snow that makes the national news. The land is a patchwork of apple orchards, pine woods, pasture and araucaria trees, the monkey-puzzle conifers whose seeds, the pinhão, are boiled and eaten in winter.

  2. 2. Serra do Corvo Branco

    The Serra do Corvo Branco is a road cut through solid rock, and the walls on either side are so high that the sky narrows to a strip. The route was cut through the escarpment in recent decades, leaving sheer cliffs of sandstone and basalt towering above the track, in places tens of metres tall. It lies in the highlands of Santa Catarina, near the town of Urubici, an area that has the coldest winters in Brazil and where frost and occasional snow are normal between June and August.

  3. 3. Bom Jardim da Serra

    Bom Jardim da Serra sits on the high plateau of Santa Catarina, a cold, windswept, rolling grassland dotted with araucaria pines and apple orchards, at the very top of the Serra Geral escarpment. It is a small place, and the larger towns are close: Lauro Muller, about ten thousand people, lies at the foot of the escarpment, and Sao Joaquim, sixteen thousand, is up the plateau and known as one of the coldest towns in Brazil, where snow falls in some winters. Fuel and services are in those towns, so plan accordingly.
