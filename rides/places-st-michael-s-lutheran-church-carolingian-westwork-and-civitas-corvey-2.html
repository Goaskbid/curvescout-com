Why this ride

A 297 km round trip from Hildesheim - roughly 5 h 07 in the saddle. The stops in between: Mines of Rammelsberg, Carolingian Westwork and Civitas Corvey and back through Mines of Rammelsberg. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. St Michael's Lutheran Church

    St Michael's in Hildesheim is a church that was built to a vision. Bishop Bernward, a teacher and craftsman as well as a churchman, began it around the year 1010 and died in 1022, and the church was consecrated in 1033. He designed it with a double apse, with a choir at each end, and with two transepts and six towers, a symmetrical plan that is one of the best examples of Ottonian architecture. The layout reflects a view of the world in which the church was a model of heaven.

  2. 2. Mines of Rammelsberg

    The two belong to the same story. The Upper Harz was one of the great silver and lead mining districts of Europe, and its engineers built an astonishing network of ponds, ditches and tunnels to bring water to the wheels that drove the pumps and hoists. That water system is part of the UNESCO listing, which took in Rammelsberg and Goslar in 1992 and was later extended to cover the Upper Harz. Today the ponds look like natural lakes, and it takes a moment to realise that someone dug every channel by hand.

  3. 3. Carolingian Westwork and Civitas Corvey

    Corvey sits in a loop of the Weser just outside Höxter, a baroque abbey palace set among meadows with the wooded hills of the Solling and the Weser Uplands closing in around it. The baroque part is handsome but not why people come. Hidden behind it, forming the entrance to the church, is a westwork built between 873 and 885, and it is one of the very few Carolingian buildings in the world still standing more or less as it was built.
