From Wesel it runs 283 km to Wesel; allow about 4 h 53 of riding. It goes by way of Berg en Dal-De Holdeurn | North, Bunnik-Vechten | Provincialeweg and back through Berg en Dal-De Holdeurn | North. Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Wesel-Flüren | Flürener Feld 4

    Here the Rhine bends through some of the emptiest farmland in western Germany. The fields at Flüren, north of Wesel, are flat enough to see a church tower from ten kilometres away, and the land runs in long, straight lines of willows, poplars and drainage ditches towards the Dutch border. This is the Niederrhein, a region where people have lived with floods for as long as they have farmed, and where the cycling routes are signposted better than the main roads.

  2. 2. Berg en Dal-De Holdeurn | North

    Just east of Nijmegen, where the Netherlands suddenly remembers how to make hills, a wooded slope near the village of Berg en Dal hides the remains of a Roman industrial site. De Holdeurn was where the Tenth Legion, Legio X Gemina, stationed at Nijmegen in the late first and early second centuries, made roof tiles, bricks and pottery, stamped with the legion's name and shipped all over the region. The kilns and workshops have been excavated, and the site is one of the many places that make up the Frontiers of the Roman Empire – the Lower German Limes, listed by UNESCO in 2021.

  3. 3. Bunnik-Vechten | Provincialeweg

    A modern fort and a Roman one stand within a couple of kilometres of each other here, and both exist because of the same river. Vechten, on the Kromme Rijn just east of Utrecht, was the Roman fort of Fectio, one of the best-known sites on the Lower Germanic frontier. It lay at a point where the Rhine split, with the branch that became the Vecht heading north, and that made it a perfect depot. Soldiers, grain, and building stone all went through here, and archaeologists have pulled a remarkable amount out of the ground, including altars, bronze statuettes and the debris of a busy harbour.
