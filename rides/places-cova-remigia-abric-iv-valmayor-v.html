Why this ride

165 km from Ares del Maestre to Ares del Maestre, about 2 h 51 of riding. On the way it passes La Saltadora- Abric I, Abric del mas dels Ous, Valmayor V and back through Abric del mas dels Ous. The highest point is about 1,100 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cova Remigia- Abric IV

    Red paint in these shelters is mostly iron oxide, ground from ochre and mixed with something that has not been identified for certain, perhaps animal fat, blood, egg or plant sap, and some figures were drawn in black from charcoal or manganese. That is more or less the entire chemistry of Levantine art, and the fact that a handful of earth pigments have survived thousands of years in open air under an overhang is one of the minor miracles of Spanish archaeology. Cova Remigia, in the Gasulla ravine near Ares del Maestrat, is the place where you can see how well it has worked.

  2. 2. La Saltadora- Abric I

    Albocàsser sits on a limestone shelf in the northern Maestrat, a farming village of about fourteen hundred people with a stone church, a market-day bustle and almond trees all around. A few kilometres from it, the Gasulla ravine and its neighbours hold Levantine rock paintings, and La Saltadora, the Jumper, is one of the shelters, named perhaps for a leaping figure or a nimble ledge. The Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula was added to the UNESCO list in 1998, and Abric I is a small but significant piece of that story.

  3. 3. Abric del mas dels Ous

    Xert is the kind of village you pass through on the way to somewhere else and then wonder why you did not stop. It lies in the Baix Maestrat in northern Castellón, below a ridge of bare limestone, with the old village climbing the slope and a newer part down by the road. The shelter called the Abric del Mas dels Ous, the shelter of the farmhouse of the eggs, is out in the dry hills nearby, between Xert, Canet lo Roig and Rosell.

  4. 4. Valmayor V

    The Matarraña, in the far east of Teruel province, is often called the Tuscany of Aragon, and for once the comparison holds. Stone villages crown hilltops above olive groves and vineyards, rivers run clear through limestone gorges, and the light in the evening is honey-coloured. Valmayor is a ravine in the hills here, between Valderrobres, Fuentespalda and the Catalan border, and its shelters hold prehistoric paintings, part of the UNESCO-listed Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin. As elsewhere, they are protected, and visits are best arranged through local tourist offices.
