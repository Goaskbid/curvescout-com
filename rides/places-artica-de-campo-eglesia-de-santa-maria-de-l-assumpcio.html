Why this ride

434 km from Alquézar to Alquézar, about 7 h 23 of riding. On the way it passes Cueva de Revilla, Eglésia de Santa Maria de l'Assumpció and back through Cueva de Revilla. It tops out around 1,850 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Pyrenees N260 Route

Cueva de Revilla is 106 km from Artica de Campo, mostly on A-138. It climbs 770 m to the top at 1,087 m and drops 360 m again. Fill the tank before the climb.

The stops

  1. 1. Artica de Campo

    Start here.

    Artica is the kind of small place name that appears on only the most detailed maps, and Artica de Campo, one and a half kilometres from Alquézar, is a field or small farm on the slope above the village. Its shelter is the closest after Cueva Palomera to the centre of Alquézar, and it belongs to the rock art of the Vero listed by UNESCO in 1998. The ride that features it also links to Navarra's beech forests at Aztaparreta, a long way to the west in the Pyrenees, which are a lovely place in October.

    Read the complete Artica de Campo write-up.

  2. 2. Cueva de Revilla

    106 km in.

    Revilla is one of the most spectacularly placed hamlets in the Spanish Pyrenees, perched on a ledge above the Garganta de Escuaín, a deep gorge cut into the limestone of the Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park. It looks down into the canyon from a terrace of meadows, with the high peaks behind. The Cueva de Revilla is a cave in the cliffs near the hamlet, and the area between Bielsa, Plan and Boltaña is some of the wildest country in Aragón.

    Read the complete Cueva de Revilla write-up.

  3. 3. Eglésia de Santa Maria de l'Assumpció

    219 km in.

    Valleys in the high Pyrenees of Catalonia and Aragon are full of small Romanesque churches, and the area around the Alta Ribagorça is among the richest. They were built in the eleventh and twelfth centuries by craftsmen working in what is called the Lombard style, with plain stone walls, round arches and tall, slender bell towers decorated with blind arcades, and they stand in villages that often have only a few houses.
