The stops

  1. 1. Perito Moreno town

    Start here.

    Perito Moreno is a town of about three and a half thousand people in the north of Santa Cruz province, and it is not to be confused with the famous glacier of the same name far to the south. It sits on the high Patagonian steppe, near the Chilean border, where the land is dry and windswept and the Andes appear as a distant blue wall. The name honours Francisco Moreno, the explorer and scientist known as the Perito, or expert, who surveyed the region in the late nineteenth century and helped settle the border question with Chile.

  2. 2. Los Antiguos

    The end: 59 km.

    Los Antiguos is the improbable orchard of Patagonia. The town of about two thousand people sits on the south shore of Lago Buenos Aires, a huge, wind-chopped lake that the Chileans call General Carrera, and the microclimate here, sheltered by the surrounding mountains and warmed by the water, allows cherries, apricots and berries to ripen where the rest of the province is bare steppe. Every January the town celebrates the cherry harvest with a festival, and in summer, fruit stalls line the roadside. The name means the ancient ones, a reference to the Tehuelche people who once lived and hunted here.
