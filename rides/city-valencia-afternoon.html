Why this ride

From Valencia it runs 172 km to Valencia; allow about 2 h 58 of riding. The stops in between: Covacha del Barranc del Diable, Covacha Picayo- abrigo l, Covacha Picayo- abrigo II and Barranc de la falaguera- Abric I.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Valencia

    Valencia has a river that is no longer a river. After a catastrophic flood in 1957, the Turia was diverted around the south of the city, and the old riverbed, which runs through the centre in a long curve, was turned into a park, a green ribbon of nearly nine kilometres filled with gardens, sports fields, bridges and playgrounds. It is one of the best urban parks in Europe, and it ends at the City of Arts and Sciences, the fantastic white complex of buildings and pools designed by the local architect Santiago Calatrava, which looks like a fleet of spaceships moored in a lake.

  2. 2. Covacha del Barranc del Diable

    Puçol is a modest, busy town on the coast north of Valencia, famous locally for its orange groves, its beach and its long sandy shore. Inland from it the land rises quickly into the Sierra Calderona, a low range of pine, rosemary and dry gullies. The Barranc del Diable, the Devil's Ravine, is one of many small ravines cut into the foothills, and the covacha, a shallow cave, holds a few rock paintings from the Levantine tradition. These belong to the Mediterranean-arc rock art UNESCO listed in 1998, and they are typically small, red and easy to miss unless a guide shows you where to look.

  3. 3. Covacha Picayo- abrigo l

    Hannibal is the name that should come to mind as you ride toward this part of the Valencian coast. In 219 BC the Carthaginian general besieged the Iberian city of Saguntum, now Sagunto, just north of Puçol, and the eight-month siege that followed set off the Second Punic War. The city fell, and Rome declared war. The castle that crowns the hill at Sagunto today is a long, thin fortress with Roman, Moorish and medieval layers, and its Roman theatre has been restored to a degree that some people find controversial but nobody finds dull.

  4. 4. Covacha Picayo- abrigo II

    The Sierra Calderona is the wooded ridge behind Valencia's northern suburbs, and it is the best surprise on this coast. It is a natural park of pine, holm oak and rosemary, with low summits, deep ravines and a sense of space that is hard to believe so close to a large city. Wild boar wander the slopes, along with foxes, genets and a growing population of birds of prey, and in spring the slopes are full of orchids and cistus flowers.

  5. 5. Barranc de la falaguera- Abric I

    Alginet sits in the Ribera Alta, the flat, fertile plain south of Valencia where the Júcar river spreads out among orange groves, rice fields and small towns. To the west, the plain meets the first hills of the interior, low ridges of limestone covered in pine and scrub. The Barranc de la Falaguera, the ravine of the fern, cuts into these hills near Alginet, Alfarp and Benifaió.
