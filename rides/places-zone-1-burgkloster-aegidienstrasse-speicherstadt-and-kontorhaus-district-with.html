  1. 1. Zone 1: Burgkloster - Aegidienstrasse

    Lübeck's old town is an island, wrapped in the arms of the river Trave and its canal, and you cross water to reach almost every part of it. The island was the capital of the Hanseatic League, the trading alliance of north German and Baltic cities that dominated the sea for centuries, and the wealth of those merchants is visible in the seven great church spires that fill the skyline. UNESCO listed the old town in 1987, and unlike many cities, it has the feel of a place still in use rather than a museum.

  2. 2. Wismar

    Wismar is a red brick town on the Baltic, on a sheltered bay in Mecklenburg, and its story is that of the Hanseatic League, the medieval trading network of North German cities that dominated commerce in the Baltic and the North Sea. The town grew rich on beer, grain and the herring trade, and put its wealth into huge Gothic churches in dark brick, with towers that serve as landmarks at sea. Together with Stralsund it was listed by UNESCO in 2002 for the quality of its Hanseatic architecture, and the market square, one of the largest in northern Germany, is the heart of the place, with gabled houses of many centuries and a curious twelve-sided fountain house, the Wasserkunst, built in the Dutch Renaissance style in the early seventeenth century.

  3. 3. Speicherstadt and Kontorhaus District with

    There is nowhere in Europe quite like the Speicherstadt, a whole district of warehouses built of dark red brick, rising straight out of the canals as if the water were a street. The buildings date from the 1880s, when Hamburg joined the German customs union and needed a place to store goods outside the customs zone, and they stand on thousands of oak piles driven into the soft mud. Their neo-Gothic gables, turrets and copper roofs make them look like a row of castles, and the streets between them are so narrow that you can touch both sides with outstretched hands. UNESCO recognised the district in 2015, together with the neighbouring Kontorhaus quarter.
