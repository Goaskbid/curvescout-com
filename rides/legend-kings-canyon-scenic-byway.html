Why this ride

National Scenic Byway. sequoias, deep canyon, Sierra Nevada. A one-way ride of 101 km, Fresno to the finish - roughly 1 h 50 in the saddle. The highest point is about 3,000 m. The line favours views over speed.

Scenic road chapters

Kings Canyon Scenic Byway

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 25 km this is Kings Canyon Scenic Byway, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fresno

    Fresno is the biggest city of California's Central Valley, a flat, sunburnt metropolis of over half a million people surrounded by some of the most productive farmland in the world. Raisins, almonds, grapes, figs, peaches and oranges all come from this valley, and the city was once the raisin capital of the world, a distinction it still takes some pride in. In summer it can be brutally hot, with temperatures above forty degrees Celsius for days at a time, while in winter, a thick ground fog known as tule fog sometimes hangs over the roads for days.

  2. 2. Kings Canyon Scenic Byway point 2

    The climb from the San Joaquin Valley up Highway 180 is a lesson in how quickly California changes. You leave orange groves and dusty fields near Squaw Valley, wind up through oak and chaparral, and within an hour you are riding among sugar pines and giant sequoias at well over 1,800 metres.

  3. 3. Kings Canyon Scenic Byway point 3

    From Junction View the ground simply falls away. Highway 180 tips over the rim of the South Fork of the Kings River and begins one of the great descents in the American West, dropping well over a thousand metres in a long series of ledges and bends cut into the canyon wall. Below you the river is a thin white thread, and across the gorge the slopes rise in grey and rust-coloured rock to peaks that hold snow into summer. Kings Canyon is among the deepest canyons in North America, deeper in places than the Grand Canyon, though it hides it well behind its curves.

  4. 4. Kings Canyon Scenic Byway point 4

    Highway 180 ends where the glaciers stopped. At Road's End, a few kilometres past Cedar Grove, the tarmac gives up at the foot of sheer granite walls, and from here on the only way forward is on foot or horseback into the backcountry of the Sierra. Turning a motorcycle around at the end of a road this good feels wrong, but the canyon floor is reason enough to linger.
