Why this ride

Out of Salvador and back again: 744 km, roughly 12 h in the saddle. On the way it passes Morro do Pai Inacio, Lencois, Mucuge and Ibicoara. The highest point is about 1,000 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Chapada Diamantina Loop

Expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast. For 15 km this is Chapada Diamantina Loop, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Salvador

    Salvador is the oldest city in Brazil in the sense that matters, because it was the first capital, founded by the Portuguese in 1549 and ruling the colony until 1763. It sits on a peninsula at the mouth of the Baía de Todos os Santos, a big bay with islands, and its old centre, the Pelourinho, is a hillside of colourful colonial houses, baroque churches and cobbled streets, listed by UNESCO in 1985. The name Pelourinho means pillory, which tells you something about what once happened in the square.

  2. 2. Morro do Pai Inacio

    Morro do Pai Inácio is a flat-topped hill that rises out of the scrub of the Chapada Diamantina in the interior of Bahia, standing a little above a thousand metres. It is the sort of place where the earth seems to have been cut and left. The sandstone plateaus of this region, called chapadas, are ancient, eroded into cliffs, canyons and tabletop hills, and the Morro is one of the most photographed. From the top, a short, steep walk of perhaps fifteen minutes from the road, you look out over the valley of the Rio Utinga and the plateaus beyond, in a view that stretches to the horizon.

  3. 3. Lencois

    Lençóis is the town that the diamond rush built, and it still looks like it. In the 1840s prospectors poured into this corner of Bahia, and the name is said to come from the white tents they pitched along the river, which from the hills looked like sheets laid out to dry. The stone houses along the cobbled lanes are a leftover from that money, painted in faded colours, with a river running clear and tea-brown just below the square, stained by the plants upstream rather than by anything dirty.

  4. 4. Mucuge

    Mucugê has a cemetery that nobody expects. Walk up from the main street and you find the so-called Byzantine cemetery, a field of small white chapel-shaped tombs on a dusty hillside, a legacy of the diamond miners who made this town rich in the nineteenth century and then left it half-empty when the stones ran out. It is a quiet, slightly eerie place at the end of a long day on the road.

  5. 5. Ibicoara

    Ibicoara is a small town at the southern end of the Chapada Diamantina, in the highlands of Bahia, far from the more touristy centre around Lençóis. The town sits at a cool altitude, which makes it good for growing coffee, and the hillsides around are covered with plantations, as well as potatoes and other vegetables. It is known for its waterfalls, in particular the Cachoeira do Buracão, an eighty-five-metre cascade that drops into a great rock amphitheatre, reached by a walk and a swim across a pool, and often described as one of the most beautiful in the region.
