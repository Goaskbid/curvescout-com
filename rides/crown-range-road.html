Why this ride

Out of Queenstown, one way: 70 km, roughly 1 h 09 in the saddle. On the way it passes Crown Terrace, Crown Range Summit and Cardrona. The highest point is about 1,100 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Crown Range Road

The valley floor is behind you and the road starts working for its height at about 10.9%. Corners stack above one another where the ground allows nothing straighter — first and second gear more often than not. Look through each corner to the next one and let the engine do the holding.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Queenstown

    The town sits on the shore of Lake Wakatipu, a long, dark, glacier-carved lake shaped like a lightning bolt, with the Remarkables mountain range rising straight from the far side in a wall of grey rock and snow. A Māori legend says the lake is the burnt outline of a giant, Matau, whose heart still beats under the water, and the lake level does rise and fall in a slow rhythm, a seiche caused by wind and pressure, which is the scientific explanation for the legend. The old coal-fired steamship TSS Earnslaw, launched in 1912, still crosses the lake to a high-country farm.

  2. 2. Crown Terrace

    Crown Terrace is a flat shelf of farmland high above the Arrow River, a wide green apron tipped against the Crown Range, and the first place on the road up from Queenstown where you can breathe out. Behind you the Wakatipu basin falls away to the lake and the zigzag of the Remarkables. Ahead, the tussock-covered slopes of the range rise in folds, brown in summer and white in winter. Sheep, cattle and deer graze the paddocks, and the roadside fences are lined with hawthorn, poplars and old stone walls. On a clear, still morning the light here is brilliant, and the air smells of cut grass and dry earth.

  3. 3. Crown Range Summit

    The Crown Range summit reaches 1,121 metres, which makes this the highest paved main road in New Zealand, and it earns the title with a steep, twisting climb out of the Wakatipu basin. From the lookout at the top, the whole country seems to fall away: to the south, the Wakatipu basin and the jagged line of the Remarkables, and to the north the bare brown folds of the Cardrona valley, with Wanaka's lake beyond. The land is tussock and rock, treeless and bleached by wind, and the sky is enormous. Snow lies on the tops from May to October, and sometimes lingers into December.

  4. 4. Cardrona

    Cardrona is a handful of buildings in a bare valley below the Crown Range, and it is one of the best-preserved relics of the Otago gold rush. In the 1860s the valley held thousands of people, with stores, hotels, a school and a brewery, all of them chasing gold in the river gravels. The rush faded, the crowds left, and the town shrank to almost nothing. What remains is the Cardrona Hotel, a white wooden building with a verandah that has stood since 1863 and still serves meals and drinks, along with a few old buildings and the remains of the diggings. The place has the unmistakable feel of a ghost town that has been revived, and the hotel's open fire is welcome on a cold day.

  5. 5. Wanaka

    Wānaka is a lakeside town that grew up without quite deciding to. A few thousand people live here, on the southern shore of a long glacial lake, with mountains in every direction and the white bulk of the Southern Alps closing the northern horizon. The great peak of Mount Aspiring, nearly three thousand metres high, is a pyramid you may glimpse on a clear day beyond the lake, and it is a rewarding sight after the climb from Queenstown.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

Queenstown to Crown Terrace

Queenstown → Crown Terrace is 16 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Crown Terrace to Crown Range Summit

Crown Terrace → Crown Range Summit climbs about 1121 m in 10 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Crown Range Summit to Cardrona

Crown Range Summit → Cardrona drops about 1121 m over 16 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Cardrona to Wanaka

Cardrona → Wanaka is 31 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
