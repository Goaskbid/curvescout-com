Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sofia

    Sofia sits under a mountain, and that is the first thing to understand about it. Vitosha rises right behind the southern suburbs, a broad, dome-shaped massif topping out at Cherni Vrah, around 2,290 metres, and on a clear morning the snow is visible from the boulevards. People ski there in winter and hike in summer, and the city's residents treat it like a back garden. The capital of Bulgaria is a plain, solid place of about one and a quarter million, with Soviet-era blocks, Ottoman remnants and an unexpected number of small parks.

  2. 2. Lakatnik

    The Iskar is the only river that saws right through the Balkan range, and at Lakatnik it does it in style. On the eastern side of the gorge a wall of grey limestone rises almost straight up from the water, pocked with caves and streaked with lichen, and the railway, the road and the river squeeze side by side through the gap at its foot. Lakatnik itself is little more than a railway halt and a scattering of houses; the Lakatnik Rocks are the reason to stop, and on a summer weekend you will see climbers hanging off them like spiders.

  3. 3. Mezdra

    Mezdra sits right where the Iskar escapes from the mountains and starts to wander north across the plain toward the Danube, and its whole reason for existing is that position. It grew as a railway junction at the northern mouth of the gorge, where lines from Sofia split toward Ruse and Varna and toward the north-west, and it still has the workaday feel of a place that people pass through. Do not let that put you off: the rocky hill in the middle of town hides one of the more interesting small sites in this part of Bulgaria.

  4. 4. Vratsa

    Vratsa's backdrop does all the talking. Behind the town, the Vrachanski Balkan rises in a sudden wall of limestone, and just above the last houses the Leva river has cut the Vratsata gorge, a slot between cliffs some four hundred metres high that are among the tallest in the Balkans. The town spreads out at their foot, a regional centre with a busy pedestrian heart and the usual mix of communist blocks and older houses, and every street seems to end with a view of rock.
