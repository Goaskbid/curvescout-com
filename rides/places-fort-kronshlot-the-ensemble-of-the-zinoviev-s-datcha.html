Why this ride

From Kronstadt it runs 385 km to Kronstadt; allow about 6 h 38 of riding. The stops in between: Primorskoe Highway, Fort «Seraya Loshad», Krasnoe Selo and The Ensemble of the Zinoviev's Datcha. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Fort «Kronshlot»

    Fort Kronshlot is a stone fortress sitting on an artificial island in the middle of the water, and it has been there, in one form or another, since 1704. Peter the Great ordered it built to guard the shallow channel to the new city of Saint Petersburg, and the first version was a wooden tower on piles driven into the seabed and filled with stone. The name comes from the German for crown castle. The current structure is mostly of the nineteenth century, a long oval of brick and granite with gun casemates facing the water.

  2. 2. Primorskoe Highway

    Zelenogorsk used to be Terijoki, a Finnish resort town on the Gulf of Finland, and it belonged to Finland until the Winter War of 1939 to 1940, when this stretch of the Karelian Isthmus passed to the Soviet Union. Before that, Terijoki was a place where Russian aristocrats and artists kept summer houses, and the wooden villas that survive among the pines still have some of that air. The Primorskoe highway runs north-west from Saint Petersburg along the shore to Vyborg, and this is the part of it where the road threads a sandy, forested coastal strip with dunes on one side and wet pinewoods on the other.

  3. 3. Fort «Seraya Loshad»

    Along the southern shore of the Gulf of Finland, west of Saint Petersburg, a low strip of pine forest and sand runs for kilometres between the road and the sea. Fort Seraya Loshad, the Grey Horse, stands in this coastal country, eleven kilometres from the village of Lebyazhye, among a group of forts built for the defence of the capital and the naval base at Kronstadt.

  4. 4. Krasnoe Selo

    Readers of Anna Karenina will know Krasnoye Selo, even if they have never been within a thousand kilometres of it. It was here, at the summer camp of the imperial guards, that Tolstoy set the officers' steeplechase in which Vronsky rides his mare Frou-Frou to disaster in front of the court. For more than a century, from the early nineteenth century until the revolution, the guards regiments of the Russian army gathered each summer in this area south-west of Saint Petersburg for manoeuvres, parades and horse races, and the tsar and the court came to watch. It is listed as one of the monuments associated with the historic centre of Saint Petersburg.

  5. 5. The Ensemble of the Zinoviev's Datcha

    Here the Neva is a working river, broad and fast, carrying barges and the outflow of Lake Ladoga past factories and housing blocks on its way to the city. Yet on the left bank near Ust-Izhora, among the industry, survive the traces of a much older riverside life: the country houses, datchas in the old aristocratic sense, that Saint Petersburg's wealthy families built along the Neva in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, with parks running down to the water. The ensemble of the Zinoviev datcha is one of them, included in the long list of estates, parks and buildings that make up the World Heritage Site around the historic centre of Saint Petersburg.
