Why this ride

From Naranjal it runs 795 km to Naranjal; allow about 17 h of riding. On the way it passes Segment Santa Martha - Santa Martha, Segment Mamamag - Mamamag, Segment Cerro de Cojitambo and Subsection Achupallas - Ingapirca. It tops out around 3,750 m. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Avenue of the Volcanoes

The 60 km between Segment Mamamag - Mamamag and Segment Cerro de Cojitambo on Autopista Cuenca Azogues. Mostly downhill - about 1590 m down, from a high point of 3,979 m. Cold up there even in July - pack a warm layer and check it is open.

The stops

  1. 1. Segment Botija Paqui - Botija Paqui

    Start here.

    Naranjal is the opposite of the high Andes. Down on the coastal plain of southern Ecuador, the air is hot and humid, the land is green and flat, and the farms grow bananas, cacao and, increasingly, shrimp in ponds along the estuaries. Yet the same Inca road system that crosses the mountains near Cuenca came down to this coast, and the segment at Botija Paqui is a stretch of it that UNESCO lists among the Qhapaq Ñan sites. The road was both a highway and a statement, linking distant provinces to the empire's heart.

    Read the complete Segment Botija Paqui - Botija Paqui write-up.

  2. 2. Segment Santa Martha - Santa Martha

    79 km in.

    If Mamamag is the top of the climb, Santa Martha is the other end of the story: the foothills where the Andes tip into the coastal plain, thirty-odd kilometres from Naranjal and a long way from anywhere that feels like highland. The air is thicker and wetter here, and the vegetation changes from tussock grass to ferns, bamboo and the dense green of the western slopes.

    Read the complete Segment Santa Martha - Santa Martha write-up.

  3. 3. Segment Mamamag - Mamamag

    189 km in.

    West of Cuenca the Andes lift quickly, and the Mamamag label sits on the edge of that rise, about twenty-four kilometres from the city, where the cultivated valleys give way to cold, open páramo. The nearest real town is Cuenca itself, a university city of cobbled colonial streets at roughly two and a half thousand metres, and anything you need, from fuel to a decent soup, you should sort out there before you climb.

    Read the complete Segment Mamamag - Mamamag write-up.

  4. 4. Segment Cerro de Cojitambo

    249 km in.

    Cojitambo is a rocky hill that rises abruptly above the valley of Azogues, in the province of Cañar, in the southern Andes of Ecuador. Its summit and slopes hold the remains of a Cañari and Inca settlement, with walls, terraces and stairways, and the Inca road ran past it, a segment of the Qhapaq Ñan listed by UNESCO. The Cañari were the people of this region before the Inca conquest, and they resisted fiercely, later siding with the Spanish against their Inca overlords. Today the hill is also a popular spot for rock climbing, with routes on its cliffs.

    Read the complete Segment Cerro de Cojitambo write-up.

  5. 5. Subsection Achupallas - Ingapirca

    398 km in.

    Achupallas is a high village in Chimborazo province, about sixteen kilometres from Alausí, and the walking trail named after it, which runs south towards Ingapirca, is one of Ecuador's best-known treks. For motorcyclists the point is the landscape and the road beside it: bare páramo at over three thousand metres, with the volcanic cone of Sangay on a clear day, and little else but sheep, lupins and the odd shepherd's hut.

    Read the complete Subsection Achupallas - Ingapirca write-up.
