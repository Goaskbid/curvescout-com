Why this ride

A 186 km round trip from Albocàsser - roughly 3 h 12 in the saddle. The stops in between: Cova Remigia- Abric l, Barranco de Gibert II, Abrigo del Arenal de la fonseca ó Abrigo d and back through Barranco de Gibert II. The highest point is about 1,400 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. La Saltadora- Abric I

    Albocàsser sits on a limestone shelf in the northern Maestrat, a farming village of about fourteen hundred people with a stone church, a market-day bustle and almond trees all around. A few kilometres from it, the Gasulla ravine and its neighbours hold Levantine rock paintings, and La Saltadora, the Jumper, is one of the shelters, named perhaps for a leaping figure or a nimble ledge. The Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula was added to the UNESCO list in 1998, and Abric I is a small but significant piece of that story.

  2. 2. Cova Remigia- Abric l

    Ares del Maestrat hangs on a rocky crest in northern Castellón, one of the highest villages in the region, with a ruined castle above and a grid of narrow, stone-paved streets below. A few kilometres away the Gasulla ravine cuts a deep, wild line through the limestone, and its cliffs hold some of the great shelters of Levantine rock art. Cova Remigia, which has a number of separate shelters, or abrics, is among the best-known, with dozens of figures of archers, deer and ibex, many of them captured in mid-run. The Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula has been on the UNESCO list since 1998.

  3. 3. Barranco de Gibert II

    Up on the roof of the Maestrazgo, where Teruel and Castellón meet, the land rises above fourteen hundred metres into a bleak, beautiful plateau of pasture, pine and limestone. Mosqueruela, on the Aragonese side, is one of the highest villages in the area, and Vilafranca, on the Valencian side, sits in a valley below. The Barranco de Gibert cuts into the hills between them, close also to La Iglesuela del Cid, and its second shelter holds prehistoric paintings.

  4. 4. Abrigo del Arenal de la fonseca ó Abrigo d

    The Guadalope river cuts a deep canyon through the limestone of the Maestrazgo in eastern Teruel, and the Abrigo del Arenal de la Fonseca lies in this wild country near the hamlet of Ladruñán, between the villages of Castellote and Cantavieja. It is one of the important rock-art shelters of Aragon, part of the UNESCO listing of Mediterranean prehistoric art, with Levantine paintings of animals and human figures in red pigment on the rock. The setting is spectacular: a narrow gorge with towering cliffs, the river flowing below, and pine and holm oak clinging to the slopes. Access involves a walk, and the paintings are protected by a fence.
