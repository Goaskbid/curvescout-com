Why this ride

164 km from Estada to Estada, about 2 h 50 of riding. The stops in between: Muriecho E1, Abrigo de Arilla, Muriecho L and Mascún V. It tops out around 1,050 m.

The stops

  1. 1. El Remosillo

    Start here.

    If you ride north from Barbastro toward Graus, the Ésera river eventually forces its way through a ridge of limestone, and the result is the Congosto de Olvena, a short, deep gorge with the road squeezed between rock and water. Up on the cliffs around here are rock shelters with prehistoric paintings, and El Remosillo is one of the best known in this corner of Aragón, between Estadilla, Graus and Fonz.

    Read the complete El Remosillo write-up.

  2. 2. Muriecho E1

    About 29 km in.

    The Sierra de Guara is the first big step of the Pyrenean foothills, a tumbled mass of limestone that has been cut into canyons so deep and narrow that people come from all over Europe to descend them with ropes. Muriecho is a tiny place on the southern fringe of the range, in the Somontano, the land at the foot of the mountain, and E1 is one numbered shelter of several recorded around it. The paintings belong to the Mediterranean-arc rock art that UNESCO listed in 1998, and this region of Huesca has some of the best examples in Aragon.

    Read the complete Muriecho E1 write-up.

  3. 3. Abrigo de Arilla

    About 46 km in.

    The Sierra de Guara is the southern edge of the Pyrenees, a rough range of sandstone and limestone where rivers have cut deep, dark canyons that are among the best in Europe for canyoning. The Abrigo de Arilla lies in that country, well off the main roads, in the lonely hills between Alquézar and the Sierra, and is one of the rock shelters of the Río Vero cultural park. The paintings are mostly Levantine and schematic, thousands of years old, and are part of the Mediterranean Basin rock art that UNESCO listed in 1998. Reaching the shelters generally requires a walk, and regulations apply.

    Read the complete Abrigo de Arilla write-up.

  4. 4. Muriecho L

    About 63 km in.

    The Somontano, the land at the foot of the mountains, is the soft belt of the Huesca province, where the high Pyrenees give way to rolling hills of vines, almonds and olives, and the Sierra de Guara rises like a wall to the north. Muriecho is a hamlet in that country, and the shelter that carries its name, Muriecho L, lies about five kilometres from Alquézar. It holds painting in the Levantine style, protected by UNESCO as part of the rock art of the Mediterranean basin in Spain, and the ride links it with El Remosillo and Mascún V, other shelters in the canyons of the Guara.

    Read the complete Muriecho L write-up.

  5. 5. Mascún V

    About 83 km in.

    The Barranco de Mascún is one of the great canyons of the Sierra de Guara, a limestone range in the pre-Pyrenees of Huesca, where rivers have carved deep, narrow gorges, caves and towers into the rock. The canyon, near the village of Rodellar, is famous among climbers for its overhanging walls and among canyoners for its pools and waterfalls, and it also holds traces of prehistoric painting in its shelters. Mascún V is one of these sites, part of the group of Mediterranean rock art sites listed by UNESCO, and the setting, with sheer orange and grey walls rising above the river, is extraordinary.

    Read the complete Mascún V write-up.
