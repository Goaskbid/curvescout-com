Why this ride

  1. 1. Palisades Parkway north

    Riverdale sits at the northern tip of the Bronx, where New York City presses up against the Hudson and the Palisades rise across the water, and it is probably the greenest and quietest corner of the borough. Big old houses sit on a ridge above the river, and the Henry Hudson Parkway runs along the shore, an elegant, curving road from the 1930s built to give city families a pleasant drive.

  2. 2. Bear Mountain Bridge

    The Bear Mountain Bridge, opened in 1924, was the longest suspension bridge in the world at the time of its construction and the first to span the Hudson south of Albany. It leaps across the river at its narrowest and deepest, between the steep granite flanks of Bear Mountain on the west and Anthony's Nose on the east, and the setting is dramatic: dark cliffs, a wide gleaming river, forested hills layered one behind the other. A toll is collected, and motorcycles pay like everyone else.

  3. 3. Cold Spring food stop

    Cold Spring is the kind of Hudson river village that looks like a stage set, a single steep Main Street of antiques shops, cafés and brick storefronts running down to the river and a small bandstand on the waterfront. It has fewer than two thousand residents, and it fills each weekend with day-trippers from the city who arrive on the Metro-North train. Across the water rise the walls of West Point, and the Hudson narrows here between steep, wooded hills, a fjord-like stretch called the Highlands.

  4. 4. Storm King Highway

    Highway 218 cuts along the side of Storm King Mountain like a shelf, and few roads in New York State feel so dramatic. The route was blasted out of the cliff in the early twentieth century, a narrow ledge above the Hudson with a low stone parapet and a sheer drop to the river on one side and rock on the other. Opened in the early 1920s, it was among the first scenic roads built expressly for motorists, and for a rider the experience is intimate, twisting and slow, with the river shining far below.

  5. 5. Seven Lakes Drive

    Harriman State Park is the second largest park in New York, and Seven Lakes Drive is its backbone: a quiet, winding road across the Ramapo hills that most people in New York City never realize is an hour from Times Square. It was built in the 1920s as a parkway for pleasure driving, long before anyone thought of it as a motorcycle road, and it still has the character of its time, with stone culverts, gentle grades and no commercial vehicles allowed. The park exists thanks in part to Mary Harriman, who gave land in 1910 in memory of her husband, the railroad magnate, with the aim of keeping the Highlands from the quarries.

  8. 8. Near Woodbury, on the way back

    Central Valley is a small community in Orange County, New York, wedged between the Hudson Highlands and the New York State Thruway, and its claim to fame is that it sits beside Woodbury Common, one of the largest outlet shopping centres in the United States. On a weekend the shops draw tour buses from all over the region, and traffic backs up on the approach roads. Woodbury, the larger town about six kilometres away, has about eleven thousand people and plenty of fuel, supermarkets and diners.

  10. 10. Photo stop near Dayton

    Central New Jersey is not where anyone goes looking for mountain roads, and Dayton, part of South Brunswick township, makes no pretence otherwise. It sits in the flat country between New Brunswick and Princeton, crossed by Route 130, the New Jersey Turnpike and US 1, with warehouses, office parks, farms that have survived development and a lot of commuters. Think of this as a place to regroup on the way to the good stuff rather than a destination in itself.

  11. 11. Concord

    The Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge fills the sky over this part of Staten Island. Concord is a residential neighbourhood on the island's eastern side, a few streets back from the water and just inland from the bridge, a quiet mix of detached houses, small apartment blocks, churches and corner shops. When the bridge opened in 1964 it was the longest suspension span in the world, and it changed Staten Island forever, linking it by road to Brooklyn and turning a semi-rural borough into a commuter suburb almost overnight.

  12. 12. Sea Bright

    Sea Bright is barely a town at all in the geographic sense: a strip of sand a few hundred metres wide, with the Atlantic on one side, the Shrewsbury River on the other, and Ocean Avenue running down the middle. A great grey seawall shields the road from the ocean, and on a windy day you can feel the spray even though you cannot see the waves. Beach clubs, seafood bars and surf shops line the street, and in summer the whole place hums.

    The Hudson Highlands are a long way up the river, so think of this as the opposite end of the day: a quiet suburban finish after the mountain miles. It is a place that rewards slowness, with leafy streets, stone walls and the odd glimpse of an estate gate behind high hedges.
