Why this ride

A 82 km round trip from Gâlgău - roughly 1 h 25 in the saddle. The stops in between: Rogna - La Bontauă, Surduc - Deasupra Văii Hrăii and back through Rogna - La Bontauă. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Gâlgău - Șaua Dealul Arsurei

    A șa, in the language of the Carpathians, is a saddle, a low point between two summits that allows a road or a path to cross a ridge. Șaua Dealul Arsurei, the saddle of the burnt hill, lies near Gâlgău, a village of fewer than nine hundred people in the hills of Sălaj, and the name is a good example of how such crossings are remembered. Arsură means a burn or a scorched place. It might refer to a fire in the forest, or to the old practice of clearing woodland for pasture by burning it, which left a hillside bare and blackened.

  2. 2. Rogna - La Bontauă

    Rogna is a scatter of farms in the hills of northern Sălaj, close to the Someș valley and the border with Maramureș, and La Bontauă is the name the locals give to a piece of high ground where a Roman frontier site has been recorded. The northern edge of Roman Dacia ran through these hills, and the Romans placed watchtowers and small forts on high points to keep an eye on the river valleys and the free Dacians to the north. Today there is little to see apart from the shape of the land, but the view over the rolling hills makes it easy to imagine a sentry looking out across the valley.

  3. 3. Surduc - Deasupra Văii Hrăii

    Surduc is a village in the Someș valley in Sălaj county, north-western Romania, where the river cuts a narrow path through hills on its way toward Baia Mare. Above the village, on a ridge overlooking the valley of the Hrăii stream, there are traces of a Roman watchtower, part of the frontier of the province of Dacia. The nearby villages of Tihău and Băbeni, and the town of Jibou, are all close by.
