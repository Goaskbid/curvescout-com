Why this ride

248 km from Nerpio to Nerpio, about 4 h 17 of riding. It goes by way of Gabar, Cueva de la Plata and back through Gabar. The highest point is about 1,350 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cavidad IV

    Cavidad is the archaeologist's word for a hollow, and the numbered Cavidades near Nerpio are a series of small shelters scattered across the plateau, seven or eight kilometres from the village. Cavidad IV is on a route with the Cañada de la Cruz and Las Cazuelas, the Little Pots, a name that suggests rounded hollows weathered into the rock. They belong to the cluster of rock paintings that UNESCO listed in 1998 as part of the Mediterranean arc, and which in Nerpio includes some of the most varied figures in Spain.

  2. 2. Gabar

    High in the north of Almería province, far from the beaches most people associate with the name, the Sierra de María rises in grey limestone above a plateau of almond orchards and cereal fields. Gabar is a point in the hills between the villages of María and Vélez-Blanco, part of a cluster of rock-art shelters in the Los Vélez district. This corner of Spain is famous for one painted figure in particular: the Indalo, a little stick man holding an arc over his head, found in the Cueva de los Letreros just outside Vélez-Blanco. It has become the emblem of the whole province, painted on doors and sold as a lucky charm, which is a remarkable career for a prehistoric doodle.

  3. 3. Cueva de la Plata

    Aledo is a tiny village on a rocky spur in the hills of Murcia, a place that punches well above its weight in history. In the eleventh century, it was a fortress on the frontier between Christian Castile and the Muslim taifa kingdoms, and the siege of Aledo in 1088 was a major episode in the struggle for the peninsula. A tower still stands above the village. The Cueva de la Plata, the silver cave, lies in the hills nearby, between Aledo, Totana and Alhama de Murcia.
