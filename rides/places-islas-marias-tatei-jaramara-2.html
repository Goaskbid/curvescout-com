  1. 1. Islas Marias

    The Islas Marias lie about a hundred kilometres off the coast of Nayarit, in the Pacific, and for more than a century they were a federal prison colony, one of the most notorious in Mexico. The archipelago has four islands, of which Maria Madre is the largest, and the penal colony operated there from 1905 until it closed in 2019. The prisoners lived in a relatively open regime, working farms and workshops, and the writer Jose Revueltas, who spent time there in the 1930s, wrote about it with powerful effect. The islands are now designated as a nature reserve and a UNESCO biosphere reserve, with tourism slowly being developed.

  2. 2. Tatei Jaramara

    Tatei Haramara, Our Mother the Sea, is the Wixarika name for the Pacific, and the place sacred to the community lies on the coast of Nayarit, near San Blas. Pilgrims walk or travel from the mountains each year to make offerings to the sea goddess, who brings rain, and the coast is part of the long ritual route that also includes the desert of Wirikuta. It is a working shoreline of beaches and rocky points, where the sea is strong and the surf can be heavy. Visitors who come here should remember they are guests at a sacred place and treat offerings and ceremonies with respect.
