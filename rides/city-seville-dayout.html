Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Seville

    The Ruta de la Plata sounds like a silver road, and generations of travellers have assumed it carried silver from the Americas. In fact the name probably comes from the Arabic word balata, meaning a paved way, and the road itself is much older: a Roman route running north from Seville to Mérida and on to Astorga, which pilgrims and drovers kept using for two thousand years. Seville is where it begins, and the city makes a fine place to start any ride.

  2. 2. Doñana National Park

    Where the Guadalquivir finally reaches the Atlantic, the river loses its way, spreading into marshes, dunes and pine woods that make up Doñana, one of the great wetlands of Europe. The park is a refuge for creatures you do not expect to see in southern Spain: the Iberian lynx, the world's most endangered cat, the Spanish imperial eagle, and in winter, hundreds of thousands of migrating geese and ducks. Flamingos feed in the shallows, and wild boar and red deer move through the cork oak woods.
