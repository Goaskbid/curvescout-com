Why this ride

389 km from Luang Prabang to Luang Prabang, about 6 h 42 of riding. It goes by way of Site 28, Site 52 and back through Site 28. The highest point is about 1,250 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Town of Luang Prabang

    Before the sun is properly up, a line of monks in saffron robes walks barefoot along the main street of Luang Prabang, and townspeople kneel on mats to put handfuls of sticky rice into their bowls. The alms round, tak bat, is the daily heartbeat of the town, and it has been going on for centuries. Luang Prabang sits on a peninsula where the Nam Khan river meets the Mekong, ringed by green mountains, and for several hundred years it was the royal capital of the Lao kingdom of Lan Xang. The old town, with its gilded temples, wooden houses and French colonial villas, was listed by UNESCO in 1995, and it is one of the best-preserved towns in Southeast Asia.

  2. 2. Site 28

    The Plain of Jars is one of the great archaeological puzzles of Southeast Asia: hundreds of carved stone jars, some taller than a person and weighing several tonnes, scattered across hillsides and meadows of the Xieng Khouang plateau in northern Laos.

  3. 3. Site 52

    On a grassy ridge in the highlands of Xieng Khouang Province in northern Laos, hundreds of huge stone jars sit scattered among the grass, some as tall as a person, some tipped on their sides and some leaning against each other. This is the Plain of Jars, and Site 52, also called Ban Phakeo, is the most dramatic of the places where the jars can be seen. It has several hundred jars spread over hillsides with wide views across the valley, and it is the one that most tour groups choose. UNESCO listed the Plain of Jars in 2019.
