Why this ride

From Heubach it runs 268 km to Heubach; allow about 4 h 17 of riding. The stops in between: Watchtowers WP 9/36 to 9/68, Maisons de la Weissenhof-Siedlung and back through Watchtowers WP 9/36 to 9/68. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Watchtowers WP 12/40 to 12/59

    Start here.

    The Romans called it the edge of the world they cared about, and in the Rems valley east of Schwäbisch Gmünd you can still trace where they drew the line. A string of watchtower sites, numbered by archaeologists along the stretch between Mögglingen, Heuchlingen and Böbingen, marks the course of the Raetian Limes, the frontier that guarded the province of Raetia in the second and third centuries. Here the frontier was a stone wall, which later locals, puzzled by the long ruined bank, called the Teufelsmauer, the Devil's Wall.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 12/40 to 12/59 write-up.

  2. 2. Watchtowers WP 9/36 to 9/68

    60 km in.

    Between Öhringen and Mainhardt, the Upper German Limes does something a Roman engineer must have been proud of: it runs dead straight for many kilometres, up hill and down dale, ignoring the terrain as if drawn with a ruler on a map. The watchtower sites numbered along this section lie on that line, in the Hohenlohe country of northern Württemberg, between the open farmland around Pfedelbach and the dark wooded hills of the Swabian-Franconian Forest.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 9/36 to 9/68 write-up.

  3. 3. Maisons de la Weissenhof-Siedlung

    133 km in.

    In 1927, on a hillside overlooking Stuttgart, a group of the most radical architects in Europe built an entire housing estate in a few months as an exhibition of what modern living could look like. The Weissenhof Estate, organised by the Deutscher Werkbund under the direction of Ludwig Mies van der Rohe, brought together Le Corbusier, Walter Gropius, Hans Scharoun, J.J.P. Oud and others, and their flat-roofed, white-walled houses became a manifesto for the International Style.
