Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Marienfelde

    Between 1953 and 1990, more than a million people who had fled or left East Germany passed through a camp in Marienfelde, in the far south of West Berlin. The Notaufnahmelager, the emergency reception centre, was where they were registered, questioned by Western intelligence services, housed and sent on to new lives in the West. Part of it is now a museum, with exhibits and personal stories that bring the divided city vividly to life, and it is one of the more moving places in Berlin to spend an hour.

  2. 2. High point near Spreenhagen

    Spreenhagen is a scattered rural municipality south-east of Berlin, where the River Spree winds through forests and meadows between Fürstenwalde and the capital. The villages here are small and quiet, with old churches, farmhouses and modern homes for commuters, and the landscape is a typical Brandenburg mix of pine forests, wet meadows and lakes.

  4. 4. Back to Spreewald Canals

    The Barnim plateau looks plain until you ride it. East of Bernau and north of Berlin, it is a broad swell of land left by the glaciers, with fields of rye and rapeseed rolling to the horizon, small woods of pine and oak, and tidy villages built around churches made of fieldstone, the rough granite boulders that the ice dropped across the whole region.
