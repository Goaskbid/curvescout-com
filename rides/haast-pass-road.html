Why this ride

The stops in between: Haast Pass, Blue Pools and Lake Hawea. The highest point is about 1,250 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Haast → Haast Pass

The ground tilts up and stays tilted for 74 km. 562 m of it, taken steadily rather than in one wall. A steady rhythm suits this better than bursts. The height arrives whether you hurry or not.

  1. 1. Haast

    Haast is the last outpost before the wilderness. For decades, it was cut off by rivers and forest, and the road north to Fox Glacier was only completed in 1965, making it the last link in the West Coast highway.

  2. 2. Haast Pass

    Haast Pass is the lowest crossing of the Southern Alps, at 562 metres, and the road over it carries State Highway 6 from the inland lakes of Otago to the wild West Coast. It is a gentle summit in a high, steep landscape, with beech forest on the slopes and snow peaks above, and it feels like a doorway between two worlds. On the east side, the land is dry and open, with tawny tussock and the pale blue of lakes. On the west side, the rain falls heavily, and the forest is dense, green and hung with moss, a temperate rainforest that reaches almost to the sea.

  3. 3. Blue Pools

    The Blue Pools are a spot on the Makarora river where meltwater from the Southern Alps runs clear and still over smooth, round stones, and the colour is something between sapphire and turquoise. A short walk of about fifteen minutes from the road leads through beech forest to a swing bridge across the river, from which you look down into deep, glassy pools where trout hang in the current. The water is so clear that the stones on the bottom seem within reach, and the colour changes with the light and the weather. After rain, the river turns a milky grey, and the pools lose their magic, so a dry spell is best.

  4. 4. Lake Hawea

    Lake Hawea is a deep glacial lake about thirty kilometres long, lying in a valley carved by ice between the dry hills of Central Otago, and the colour of it is the first thing you notice: a cold, dark blue, almost black in the middle, fading to emerald in the shallows. It sits just east of its twin, Lake Wanaka, separated from it by a narrow ridge called The Neck, and both were scooped out by the same great glaciers that once filled the valleys. The hills around are brown tussock and bare schist, with scattered willows and poplars along the shore, and in autumn the colours are gold and orange against the blue water.

    Read the complete Lake Hawea write-up.

  5. 5. Wanaka

    Wānaka is a lakeside town that grew up without quite deciding to. A few thousand people live here, on the southern shore of a long glacial lake, with mountains in every direction and the white bulk of the Southern Alps closing the northern horizon. The great peak of Mount Aspiring, nearly three thousand metres high, is a pyramid you may glimpse on a clear day beyond the lake, and it is a rewarding sight after the climb from Queenstown.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Haast to Haast Pass

Haast → Haast Pass climbs about 562 m in 44 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Haast Pass to Blue Pools

Haast Pass → Blue Pools drops about 562 m over 14 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Blue Pools to Lake Hawea

Blue Pools → Lake Hawea is 62 km of getting across. Ride it tidily and keep the enthusiasm for what comes after.

Lake Hawea to Wanaka

Lake Hawea → Wanaka is 17 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
