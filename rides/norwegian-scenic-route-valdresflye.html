Why this ride

A one-way ride of 169 km, Heggenes to Vågåmo - roughly 2 h 17 in the saddle. On the way it passes Valdresflye and Gjende. The highest point is about 1,500 m. Valdresflye is subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Norwegian Scenic Route Valdresflye

The valley floor is behind you and the road starts working for its height at about 7.1%. Corners stack above one another where the ground allows nothing straighter — first and second gear more often than not. Look through each corner to the next one and let the engine do the holding.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Garli

    Garli is a remote spot on the Valdresflye, the high plateau road between Valdres and Gudbrandsdalen, and it sits in a landscape of bare mountain, lake and marsh at about 1,000 metres. The Valdresflye is a National Tourist Route, and the road crosses the plateau through the Jotunheimen and Jotunheimen-adjacent country, with views of the great peaks to the north, including the Besseggen ridge and the Gjende lake. The road was built in the 1930s as a tourist route, and it is surprisingly smooth, with long gentle curves and a clear sense of space. Fagernes, over thirty kilometres away, is the nearest town, with fuel and services.

  2. 2. Valdresflye

    At 1,389 metres the Valdresflye is the roof of Fv51, a broad shoulder of bare mountain between Valdres and the Jotunheimen range, and the nearest thing southern Norway has to a lunar landscape. Nothing grows taller than your ankle. The ground is stone, lichen and cushions of moss, scattered with boulders left by the ice, and small lakes lie in the hollows, still holding snow at their edges well into summer. To the north rise the big peaks of Jotunheimen, the highest mountains in northern Europe, with Galdhøpiggen above 2,400 metres, and the line of them against the sky changes with every kilometre.

  3. 3. Gjende

    Gjende is a long, narrow lake in the heart of Jotunheimen, lying at about 984 metres between steep ridges, and it is famous for its colour. The water is a vivid green, almost milky, because it carries rock flour ground fine by glaciers upstream, and in sunlight it glows like a cut gemstone. Standing at the shore at Gjendesheim, where the road ends and the paths begin, you look down the lake between steep slopes, with the dark outline of the ridge rising on one side.

  4. 4. Vagamo

    Vågåmo is a village in the Gudbrandsdalen valley, in the middle of Norway's mountain heartland, and it sits beside the river Otta, with the Jotunheimen range to the west. The village has about fifteen hundred people, a church, a school, shops and a fuel station, and it is the place where the Valdresflye road comes down to meet the main valley. The Vågåvatnet lake lies just beside the village, and the farms stretch in lines along the hillsides. The name is thought to derive from an old word for a bay or inlet, and the lake was once much larger.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Garli to Valdresflye

Garli → Valdresflye climbs about 1389 m in 19 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Valdresflye to Gjende

Valdresflye → Gjende drops about 1389 m over 18 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Gjende to Vagamo

Gjende → Vagamo is 58 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
