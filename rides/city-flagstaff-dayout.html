Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Flagstaff

    Flagstaff stands at about two thousand one hundred metres, in a ponderosa forest at the foot of the San Francisco Peaks, and the air has the thin, clean taste of altitude. Humphreys Peak, the tallest mountain in Arizona at over 3,800 metres, is the old volcano that looms over the town, and in winter its slopes are white while the desert below is warm. That contrast is what makes Flagstaff a favourite for riders heading to the Grand Canyon: they start in cool pines and end in a gorge a mile deep.

  2. 2. Grand Canyon National Park

    The first thing the Grand Canyon does is refuse to make sense. You walk to the South Rim, a flat forest of pine and juniper at about two thousand one hundred metres, and the ground simply ends. Below is a gorge up to sixteen kilometres wide and a mile deep, a stack of red, buff and violet rock layers that record nearly two billion years, with the Colorado River a thin brown thread at the bottom. Photos flatten it, and nothing prepares you for the scale.
