  1. 1. Midelt

    Midelt is a market town on a high, windy plain between two mountain ranges, at about 1,500 metres, and it feels like a place that exists in order to be passed through. The Middle Atlas lies to the north and the High Atlas to the south, with the great bulk of Jbel Ayachi, close to 3,750 metres, rising to the south-west, often snow-capped into spring. The town has about forty-five thousand people, a long main street of shops, cafés and mechanics, and a surrounding landscape of orchards, mines and open steppe.

  2. 2. Imilchil

    Imilchil is a village at the end of the road in the best sense, a high settlement of the Aït Haddidou Berbers on a plateau at over 2,000 metres in the central High Atlas. It is known for two lakes nearby, Isli and Tislit, whose names mean groom and bride in Tamazight, and for the legend that explains them. According to the story, a young man and a young woman from feuding tribes fell in love, were forbidden to marry, and cried so much that their tears filled two lakes. The tale is the origin of the annual marriage festival, held in early autumn, when people from many tribes gather at a large market and young couples may choose partners.
