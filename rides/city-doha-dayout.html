Why this ride

The stops

  1. 1. Doha

    Start here.

    Doha is a young city on a flat, dry peninsula, and it grew almost from nothing in a lifetime. Fishing and pearling villages were what the coast held until oil and gas arrived, and in the decades since, the skyline of West Bay has risen along the curve of the harbour like a glass forest. The Corniche, a long waterfront promenade, is the place to see the contrast, with the towers on one side and the traditional wooden dhows drifting on the other. Across from the promenade, the Museum of Islamic Art stands on its own island, a pale, geometric building designed by I. M. Pei and opened in 2008, with collections that reach from Spain to India.

    Read the complete Doha write-up.

  2. 2. Al Zubarah Archaeological Site

    104 km in.

    Out on the north-west coast of Qatar, where the desert slides into a shallow turquoise sea, stand the low ruins of Al Zubarah, once one of the busiest pearling and trading towns in the Gulf. In the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the town thrived on pearls dived from the oyster beds offshore, traded on to India, Persia and beyond. Then it declined, was abandoned in the early twentieth century, and the sand covered it. Archaeologists have since uncovered streets, houses, a souk, mosques and a long town wall running in an arc around the old harbour. UNESCO inscribed the site in 2013.
