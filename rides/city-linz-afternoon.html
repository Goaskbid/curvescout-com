Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Linz

    Linz sits on the Danube like a working city that has learned to dress for dinner. For most of the twentieth century it was an industrial town, famous for its steelworks and chemical plants, and for being the place people drove through on the way to somewhere better. Then it reinvented itself as a centre of media art, with the Ars Electronica Center on the north bank and a string of modern buildings along the river, and in 2009 it was a European Capital of Culture. About two hundred thousand people live here, making it the third city of Austria.

  2. 2. Enns - Canabae Nordwest

    Enns claims to be the oldest town in Austria, and it has some evidence. The town received city rights in 1212, but people have lived here since long before that. The Romans built a legionary fortress on this spot, called Lauriacum, in the second century, and it was home to the Second Italic Legion, one of the units that guarded the Danube frontier. The civilian settlement that grew up outside the walls, known as the canabae, held traders, craftsmen and families, and the whole complex became a major provincial centre.

  3. 3. Enns - Gräberstraße

    Enns claims to be the oldest town in Austria, on the strength of a charter granted in 1212, but its roots go much deeper. Just outside the modern town lay Lauriacum, a Roman legionary fortress that housed the Second Italian Legion and grew into one of the most important places on the Danube frontier, with a civilian town, temples and, as the Gräberstraße name suggests, roads lined with cemeteries. The Romans buried their dead along the roads outside settlements, and excavations here have found hundreds of graves, stones and offerings. The site is part of the Danube Limes World Heritage listing.

  4. 4. Enns - St. Laurenz

    Enns claims to be the oldest town in Austria, and it has the paperwork to prove it. The town received its city rights in 1212, and long before that, the Romans built a legionary fortress here named Lauriacum, one of the major bases on the Danube frontier. A civilian settlement, the canabae, grew up outside its walls, and the church of St Laurenz stands on part of that ground, with a basilica that has Roman-era origins and layers of medieval rebuilding.

  5. 5. Enns - Canabae Nordost

    Enns calls itself the oldest town in Austria, and it has a very good case, since a Roman settlement stood here long before there was an Austria to speak of. Roman Lauriacum was a legionary base on the Danube frontier, and around the fort grew what the Romans called the canabae, a sprawling civilian settlement of traders, craftsmen, veterans' families, innkeepers and anyone else who made a living from the soldiers. This corner of the map marks the north-eastern end of that civilian quarter.
