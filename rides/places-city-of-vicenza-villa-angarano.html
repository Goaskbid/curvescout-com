Why this ride

83 km from Vicenza to Vicenza, about 1 h 26 of riding. It goes by way of Villa Forni Cerato, Villa Angarano and back through Villa Forni Cerato. The highest point is about 850 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. City of Vicenza

    Vicenza is Palladio's town, and almost everything worth seeing is a version of one man's idea of what a building should be. Andrea Palladio, born in Padua and trained as a stonemason, designed palaces, villas and churches in and around Vicenza in the sixteenth century, and his work, with its Roman columns, pediments and strict proportions, shaped architecture across the world, from English country houses to the White House. The city and the villas in its surrounding countryside are together a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and a walk down Corso Palladio feels like a stroll through an architectural textbook.

  2. 2. Villa Forni Cerato

    Not every Palladian villa is a palace. Villa Forni Cerato, in Montecchio Precalcino north of Vicenza, is a small, modest building, a cube with a simple loggia and pediment, built in the mid-sixteenth century for Girolamo Forni, a timber merchant and amateur painter. Its attribution to Palladio has been debated, but it is included in the UNESCO-listed City of Vicenza and the Palladian Villas of the Veneto, and its compact harmony shows how Palladio's ideas worked even at a small scale. It is privately owned and not always open, so check before planning a visit.

  3. 3. Villa Angarano

    Bassano del Grappa sits where the river Brenta leaves the mountains and spreads onto the Venetian plain, and its symbol is a covered wooden bridge, the Ponte Vecchio, designed by Palladio in the sixteenth century and rebuilt several times since after floods and wars. On the right bank of the river, just outside the town, stands Villa Angarano, a country house whose two long colonnaded wings were designed by Palladio, while the central residence was built later, in a different style, by the Venetian architect Baldassare Longhena. The villa is still a working wine estate and belongs to the UNESCO site of the City of Vicenza and the Palladian Villas of the Veneto.
