Why this ride

356 km from Sedro-Woolley to the finish, about 4 h 49 of riding. On the way it passes Diablo Lake and Washington Pass. The highest point is about 1,650 m. Washington Pass is subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Scenic road chapters

Diablo Lake → Washington Pass

The ground tilts up and stays tilted for 91 km. 1669 m of it, taken steadily rather than in one wall — a steady rhythm suits this better than bursts. The height arrives whether you hurry or not.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sedro Woolley

    Sedro-Woolley is two towns that stuck together. Sedro and Woolley were separate rivals on the Skagit River until they merged in 1898, and the double-barrelled name, with its hyphen, has stayed ever since. It is a working town of about eleven thousand people on the flat, green floodplain of the Skagit valley, in north-western Washington, with farms, sawmills, a timber heritage and a loggers' festival in July. For riders it is the start of the North Cascades Highway, and the last easy place to buy supplies.

  2. 2. Diablo Lake

    Diablo Lake is the colour that nobody believes in photographs. The water is a milky, luminous turquoise, as though someone has dissolved a gemstone in it, and it is not a trick of the light. Glaciers on the surrounding peaks grind the rock beneath them into a fine powder, rock flour, which washes down in meltwater and stays suspended in the lake, scattering blue and green light back to the surface. The effect is strongest in late summer, when the melt is at its peak and the sun is high.

  3. 3. Washington Pass

    At 1,669 metres, Washington Pass is the highest point on the North Cascades Highway, and from the overlook just beside the road you look straight across at a wall of grey granite that seems to have been designed by a rock climber. Liberty Bell Mountain stands out as a smooth, blunt dome, with the sharp spires of the Early Winters group beside it, and the whole group rises above a basin of scree, larch and bare rock. The overlook is reached by a short, paved path from the car park, with a viewing platform above a cliff, and it is one of the best easy views in the state.

  4. 4. Winthrop

    Winthrop has dressed up as the Wild West, and it does so with enthusiasm. The town sits where the Chewuch and Methow rivers meet, in a sunny valley on the dry eastern side of the North Cascades, and its main street is lined with wooden false fronts, boardwalks and hitching posts. In the 1970s, with the new highway about to bring tourists from Seattle, local businesses agreed on an Old West theme, and the plan worked. Today there are saloons, a general store, a small museum in an old log cabin, and at weekends a cowboy hat is not out of place.
