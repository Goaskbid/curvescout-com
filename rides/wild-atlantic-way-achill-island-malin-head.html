  1. 1. Achill Island

    Achill is Ireland's largest island, and you reach it by driving across a bridge, which takes a little of the romance away until you see the landscape. It lies off the coast of County Mayo, joined to the mainland at Achill Sound, and it is a rough, treeless land of heather bog, steep headlands and wide empty beaches. At Slievemore, the mountain that rises above the northern shore, a ruined village of stone cottages known as the deserted village stands on the slope, abandoned over generations after the Famine. At its foot, the winds sweep across the grass.

  2. 2. Slieve League

    Slieve League is one of the tallest sea cliffs in Europe, and it is a very different thing from the more famous cliffs on the other side of the country. The mountain drops about six hundred metres straight into the Atlantic, in bands of red, yellow and grey rock that look like a painter's palette in clear light. From the viewpoint at Bunglass, you look along a curtain of cliff that is far taller than anything you have seen on the coast of Clare, and the sound of the sea reaches you from a very long way down.

  3. 3. Malin Head

    The headland has a name that most people know without knowing why: Malin is one of the sea areas in the shipping forecast, and the weather reports for it are read out on radio across the British Isles. Standing here in a gale, you understand why.
