Why this ride

107 km from Albarracín to Albarracín, about 1 h 51 of riding. It goes by way of Barranco del Pajarejo, Torre de la iglesia del Salvador and back through Barranco del Pajarejo. The highest point is about 1,450 m. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abrigo de los Toros del Prado del Navazo

    Near the high plateau of the Sierra de Albarracín, a few kilometres from the red-walled town of Albarracín itself, there is a patch of pine woodland on a very particular rock. It is called rodeno, a russet sandstone that glows orange when the sun is low and has been carved by wind and water into tall fins, mushrooms and overhangs. The Abrigo de los Toros del Prado del Navazo is one of the painted shelters in these Pinares de Rodeno, and its bulls, cattle depicted in red, are part of the same body of Levantine and schematic art that UNESCO listed in 1998.

  2. 2. Barranco del Pajarejo

    Red rock, dark pines and a sky that seems higher than it should be: that is the country south of Albarracín, where the Barranco del Pajarejo cuts into the sandstone. Geologists call this stone rodeno, a coarse red sandstone that weathers into towers, overhangs and smooth shelters, and prehistoric people used those shelters as canvases. The Pinares de Rodeno protected landscape around Albarracín holds a well-known group of painted shelters with bulls, deer and human figures in white and red, and this gully is part of that wider area, quiet and seldom visited.

  3. 3. Torre de la iglesia del Salvador

    Teruel spent decades as the Spanish joke about forgotten provinces, until its people adopted the slogan Teruel existe, Teruel exists, and turned it into a political movement. Ride into the old centre and you will agree it exists rather vividly. The Torre del Salvador is one of its four great Mudéjar towers, built of brick and glazed ceramic in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries by craftsmen trained in the Islamic tradition. The road, or rather a street, still passes through an archway at its base, and you can climb inside, between the outer and inner shells of the tower, up narrow stairs to the bells.
