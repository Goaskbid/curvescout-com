Why this ride

A 254 km round trip from Zalău - roughly 4 h 23 in the saddle. It goes by way of Gâlgău - Valea Strâmturei, Groșii Țibleșului - Izvorul Șurii and back through Gâlgău - Valea Strâmturei. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Ortelec - Dealul Clocoțăl

    Clocotal is one of the low hills that crowd around Zalau, in northwestern Romania, and its name, Dealul Clocotal, simply means Clocotal Hill. Nothing marks it as a famous monument, and honesty is the best approach here: this is a stop in the country, and the interest is in the land itself. The hill is part of the rolling Salaj landscape, a region of wooded ridges, small valleys and farmland on the western flank of the Transylvanian basin, where the foothills of the Meses Mountains slope toward the Somes plain.

  2. 2. Gâlgău - Valea Strâmturei

    Strâmtură means a narrowing, and the name fits. Just outside Gâlgău, a small Sălaj village on the Someș, the valley pinches between wooded slopes, and the Roman frontier made use of exactly such pinch points to control movement along the river. The site listed as Valea Strâmturei belongs to that northern Dacian line, and like most of its neighbours it is earthwork and setting rather than standing masonry. You come for the geography and the sense of a border that ran through these quiet hills nearly two thousand years ago.

  3. 3. Groșii Țibleșului - Izvorul Șurii

    The Țibleș mountains rise like a dark wall between the Lăpuș country and the valleys of Maramureș, a volcanic range of dense spruce and beech forest topping out at nearly two thousand metres. On their slopes, near the source known as Izvorul Șurii above the village of Groșii Țibleșului, the forest feels almost untouched: springs bubbling out of mossy banks, fallen trunks covered in fungi, and a silence broken only by woodpeckers and the wind. This is bear country, and wolves and lynx roam these woods too. The area lies on the edge of the Maramureș region, famous for its wooden churches, carved gates and deeply traditional village life.
