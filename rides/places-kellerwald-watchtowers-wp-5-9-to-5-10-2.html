Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Kellerwald

    Beech trees cover the hills of the Kellerwald in north Hesse, and they are old ones. This is one of the places where UNESCO recognised the ancient beech forests of Germany, listed in 2011, because here the trees have been left largely alone, and the forest has the feel of what Central Europe looked like before farming took over. The trunks are smooth and grey, the canopy is high and the floor in spring is covered with a sheet of white wood anemones. In autumn, the leaves turn copper and gold, and the forest is full of light.

  2. 2. Watchtowers WP 4/45 to 4/52 including the

    North of Frankfurt, the Wetterau opens out as a wide, fertile basin between the Taunus and the Vogelsberg, and the Roman frontier made a long bulge around it to bring this rich farmland inside the empire. This section of the Upper Germanic Limes, with watchtowers WP 4/45 to 4/52, runs through the woods and fields between Langgöns and Lich, at the northern tip of that bulge. The towers are long gone, but traces of the wall and ditch can still be found in the forest.

  3. 3. Watchtowers WP 5/9 to 5/10

    The label gives only a pair of watchtower numbers, and the place itself is a swathe of flat country between Hanau and the Kinzig, where the suburbs of Erlensee, Rodenbach and Langenselbold have grown together into a continuous belt of houses, orchards and sports fields. In the second century the Roman frontier ran along this side of the Main plain before it turned south to the river, and the small towers, spaced at easy shouting distance, were the first line of warning. Nothing much remains above ground, and the nearby fort at Rückingen is known mostly from excavation. This is a stop for imagination rather than sightseeing.
