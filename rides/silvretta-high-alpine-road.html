Why this ride

Out of Bludenz, one way: 87 km, roughly 1 h 25 in the saddle. On the way it passes Partenen, Bielerhohe and Galtur. The highest point is about 2,300 m. Bielerhohe is subject to seasonal closure - check it is open before you set off. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Silvretta High Alpine Road

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 2032 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance. Cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bludenz

    Bludenz sits at the meeting of several valleys, and that is its whole story. The Walgau runs northwest toward the Rhine, the Klostertal heads east to the Arlberg, the Brandnertal climbs south to the Lünersee, and the Montafon carries the Ill river up to the Silvretta. At 588 metres, the town is a low point in a landscape of high peaks, with the Rätikon walls rising behind the old centre and a compact medieval core of arcades and painted houses. It is an ideal place to begin a ride into the mountains.

  2. 2. Partenen

    Partenen is the last proper village at the head of the Montafon, the long valley that runs south-east from Bludenz toward the Silvretta range. After here the valley narrows, the trees close in and the road starts to climb toward the high lakes. It is a small place, a scatter of farmhouses and guest houses along the Ill, with the mountains standing so close that the sun arrives late and leaves early.

  3. 3. Bielerhohe

    The Bielerhöhe is the high point of the Silvretta High Alpine Road, a broad saddle at 2,032 metres where Vorarlberg meets Tyrol and where the water changes its mind about where it wants to go. Rain that falls on the western side runs into the Ill and the Rhine, eventually reaching the North Sea; rain on the eastern side drains into the Trisanna, the Inn and the Danube, and finally the Black Sea. Standing at the top, with a lake beside you, it is satisfying to know you are on a European watershed.

  4. 4. Galtur

    Galtür stands at about 1,600 metres at the head of the Paznaun valley, and the first thing you notice is a concrete wall. It is long, curved and tall, and it was built after the avalanche of February 1999, when a wall of snow roared down into the village and killed thirty-one people. The wall now shields the centre, and a museum beside it, the Alpinarium, tells the story of the disaster and, more broadly, of living under snow. It is a serious place, and a thoughtful stop even for riders who normally only want coffee.

  5. 5. Landeck

    Landeck sits at the meeting of valleys in the Tyrolean Alps, where the Inn river swings from its long upper valley into a gorge and the Sanna comes tumbling down from the Arlberg side. At around 800 metres, it is a modest height for the Alps, but the mountains rise steeply on all sides, and the old castle on its rock above the town gives the place the look of a doorway. Burg Landeck dates from the thirteenth century and now houses a museum. The town has about 7,700 people, and the old centre is small, with painted houses and cafes.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

Bludenz to Partenen

Bludenz → Partenen is 34 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Partenen to Bielerhohe

Partenen → Bielerhohe climbs about 2032 m in 8 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Bielerhohe to Galtur

Bielerhohe → Galtur drops about 2032 m over 10 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Galtur to Landeck

Galtur → Landeck is 43 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
