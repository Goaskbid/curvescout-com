  1. 1. Tupiza

    The first thing that strikes you about Tupiza is the colour. The town sits in the south of Potosí department at about 3,000 metres, in a basin ringed by red and orange cliffs, pinnacles and canyons that wouldn't look out of place in the American Southwest, and the comparison is not accidental. Tupiza is the nearest town to the area where, according to the story most often told, the outlaws Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid met their end in 1908, after robbing a mine payroll in the district. The tale has been told in local shops and guesthouses ever since.

  2. 2. Villazon

    Villazón is where Bolivia ends, and it is the sort of border town that lives off crossing and trade. It sits in the south of Potosí department at about 3,400 metres, with only a bridge and a busy street separating it from La Quiaca, its Argentine twin. Together they form one noisy urban stretch, with porters pushing loaded carts across, minibuses grinding through the gears and vendors selling everything from phone covers to coca leaves.
