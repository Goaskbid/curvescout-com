Why this ride

Deals Gap is famous for a reason and infamous for another: 318 curves packed into eleven miles, heavily policed, and photographed by professionals at the bends. Ride it once for the story, then take the Cherohala Skyway back - longer, higher, emptier and arguably the better road.

  1. 1. Deals Gap

    Deals Gap is a place where a road made a reputation. US 129, which crosses the state line between North Carolina and Tennessee here, packs three hundred and eighteen curves into eleven miles, and riders from across the world come to try them. The road is known as US 129 at Deals Gap, and the nickname comes from the way it twists along the mountainside, with no junctions and hardly a straight section.

  2. 2. US 129 Deals Gap summit

    US 129 at Deals Gap is eleven miles of US 129 that has made a small place called Deals Gap famous around the world. On this short road, on the border between Tennessee and North Carolina, there are some three hundred curves, with no junctions and no driveways, squeezed between the Great Smoky Mountains and the Cheoah River gorge. For many motorcyclists, it is a pilgrimage, and the summit area is the place where riders gather to compare notes, buy a souvenir, and look at the wreckage of bikes that did not make it.

  3. 3. Tapoco / Cheoah Dam

    Cheoah Dam is a curved wall of concrete at the bottom of one of the most famous motorcycle roads in the world, and it is also, rather improbably, a film location. In the 1996 movie The Fugitive, Harrison Ford's character leaps from the top of a dam into the water below, and that dam is this one, high above the Cheoah River in the mountains of western North Carolina, close to the Tennessee line. You can see it from the road, and a surprising number of riders stop just to stand where the film crew stood.

  4. 4. Robbinsville

    Robbinsville is a quiet mountain county seat of fewer than six hundred people, and for a lot of riders it is the stop where a very good day gets summed up. It lies at about 620 metres in western North Carolina, in Graham County, one of the least populated corners of the state, and it makes a convenient place to finish or to start because it has fuel and a few places to eat, which the mountains around it do not.

  6. 6. Tellico Plains

    Tellico Plains sits where the Tellico River comes out of the Unaka Mountains into the broad valley of eastern Tennessee, at about two hundred and seventy metres, and it is the gateway to the Cherohala Skyway. The town is small, with a main street, a few cafes and a general air of being at the end of the road, which in a sense it is. Behind it the mountains of the Cherokee National Forest rise in wave after wave, and in front the valley opens toward Madisonville and the Tennessee River.
