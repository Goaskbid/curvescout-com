Why this ride

A 541 km round trip from Lebyazh’ye - roughly 8 h 52 in the saddle. The stops in between: Fort «Ino», Northern battery No. 4, Vyborgskaya Road and The Ensemble of the Zinoviev's Datcha. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

The 56 km between Fort «Ino» and Northern battery No. 4 on Приморское шоссе. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 0 and 33 m. Best in the early light, before the traffic.

The stops

  1. 1. Fort «Krasnaya Gorka»

    Start here.

    On the southern shore of the Gulf of Finland, near the village of Lebyazhye, the remains of Fort Krasnaya Gorka sit among pine woods and sand dunes. The fort was built in the years before the First World War as part of a chain of coastal defences protecting the approaches to St Petersburg, with heavy guns facing the sea and their counterparts on the opposite shore at Ino, in what was then the Grand Duchy of Finland. Together, the forts were designed to close the gulf to an enemy fleet.

    Read the complete Fort «Krasnaya Gorka» write-up.

  2. 2. Fort «Ino»

    105 km in.

    In pine woods on the northern shore of the Gulf of Finland, west of the resort strip of Zelenogorsk, lie the shattered concrete remains of Fort Ino. It was built from 1909 as part of the defences of Saint Petersburg, a key battery of Peter the Great's Naval Fortress, designed to close the gulf to enemy fleets with heavy guns. When Finland became independent, the fort ended up on Finnish territory, and under the Treaty of Tartu in 1920 its fortifications were to be dismantled, which is why what remains today is mostly blasted concrete, scattered among the trees and dunes.

    Read the complete Fort «Ino» write-up.

  3. 3. Northern battery No. 4

    161 km in.

    Kotlin Island lies in the Gulf of Finland, at the entrance to the Neva, and its job for three centuries has been to guard St Petersburg from the sea. Peter the Great founded the naval base of Kronstadt here in 1704, and the island, long and narrow, was ringed with forts, batteries and artificial islands. The northern batteries, of which No. 4 is one, belong to that long chain, built in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries as the guns grew bigger and the ships more dangerous. They are low, grass-covered earthworks and brick casemates facing the water, and on grey days the place has a lonely, military feel. The sea is shallow here and often frozen in winter.

    Read the complete Northern battery No. 4 write-up.

  4. 4. Vyborgskaya Road

    190 km in.

    Every road out of Saint Petersburg to the north-west ends up in Finland or in the memory of it, and the Vyborg highway is the old way. It leaves the city through the northern suburbs of Shuvalovo, Pargolovo and Parnas, passing parks, lakes and rows of tower blocks, and then continues over the Karelian Isthmus to Vyborg, a town that belonged to Finland until 1940.

    Read the complete Vyborgskaya Road write-up.

  5. 5. The Ensemble of the Zinoviev's Datcha

    230 km in.

    Here the Neva is a working river, broad and fast, carrying barges and the outflow of Lake Ladoga past factories and housing blocks on its way to the city. Yet on the left bank near Ust-Izhora, among the industry, survive the traces of a much older riverside life: the country houses, datchas in the old aristocratic sense, that Saint Petersburg's wealthy families built along the Neva in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, with parks running down to the water. The ensemble of the Zinoviev datcha is one of them, included in the long list of estates, parks and buildings that make up the World Heritage Site around the historic centre of Saint Petersburg.

    Read the complete The Ensemble of the Zinoviev's Datcha write-up.
