Why this ride

An 81 km round trip from Neustadt an der Donau - roughly 1 h 41 in the saddle. The stops in between: Watchtowers WP 15/11 to 15/26 and fortlets, Fort site and civil settlement of Pfoerrin and back through Watchtowers WP 15/11 to 15/26 and fortlets.

The stops

  1. 1. Eining‐Weinberg - Wachtturm und Heiligtum

    Start here.

    At Eining, on the Danube in Lower Bavaria, the Roman frontier of Raetia met the river. The Raetian Limes, a stone wall running for over a hundred and sixty kilometres across southern Germany, ended near here at Hienheim on the opposite bank, and from there the Danube itself formed the border all the way to the Black Sea. The Romans built the fort of Abusina at Eining, whose stone foundations are among the best preserved in Bavaria, and on the Weinberg, a hill nearby, they built a watchtower and a sanctuary, where soldiers worshipped their gods. Unusually, two separate World Heritage frontiers touch here: the Raetian Limes on land and the Danube Limes along the river.

    Read the complete Eining‐Weinberg - Wachtturm und Heiligtum write-up.

  2. 2. Watchtowers WP 15/11 to 15/26 and fortlets

    27 km in.

    The Altmühl valley in Bavaria is one of Germany's best kept riding secrets, a winding river valley cut deep into Jurassic limestone, lined with juniper heaths, rock towers and castles. Near where the valley meets the Danube, the Roman frontier, the Raetian Limes, ran across the land, and the stretch of watchtowers and small forts numbered WP 15/11 to 15/26 lies in the wooded hills here, between Riedenburg, Dietfurt and the Danube town of Vohburg.

    Read the complete Watchtowers WP 15/11 to 15/26 and fortlets write-up.

  3. 3. Fort site and civil settlement of Pfoerrin

    39 km in.

    Between Ingolstadt and Kelheim, the Danube flows through a broad, flat valley in Bavaria, with hop gardens, fields of maize and asparagus beds on its banks. Pförring is a small market town on the north bank, with Neustadt an der Donau and Vohburg on the other side. In Roman times, this was the frontier: the Raetian Limes ended at the Danube just downstream, and the Romans built a fort here, known as Celeusum, with a civil settlement outside its walls.
