Why this ride

From Uedem it runs 201 km to Uedem; allow about 3 h 28 of riding. The stops in between: Kleve-Reichswald | East, Elst-Grote Kerk, Tiel Inundation Canal and back through Elst-Grote Kerk. It crosses from Germany into Netherlands - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules. The line favours views over speed.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Uedem-Hochwald | Hochwald 11

    Lower Rhine forests do not announce themselves, and the Hochwald between Uedem, Xanten and Kalkar is no exception: a dark block of beech, oak and pine on a low ridge, with flat farmland on every side. Point 11 is a spot somewhere inside that woodland, about six kilometres from Uedem, so think of it as a way into the forest rather than a particular junction or bench.

  2. 2. Kleve-Reichswald | East

    South of Kleve, near the Dutch border, the Reichswald is one of the largest forests in the Lower Rhine region, a mix of oak, beech and pine on a low ridge of sand and gravel left behind by the glaciers of the Ice Age. On its edge, the Romans built military installations as part of the Lower German Limes, the frontier along the Rhine, and the remains in the Reichswald, including traces of camps, are part of the World Heritage site listed by UNESCO in 2021. There is little to see above ground, but the forest itself is a quiet, beautiful place to walk.

  3. 3. Elst-Grote Kerk

    Elst is a commuter town in the Betuwe, the flat river island between the Rhine and the Waal, halfway between Arnhem and Nijmegen. At first glance there is nothing remarkable about its big brick church on the market square. The surprise lies underneath it. Excavations in the twentieth century found the remains of two Roman temples beneath the floor, built one after another on the same spot, dedicated to a local god adopted into the Roman world. The site belongs to the Frontiers of the Roman Empire, Lower German Limes, inscribed by UNESCO in 2021, and the foundations can be seen in the church cellar when it is open.

  4. 4. Tiel Inundation Canal

    Tiel is a river town on the Waal, the main branch of the Rhine in the Netherlands, in the middle of the Betuwe, a region of orchards, dykes and meadows between the Waal and the Lower Rhine. Just outside the town, a wide, straight channel runs through the fields, crossed by bridges and lined by dykes. This is the Tiel inundation canal, built in the late nineteenth century as part of the New Dutch Waterline, the defensive system of controlled flooding that protected the heart of Holland, and it is now part of the UNESCO-listed Dutch Water Defence Lines.
