Why this ride

Open coast, Schlei crossings and enough villages to keep it honest. The best part sits around Boknis Eck viewpoint, Kappeln bridge, Schleswig return, while Eckernförde lunch keeps the day practical instead of turning it into a fuel or food hunt. Check the OSM map, Google preview and GPX checkpoint order before departure; they use the same waypoint sequence. Ride it with legal pull-offs, patience through villages and enough daylight to stop without rushing.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Boknis Eck viewpoint

    Boknis Eck is a headland on the coast of the Eckernförde Bay, on the Baltic side of Schleswig-Holstein, and the first thing you notice is how low everything is. The land at this point stands only a few metres above the sea. It is a coastline of small cliffs, beaches strewn with flint and boulders left by the glaciers, and long views across the water toward Denmark. The sea off Boknis Eck is also known to marine scientists, who have run a time series station here since the 1950s, measuring temperature, salinity and nutrients in the bay.

  2. 2. Eckernförde lunch

    Eckernförde sits on a bay that curves into the Baltic like a cupped hand, and its old harbour is where a day in the northern lowlands comes to rest. The town is known above all for smoked fish, and in particular for the Kieler Sprotten, small, golden-brown sprats that are named after Kiel but are traditionally made here, in smokehouses along the water. They are eaten whole, by hand, with a bit of bread, and they taste of oak smoke and salt. If you eat one thing in Eckernförde, make it that. A fish roll with fried herring or plaice is the safer alternative, and plenty of harbour kiosks sell them.

  3. 3. Kappeln bridge

    Kappeln guards the middle of the Schlei, and its bridge is the town's pulse. The Schlei is a long, narrow inlet of the Baltic that winds forty kilometres inland to Schleswig, and the bridge at Kappeln is where the road crosses it. It is a double-leaf bascule bridge, which opens at set times to let sailing boats pass, and when it lifts, traffic stops and everyone gets out to watch. Waiting for it is part of the local rhythm, and you will not be hurried.
