Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Auckland

    Auckland is built on a volcanic field. More than fifty cones and craters, some now covered in grass and sheep, others in houses, stand across the narrow isthmus between two harbours, the Waitematā to the north and the Manukau to the south. The Māori name for the city, Tāmaki Makaurau, is often translated as Tāmaki desired by many, a nod to the fertile soil and the sea on either side that made it a prized place to live long before Europeans arrived. The largest cones, such as Maungawhau, Mount Eden, and Maungakiekie, One Tree Hill, still carry the terraces of old fortified villages.

    Read the complete Auckland write-up.

  2. 2. Paeroa

    Every New Zealander knows the slogan: L&P, World Famous in New Zealand. The soft drink, originally Lemon and Paeroa, was made with mineral water from a spring near this small Hauraki Plains town, and Paeroa has never let anyone forget it. A giant L&P bottle stands by the main road, and you will probably stop for the photo. Nobody will blame you.

    Read the complete Paeroa write-up.

  3. 3. Rotorua

    Rotorua smells of eggs, and everyone is soon used to it. The city sits in the crater of an ancient volcano, on the shore of a lake, and the ground beneath it is alive: steam rises from roadside drains, mud pools bubble in parks, and in the thermal reserve of Te Puia the Pohutu geyser erupts many times a day, throwing a column of boiling water twenty metres or more into the air. The sulphur is the price of living on a hot spot, and the nickname of Rotovegas, half affection and half mockery, covers the tourist bustle.
