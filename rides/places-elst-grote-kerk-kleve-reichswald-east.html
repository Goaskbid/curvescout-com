Why this ride

A 171 km round trip from Elst - roughly 2 h 57 in the saddle. On the way it passes Kleve-Reichswald | West, Uedem-Hochwald | Hochwald 1, Kleve-Reichswald | East and back through Uedem-Hochwald | Hochwald 1. It crosses from Netherlands into Germany - carry ID and check vignette or toll rules.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Elst-Grote Kerk

    Elst is a commuter town in the Betuwe, the flat river island between the Rhine and the Waal, halfway between Arnhem and Nijmegen. At first glance there is nothing remarkable about its big brick church on the market square. The surprise lies underneath it. Excavations in the twentieth century found the remains of two Roman temples beneath the floor, built one after another on the same spot, dedicated to a local god adopted into the Roman world. The site belongs to the Frontiers of the Roman Empire, Lower German Limes, inscribed by UNESCO in 2021, and the foundations can be seen in the church cellar when it is open.

  2. 2. Kleve-Reichswald | West

    Kleve sits on the lower Rhine, a few kilometres from the Dutch border, in a landscape so flat that the one low hill outside the town counts as an event. West of it, the Reichswald is a big, dark forest of pine and beech, one of the largest wooded areas in this part of North Rhine-Westphalia, and for a rider coming from the Ruhr it feels like an exhale. The forest roads are straight and shaded, and the surrounding villages, Kranenburg among them, are neat, brick-built and utterly peaceful.

  3. 3. Uedem-Hochwald | Hochwald 1

    Hochwald is a ridge of woods, which is a surprising thing to find in the flat country of the Lower Rhine. The ground here was pushed up by the glaciers of the Ice Age, which shoved sand and gravel into long, low ridges known as push moraines, and the woods that cover the ridge near Uedem have been left in peace for generations. Beech and oak form a canopy over the old lanes, and in autumn the leaf litter is deep and the air smells of mushrooms. For a rider used to the endless flatness of the Niederrhein, even a modest hill feels like an event.

  4. 4. Kleve-Reichswald | East

    South of Kleve, near the Dutch border, the Reichswald is one of the largest forests in the Lower Rhine region, a mix of oak, beech and pine on a low ridge of sand and gravel left behind by the glaciers of the Ice Age. On its edge, the Romans built military installations as part of the Lower German Limes, the frontier along the Rhine, and the remains in the Reichswald, including traces of camps, are part of the World Heritage site listed by UNESCO in 2021. There is little to see above ground, but the forest itself is a quiet, beautiful place to walk.
