Why this ride

A 244 km round trip from Lebyazh’ye - roughly 4 h 33 in the saddle. It goes by way of Tolbukhin Lighthouse on Tolbukhin Island, Terioki, Sestroretsky Razliv and back through Terioki. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Saint Petersburg Imperial Shore

It is 63 km from Tolbukhin Lighthouse on Tolbukhin Island to Terioki, mostly on КАД. Fairly level - 0 to 40 m end to end. Watch for sand and spray near the water.

The stops

  1. 1. Fort «Krasnaya Gorka»

    Start here.

    On the southern shore of the Gulf of Finland, near the village of Lebyazhye, the remains of Fort Krasnaya Gorka sit among pine woods and sand dunes. The fort was built in the years before the First World War as part of a chain of coastal defences protecting the approaches to St Petersburg, with heavy guns facing the sea and their counterparts on the opposite shore at Ino, in what was then the Grand Duchy of Finland. Together, the forts were designed to close the gulf to an enemy fleet.

    Read the complete Fort «Krasnaya Gorka» write-up.

  2. 2. Tolbukhin Lighthouse on Tolbukhin Island

    37 km in.

    Ships have been guided by a light on Tolbukhin island for about three centuries, and it is one of those places that makes more sense on a chart than on a road. The island lies in the Gulf of Finland, south-west of Kronstadt and out in the shallow channel that leads to Saint Petersburg, and the lighthouse on it marks the southern passage into the Neva Bay. The surrounding waters are shallow, and in the grey light of autumn the sea and the sky merge into a single pale band.

    Read the complete Tolbukhin Lighthouse on Tolbukhin Island write-up.

  3. 3. Terioki

    100 km in.

    Terioki, or Terijoki in Finnish, is the old name of Zelenogorsk, a resort town on the northern shore of the Gulf of Finland, about fifty kilometres from St Petersburg. Until the Winter War, it was part of Finland, and before the revolution it was one of the favourite dacha destinations for the wealthy of the imperial capital, who came by train to spend summers among the pine trees and sandy beaches. After the Soviet Union took the Karelian Isthmus in 1940 and again in 1944, the Finnish population left, and in 1948 the town was renamed Zelenogorsk, the green hill.

    Read the complete Terioki write-up.

  4. 4. Sestroretsky Razliv

    133 km in.

    Sestroretsk is a small town on the north shore of the Gulf of Finland, in a landscape of pine-covered dunes, shallow bays and lakes, and it has an industrial origin that surprises visitors. In 1721 Peter the Great founded an arms factory on the Sestra river here, and a dam created a broad reservoir to power it. That reservoir, Razliv, which means flood or spill, is the lake that gives the place its name. The water, ringed by reeds and birches, is a favourite spot for locals to swim, fish and walk.

    Read the complete Sestroretsky Razliv write-up.
