  1. 1. Near Waynesboro

    Rockfish Gap is a low saddle in the Blue Ridge above Waynesboro, Virginia, and it is one of the great meeting points of American motorcycling. Here, on the crest of the ridge, the Skyline Drive ends its 105-mile run south through Shenandoah National Park, and the Blue Ridge Parkway begins its 469-mile journey south to the Great Smoky Mountains. Interstate 64 crosses the gap too, along with the Appalachian Trail, and on a summer weekend you will see riders from every state stopping for photos at the milepost zero sign.

  3. 3. Blue Ridge Parkway point 3

    Mabry Mill has to be the most photographed building on the parkway, and for once the cliche is justified. A little red-brown mill, with a big overshot water wheel turning beside a pond and a wooden flume carrying water to it, stands among rhododendrons beside the road. It was built by Edwin Boone Mabry in the early 1900s, and for decades it ground corn and sawed timber for neighbours who farmed these hills. Today it is a living museum, with demonstrations of blacksmithing, weaving and milling in the summer months.

  6. 6. Blue Ridge Parkway point 6

    Mount Mitchell is the highest point in the eastern United States, at 6,684 feet, and a short spur road from the parkway leads almost to the top. The mountain is named for Elisha Mitchell, a professor at the University of North Carolina who measured the peak in the 1830s and died in a fall near the summit in 1857, while trying to prove his claim against a rival. He is buried at the top, under a stone tower, and the peak has been a state park since 1915, the oldest in North Carolina.

  8. 8. Asheville

    Asheville is the city of the Blue Ridge, an arty, mountain-framed town at about 650 metres where the French Broad River meets the Swannanoa. The mountains surround it on all sides, soft and blue in the haze, and the air has a clean, woody smell. It has a population of nearly ninety thousand, a lively downtown of art deco buildings from the 1920s, and a reputation as a place for craft beer, folk music, galleries and good food. Locals proudly call it Beer City, with dozens of breweries, and the cafes and farm-to-table restaurants have a loyal following.

  10. 10. Cherokee

    Cherokee sits at the southern end of the Blue Ridge Parkway, in the western corner of North Carolina, and it is the capital of the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians. The people here are descended from those who hid in the mountains in the 1830s, when most of their nation was forced west on the Trail of Tears, and the community holds a long lease on a tract of land called the Qualla Boundary. The Museum of the Cherokee Indian in town tells the story with real depth, and the sound of the Cherokee language, spoken and printed on street signs, is a quiet fact of daily life.
