  1. 1. Salem

    Salem is the gateway town on the northern edge of the Ozarks' best riding country, a county seat of about five thousand people where the farmland of the plateau starts to break into the steep, forested hills that drain south toward the Current River. It is the last place with a real choice of gas stations, groceries and restaurants before Route 19 heads into a long, empty stretch toward Eminence, so this is where you fill the tank, eat properly and check the weather.

  2. 2. Eminence

    Eminence has fewer than six hundred residents and, on a summer weekend, several thousand visitors, most of them carrying canoes. The tiny town sits on the Jacks Fork River in the heart of the Ozark National Scenic Riverways, the first national park area in the United States created to protect a river system, established in 1964 to keep the Current and Jacks Fork rivers free of dams. The water here is famously clear, cold and blue-green over gravel bars, fed by enormous springs pouring out of the limestone.

  3. 3. Winona

    Winona sits where Route 19 meets US Highway 60, a small Shannon County town of about thirteen hundred people strung along the main road among the hills of the Mark Twain National Forest. It is not a destination in its own right, and it does not pretend to be. What it offers is a crossroads, a gas station or two, a few places to eat and a quick pause between the rivers of the Ozark National Scenic Riverways to the north and the timber country to the south.
