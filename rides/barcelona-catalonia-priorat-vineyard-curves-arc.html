Why this ride

A 404 km loop that starts and ends near Vilassar de Mar, about 7 h 21 of riding. The stops in between: Sant Feliu de Pallerols, near Prats de Lluçanès, near Bonastre and El Prat de Llobregat. The climb of the day is la Marina del Prat Vermell. It tops out at about 1,200 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Vilassar de Mar

    Vilassar de Mar is a Maresme town with salt in its blood. It stretches along the coast northeast of Barcelona, between the railway, the main road and a broad sandy beach, with fishing boats pulled up on the sand and an old centre of narrow streets and low whitewashed houses. Behind the town, fields and greenhouses climb toward the wooded hills of the coastal range, and on a clear day you can see along the shore toward Mataró and back to Barcelona.

  2. 2. Sant Feliu de Pallerols, on the way back

    The river Brugent runs down the Vall d'Hostoles between wooded slopes, and in summer its clear pools, gorgs in Catalan, are where the people of the valley go to cool off. The point lies near Sant Feliu de Pallerols, a small village in the Garrotxa, between Olot and Girona, and on a hot afternoon on the way back from a long ride, a dip in the river is exactly what the body asks for.

  3. 3. Food stop near Prats de Lluçanès

    The Lluçanès is one of those Catalan districts that most visitors never find. It is a high plateau between the plains of Osona and the Berguedà mountains, a land of rolling fields, oak and pine woods, Romanesque churches and isolated farmhouses, the great stone masies that are typical of rural Catalonia. Prats de Lluçanès, a small town of a few thousand people, is its main centre.

  4. 4. Photo stop near Bonastre

    Bonastre is a small hill village in the Baix Penedès, inland from the coast between Tarragona and Barcelona, surrounded by vineyards, olive groves and pine-covered hills. It has a castle tower, a church and narrow streets, and from its edges the views stretch over the vines to the Mediterranean. It is a quiet place, a world away from the beach resorts on the coast.

  5. 5. Near El Prat de Llobregat

    The Llobregat delta is flat, green and noisy in equal measure. Jets climb out of Barcelona's airport over the sea, the city's port stretches along the coast to the north, and between them lie fields of artichokes, orchards, marshes and pine-backed beaches.

  6. 6. Castelldefels

    Castelldefels is where Barcelona goes to the beach, and where the coast suddenly stops being flat. Behind the town's long sandy shore, the limestone hills of the Garraf massif rise abruptly, and just south of town the coast road begins its famous run along the cliffs to Sitges. A castle on a small hill, rebuilt many times, gives the town its name and keeps watch over the plain of the Llobregat delta to the north.

  7. 7. la Marina del Prat Vermell

    This is not the Barcelona of postcards, and that is half its charm for a rider. La Marina del Prat Vermell sits on the flat ground between the southern slope of Montjuïc and the Zona Franca, the city's great industrial and logistics zone, with the port cranes on one side, warehouses and new apartment blocks on the other, and the motorway ring roads that make escape from the city fast. It is a neighbourhood in transition, long a pocket of workers' housing among factories, now slowly being rebuilt.
