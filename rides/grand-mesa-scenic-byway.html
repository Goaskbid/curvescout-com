Why this ride

A 73 km one-way ride from near Cedaredge, about 1 h 12 of riding. The stops in between: Grand Mesa and Mesa Lakes. The highest point is about 3,300 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Grand Mesa Scenic Byway

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 3300 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance — cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Cedaredge

    Cedaredge is a small orchard town on the south-eastern flank of the Grand Mesa, a place of about two thousand people where apples, peaches, cherries and pears are grown in the warm lower slopes and the main street holds a few cafes and a museum of pioneer buildings. It sits at about six thousand feet, with the huge bulk of the mesa filling the sky to the north, and from here the byway starts to climb. The town is a quiet, friendly stop, and Delta, with larger stores, is down the valley.

  2. 2. Grand Mesa

    Grand Mesa is said to be the largest flat-topped mountain in the world, and when you climb onto it you understand why. From the orchards and desert canyons of western Colorado, the road rises through scrub oak and aspen onto a lava-capped plateau about forty miles long, at around eleven thousand feet. The top is forest, meadow and lake, cool in summer and buried in snow in winter, and it feels like a different region from the hot valley floor below.

  3. 3. Mesa Lakes

    Mesa Lakes is a cluster of small lakes and forest cabins on the north slope of Grand Mesa, just below the rim, and it is where Coloradans go to fish, walk and avoid the summer heat. The lakes sit in hollows scooped out by ice, surrounded by spruce, fir and aspen, with meadows opening between them and the air cooler by many degrees than the valley of the Colorado River far below. There is a resort feel to the place, with a lodge, a few cabins and a campground, but it is quiet and woodsy, not busy.

  4. 4. Palisade

    Palisade is a small town in the Grand Valley of western Colorado, and it smells of peaches in summer. The orchards that surround it, nourished by water from the Colorado River and baked by long hot days and cool nights, produce fruit so good that people drive from Denver to buy it from roadside stands. The harvest runs from July through September, with peaches at their best in August, and the town holds a festival to celebrate. It sits at about forty-seven hundred feet, which makes it one of the warmest places in the state.

Cedaredge to Grand Mesa

Cedaredge → Grand Mesa climbs about 3300 m in 25 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Mesa Lakes to Palisade

Mesa Lakes → Palisade is 32 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
