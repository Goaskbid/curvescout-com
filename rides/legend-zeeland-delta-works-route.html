Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Rotterdam

    Rotterdam is a young city by Dutch standards, and that is because the old one was destroyed. German bombers flattened the centre in May 1940, and rather than rebuild a historic town the Dutch built a modern one, bold and experimental, with towers, bridges and cubic houses that tilt on their corners. Nothing here is quiet or pretty in the postcard sense, and that is its appeal. The Erasmus Bridge, a white swan-necked span over the Maas, is the city's symbol, and the Markthal, a vast arched market hall with a ceiling painted in giant fruit and flowers, is the best place to eat. Cheap, quick, varied food is sold from stalls there, and Dutch herring, eaten raw with onion, is available at every street corner.

  5. 5. Middelburg

    Middelburg is the sort of Dutch town that makes you slow down on purpose. It is the capital of Zeeland, set on the old island of Walcheren, and the canals, brick houses and a huge Gothic town hall give it a settled, prosperous look. Around forty-six thousand people live here, enough to guarantee fuel, coffee and a proper lunch, but small enough that you can see the centre on foot in an afternoon.
