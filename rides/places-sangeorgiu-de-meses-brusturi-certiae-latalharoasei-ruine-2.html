Why this ride

From Cizer it runs 72 km to Cizer; allow about 1 h 14 of riding. It goes by way of Treznea-Dealul Mănăstirii, Brusturi - Certiae / LaTâlhăroasei Ruine and back through Treznea-Dealul Mănăstirii. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Sângeorgiu de Meseș

    The village of Sangeorgiu de Meses lies on the southern flank of the Meses mountains, in the old Hungarian-Romanian borderland of Salaj, and its name carries the saint of horsemen, George, and the mountain that gives the area its character. It is a place that rides begin and end, which suits it well: a quiet village with a church, a shop, a couple of bars, and an easy exit onto roads that climb in several directions. A few kilometres away is Cizer, and a little further, the small settlements of Starciu and Mal, each with its own cluster of orchards and farm tracks.

  2. 2. Treznea-Dealul Mănăstirii

    The Meseș mountains, a low, wooded range in north-western Romania, formed a natural barrier for the Roman province of Dacia, and the Romans fortified it with a line of watchtowers, walls and fortlets. Near the village of Treznea, on a hill known as Dealul Mănăstirii, the Monastery Hill, the remains of one of these defences overlook the valleys. It is part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire, Dacia, listed by UNESCO in 2024, and a short distance away lies Porolissum, one of the largest Roman settlements in Dacia, with a reconstructed gate and an amphitheatre.

  3. 3. Brusturi - Certiae / LaTâlhăroasei Ruine

    Zalău is the county town of Sălaj, a modern small city of about sixty thousand people that grew fast in the communist period, and the countryside around it is far more interesting than the blocks of flats. Brusturi, a few kilometres off, sits in the hills that lead up toward the Meseş mountains, and the name on our list points to a Roman fort called Certiae, one of the line of military posts that kept watch on the northern edge of Dacia, along with a ruin the locals link with an old legend of robbers.
