Why this ride

84 km from Guatemala City to Guatemala City, about 1 h 27 of riding. It goes by way of San Cristobal \El Alto\, Antigua Guatemala, San Juan del Obispo and back through Antigua Guatemala. The highest point is about 2,000 m.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Guatemala City

    Guatemala City sits in a high valley, at around 1,500 metres, ringed by volcanoes. On a clear morning, from a rooftop or a ridge, you can see the cones of Pacaya, Agua, Fuego and Acatenango lined up along the horizon, and Fuego often sends up a white plume of ash. The city has been rebuilt several times after earthquakes, most seriously in 1917 and 1976, and its landscape of gullies and ravines, crossed by high concrete bridges, makes it feel both modern and fragile. At its centre, in Zone 1, stand the cathedral, the National Palace and a big plaza where families come to sit and talk.

  2. 2. San Cristobal \El Alto\

    San Cristóbal El Alto clings to the hills above Antigua Guatemala, on the road that climbs toward San Juan del Obispo and Santa María de Jesús. The air here is cooler than in the valley, scented with pine smoke and coffee, and on clear mornings the three great volcanoes of the area, Agua, Fuego and Acatenango, stand along the skyline like a drawn curtain. Fuego, the active one, is often puffing a plume of ash into the sky, which makes for a dramatic backdrop.

  3. 3. Antigua Guatemala

    Antigua Guatemala is a city that fell down and got up again. For more than two centuries it was the capital of Spanish Central America, a grand colonial town of churches, convents and mansions at the foot of three volcanoes, Agua, Fuego and Acatenango. In 1773 a series of earthquakes shook it so badly that the authorities moved the capital to a new site, now Guatemala City, in 1776. The old city was partly abandoned, and many of its churches stand today as roofless ruins, their arches and walls covered with moss and flowers.
