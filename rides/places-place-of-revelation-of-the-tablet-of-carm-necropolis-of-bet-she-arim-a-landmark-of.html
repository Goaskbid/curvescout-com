  1. 1. Place of Revelation of the “Tablet of Carm

    Carmel means garden, or vineyard of God, and the mountain has been holy for a very long time. The prophet Elijah is said to have confronted the priests of Baal on its summit, in a story told in the Book of Kings, and the Carmelite order, founded by crusader-era hermits, took its name from the mountain and built its first monastery here. The Stella Maris monastery, with its lighthouse and white church, still stands above the port. In 1891, the founder of the Bahá'í faith, Bahá'u'lláh, visited Haifa and, according to tradition, revealed the Tablet of Carmel on the slopes of the mountain, a short text addressed to the mountain itself.

  3. 3. Necropolis of Bet She’arim: A Landmark of

    In the second century AD, after the failed revolts against Rome, the centre of Jewish life moved to Galilee, and the Sanhedrin, the supreme rabbinical council, settled for a time at Bet She'arim, on a hill at the western edge of the Jezreel valley. Rabbi Judah ha-Nasi, the patriarch who compiled the Mishnah, the first great codification of Jewish law, lived here, and when he was buried in the necropolis, Jews from all over the diaspora wanted to be buried near him. The result is a vast network of catacombs carved into the soft limestone, listed by UNESCO in 2015.
