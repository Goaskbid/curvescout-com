Why this ride

From Whistler it runs 244 km to Lillooet; allow about 3 h 20 of riding. The stops in between: Pemberton, Cayoosh Pass and Duffey Lake. The highest point is about 1,800 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Whistler

    Whistler began as an idea in the 1960s, when a group of Vancouver businessmen decided that a mountain north of the city might host an Olympic ski area, and it ended as one of the largest winter resorts in North America. The village itself is purpose built, a pedestrian maze of shops, bars and hotels at the foot of two mountains, Whistler and Blackcomb, joined by a gondola called Peak 2 Peak that spans a huge gap between the summits. The name comes from the hoary marmots that whistle from the rockfalls, and the surrounding land is the shared territory of the Squamish and Lil'wat peoples, whose cultural centre in the village is worth an hour.

  2. 2. Pemberton

    Pemberton sits in a flat, green valley surrounded by high, jagged mountains, a short distance north of Whistler, and it feels like a different world from the resort next door. Mount Currie, a rugged peak with a grey crest, towers over the farmland, and the Lillooet River winds across the valley floor. Farmers here grow seed potatoes, which are prized for their disease-free quality, and the fields with their neat rows are the first thing you see on arrival. The town has about two thousand people, a few cafes, a brewery and a laid-back air.

  3. 3. Cayoosh Pass

    Cayoosh Pass is the high point of the Duffey Lake Road, the stretch of British Columbia Highway 99 that connects the dry, sage-scented interior at Lillooet with the green valleys of Pemberton and the coast. At about 1,275 metres, it is not especially tall, but this road is a rider's favourite, and the pass is an important marker, the place where the road stops climbing and begins its long descent through rock and snow toward the Pacific. The mountains around it are sharp, glaciated and close.

  4. 4. Duffey Lake

    Duffey Lake is a long, green-blue lake lying beside the road in a bowl of high mountains, and when the wind drops, it holds a clear mirror image of the peaks. On the far shore the slopes rise in forest and then in grey rock, topped with snowfields and small glaciers, and the whole scene feels remote, though the road runs right beside it. The provincial park at the lake provides a pull-out, a picnic area and a trail, so it is easy to stop and look.

  5. 5. Lillooet

    Lillooet announces itself as a change of climate. You come down from the mountains of the Duffey Lake road, with their dark spruce and old snow, and within an hour the land has turned to sage, dry benches and bare golden slopes above the Fraser River. The town sits right on that river, in one of the hottest and driest corners of British Columbia, and a summer afternoon here can feel like Spain has been dropped into the Rockies.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Whistler to Pemberton

Whistler → Pemberton is 32 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.

Pemberton to Cayoosh Pass

Pemberton → Cayoosh Pass climbs about 1275 m in 35 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Cayoosh Pass to Duffey Lake

Cayoosh Pass → Duffey Lake drops about 1275 m over 11 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Duffey Lake to Lillooet

Duffey Lake → Lillooet is 52 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
