Why this ride

From Klin it runs 92 km to Konakovo; allow about 1 h 46 of riding. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Roadbook

  1. Klin start

    Klin is a working town of eighty thousand on the old road between Moscow and St Petersburg, and for a rider on a long day it is where the choice is turning back or pushing on toward Tver. The Leningrad highway runs right past, the railway too, and the town itself sits on rolling country at the edge of the Valdai hills, with the Sestra River slipping through.

    The reason to remember it is Tchaikovsky. He settled in the Klin area in the 1880s, and the house where he lived and worked in his last years is now a museum, kept more or less as he left it, with his piano in the room. His final symphony was written here, and he died in 1893 not long after a visit to St Petersburg. Klin also has a long industrial history, and the glass Christmas ornaments made here are still part of the local identity. In a country that takes New Year seriously, that matters. If you want a souvenir that will survive a motorcycle pannier, a small glass bauble from a local shop is the safest bet you will find.

    Riding around Klin, the pleasure is in the quieter roads. North of the main highway, the lanes toward Vysokovsk and the Dmitrov country cross low ridges, small rivers and big, empty fields. Corners are gentle rather than dramatic, the horizons are wide, and the villages are strung with wooden houses with painted window frames. Surfaces vary a lot. Good asphalt gives way to patched tar and sometimes gravel at the edges, so treat blind crests with respect. On the big highway, trucks are frequent and fast, and the wind sheet from a passing lorry can move you across your lane. Better to leave the trunk road at the first sensible exit.

    Practically, a town this size has plenty of fuel and food, with cafes and shops near the centre and along the highway. Mornings are best, before traffic builds from the capital, and early autumn has a lovely clear light on the birch woods. In winter the whole area is under snow and the roads become a different problem. Allow time for the museum if you are a music person, because the quiet garden alone is worth a short break.

  2. Zavidovo scenic

    Zavidovo is known across Russia less for the village than for the forest that surrounds it. That huge woodland, between the Volga and the Shosha rivers, was set aside in the 1930s as a hunting reserve for the Soviet leadership, and generations of officials hunted its elk, deer and wild boar behind fences and checkpoints. Today the core of the reserve is a national park, and the woods that surround it are among the richest in the Moscow region, with spruce, pine and birch stands, marshes and quiet lakes.

    The Volga is the other force in the landscape. Here the river was dammed in the 1930s to create the Ivankovo Reservoir, known as the Moscow Sea, a large artificial lake that feeds the Moscow Canal and gives the capital its drinking water. The shore at Zavidovo is a favourite spot for anglers, sailors and weekenders from the capital, and the village has grown to a sizeable settlement of several thousand, with a mix of old wooden houses, new summer homes and the sort of leisure facilities that the reservoir brings.

    The old M10 highway runs through here, and it is one of the busiest roads in Russia, carrying a huge amount of freight between Moscow and St Petersburg. Riding it is a matter of patience, with trucks, roadworks and average speed controls. The better experience is on the quiet roads that run through the forest and along the shore of the reservoir, where the surface is variable but the traffic is slight. The woods are most beautiful in autumn, when birch and aspen turn yellow among the dark conifers, and the mushroom pickers come out in force, so watch for cars parked along the verges.

    Zavidovo has fuel, shops and a few cafes, with a population of seven thousand or so, and Novozavidovsky, a short way off, is similar. It is a practical place to stop on the way between Moscow and Tver, and there is enough food and rest to make it worthwhile. If you are interested in nature, check whether the park allows access on the day, since parts of the reserve are restricted. Summer evenings by the water are pleasant, but the mosquitoes are numerous in the woods, so carry repellent.
