Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bco. de la Plana I

    Mequinenza sits where the Segre and the Cinca join the Ebro, and the Barranco de la Plana, a dry gully less than a kilometre from the town, is one of the painted shelters scattered through the hills above them. The ride that includes it also touches Roca Roja, the Red Rock, and another shelter of the Abrics d'Ermites group far to the south, which shows how far these rock-art routes range. The paintings belong to the Levantine tradition, protected by UNESCO along with the rest of the Mediterranean rock art of Spain, and in this semi-arid corner of Aragón, where there is little to shelter a painted wall from the weather, what survives is a small miracle.

  2. 2. mas de Torres

    The Monegros in high summer is the closest thing Europe has to a desert, a wide, dry plateau of gypsum hills, wheat stubble and steppe scrub under a hard, white sky. Near the village of Candasnos, close to the main road between Zaragoza and Lleida, the shelter labelled Mas de Torres is one of the rock art sites of the Mediterranean Basin listed by UNESCO, a reminder that people lived and hunted here long before the land dried out to its present state.

  3. 3. Mallata C

    Ontiñena is a small farming village on the Alcanadre river, at the edge of the Monegros, the dry, wind-scoured steppe of central Aragón. Out in the ravines around it, prehistoric people left painted figures in rock shelters, among them the site labelled Mallata C, one of the many small components of the UNESCO listing of Mediterranean rock art. The paintings are small, simple and best seen with a local guide, and the walk there crosses a landscape that seems built for epic films: gypsum hills, eroded gullies and wide skies.

  4. 4. Les Coves

    The western edge of Catalonia, around Lleida, is fruit country. Irrigation canals bring water from the Pyrenean rivers to a broad, flat plain of orchards growing apples, pears and peaches, laid out in neat lines that stretch to the horizon. Les Coves lies in this area, close to the Aragonese border, between Alfarràs, Albelda and Algerri, where the valley of the Noguera Ribagorçana spills out of the hills and onto the plain.
