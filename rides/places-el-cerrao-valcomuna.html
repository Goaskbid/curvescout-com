Why this ride

210 km from Obón to Obón, about 3 h 37 of riding. On the way it passes Covacho esquemático, Abric del mas de Barberà, Valcomuna and back through Abric del mas de Barberà. The highest point is about 1,200 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. El Cerrao

    The Río Martín is a modest river by Spanish standards, but it has carved one of the most interesting canyons in Aragon, and the Cultural Park that takes its name protects an extraordinary collection of rock art. El Cerrao is one of the shelters in that park, in the district of Montalbán in the province of Teruel, among cliffs of pale limestone above a dry valley. The paintings here belong to the Levantine tradition and are part of the group UNESCO inscribed in 1998.

  2. 2. Covacho esquemático

    Some shelters hide in plain sight, and the covacho esquemático is one of them, a shallow overhang in the limestone above the Río Martín, with a few schematic figures painted in red. Schematic art is the shorthand of prehistory, stripped to essentials: a stick-like human, a dot, a zigzag, a sun disc. It belongs to a later phase than the more naturalistic hunting scenes of the Levantine tradition, and it is found across the Iberian peninsula, here in the district around Alacón and Oliete in Teruel province, which forms part of the Río Martín Cultural Park.

  3. 3. Abric del mas de Barberà

    Alcañiz is the capital of the Bajo Aragón, a region of olive groves, cereal fields and dry hills crossed by the Guadalope river in the south-east of Aragón. The town is crowned by a castle built for the Order of Calatrava, now a parador, and its main square, with a Gothic loggia and the town hall, is one of the finest in the region. The shelter of the Mas de Barberà lies in the hills just outside town, toward Castelserás and Calanda.

  4. 4. Valcomuna

    Lower Aragon is a country of dry hills, olive groves and red earth, and in its ravines people painted on the rock thousands of years ago. Valcomuna is one of those rock art sites, between Alcañiz and Caspe near the village of Maella, part of the great family of painted shelters running down Spain's Mediterranean side that UNESCO listed as the Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin on the Iberian Peninsula. The figures in this tradition are small and lively, archers, deer, goats and people in motion, painted in red and black under overhangs where the weather could not wash them away.
