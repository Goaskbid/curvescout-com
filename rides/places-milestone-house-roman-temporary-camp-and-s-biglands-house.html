Why this ride

A 331 km round trip from Haltwhistle - roughly 4 h 14 in the saddle. It goes by way of Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 8 f, Biglands House and back through Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 8 f. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Milestone House Roman temporary camp and s

    Start here.

    Long before the wall existed, the Romans ran a road across the narrow neck of northern England, the Stanegate, linking the forts of Corbridge on the Tyne and Carlisle on the Eden. It was built around AD 80, in the time of the general Agricola, and it served as the frontier line for a generation before Hadrian ordered the wall to be built a little to the north. Forts and fortlets sat along it, among them Vindolanda and the small post at Haltwhistle Burn, and temporary camps like the one at Milestone House show where troops stopped on the move.

    Read the complete Milestone House Roman temporary camp and s write-up.

  2. 2. Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 8 f

    53 km in.

    Here, at the western edge of Newcastle, the Roman frontier starts to escape the city. Wall mile 8 lies around Newburn and Throckley, with Blaydon across the Tyne and the suburbs thinning into fields as you head west. The masonry itself has mostly vanished under roads, houses and farmland, but the line of the Wall and the great earthwork of the Vallum to its south can still be followed in hummocks, ditches and the occasional consolidated stretch of stone, most famously a little further west at Heddon-on-the-Wall, where a long run survives at nearly its full original width.

    Read the complete Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 8 f write-up.

  3. 3. Biglands House

    166 km in.

    Out on the Cumbrian shore of the Solway Firth, at the far western end of Hadrian's frontier, Biglands is a farmhouse name attached to the site of a small Roman milefortlet, one of the posts that carried the Wall's chain of defences down the coast once the stone curtain itself had stopped at Bowness. There is very little to see above ground; the fortlet was excavated and then returned to the grass. What you come for is the setting and the sense of standing at the edge of an empire, looking across a few kilometres of mud and water at Scotland.

    Read the complete Biglands House write-up.
