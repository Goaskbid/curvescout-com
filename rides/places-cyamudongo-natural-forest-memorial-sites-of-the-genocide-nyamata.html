Why this ride

A 274 km round trip from Cyangugu - roughly 7 h 44 in the saddle. It goes by way of Nyungwe National Park, Memorial sites of the Genocide: Nyamata and back through Nyungwe National Park. It tops out around 2,200 m. Much of it runs through woodland.

The roads

Rwanda Congo Nile Scenic Road

Cyamudongo Natural Forest to Nyungwe National Park: 64 km, mostly on DR2. Up about 770 m to 2,038 m, then 970 m back down the far side. Above 2,000 m the weather turns fast; have a layer and a plan B.

The stops

  1. 1. Cyamudongo Natural Forest

    Start here.

    In the far southwest corner of Rwanda, the land drops toward Lake Kivu and the Ruzizi river, and the green hills here are mostly tea, banana and small farms. Cyamudongo is the exception: a patch of natural forest, a few hundred hectares at most, standing as an island of tall trees in the middle of cultivation. It is separated from the great Nyungwe forest by a belt of farmland, yet it still shelters a small population of chimpanzees, along with other primates and a remarkable range of birds and butterflies.

    Read the complete Cyamudongo Natural Forest write-up.

  2. 2. Nyungwe National Park

    64 km in.

    Nyungwe is one of the oldest rainforests in Africa, a mountain forest spread over the ridges of south-western Rwanda between Lake Kivu and the Burundi border. It is high, wet and green in a way that makes the rest of the world feel dry: tree ferns taller than a house, orchids on the branches, mist pouring through the valleys in the morning, and the sound of water everywhere. The ridges here form part of the great divide between the Congo and Nile basins, and Rwanda proudly claims one of the remotest sources of the Nile in these hills.

    Read the complete Nyungwe National Park write-up.

  3. 3. Memorial sites of the Genocide: Nyamata

    137 km in.

    This is a place to arrive quietly. Both belong to the group of four genocide memorials, Nyamata, Murambi, Gisozi in Kigali and Bisesero, that UNESCO inscribed on the World Heritage list in 2023, recognising them as places of remembrance of the genocide against the Tutsi, in which some eight hundred thousand to a million people were murdered in about a hundred days.

    Read the complete Memorial sites of the Genocide: Nyamata write-up.
