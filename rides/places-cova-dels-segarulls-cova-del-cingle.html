Why this ride

217 km from Vilafranca del Penedès to Vilafranca del Penedès, about 3 h of riding. It goes by way of Paleochristian cemetery, Cova del Cingle and back through Paleochristian cemetery.

The stops

  1. 1. Cova dels Segarulls

    Start here.

    The vineyards of the Penedès roll out in every direction from Vilafranca, and the Cova dels Segarulls lies among them, a few kilometres outside the town in the low hills toward the coastal range.

    Read the complete Cova dels Segarulls write-up.

  2. 2. Paleochristian cemetery

    57 km in.

    When the Roman city of Tarraco converted to Christianity, its people began burying their dead beside the Francolí river, west of the town, and the necropolis that grew up there over the fourth and fifth centuries is now one of the most important early Christian burial sites in the western Mediterranean. Hundreds of tombs were found, from simple graves to carved marble sarcophagi, along with remains of a basilica. Tradition links the site with Saint Fructuosus, the bishop of Tarraco burned in the amphitheatre in the year 259 along with two deacons.

    Read the complete Paleochristian cemetery write-up.

  3. 3. Cova del Cingle

    109 km in.

    The Ebro still has rope ferries. At Miravet, a flat boat attached to a cable across the river carries cars, bikes and walkers from bank to bank, driven only by the current and the skill of the ferryman, as such ferries have done for centuries. Above the village, on a rocky hill, stands one of the great Templar castles of Catalonia, a stern stone fortress that controlled the river. Nearby, among the hills of the Ribera d'Ebre near Ginestar and Rasquera, the Cova del Cingle is a rock shelter with prehistoric paintings, part of the World Heritage Site of Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin.
