Why this ride

A 323 km round trip from Benigànim - roughly 6 h in the saddle. It goes by way of Barranco del Cabezo del moro, Abrigo del Buen Aire I and back through Barranco del Cabezo del moro. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Cova de la Petxina

    Start here.

    Bellús is a small village in the valley of the Albaida river in southern Valencia, just upstream from where the river flows into a reservoir and then joins the Júcar. The old village was partly flooded by the reservoir, and the new one stands on higher ground. The Cova de la Petxina, the cave of the shell, is in the hills near the village, close to Guadasequies and Genovés.

    Read the complete Cova de la Petxina write-up.

  2. 2. Barranco del Cabezo del moro

    91 km in.

    Almansa is one of those towns you see from the motorway and do not stop at, and that is a mistake. Its castle is spectacular, a Gothic fortress built on a sharp limestone outcrop right in the middle of town, looking exactly like a child's drawing of a castle. Around it spreads a wide plain at about seven hundred metres, the meeting point of the Valencian, Murcian and Castilian lands, and a few kilometres out in the surrounding hills lies the ravine known as the Barranco del Cabezo del Moro.

    Read the complete Barranco del Cabezo del moro write-up.

  3. 3. Abrigo del Buen Aire I

    162 km in.

    Jumilla country is dry, wide and honest. The town sits in the far north of Murcia where it bulges up towards Albacete, surrounded by limestone sierras, almond groves and vineyards of Monastrell grapes that make some of the most powerful red wine in Spain. A few kilometres outside town, in the folds of these hills, lie a group of small rock shelters known as the Abrigos del Buen Aire. They belong to the long chain of prehistoric painted sites strung along the eastern side of the peninsula that UNESCO lists together as the rock art of the Mediterranean Basin.

    Read the complete Abrigo del Buen Aire I write-up.
