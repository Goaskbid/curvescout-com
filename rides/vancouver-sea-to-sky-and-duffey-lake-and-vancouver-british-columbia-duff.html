Why this ride

From Squamish it runs 638 km to Anmore; allow about 11 h 36 of riding. The stops in between: Whistler, Pemberton, Duffey Lake Road and near Anmore. Vancouver / British Columbia is the high point. It tops out at about 1,600 m. Back roads rather than main roads, where the map allows.

Sea-to-Sky Highway

For 37 km this is Sea-to-Sky Highway, a road with a name of its own and a reason for it — expect company on a summer weekend. Early is better than fast.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Squamish fuel

    Squamish sits at the head of Howe Sound, where the fjord ends against a wall of granite, and the first thing it shows you is the Stawamus Chief, a monolith of rock rising some seven hundred metres straight out of the forest. It is one of the largest free-standing granite faces in the world, and on any good day the white specks on its walls are climbers, which has turned this small town into a minor capital of the sport. Beside it Shannon Falls drops in a long white thread, easily seen from the highway.

  2. 2. Whistler food

    Whistler is a village built for pleasure in an old logging valley, and it knows it. The pedestrian village at the foot of Whistler and Blackcomb mountains, with its cobbled lanes, hotels and rows of restaurants, was planned in the late 1970s and took off when the two ski areas were developed, and then hit the world stage when the Winter Olympics came here in 2010. The Peak 2 Peak gondola, which links the two mountains across a deep valley, opened in 2008 and holds records for its span and height. In summer the ski slopes become mountain bike parks, and in winter the village is packed with skiers from around the world.

  3. 3. Pemberton

    Pemberton sits in a flat, green valley surrounded by high, jagged mountains, a short distance north of Whistler, and it feels like a different world from the resort next door. Mount Currie, a rugged peak with a grey crest, towers over the farmland, and the Lillooet River winds across the valley floor. Farmers here grow seed potatoes, which are prized for their disease-free quality, and the fields with their neat rows are the first thing you see on arrival. The town has about two thousand people, a few cafes, a brewery and a laid-back air.

    Read the complete Pemberton write-up.

  4. 4. Duffey Lake Road

    The Duffey Lake Road is the part of British Columbia's Highway 99 that nobody forgets. It leaves Pemberton, in a flat green valley of farms and potato fields, and in less than an hour climbs into the Coast Mountains and drops into the dry interior, one of the sharpest changes of landscape in Canada. The road crosses Cayoosh Pass, a little over a thousand metres, and passes the shores of Duffey Lake, a long, green, glacier-fed water beneath towering peaks, and the trailheads for Joffre Lakes, three tiers of turquoise lakes beneath a glacier that have become some of the most popular walks in the province.

  6. 6. Fuel stop near Anmore

    Anmore is a small, wooded village in the hills above Port Moody, just east of Indian Arm, the steep fjord that runs north off Burrard Inlet. It is a quiet, semi-rural place, with large lots, forest and a strong sense of being on the edge of the wilderness, despite being only half an hour from downtown Vancouver.

  7. 7. Vancouver / British Columbia return

    The nearest town of any size is Squamish, twenty-odd kilometres to the north-east, and Lions Bay, a small village clinging to the opposite shore, is more than thirty kilometres away. What lies between is steep forested slope, small lakes and rock, with the Tantalus Range showing its white shoulders to the east.

  8. 8. Sechelt

    The Sunshine Coast is not an island, but it might as well be. Although it is part of the mainland of British Columbia, no road connects it to Vancouver; you have to take a ferry from Horseshoe Bay across Howe Sound to Langdale, and the forty-minute crossing, through a fjord of forested mountains, sets the tone for a place that feels removed from the rush of the city. Sechelt, the main town, sits on a narrow isthmus between the Strait of Georgia and the long, sheltered Sechelt Inlet.

  10. 10. Richmond

    Richmond is built on mud, and proud of it. The city occupies Lulu Island and neighbouring islands in the delta of the Fraser River, just south of Vancouver, land so flat and so low that much of it sits barely above sea level behind a ring of dykes. Vancouver's airport takes up Sea Island to the west, the North Shore mountains rise beyond the city skyline, and on a clear day Mount Baker floats above the delta to the southeast, a white volcano across the border in Washington.

  11. 11. High point near Langley

    Langley spreads across the Fraser Valley east of Vancouver, a mix of suburbs, horse farms, berry fields and wineries under a wide sky. It is not high in any alpine sense, but the gentle uplands here lift you just enough to see the Coast Mountains to the north and, on a clear day, the white cone of Mount Baker floating across the border to the south-east.

  12. 12. Scenic stretch near Pitt Meadows

    Pitt Meadows lies on the flat delta land where the Pitt River meets the Fraser, east of Vancouver. Dykes hold back the rivers, and behind them stretch blueberry and cranberry fields, wetlands and farmland, with the steep, forested peaks of the Golden Ears rising dramatically to the north. On a clear evening, with the sun catching the mountains and the fields glowing, it is a beautiful place to end a ride.

  13. 13. Back to Duffy Lake Road

    Anmore is a small village in the forested hills above Burrard Inlet, a short ride from Port Moody and the eastern edge of Vancouver's urban area, and the dot sits here because the Duffy Lake Road loop ends back in this part of the Lower Mainland. The land is dense with hemlock and cedar, the air is damp and cool, and the roads wind between lakes and ridges with a sense of being close to wilderness while only a few kilometres from the shops.
