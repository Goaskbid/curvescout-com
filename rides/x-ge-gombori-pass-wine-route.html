  1. 1. Tbilisi

    Tbilisi spreads along the Mtkvari river in a bowl of dry hills, and its first impression is of balconies. Old houses lean over narrow streets with carved wooden verandas, sulphur bathhouses sit under domes of brick in the Abanotubani district, and on the ridge above, the ruined Narikala fortress watches the city. The name Tbilisi comes from a Georgian word for warm, and the story says that a hunting king found hot springs here and decided to found a city.

  2. 2. Gombori Pass

    The Gombori Pass is the doorway between two Georgias. On one side lies the dry, open country of the Iori valley and the capital's hinterland; on the other, beyond the ridge, the lush Alazani valley that is the heart of Kakheti wine country. The road crosses the Gombori range at roughly 1,600 metres, climbing through thick oak and beech forest in tightly packed bends, and for the rider it is a short, sweet change of scene: cool shade, damp leaves, and a drop into a different climate on the other side.

  3. 3. Telavi

    Telavi is the capital of Kakheti, a town of about twenty-two thousand people that serves as the regional hub for wine, farming and old-fashioned Georgian hospitality. It sits on the northwest side of the Alazani valley, with the Tsiv-Gombori range behind and, on a clear day, the white wall of the Greater Caucasus ahead across the plain. It is a good place to stop, with real shops and a real market, and with enough history to keep you interested between mouthfuls.
