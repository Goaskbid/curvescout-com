Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bar Harbor

    Bar Harbor is the door to Mount Desert Island, the large granite island off the coast of Maine where Acadia National Park spreads over mountains, ponds and rocky shore. The town sits on Frenchman Bay, with the humped shapes of the Porcupine Islands offshore, and it has been a summer destination since the 1800s, when wealthy families from Boston and Philadelphia built huge shingle mansions they called cottages. A great fire in 1947 burned a lot of them, but the town rebuilt, and its streets are now a mix of old inns, gift shops, and lobster restaurants.

  2. 2. Near Bar Harbor

    Bar Harbor looks out over Frenchman Bay from the north-east shore of Mount Desert Island, with the Porcupine Islands humped in the water in front of it and the granite domes of Acadia National Park rising behind. It is a small town of a couple of thousand year-round residents, but in summer it swells with visitors, cruise ship passengers and cyclists, and its streets fill with ice cream shops, lobster restaurants and outfitters.

  3. 3. Near Bar Harbor

    South of Bar Harbor, the Park Loop Road reaches the open Atlantic, and the scenery of Acadia changes from forest and lakes to pink granite cliffs, crashing surf and the smell of salt and spruce. This is the stretch that most people come for, and it is worth taking slowly, stopping often, and leaving the bike for short walks along the rocks.
