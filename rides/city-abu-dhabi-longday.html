Why this ride

A 426 km round trip from Abu Dhabi - roughly 7 h 21 in the saddle. On the way it passes Bidaa Bint Saud, Al Muwaiji Oasis, Al Jimi Oasis and Al Qattara Oasis. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Abu Dhabi

    Abu Dhabi sits on an island, joined to the mainland by bridges, and the flat green and white city that spreads across it grew from a fishing village with a fort. Qasr Al Hosn, the old white coral-and-sea-stone fort dating from the eighteenth century, still stands among the high-rises as the oldest building in the city, and it was the home of the ruling family before the oil era. Oil was found offshore in the late 1950s and exports began in the early 1960s, and when Sheikh Zayed became ruler he put much of the money into trees, roads and schools. In 1971, he became the first president of the United Arab Emirates.

  2. 2. Bidaa Bint Saud

    Jebel Hafit rises out of the flat gravel desert near Al Ain like a lizard's back, a bare, folded limestone ridge about twelve hundred metres high that marks the border between the United Arab Emirates and Oman. It is the best-known landmark in the area, and the scatter of ancient sites around it tells the story of the people who lived here. On the lower slopes are about five thousand years old beehive-shaped stone tombs, the first examples of a type of grave that is now named after the mountain, and the Hafit period is a recognised phase of Bronze Age archaeology in south-east Arabia.

  3. 3. Al Muwaiji Oasis

    Al Ain is called the Garden City, and the Al Muwaiji area gives you the reason. Inside a ring of mud-brick walls, date palms grow thick and tall, shading small plots of vegetables, citrus trees and fodder, watered by the old irrigation channels called aflaj. These are underground and open channels that bring water from the hills, some of them used for around three thousand years, and the system is still in use. UNESCO included the oases of Al Ain in its 2011 listing of the area's cultural sites, alongside the Bronze Age tombs of Hafit and Hili.

  4. 4. Al Jimi Oasis

    Al Jimi is one of those places where the desert simply stops being the main character. The oasis sits in the old part of Al Ain, a city the Emiratis call the Garden City, and a few kilometres on the other side of the line lies Al Buraymi in Oman. The two towns grew up around the same underground water and the same date gardens, and for most of history nobody drew a border through them.

  5. 5. Al Qattara Oasis

    Qattara is one of the old oases of Al Ain, a cluster of palm gardens that once fed a handful of mudbrick houses and towers. The area has been inhabited for thousands of years, and the whole group of oases is recognised by UNESCO for the way people have managed water in a place where it hardly rains. At Qattara you can still see the old layout: shaded lanes between walls, small farm plots under the palms, and a fort that was built to protect the wells and the people who relied on them.
