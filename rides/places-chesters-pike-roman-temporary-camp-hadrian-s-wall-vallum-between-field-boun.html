Why this ride

A 62 km round trip from Haltwhistle - roughly 53 min in the saddle. The stops in between: Hadrian's Wall and vallum between the fiel, Hadrian's Wall & vallum between field boun and back through Hadrian's Wall and vallum between the fiel. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The stops

  1. 1. Chesters Pike Roman temporary camp

    Start here.

    The country around Haltwhistle is where Hadrian's Wall is at its most dramatic. The Romans built it along the Whin Sill, a ridge of hard volcanic rock that rises in a long wall of crags above the farmland, and the ruins of milecastles, turrets and forts run along the top, making a line that climbs and dips like a stitched seam. Haltwhistle claims to be the centre of Britain, which no one can prove, and the town has a quiet, grey-stone high street. A Roman temporary camp, like the one at Chesters Pike, was a marching camp, where an army dug a ditch and raised a bank around a night's lodging, and left almost nothing behind. Often the only trace is a faint rectangle in the grass.

    Read the complete Chesters Pike Roman temporary camp write-up.

  2. 2. Hadrian's Wall and vallum between the fiel

    14 km in.

    Here the Wall is at its most dramatic. In the central sector of Hadrian's Wall, north of Haydon Bridge and Hexham in Northumberland, the Roman frontier runs along the top of the Whin Sill, a long ridge of hard volcanic rock that breaks the moorland into a series of north-facing cliffs. The Romans built their wall right along the crest, using the natural defences, and the result is a line of stone that rises and falls with the crags like the spine of a great animal. Behind it, to the south, runs the vallum, a broad earthwork ditch with mounds on either side that marked the military zone, still clearly visible across the fields.

    Read the complete Hadrian's Wall and vallum between the fiel write-up.

    27 km in.

    Hadrian's Wall runs for about seventy-three miles across the north of England, from the Solway Firth in the west to the Tyne in the east, built on the orders of the Emperor Hadrian after his visit to Britain in 122 AD.
