Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Waterbury

    Waterbury is a brick-and-clapboard village on the Winooski River, tucked into the valley where Vermont Route 100 meets the interstate, and it is the sort of place where you slow down on instinct. Mount Mansfield, Vermont's highest summit at 4,393 feet, is a short way north behind Stowe, and the Green Mountains close in on both sides here with steep, wooded flanks that go copper and scarlet in the first weeks of October.

  2. 2. Stowe

    Stowe sits in a bowl of the Green Mountains, with the long ridge of Mount Mansfield rising behind it, and its white church steeple is perhaps the most photographed in Vermont. Mansfield, at 4,393 feet the highest point in the state, has a profile that resembles a human face, with a forehead, nose, lips and chin along the ridgeline, and the summit is reached by a toll road in summer and by ski lifts in winter. The town has about four thousand residents, a lively main street of shops and restaurants, and a long tradition of skiing that goes back to the 1930s.
