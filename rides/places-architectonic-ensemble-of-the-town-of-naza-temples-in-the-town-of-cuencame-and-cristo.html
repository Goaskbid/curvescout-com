Why this ride

A 151 km round trip from Nazas - roughly 2 h 24 in the saddle. On the way it passes Chapel of the Refugio of the former hacien, Temples in the town of Cuencamé and Cristo and back through Chapel of the Refugio of the former hacien. It tops out around 1,850 m.

The stops

  1. 1. Architectonic ensemble of the Town of Naza

    Start here.

    Nazas is a small town on the river of the same name in the north of the state of Durango, where the Sierra Madre Occidental gives way to the high, dry basins of the Mexican interior. It sits on the route of the Camino Real de Tierra Adentro, the old royal road that ran north from Mexico City to Santa Fe, carrying silver, mercury, goods and settlers for nearly three centuries. A group of its historic buildings, around the main square and church, was included in the UNESCO listing of the Camino Real in 2010.

    Read the complete Architectonic ensemble of the Town of Naza write-up.

  2. 2. Chapel of the Refugio of the former hacien

    47 km in.

    Hacienda chapels tell you a lot about colonial Mexico. They were built by landowners who controlled vast estates of cattle, crops or mines, and they served as places of worship for the owners, their families and the workers who lived and laboured on the estate. The Chapel of the Refugio, once part of a hacienda near Cuencamé in the state of Durango, is one of the sites included in the UNESCO listing of the Camino Real de Tierra Adentro, the royal road that ran from Mexico City to Santa Fe and carried silver, goods, missionaries and settlers across the northern frontier for nearly three centuries.

    Read the complete Chapel of the Refugio of the former hacien write-up.

  3. 3. Temples in the town of Cuencamé and Cristo

    76 km in.

    Cuencamé is a small town in the semi-desert of eastern Durango, on the old Camino Real de Tierra Adentro, and it is known throughout northern Mexico for its devotion to the Cristo de Mapimí, a crucifix that is venerated in a sanctuary in the town. The image, according to tradition, was brought here in the colonial period after miracles attributed to it, and each year pilgrims arrive from across the region to pray and give thanks. The town's temples, including the parish church and the sanctuary, are part of the UNESCO listing of the Camino Real. They are simple, solid buildings, with thick walls designed to resist the heat, and their interiors are filled with offerings, candles and the quiet murmur of prayer.
