Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Korčula

    Korčula old town stands on a small peninsula that sticks out into the channel between the island and the mainland's Pelješac, and from the sea it looks like a fortress made of honey-coloured stone: a ring of walls, towers and a cathedral with a belltower on the top of a rise. The streets inside are laid out in a herringbone pattern, with the main street along the ridge and the side lanes angled to break the force of the wind and let in the breeze, a clever piece of medieval planning that is still obvious on a map.

  2. 2. Vela Luka

    Vela Luka lies at the very western end of Korčula, at the head of a long, well-sheltered bay that gave the town its name, which simply means big harbour. After the old walled town of Korčula on the eastern tip, this is the island's second centre, and it feels more like a working port than a postcard. Ferries to Split come and go, small fishing boats work the bay, and the pace of the place follows the boats rather than the tourists.
