Why this ride

49 km from Alicante to Alicante, about 53 min of riding. It goes by way of Cueva de los mosquitos, Cimbarrillo Prado Reches, Cueva de la mina and Cimbarrillo ma Antonia I.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Alicante

    Alicante is a bright, easy city built on a rock. The Castillo de Santa Bárbara sits on top of Mount Benacantil and watches the harbour from about 170 metres up, and on a clear day the view runs all the way along the coast. The promenade below, the Explanada de España, is paved with a wave pattern in red, cream and black marble, with palms on both sides, and it is the natural place to start an evening: the whole town seems to be on that walk.

  2. 2. Cueva de los mosquitos

    Alicante is the city that everybody knows, but the hills behind it are the part you can have to yourself. This shelter lies in the dry interior north of the city, between El Campello on the coast and the towns of Mutxamel and San Juan, in a landscape of marl, pine and abandoned almond terraces. It is a long way from the seafront.

  3. 3. Cimbarrillo Prado Reches

    The hills behind Alicante rise quickly. A few kilometres inland from the Costa Blanca, the flat coastal strip gives way to dry limestone ridges covered in scrub, pine, carob and almond, with deep ravines cut by streams that run only after heavy rain. These are the foothills of the Betic ranges, and in this part of the province the land climbs towards the sierras of Aitana and Mariola. El Campello, a former fishing town of about 26,000 people on the coast, is the closest sizeable place, and the nearby resort towns fill with visitors every summer, but the hinterland stays quieter and more rural.

  4. 4. Cueva de la mina

    The Cueva de la Mina lies in the hills behind Alicante's northern suburbs, close enough to the coast that you can leave a beach town after lunch and be among rock and scrub within the hour. That gives it a particular pleasure: the contrast. One moment you are among apartment blocks and palm trees at El Campello, a former fishing village that has become a seaside town of twenty-six thousand people, and the next, you are on a narrow lane with the sea shrinking behind you and the sun pressing on the stones.

  5. 5. Cimbarrillo ma Antonia I

    Cimbarrillo is a name that does not appear on tourist maps, and Ma Antonia, the woman or the farm it recalls, has been lost to anyone but the locals. The shelter, listed as number I, lies in the dry hills of the Alicante coast, between El Campello and Villajoyosa, in the same band of broken limestone as the Garganta de la Hoz shelters. Its red paintings are in the Levantine style and belong to the UNESCO listing for the rock art of the Mediterranean basin.
