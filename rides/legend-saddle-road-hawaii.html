Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Near Hilo

    Rain is the first fact about Hilo. The town gets well over a hundred inches a year, making it one of the wettest cities in the United States, and the rain is what turned this side of the Big Island into a lush tangle of ferns, orchids, bamboo and waterfalls. Hilo is a low, tidy, slightly faded town of forty thousand people curved around a bay, with old wooden shopfronts, a farmers market that sprawls across several blocks, and the vast shoulder of Mauna Kea rising unseen behind its cloud.

  4. 4. Waimea

    Waimea is cowboy country, and that is a strange thing to find on a tropical island. The town, also known by its postal name Kamuela, lies on a high, cool, windy plateau at about twenty-five hundred feet, between the Kohala Mountains and the huge bulk of Mauna Kea. The land is open grass, dotted with eucalyptus windbreaks, cattle and horses, and on a misty morning it looks a lot more like Scotland or Wyoming than Hawaii.
