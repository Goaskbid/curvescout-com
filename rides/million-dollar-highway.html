Why this ride

Out of Silverton, one way: 70 km, roughly 1 h 12 in the saddle. The stops in between: Red Mountain Pass and Bear Creek Falls. The highest point is about 3,600 m. It suits an unhurried touring pace.

Scenic road chapters

Million Dollar Highway

The last of the trees go and the road runs out onto open ground. The summit sits at 3358 m, and what surrounds it is rock, weather and distance — cold comes early up here and stays late. Ride it with something in reserve: the summit is not the end of the work, only the middle of it.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Silverton

    Silverton is a mining town that never quite stopped, and the proof is in the muddy streets. It sits at about 9,300 feet in a high basin in the San Juan Mountains, ringed by peaks of more than 13,000 feet, and the single paved main street, Greene Street, is lined with false-fronted wooden buildings from the 1880s, all in good repair. About six hundred people live here year-round, and in winter the town is cut off by snow and avalanches, while in summer it fills with visitors from the train and the road.

  2. 2. Red Mountain Pass

    Red Mountain Pass crosses the San Juan Mountains at 11,018 feet, and the road over it, US 550 between Ouray and Silverton, is part of the stretch known as the Million Dollar Highway, one of the most famous and most frightening roads in the United States. The old mining district of Red Mountain lay to the south, and the rusty remains of mills and shafts can still be seen on the slopes, where the Yankee Girl and other mines once produced silver.

  3. 3. Bear Creek Falls

    Between Ouray and Red Mountain Pass, the Million Dollar Highway does something that makes first-time riders slow right down: it clings to a cliff, with no guardrails in places, while a waterfall drops into the gorge beside it. Bear Creek Falls is that moment. The creek tumbles hundreds of feet from the heights above, passes beneath the road on a bridge, and then plunges into the canyon of the Uncompahgre River far below. A small pull-off lets you stop, though you should do it deliberately, because the traffic behind you will not.

  4. 4. Ouray

    Ouray sits at the bottom of a box. Tall red and grey cliffs rise on three sides of the town, leaving only a narrow gap to the north, and the whole place looks like a Swiss village that took a wrong turn into Colorado, which explains why it calls itself the Switzerland of America. About a thousand people live here, in Victorian wooden houses along a main street lined with shops, hotels and a hot springs pool fed by the same geothermal water that bubbles up around the town. An hour in the pool, with the cliffs above, is the standard remedy for stiff shoulders after a long ride.

Recorded leg-by-leg notes

These source notes may contain earlier estimates. They are not turn-by-turn directions or current measured distances.

Silverton to Red Mountain Pass

Silverton → Red Mountain Pass climbs about 3358 m in 13 km. This is the part worth slowing down for - look through the bends and use the pull-offs.

Red Mountain Pass to Bear Creek Falls

Red Mountain Pass → Bear Creek Falls drops about 3358 m over 14 km. Brakes and eyes forward; the views are behind the corners, not in them.

Bear Creek Falls to Ouray

Bear Creek Falls → Ouray is 4 km of rhythm rather than effort. Let it flow and let the view breathe.
