  1. 1. La Junta Chile

    La Junta is a small town where two rivers meet, which is exactly what the name says, and it marks the point where the Carretera Austral leaves the Palena valley and its wet green forests and enters the northern edge of the Aysen region. About twelve hundred people live here, scattered along wide, quiet streets, with lakes close by and snow-capped peaks showing above the trees. It is a handy place on a very empty road, with a fuel pump, a few shops, simple restaurants and guesthouses.

  2. 2. Puyuhuapi

    Puyuhuapi lies at the head of a long, narrow fjord on the Carretera Austral, in a part of Chilean Patagonia where the mountains drop straight into the sea and the rain seems to follow you from one valley to the next. The village is small, with a few hundred people, and it was founded in 1935 by a handful of settlers from Central Europe who arrived in a wild, roadless land and built a life around a sawmill, a pier and later a carpet workshop that still weaves rugs. The wooden houses with steep roofs, the pier on the water and the boats at anchor give the place a quiet, slightly northern look.

  3. 3. Queulat

    Queulat is one of the great set pieces of the Carretera Austral, a national park where a glacier hangs from a high ridge and spills a waterfall from its edge, and where the road climbs and twists through deep forest on the way to Puyuhuapi and the south. The park lies on the Pacific side of the Andes, in the rainy zone, and the nearest settlement of any size is Puerto Cisnes on the coast, with Puyuhuapi a little north on the fjord. Fuel is available in Puyuhuapi and along the main road, but not in the park.
