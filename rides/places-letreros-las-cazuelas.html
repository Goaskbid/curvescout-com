Why this ride

A 255 km round trip from Velez Rubio - roughly 4 h 24 in the saddle. On the way it passes Cueva de la Plata, Las Cazuelas and back through Cueva de la Plata. The highest point is about 1,150 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Letreros

    There is a little figure on a postcard from Almería that you will see on car stickers, jewellery and shop signs across the province, and it started here. The Indalo, a stick-like man holding an arch above his head, is the symbol of Almería, and it comes from a painting in the Cueva de los Letreros near Vélez-Blanco. The painted figure, called the Brujo or Sorcerer, was found in the early twentieth century, and local people adopted the shape as a good-luck charm and a mark of local pride.

  2. 2. Cueva de la Plata

    Aledo is a tiny village on a rocky spur in the hills of Murcia, a place that punches well above its weight in history. In the eleventh century, it was a fortress on the frontier between Christian Castile and the Muslim taifa kingdoms, and the siege of Aledo in 1088 was a major episode in the struggle for the peninsula. A tower still stands above the village. The Cueva de la Plata, the silver cave, lies in the hills nearby, between Aledo, Totana and Alhama de Murcia.

  3. 3. Las Cazuelas

    Moratalla is the kind of hill town that looks as though it was poured down a slope: white houses tumbling below a castle tower, narrow lanes too steep for comfort, church bells echoing off the hills. Ten kilometres out, in the rugged country of north-west Murcia, the shelter of Las Cazuelas holds prehistoric paintings, part of the region's contribution to the UNESCO-listed Rock Art of the Mediterranean Basin. The paintings are fragile and protected, and access is usually by guided visit, arranged in Moratalla or through the regional authorities.
