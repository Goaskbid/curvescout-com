Why this ride

From Brampton it runs 287 km to Brampton; allow about 5 h of riding. The stops in between: Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 66, Skinburness, Burrow Walls Roman fort and Ravenglass Roman fort bath-house. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

The roads

Bewcastle Roman fort → Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 66

It is 31 km from Bewcastle Roman fort to Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 66 on Brampton Road. Hardly any climbing; it runs between 15 and 147 m. This is the bit the ride is built around.

The stops

  1. 1. Bewcastle Roman fort

    Start here.

    Bewcastle is one of the loneliest places in Cumbria, and it has been for a very long time. Roman soldiers built an outpost fort here, about ten kilometres north of Hadrian's Wall, on a plateau whose shape the walls followed, giving it an unusual six-sided plan. It was a forward base for scouts watching the tribes beyond the frontier, and it was linked to the Wall at Birdoswald by a road. Later, a medieval castle was built into one corner of the old Roman defences, and its broken walls still stand above the beck.

    Read the complete Bewcastle Roman fort write-up.

  2. 2. Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 66

    31 km in.

    The western end of Hadrian's Wall runs right through Carlisle, and the stretch counted as wall mile 66 lies on the edge of the city, close to where the wall crossed the River Eden. The Roman town of Luguvalium grew up here, and the fort at Stanwix, across the river, housed a cavalry regiment and was the largest on the wall. Very little of the stone wall survives in the city, much of it having been robbed for buildings, but the line of the wall and vallum can be traced in places, and the Tullie House museum tells the story of Roman Carlisle with excellent displays.

    Read the complete Hadrian's Wall and vallum in wall mile 66 write-up.

  3. 3. Skinburness

    67 km in.

    Skinburness is a scatter of houses at the tip of a shingle spit on the Solway coast of Cumbria, just north of Silloth, looking across the wide, shimmering estuary to the hills of Scotland. When the tide goes out, the Solway empties into vast expanses of sand and mud, with channels winding through them, and the light changes constantly as clouds race overhead. On clear evenings, the sunsets over the Scottish hills, with the dark bulk of Criffel opposite, are spectacular.

    Read the complete Skinburness write-up.

  4. 4. Burrow Walls Roman fort

    100 km in.

    On the northern edge of Workington, near the mouth of the river Derwent, a fragment of stone wall stands alone in a field, a jagged piece of masonry rising above the grass. It is part of Burrow Walls, a Roman fort that guarded the Cumbrian coast, one of the chain of defences that extended from the western end of Hadrian's Wall down the shore to watch for raiders crossing from Scotland and Ireland. Much of the fort's stone was reused in a medieval building on the site, and what survives today is modest, but it is part of the Hadrian's Wall World Heritage site and has a lonely charm, with views over the sea and the river.

    Read the complete Burrow Walls Roman fort write-up.

  5. 5. Ravenglass Roman fort bath-house

    144 km in.

    Walls Castle is the odd name for a Roman bath house, and the building still stands to a height of about four metres, one of the tallest Roman structures in northern England. It stood outside the fort of Glannoventa at Ravenglass, a harbour station at the meeting of three rivers, the Irt, the Mite and the Esk, and in the dry shelter of the dunes it has survived because its walls were thick and well built. You can walk around it on a short path from the village, and see niches and a doorway in the thick walls. The fort itself lies mostly under farmland and the railway.

    Read the complete Ravenglass Roman fort bath-house write-up.
