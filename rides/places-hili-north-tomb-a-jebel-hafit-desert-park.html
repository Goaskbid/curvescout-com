Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Hili North Tomb A

    The Hili tombs sit in the oasis landscape where Oman meets the United Arab Emirates, and the border here is a line on a map rather than a barrier on the ground. The Omani town of Buraimi and the Emirati city of Al Ain run into each other, with palms, fields and low sandy hills, and these stone tombs are among the oldest standing monuments in the region. They are circular towers of carefully cut limestone, built around 2500 BC in what archaeologists call the Umm an-Nar period, and they were used as communal graves.

  2. 2. West Ridge Hafit Tombs

    The road up Jebel Hafit is one of the best-known motorcycle climbs in the Gulf, and it is easy to understand why. It runs for about twelve kilometres, with dozens of well-surfaced bends and hairpins that wind up the flank of the mountain to a summit plateau at roughly 1,200 metres, and from there you can see the whole of Al Ain spread out below and the desert running into the haze. In a country of motorways and flat horizons, it is a gift.

  3. 3. Jebel Hafit Desert Park

    For riders in the Gulf, there is a road that people talk about with real affection. Jebel Hafit, a long, rocky ridge that rises out of the flat gravel plain on the border between the United Arab Emirates and Oman, is about 1,240 metres high, and a smooth tarmac road snakes up its eastern flank in a long series of hairpins and bends to the top, a distance of about eleven kilometres. At the summit, a hotel and a viewing terrace look out over the oasis city of Al Ain, with the desert stretching away to the horizon, and at dusk the lights come on below like a field of stars. Motorcyclists and cyclists come here in the cooler months, usually early in the morning or at sunset, and the road is one of the best in the country.
