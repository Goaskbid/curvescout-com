Why this ride

A 660 km round trip from Cusco - roughly 14 h 15 in the saddle. It goes by way of Segment Machucucho-Choquecancha, Historic Sanctuary of Machu Picchu and back through Segment Machucucho-Choquecancha. It tops out around 4,100 m. The line favours views over speed.

The roads

Sacred Valley Route

The 218 km between Segment Machucucho-Choquecancha and Historic Sanctuary of Machu Picchu, mostly on CU-105. Over the top at 2,982 m - roughly 940 m up and 2190 m down. Above 2,000 m the weather turns fast; have a layer and a plan B.

Rider note

Check the pass is open before you commit to it.

The stops

  1. 1. Segment Plaza Inca Hanan: Hauk’aypataqolla

    Start here.

    Every road of the Inca empire began here. The great square of Cusco, which the Incas called Hauk'aypata, was the ceremonial heart of Tawantinsuyu, the land of the four quarters, and from it the main roads radiated out to the four parts of the empire, to Quito in the north, to Chile and Argentina in the south, to the jungle in the east and the coast in the west. The square was much larger than today's Plaza de Armas and was covered, according to the chroniclers, with sand brought from the coast, in which offerings of gold and silver were buried. Here, mummies of past rulers were brought out for festivals, and the Inca received ambassadors and celebrated victories.

    Read the complete Segment Plaza Inca Hanan: Hauk’aypataqolla write-up.

  2. 2. Segment Machucucho-Choquecancha

    112 km in.

    The Urubamba river is the great artery of Inca country, and everything near it feels like a rehearsal for Machu Picchu. This segment of the Qhapaq Nan, the Inca road network listed by UNESCO in 2014, runs between two places named Machucucho and Choquecancha in the highlands north-east of Cusco, where side valleys climb from the main river towards high passes and snow peaks.

    Read the complete Segment Machucucho-Choquecancha write-up.

  3. 3. Historic Sanctuary of Machu Picchu

    330 km in. The ride passes about 7 km from it.

    Machu Picchu is the most famous place in the Andes, and it is also one of the hardest to reach for a rider, because no road goes there. The sanctuary is about thirty kilometres from Ollantaytambo, on a ridge nearly twenty-four hundred metres above the sea, above a loop of the Urubamba river, with cloud forest on every side. The citadel is reached only by train or by walking, and then by a bus up a zigzag road from the town of Aguas Calientes, at the foot of the mountain.
