Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Dar es Salaam

    Dar es Salaam means haven of peace, a name that seems optimistic for the largest city in Tanzania, where traffic jams last for hours and the sidewalks are crowded from dawn to late at night. The city grew from a fishing village into a port in the 1860s, when the Sultan of Zanzibar built a residence here, and was later the capital of German East Africa and then of British-ruled Tanganyika. The government has moved officially to Dodoma, but Dar remains the business capital, with a busy port, a growing skyline and a population of several million.

  2. 2. Stone Town of Zanzibar

    Stone Town is the old heart of Zanzibar City, on the west coast of the island of Unguja, and it is a maze of narrow lanes, coral-stone houses, carved doors and mosques that grew out of centuries of trade across the Indian Ocean. Monsoon winds carried dhows from Arabia, Persia and India to this coast, and Swahili, Arab, Indian and European influences have mixed here, leaving a town that is a World Heritage property. The huge wooden doors, studded with brass spikes and carved with chains and flowers, were once a sign of a merchant's wealth, and some are over two hundred years old.
