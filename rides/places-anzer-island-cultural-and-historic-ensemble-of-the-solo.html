Why this ride

It goes by way of Big Mucksalma Island, Cultural and Historic Ensemble of the Solo and back through Big Mucksalma Island. It stays on the island.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Anzer Island

    Anzer is the second-largest of the Solovetsky Islands, a small archipelago in the White Sea that has played an outsized role in Russian history. A monastery was founded on the main island in the fifteenth century, and it grew into a fortress, wealthy and powerful, with granite walls whose boulders were moved by hand. On Anzer, the monks established a hermitage, or skete, which sits on a hill: a landscape of pine, spruce, small lakes and cold sea that looks very much like Finland's coasts.

  2. 2. Big Mucksalma Island

    Bolshaya Muksalma is a low, quiet island in the White Sea, part of the Solovetsky archipelago, and it is not a place you reach by motorway. The islands lie in the cold, shallow water of Onega Bay, and getting there takes a boat or a small plane in the short summer season, so anyone thinking about this point as a motorcycle stop should first work out the logistics of crossing the water with a vehicle. The island itself is flat, with grassy meadows, small pine woods and a rocky shore, and a long stone causeway built by the monks connects it to the main island, Great Solovetsky.

  3. 3. Cultural and Historic Ensemble of the Solo

    On a cold northern island in the White Sea, a monk called Savvatiy and later a priest called Zosima and a hermit called German built a monastery in the 1430s that would become one of the richest and most powerful in Russia. The Solovetsky Islands, six in all, lie about a hundred and fifty kilometres south of the Arctic Circle, and the monks turned them into a self-sufficient little kingdom, with fish ponds, mills, salt works and a harbour, and a great fortress, built in the sixteenth century with boulders the size of cars. The Cathedral of the Transfiguration, with its massive whitewashed walls and onion domes, stands at the centre of a walled compound that looks, from the water, like a medieval city. UNESCO listed the ensemble in 1992.
