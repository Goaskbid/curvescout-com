Why this ride

A 493 km round trip from Yonggang-ŭp - roughly 7 h 08 in the saddle. On the way it passes Anak Tomb No. 3, Mausoleum of King Kongmin and back through Anak Tomb No. 3. The line favours views over speed.

The stops

  1. 1. Twin-Column Tomb

    Start here.

    Before anything else, an honest note: independent travel in North Korea is not possible, and visitors can only go on organised tours with official guides, so a ride here is a matter of reading about a place rather than taking a route. With that said, the Twin-Column Tomb sits near Yonggang, a town of about thirteen thousand on the plain near the coast, not far from the larger city of Nampo, and it belongs to the Koguryo kingdom, one of the Three Kingdoms of ancient Korea.

    Read the complete Twin-Column Tomb write-up.

  2. 2. Anak Tomb No. 3

    95 km in.

    Some tombs hold their dead, and others hold a whole world. Anak Tomb No. 3 in South Hwanghae Province is one of the great painted tombs of the Goguryeo kingdom, which ruled much of Korea and Manchuria from the first century BC to 668 AD. The chamber, built of stone slabs and covered with plaster, is decorated with murals showing a kitchen, a stable, a smokehouse, musicians and a long procession of soldiers, officials and attendants, some two hundred and fifty figures by the usual count. An inscription dated to 357 AD names a man called Dong Shou, a refugee from the Chinese states, and for years that was taken to be the tomb's owner, though some scholars argue it may have belonged to a Goguryeo king.

    Read the complete Anak Tomb No. 3 write-up.

  3. 3. Mausoleum of King Kongmin

    247 km in.

    Of all the places on the list of world heritage, the Mausoleum of King Kongmin must be one of the hardest to reach on two wheels, because it lies in North Korea, in the hills west of the old city of Kaesong.
