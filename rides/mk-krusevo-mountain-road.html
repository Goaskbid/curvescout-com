  1. 1. Prilep

    Prilep spreads across a broad plain in the south of North Macedonia, with a ring of granite hills around it, and it is a city that is both agricultural and historic. Tobacco has long been the crop here, and the surrounding fields produce much of the country's leaf, which is why the town has a tobacco institute and why drying sheds sometimes show up on the edges of the road. The beer and the old bazaar are both part of local life, and the city has a reputation for being a place where people take their time with coffee.

  2. 2. Krusevo

    Kruševo is the highest town in North Macedonia, at about 1,350 metres, and it sits on a steep mountainside above the Pelagonia plain in a cluster of old stone houses, cobbled lanes and tiled roofs. The air is cooler than in the valleys, and the winters are long, with snow that closes the higher roads for days. It is a town that tends to be remembered for its place in the story of the Ilinden Uprising, the revolt against Ottoman rule in August 1903, when the short-lived Kruševo Republic was proclaimed. A large, strange concrete monument, the Makedonium, stands on a hill above town to commemorate it, built in the 1970s in a futurist style, with a domed interior and circular windows, and from its terrace you can see across the plain to the distant mountains.

  3. 3. Demir Hisar

    Demir Hisar, the name meaning iron fortress in Turkish, is a small town in the south-west of North Macedonia, in a green hollow between the Plakenska mountains and the Ohrid highlands. It lies along the Crna Reka, the Black River, the long tributary of the Vardar that runs through the area before turning east, and the river valley is the focus of local life. The countryside around is fertile and rolling, with cherry orchards, meadows and beech woods, and the pace is slow, with older men on benches and tractors moving along the main road.
