Why this ride

curated scenic road. forest, waterfalls, mountain lakes. From Freudenstadt it runs 109 km to Waldshut-Tiengen; allow about 1 h 59 of riding. On the way it passes Triberg, Titisee-Neustadt and Todtnau. The highest point is about 1,250 m. It was put together for the scenery rather than the shortest line.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Freudenstadt

    Freudenstadt was built to a plan. In 1599, Duke Frederick I of Württemberg laid out the town as a refuge for Protestants fleeing persecution in Austria, and the heart of it is a vast market square, one of the largest in Germany, an almost perfect square about two hundred metres on each side, with covered arcades along its edges. There was meant to be a castle in the middle, but it was never built, and the open space became the town's living room instead. The town, with about twenty-four thousand people, was almost destroyed at the end of the Second World War and rebuilt in a similar style.

  2. 2. Triberg

    Triberg is built in a steep, narrow fold of the Black Forest, a town that seems to have been poured down a gorge and set hard. Houses climb both sides of the Gutach valley, rooftops overlap, and the main street runs steeply uphill past shops full of cuckoo clocks, carved wooden bears and cherry schnapps. It is touristy, no question, but the tourists come because the place is the real thing, the heartland of the Black Forest clock trade, where families have been making timepieces in workshops since the eighteenth century.

  3. 3. Near Titisee-Neustadt

    Neustadt in the Black Forest has none of the lakeside bustle of its neighbour Titisee, and it is better for it. This is a working mountain town of about twelve thousand people on the Gutach river, with a solid church, a market square and a railway station where the Höllentalbahn from Freiburg arrives after its long climb. The line was an engineering feat when it was built in the nineteenth century, winding up the Höllental gorge with a steep rack section in its early decades, and the station is a handsome reminder of the days when the railway was the great modern invention.
