Why this ride

63 km one way from Bregenz - plan on about 1 h 03 of riding. The stops in between: Egg, Bezau and Schoppernau. The highest point is about 1,950 m. A steady touring day rather than a sprint.

Waypoint introductions and full write-ups

  1. 1. Bregenz

    Bregenz is the Austrian end of Lake Constance, and the lake is the whole point. The Bodensee stretches out in front of the town like a small sea, flat and silver in the early light, with Germany and Switzerland on its far shores and the Alps rising right behind your shoulder. Bregenz is the capital of Vorarlberg, with around thirty thousand people, so you will find every kind of shop, fuel station and café you might need before you set off. It is an easy place to begin a ride and an easy place to put off beginning it.

  2. 2. Egg

    Egg is the sort of village where the first thing you notice is the wood. Houses, barns, bus shelters and cafes are all clad in timber, in an elegant, modern way that has made the Bregenzerwald one of the most admired regions in Europe for contemporary country architecture. The older farmhouses have shingled facades that have weathered to silver, and the newer ones echo the style so faithfully that you can hardly tell where one era stops.

  3. 3. Bezau

    Bezau is a handsome valley village, with a church tower, a few grand hotels from the age of early summer tourism and a pleasant, slightly sleepy main street. It stands on the Bregenzerach in the middle of the Bregenzerwald, at the foot of the Bezauer Wanne, the hollow in the mountain wall behind the village, and a cable car climbs to the pastures above if you have time for a walk with a view.

  4. 4. Schoppernau

    Schoppernau is the village where the Bregenzerwald starts to run out of valley. Beyond it the road climbs toward the Hochtannberg Pass, and the mountains that were polite and green in Egg and Bezau become taller and rockier. Houses are still clad in timber, fields are still steep and immaculately mown, but the sense of enclosure is stronger, and the big limestone walls of the Allgäu and Lechquellen mountains look down on the village from all sides.

  5. 5. Warth

    Warth is a high Walser village on the Vorarlberg side of the Arlberg, a cluster of wooden chalets and ski lifts at the end of the Lech valley's road. It sits at around 1,500 metres, a short ride from Lech, and it has a reputation among skiers as one of the snowiest places in the Alps; locals will tell you that winter can bring metres at a time. In summer it is quiet and rather beautiful, with the meadows steep above the road and the cowbells carrying across the whole valley.
