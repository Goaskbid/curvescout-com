Why this ride

Small roads, water light and a proper evening reset. A 71 km loop that starts and ends near Langnau am Albis, about 1 h 21 of riding. The stops in between: Ägerisee water stop and Zug window. Ratenpass is the high point. It tops out at 1,077 m. It is a high-mountain day.

Roadbook

  1. Albis ridge warm-up start · 791 m

    The Albis is the long, wooded ridge that rises between the Sihl valley and the Reppisch valley south of Zurich, and the road over it has been the traditional way from the city toward Zug and central Switzerland for centuries. The ride begins near Langnau am Albis, a town of about seven thousand people on the eastern flank, with the Albis Pass, at about 790 metres, a few kilometres up the hill. It is an excellent warm-up: just enough bends and gradient to get the tyres warm without asking for much, and close enough to the city for an after-work ride.

    The road climbs from the valley through beech forest in a series of easy bends, with the ridge close on one side and views over the Sihl valley, the Zurich lake and the Alps on the other. The surface is well maintained, the corners are open, and the traffic can be dense at commuting times, with heavy flows of cars and buses and the occasional cyclist grinding up the slope. After the pass, the road drops toward the Knonauer Amt and the Reuss valley, with farmland, orchards and small villages. Speed limits are strict and cameras are common, so ride accordingly. The best conditions are on dry evenings in late spring and early autumn, when the light is gold on the forest.

    The ridge has a history as a frontier. In 1799, during the Napoleonic wars, French and Russian armies fought in the area around Zurich, and the heights around the Albis were part of the lines. The old road over the pass was a major route for merchants and pilgrims. Today the Felsenegg viewpoint, on a spur near Adliswil, is a popular spot for sunset, reached by cable car, and the Uetliberg, at the northern end of the ridge, is Zurich's local mountain, with a lookout tower and a railway.

    Nature on the ridge is surprisingly wild. Beyond Langnau, the Sihlwald is a former working forest that has been left to regrow and is now part of a wilderness park, with deer, foxes and a rich bird life, and the old trees there are slowly returning to a natural state. In spring the beech leaves are an unreal fresh green, and in autumn the forest glows copper. The air smells of earth and leaves, and the quiet in the woods is welcome after the city.

    Fuel and food are plentiful in Langnau and the villages nearby. Bring a light waterproof, since the weather changes quickly on the ridge, and take care on damp leaves.

  2. Ratenpass rhythm pass · 1077 m

    The Ratenpass is a short, quick climb between the Ägerisee valley and the Biberbrugg side, topping out at 1,077 metres. It is not famous like the Gotthard or the Grimsel, and that suits the riders who use it, because the traffic is light and the road is built for fun rather than freight. From the lakeside at Unterägeri the road climbs through forest, then breaks out into open pasture with a broad view toward the Mythen and the Alps beyond. Cows graze right up to the verge, and in summer you can hear the bells from the road.

    The word rhythm fits. There are no long straights, no abrupt hairpins, and the bends flow into one another in a way that rewards smooth steering. The surface is usually good, with occasional patches where frost has cracked the asphalt. On the Schwyz side the gradient steepens a little, and the corners tighten as the road drops toward Schindellegi and the lake of Zurich. Local riders use it for after-work blasts, and it tends to be busiest on warm weekday evenings, when you may share the climb with a few like-minded riders and a handful of cyclists.

    The landscape is part of a larger story. The ridge lies in the cantonal borderland between Zug and Schwyz, with small farms, moors and woodland. Peat once cut from the bogs fed local stoves, and the traditional farmhouses with wide eaves tell you this is cheese and cattle country. The nearby moors are protected, and in spring the cotton grass and orchids bloom in the wet hollows. If you stop and listen, you can hear the quiet hum of a landscape that has changed little in a hundred years.

    Fuel is available in Unterägeri, about six kilometres away, and in Schindellegi on the other side. A few farm restaurants and inns appear near the top, but opening hours depend on the season and the day.

    Check the weather before you climb. Fog forms on the ridge, and a bright valley can hide a damp, misty summit. In winter the road can carry snow and ice on the shaded side, and it is usually cleared less promptly than the main roads. On a clear evening in early summer, though, it is one of the loveliest quick climbs in the area.

  3. Ägerisee water stop swim · 724 m

    The Ägerisee is the sort of lake that locals prefer to keep to themselves. It lies at about 724 metres in the canton of Zug, a small oval of clean water surrounded by meadows, forest and low hills, with the villages of Unterägeri at the northern end and Oberägeri on the eastern shore. The Lorze river drains it toward Zug, and in the south the ground rises to the ridge of Morgarten, where in 1315 the Swiss Confederates won their famous victory over a Habsburg army. The landscape is calm now, and it is hard to imagine knights on the narrow shore path.

    Swimming is the main reason for the stop, and the lake is good for it. The water is clear and cool, and because the lake sits a bit higher than the plains below, it never gets as warm as the lowland waters. There are public bathing areas with grass and jetties at Unterägeri, and in summer you will find families, paddleboarders and rowers. In winter, the surface sometimes freezes at the edges, though full ice cover is rare. Fishermen work from small boats, and the local restaurants serve lake fish such as whitefish, which is the usual choice.

    For riding, the roads around the lake are gentle, with fine views and a feeling of ease. They lead to the Ratenpass and Sattel, and to the climbs of the Zug mountains. Traffic is moderate, and weekends bring cyclists and walkers, so keep your speed under control near the shore. Evenings are the best time, with long light on the water and the hills turning blue and gold.

    Fuel and shops are in Unterägeri, about two kilometres from the water, and Zug is a short ride downhill. There are cafés near the lakefront, and in the village you can find bakeries and small restaurants. Parking for motorcycles is easy near the bathing areas, although it can get busy on hot Sundays, so arrive before midday if you can.

    Bring a towel, because the air near the lake can be warm while the water is bracing. A quick swim is enough to wake you up, and the walk back to the bike feels like a small reward for a day on the road.

  4. Zug food window stop · 413 m

    Zug has a reputation as a place for banks, crypto start-ups and low taxes, and the first impression, with modern glass blocks beside the lake, does not argue. But keep walking toward the water and the old town appears, tight and clean, with a medieval clock tower, painted gables and arcaded lanes, all looking over the Zugersee toward the Rigi. It is a town of about twenty-three thousand people that tends to be a lot nicer than its press, and it is especially good for a meal between the mountain roads that surround it.

    The riding here is the real attraction. East of town, the climb toward the Raten pass, at about 1,070 metres, runs through the green Ägerital, with the little Ägerisee glinting below, and beyond it the road drops to Biberbrugg and the Sihl. The bends are neat and rhythmic, the surface is good and the gradients friendly, a sort of finishing school for corners. Near the Ägerisee, a memorial marks Morgarten, where in 1315 a force of Swiss confederates ambushed an Austrian army, a battle that became part of the founding story of Switzerland. Cows with bells, orchards and little farm shops sit along the way.

    Weather in the Pre-Alps is changeable. Mist often hangs on the lake in the morning, and in summer afternoons can bring a sharp thunderstorm, while on a clear autumn day the whole chain of the Alps from Pilatus to the Glarus peaks appears to stand along the horizon. Weekends bring cyclists and other riders, and the road can bunch up behind slow cars, so a weekday evening is more enjoyable.

    As a food window, Zug is hard to beat. Lakeside restaurants serve Egli fillets and other freshwater fish, and the town's famous Kirschtorte, a cake of nut meringue layers, buttercream and local cherry brandy, is sold in every bakery. Fuel is easy to find, with several stations in town and in neighbouring Baar, about three and a half kilometres away. Parking for motorcycles is generally easy, though the old town is partly pedestrian. A coffee in the late afternoon, in the sun on the quay, is the best preparation for the ride home.
