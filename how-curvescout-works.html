CURVESCOUT / PLAN THE WHOLE RIDE

How CurveScout turns ride ideas into a plan

Discovery, route evidence and current conditions answer different questions. This page explains what each can establish.

Who publishes the site

CurveScout is the publisher. The founder conversation with Torsten Sauter explains the project and its purpose. A founder interview does not mean that the founder has personally ridden or reviewed every route.

What a catalogue record means

A public record can describe a loop, one-way itinerary or route variant. Several records may use the same road. Record counts are not counts of independently verified unique roads.

Place stories explain locations; they do not independently prove motorcycle access. Recorded distances, durations and source labels retain the scope of the underlying planning data.

What a route line means

Checkpoint corridors and calculated road geometry are different. A navigation release needs its own validation of required stops, direction, access and instructions. An SEO or reading-page build does not qualify that engine.

How dates are used

A page preparation date concerns that page. A source-review date concerns the stated source scope. An observation timestamp concerns a particular status. A fresh download cannot renew an expired observation.

How to correct a problem

Keep the affected page URL, the statement to correct and a primary source or specific observation. The correction report tool prepares a local report; it does not send one automatically.

Three separate checks

Route intent: does it retain the journey you want?

Evidence: what do geometry, notes and sources establish?

Travel date: what applies to your actual visit?
